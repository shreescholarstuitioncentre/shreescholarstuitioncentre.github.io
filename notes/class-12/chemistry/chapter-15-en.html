Chapter 15: POLYMERS

15.1 Polymers: Definition & Related Terms

Polymer (Greek poly = many, meros = parts) = a giant molecule (macromolecule) of very high molecular mass (10³–10⁷ u) formed by joining a large number of small repeating units called monomers.

  • Monomer: simple molecule that forms polymer, e.g. ethene.
  • Polymerisation: process of forming polymer from monomers.
  • Repeating (structural) unit: –CH₂–CH₂– in polythene.
  • Degree of polymerisation (n): number of repeating units in the chain. n = M(polymer) / M(repeating unit).
  • Homopolymer: one type of monomer (polythene). Copolymer: two or more different monomers (Buna-S, Nylon-6,6).

15.3 Effect of Structure on Properties

Linear chains pack closely → high density, high m.p., high tensile strength (HDPE). Branched chains pack poorly → low density, low m.p. (LDPE). Cross-linked → hard, rigid, brittle, infusible (bakelite). Greater crystallinity gives greater strength; intermolecular H-bonding (nylon) gives fibre character.

15.4 Molecular Mass of Polymers

Polymers are polydisperse (chains of different lengths), so average masses are used.

Mn = ΣNᵢMᵢ / ΣNᵢ (number average; by osmometry)   Mw = ΣNᵢMᵢ² / ΣNᵢMᵢ (weight average; by light scattering)
PDI = Mw / Mn; always ≥ 1. PDI = 1 means uniform (monodisperse, e.g. natural polymers like proteins). Synthetic polymers have PDI > 1.

15.5 Methods & Mechanism of Polymerisation

A. Addition (chain growth) – free radical mechanism

Initiator: benzoyl peroxide / H₂O₂ / tert-butyl peroxide.

  1. Initiation: C₆H₅COO–OOCC₆H₅ → 2C₆H₅COO• → 2C₆H₅• + 2CO₂ ; R• + CH₂=CH₂ → R–CH₂–CH₂•
  2. Propagation: R–CH₂–CH₂• + CH₂=CH₂ → R–CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂• … repeated n times.
  3. Termination: two chain radicals combine (coupling) to give a dead polymer.

Polythene: LDPE at 350–570 K, 1000–2000 atm, trace O₂/peroxide. HDPE: Ziegler–Natta catalyst (TiCl₄ + Al(C₂H₅)₃), 333–343 K, 6–7 atm.

B. Condensation (step growth)

Repeated condensation between bifunctional monomers with loss of small molecules (H₂O, HCl, NH₃). Nylon-6,6: hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid, 553 K, high pressure.

C. Copolymerisation

Buna-S: 1,3-butadiene + styrene (Na catalyst). Buna-N: butadiene + acrylonitrile.

D. Vulcanisation

Natural rubber (cis-1,4-polyisoprene) heated with sulphur (3–5%) at 373–415 K forms S cross-links → harder, more elastic, less sticky.

15.7 Biodegradable Polymers

Polymers that are broken down by micro-organisms into harmless products, reducing pollution. PHBV (poly-β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate) is a copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid; used in packaging, orthopaedic devices, controlled drug release. Nylon-2-nylon-6 is an alternating polyamide of glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH) and amino caproic acid.

Solved Numericals (step by step)

Easy: Polythene has Mn = 28000 u. Find degree of polymerisation.
Step 1: Repeating unit –CH₂CH₂–, mass = 12×2+4 = 28 u.
Step 2: n = 28000/28 = 1000.
Intermediate: Nylon-6,6 has n = 200. Find its Mn.
Step 1: Repeat unit C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂ = 144+22+28+32 = 226 u.
Step 2: Mn = 200×226 = 45200 u.
Hard: A sample has 10 chains of M = 20000 and 20 chains of M = 30000. Find Mn, Mw, PDI.
Mn = (10×20000 + 20×30000)/30 = 800000/30 = 26667.
ΣNM² = 10×(4×10⁸) + 20×(9×10⁸) = 2.2×10¹⁰; ΣNM = 8×10⁵.
Mw = 2.2×10¹⁰ / 8×10⁵ = 27500.
PDI = 27500/26667 = 1.03.

Q & A

Q1. Define polymer.
A macromolecule formed by repeated joining of many small monomer units through covalent bonds.
Q2. Difference between addition and condensation polymers?
Addition: monomers with double bonds add without loss of any molecule (polythene). Condensation: bifunctional monomers join with elimination of small molecules like H₂O (nylon-6,6).
Q3. Why is Bakelite infusible?
It has a three-dimensional cross-linked network of covalent bonds; heating does not soften it, so it cannot be remoulded.
Q4. Why is vulcanisation done?
Sulphur cross-links between chains increase hardness, tensile strength and elasticity and make rubber less sticky and water-resistant.
Q5. Distinguish LDPE and HDPE.
LDPE: branched, low density, free-radical, high pressure. HDPE: linear, high density, Ziegler–Natta catalyst, low pressure; stronger and more rigid.
Q6. Write the structure of Buna-S and Neoprene monomers.
Buna-S: CH₂=CH–CH=CH₂ + C₆H₅CH=CH₂. Neoprene: CH₂=C(Cl)–CH=CH₂ (chloroprene).
Q7. What are biodegradable polymers? Give two examples.
Polymers decomposed by bacteria into harmless products. Examples: PHBV, nylon-2-nylon-6.
Q8. Why is nylon a fibre?
Strong H-bonding between amide groups of adjacent chains gives high tensile strength and close packing.
