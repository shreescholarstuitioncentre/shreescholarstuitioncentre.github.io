General Principles and Processes of Isolation of Elements – Class 12 Chemistry, Ch. 6

6.1 Mode of Occurrence of Elements in Nature

Mineral: a natural substance in which a metal or its compound occurs in the earth's crust. Ore: a mineral from which the metal can be extracted economically and conveniently. All ores are minerals but not all minerals are ores (clay contains Al but Al is not extracted from it; bauxite is the ore). Gangue (matrix): earthy impurities (sand, rock) in ore.

6.2 Extraction of Metals

Metallurgy has three main stages: (1) concentration of ore (dressing), (2) extraction of crude metal (conversion to oxide, then reduction), (3) refining (purification). Aluminium ore bauxite (Al₂O₃·2H₂O), iron ore haematite, copper pyrites and zinc blende are the main examples.

6.3 Concentration of Ores

Removing gangue from the ore is called concentration, dressing or benefaction.

1. Hydraulic washing (gravity separation): an upward stream of water carries away lighter gangue; heavy ore settles (oxide ores: haematite, tin stone, gold).
2. Magnetic separation: ore or gangue is magnetic; ore on a conveyor belt passes a magnetic roller (tin stone SnO₂ with wolframite FeWO₄; iron ores).

3. Froth flotation: for sulphide ores. Powdered ore is suspended in water with collectors (pine oil, fatty acids, xanthates) which make the ore wet by oil, and froth stabilisers (cresols, aniline). Air is blown; ore particles rise with the froth, gangue is wetted by water and settles. Depressants separate two sulphides: NaCN selectively stops ZnS from forming froth, so PbS floats (ZnS later with CuSO₄).

4. Leaching: ore dissolved selectively in a chemical.
Bauxite (Bayer's process): Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH + 3H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] (473–523 K, 35–36 bar); impurities Fe₂O₃, TiO₂, SiO₂ remain. CO₂ is passed: 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + CO₂ → Al₂O₃·xH₂O + 2NaHCO₃; then ignite at 1470 K to pure Al₂O₃.
Silver/gold (cyanide process): 4M + 8CN⁻ + 2H₂O + O₂ → 4[M(CN)₂]⁻ + 4OH⁻ (M = Ag, Au), then displacement by zinc: 2[M(CN)₂]⁻ + Zn → [Zn(CN)₄]²⁻ + 2M.

6.4 Extraction of Crude Metal from Concentrated Ore

Calcination: heating ore (carbonate or hydrated oxide) strongly in absence/limited supply of air below melting point: removes moisture, CO₂, volatile impurities. Fe₂O₃·xH₂O → Fe₂O₃ + xH₂O; ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂.
Roasting: heating (usually sulphide) ore in excess air below melting point: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂; 2PbS + 3O₂ → 2PbO + 2SO₂. Calcination is carried out in a reverberatory furnace.
Reduction: oxide + carbon (coke) at high temperature → metal. Smelting: reduction in molten state with flux. Flux: substance added to remove gangue as slag: acidic gangue (SiO₂) + basic flux (CaO) → CaSiO₃; basic gangue (FeO) + acidic flux (SiO₂) → FeSiO₃.

Iron (blast furnace)

Charge: roasted haematite + coke + limestone. Hot air blast. Zones: ~500–800 K: 3Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe₃O₄ + CO₂; Fe₃O₄ + 4CO → 3Fe + 4CO₂; Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2FeO + CO₂. 900–1500 K: FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂; C + CO₂ → 2CO. Hottest zone (~1900 K at bottom): C + O₂ → CO₂. Limestone: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag floats). The product is pig iron (~4% C), remelted gives cast iron; wrought iron (purest, 0.1–0.25% C) is made in a reverberatory furnace lined with haematite.

Copper (from copper pyrites)

Roast: 2CuFeS₂ + O₂ → Cu₂S + 2FeS + SO₂. Smelt with silica in a reverberatory furnace: FeO + SiO₂ → FeSiO₃ (slag). Product matte (Cu₂S + FeS). In the Bessemer converter with silica lining: 2FeS + 3O₂ → 2FeO + 2SO₂; 2Cu₂S + 3O₂ → 2Cu₂O + 2SO₂; 2Cu₂O + Cu₂S → 6Cu + SO₂ (self-reduction). Blister copper (~98% Cu) forms; SO₂ evolution gives blisters.

Zinc

ZnS is roasted to ZnO; ZnO + C → Zn + CO at ~1673 K; zinc distils (boiling point 1180 K) and is collected as zinc dust.

6.5 Thermodynamic Principles of Metallurgy

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. For a reaction to be feasible, ΔG must be negative. ΔG° = −RT ln K. In reducing an oxide with carbon, one couples two reactions so that the net ΔG is negative. Ellingham diagram: graph of ΔG° (per mole of O₂) of formation of oxides vs temperature.

Reading the diagram: (1) Most lines slope upward because gas (O₂) is consumed, so ΔS is negative and −TΔS grows with T. (2) Sudden change of slope marks a phase change (melting/boiling) of metal or oxide. (3) The C → CO line slopes downward (ΔS positive; gas increases), so above a temperature it falls below the metal-oxide lines; there carbon can reduce the oxide. (4) A metal can reduce the oxide of any metal whose line is above it (Al reduces Cr₂O₃, Mg reduces Al₂O₃ in some ranges). (5) Aluminium oxide line is very low, so carbon cannot reduce Al₂O₃ in practice, so electrolysis is used. Limitations: it gives only thermodynamic feasibility; rate (kinetics) and the effect of reactants and products are not shown; K = e^(−ΔG°/RT) holds only at equilibrium.

Why reduce ZnO by C at ~1673 K? Above this temperature C, CO line lies below ZnO line. CO reduces Fe₂O₃ in the lower zone of the blast furnace as C → CO₂ line is below.

6.6 Electrochemical Principles of Metallurgy

Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻ → M. ΔG° = −nFE°. A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from solution (Zn displaces Cu²⁺ and Ag from [Ag(CN)₂]⁻). For highly electropositive metals (Na, Mg, Al) reduction is done by electrolysis of molten salts, since carbon cannot reduce them. Aluminium (Hall–Heroult): pure Al₂O₃ is mixed with Na₃AlF₆ (cryolite) and CaF₂ to lower melting point (>2000 K to ~1140 K) and raise conductivity. Cathode: graphite-lined steel tank, Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al(l). Anode: graphite rods, C + O²⁻ → CO + 2e⁻; C + 2O²⁻ → CO₂ + 4e⁻. Anodes are consumed and replaced. Overall 2Al₂O₃ + 3C → 4Al + 3CO₂. Liquid Al is siphoned.

6.7 Extraction of Non-Metals and Metals by Oxidation

Chlorine from brine: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (E° = −1.36 V as oxidation). Water oxidation (E° = −1.23 V) is thermodynamically easier, but due to overpotential Cl₂ is formed. Gold/silver are oxidised by cyanide into complexes (see leaching). Br₂, I₂ are obtained by oxidising the halide ions with Cl₂.

Electrolytic refining of copper: anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻; cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Electrolyte: acidified CuSO₄. Impurities Ag, Au, Pt fall as anode mud.

Mond process: Ni + 4CO → Ni(CO)₄ (330–350 K); Ni(CO)₄ → Ni + 4CO (450–470 K). van Arkel: Zr + 2I₂ → ZrI₄ (volatile), decomposes on tungsten filament at ~1800 K giving pure Zr.

6.9 Uses of Aluminium, Copper, Zinc and Iron

Al: utensils, aircraft, wires (cheap conductor), foil, thermite welding (Al + Fe₂O₃), paints. Cu: electrical wiring, coins, alloys (brass, bronze), pipes, electroplating. Zn: galvanising iron, batteries (dry cell), brass alloy, paints (ZnO), die-casting. Fe: construction, machines, steel, vehicles, ships, tools; cast iron for pipes and stoves, wrought iron for chains and anchors.

Questions & Answers (short, long, MCQ)

Q1. Differentiate between a mineral and an ore.

A mineral is any natural material containing a metal; an ore is a mineral from which the metal can be extracted profitably. Example: clay (mineral of Al) vs bauxite (ore of Al).

Q2. What is gangue? How is it removed?

Earthy impurities present in an ore. Removed by hydraulic washing, magnetic separation, froth flotation or leaching, and finally by adding flux (slag formation).

Q3. Why is froth flotation used for sulphide ores?

Sulphide ore particles are preferentially wetted by oil (pine oil) while gangue is wetted by water, so ore is carried up with air bubbles.

Q4. What is the role of NaCN in froth flotation?

It acts as a depressant: it stops ZnS from forming froth with PbS, so PbS is separated from ZnS.

Q5. Write the steps of Bayer's process.

Bauxite is digested with conc. NaOH at 473–523 K, 35–36 bar giving Na[Al(OH)₄]; CO₂ is passed to precipitate hydrated Al₂O₃; it is filtered and heated at 1470 K to pure Al₂O₃.

Q6. Differentiate between calcination and roasting.

Calcination: heating in absence/limited air to remove volatile matter (carbonates, hydrates). Roasting: heating in excess air to oxidise sulphide ores, giving oxide and SO₂.

Q7. What is the use of flux? Give example.

It combines with gangue to form fusible slag. CaO (basic flux) removes SiO₂ as CaSiO₃; SiO₂ (acidic flux) removes FeO as FeSiO₃.

Q8. Why is copper matte put in a silica-lined converter?

Silica lining takes up FeO as slag and allows self-reduction of Cu₂O with Cu₂S to blister copper.

Q9. What are pig iron, cast iron and wrought iron?

Pig iron ~4% C from blast furnace; cast iron ~3% C, melted and cast; wrought iron the purest form, 0.1–0.25% C.

Q10. What is an Ellingham diagram? State two uses.

Plot of ΔG° of formation of oxides vs temperature. Uses: tells the temperature at which a reducing agent reduces an oxide, and compares reducing power of metals/carbon.

Q11. Why does the C → CO line in the Ellingham diagram slope downwards?

Two moles of CO gas form from one mole of O₂, so ΔS is positive; ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° becomes more negative as T increases.

Q12. Why is Al₂O₃ not reduced by carbon?

The Al₂O₃ line is far below the C–CO line at feasible temperatures (ΔG stays positive), hence electrolysis of molten Al₂O₃ in cryolite is used.

Q13. Why is cryolite added in Hall–Heroult process?

It lowers the melting point of the mixture (to ~1140 K) and increases electrical conductivity.

Q14. Explain zone refining.

A mobile heater passes along a rod; impurities, which are more soluble in the melt than in the solid, move with the molten zone to one end. Used for Si, Ge, Ga.

Q15. MCQ: Anode mud in electrolytic refining of copper contains (a) Zn (b) Ag and Au (c) Fe (d) Ni

(b) Ag and Au.

Numericals – step by step (Easy → Hard)

Easy: ΔH = −400 kJ, ΔS = −0.2 kJ/K. Find ΔG at 1000 K.

Formula: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Step 1: TΔS = 1000 × (−0.2) = −200 kJ. Step 2: ΔG = −400 − (−200) = −200 kJ (spontaneous).

Intermediate: Mass of Cu deposited by 2 A current passed for 1 hour in CuSO₄ (Cu = 63.5, F = 96500 C).

Step 1: Q = I × t = 2 × 3600 = 7200 C. Step 2: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, so 2F = 193000 C deposits 63.5 g. Step 3: m = 63.5 × 7200 / 193000 = 2.37 g.

Hard: A reduction has ΔH = +500 kJ, ΔS = +0.2 kJ/K. Find the minimum temperature for feasibility.

Step 1: Feasible when ΔG < 0, so ΔH − TΔS < 0. Step 2: T > ΔH/ΔS = 500 / 0.2. Step 3: T > 2500 K. At this limit ΔG = 0.
