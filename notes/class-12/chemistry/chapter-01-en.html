1.1 Classification of Solids

Anisotropy: physical properties (refractive index, conductivity) differ along different directions in crystals – due to different arrangement along different directions.

1.2 Space Lattice and Unit Cell

Space lattice: regular 3-D arrangement of points representing particles in a crystal. Unit cell: the smallest repeating portion which, repeated in 3 directions, generates the whole lattice. Described by edges a, b, c and angles α, β, γ.

Types: Primitive (particles only at corners) · Body-centred (+1 at body centre) · Face-centred (+1 at each face centre) · End-centred (+2 at two opposite faces).

Seven crystal systems: Cubic (a=b=c, all 90°) · Tetragonal (a=b≠c, 90°) · Orthorhombic (a≠b≠c, 90°) · Rhombohedral (a=b=c, α=β=γ≠90°) · Hexagonal (a=b≠c, α=β=90°, γ=120°) · Monoclinic (a≠b≠c, α=γ=90°≠β) · Triclinic (a≠b≠c, all angles ≠90°). With centring these give 14 Bravais lattices.

1.4 Packing in Solids

1-D: C.N. 2. 2-D: square close packing (C.N. 4, AAA…) and hexagonal close packing (C.N. 6, AB AB…, more efficient). 3-D: hcp (ABAB…) and ccp/fcc (ABCABC…), both C.N. 12, efficiency 74%.

Voids: for N atoms in close packing → N octahedral voids and 2N tetrahedral voids. Tetrahedral: r ratio 0.225–0.414; Octahedral: 0.414–0.732.

Packing efficiency = (Z × 4/3 πr³ / a³) × 100. For fcc: 4×(4/3)π(a/2√2)³/a³ = π/(3√2) = 74%. For bcc: 68%; sc: 52.4%. Void fraction = 100 − efficiency.

1.6 Imperfections (Defects) in Solids

Point defects:

  • Stoichiometric: Schottky (equal cations and anions missing; density decreases; in NaCl, KCl, large similar-size ions) and Frenkel (smaller ion shifts to interstitial site; density unchanged; in AgCl, ZnS).
  • Non-stoichiometric: metal excess (F-centres – anion vacancy holds an electron, gives colour: NaCl yellow, KCl violet) and metal deficiency (FeO as Fe₀.₉₅O).
  • Impurity defects: doping NaCl with SrCl₂ creates cation vacancies.

1.7 Properties of Solids

Electrical: conductors (10⁴–10⁷ Ω⁻¹m⁻¹), semiconductors (10⁻⁶–10⁴), insulators (10⁻²⁰–10⁻¹⁰). n-type: Si doped with group 15 (extra electron); p-type: doped with group 13 (hole). Magnetic: diamagnetic (all paired), paramagnetic (unpaired, O₂, Cu²⁺), ferromagnetic (Fe, Co, Ni, CrO₂), antiferromagnetic (MnO), ferrimagnetic (Fe₃O₄). Dielectric: piezoelectric (quartz), pyroelectric, ferroelectric (Rochelle salt).

1.8 Amorphous Solids

No long-range order; irregular cut surfaces; no sharp m.p.; isotropic; slowly flow (glass panes of old buildings thicker at the bottom). Uses: glass, rubber, plastics, amorphous silicon in solar cells.

Example 1: Cu is fcc, a = 361 pm, M = 63.5 g/mol. Find density.

To find: d

Z = 4. a = 361×10⁻¹⁰ = 3.61×10⁻⁸ cm.
a³ = 4.705×10⁻²³ cm³.
d = (4×63.5)/(4.705×10⁻²³×6.022×10²³) = 254/28.33 = 8.97 g/cm³.

Example 2: NaCl (M = 58.5) is fcc with a = 564 pm. Find density.

To find: d

Z = 4; a³ = (5.64×10⁻⁸)³ = 1.794×10⁻²² cm³.
d = 234/(1.794×10⁻²²×6.022×10²³) = 234/108.0 = 2.17 g/cm³.

Example 3: Ag (M = 108), fcc, a = 409 pm. Find density.

To find: d

a³ = 6.84×10⁻²³ cm³; d = 4×108/(6.84×10⁻²³×6.022×10²³) = 432/41.19 = 10.49 g/cm³.

Example 4: fcc metal, a = 361 pm. Find atomic radius.

To find: r

r = a/(2√2) = 361/2.828 = 127.6 pm.

Example 5: Fe is bcc, a = 286 pm. Find atomic radius.

To find: r

r = √3a/4 = 1.732×286/4 = 123.8 pm.

Example 6: Prove packing efficiency of fcc is 74%.

To find: proof

Z = 4; face diagonal = 4r = √2a → a = 2√2 r.
Volume of atoms = 4×(4/3)πr³ = (16/3)πr³. Cell volume = (2√2r)³ = 16√2 r³.
Efficiency = (16πr³/3)/(16√2r³) ×100 = π/(3√2)×100 = 74%.

Example 7: A compound has atoms Y in ccp, X in 2/3 of octahedral voids. Find formula.

To find: formula

Let Y = N. Octahedral voids = N; X = 2N/3. X : Y = 2/3 : 1 = 2 : 3. Formula = X₂Y₃.

Short & Long Questions

Q1. Differentiate crystalline and amorphous solids.

See table in 1.1: crystalline has long-range order, sharp m.p., anisotropy; amorphous has short-range order, no sharp m.p., isotropy.

Q2. What is a unit cell?

The smallest portion of the space lattice which, repeated in three dimensions, generates the entire crystal.

Q3. Why is glass called a supercooled liquid?

It has no long-range order and flows very slowly under gravity, so it is a pseudo solid.

Q4. Why does Frenkel defect not change density?

No ion leaves the crystal; only its position changes, so mass and volume stay same.

Q5. What are F-centres?

Anionic vacancies occupied by electrons; they absorb light and impart colour, e.g. yellow NaCl.

Q6. Why are ionic solids brittle?

A slight shift brings like charges face to face; repulsion cracks the crystal.

Q7. What is coordination number?

Number of nearest neighbours of a particle: sc 6, bcc 8, fcc/hcp 12.

Q8. Define packing efficiency. Give values.

Percentage of cell volume occupied by atoms: sc 52.4%, bcc 68%, hcp/ccp 74%.

Q9. Explain n-type and p-type semiconductors.

n-type: Si/Ge doped with P/As; one extra electron conducts. p-type: doped with B/Al; electron hole conducts.

Q10. Explain Schottky defect with effect on density and examples.

Equal numbers of cations and anions are missing from lattice sites; electrical neutrality kept; density decreases; seen in NaCl, KCl, CsCl, AgBr.
