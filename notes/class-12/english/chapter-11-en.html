Keeping Quiet – Pablo Neruda (Notes)

Poem: Keeping Quiet  |  Poet: Pablo Neruda (1904–1973)  |  Book: Flamingo, Class 12 English (Poetry, Ch.3)

1. About the Poet

Pablo Neruda (real name Neftalí Reyes) was a Chilean poet, diplomat and politician. He won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1971. His poetry covers love, nature and politics. Famous works: Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair, Canto General, Residence on Earth. The poem was written in Spanish and is read here in English translation.

2. Central Idea (≈150 words)

In 'Keeping Quiet', Neruda invites everyone to count to twelve and then become still and silent. This pause is not death or laziness; it is a moment of self-reflection. He imagines that if all people stopped rushing, harming and fighting, the world would change. Fishermen would spare the whales, salt-gatherers would see their wounded hands, and those preparing destructive wars would walk peacefully with their brothers. The poet says we are always busy and do not understand ourselves, which leads to sadness and self-destruction. A moment of silence could bring understanding and unity. He uses the example of nature: winter appears dead, yet spring brings life again. In the same way, stillness leads to renewal. The poem is a message of peace, brotherhood and respect for life and nature, urging mankind to pause, think and choose harmony over violence.

3. Stanza-wise Explanation

Part 1 – The request

The poet asks everyone to count to twelve and stay motionless, speaking no language and moving no arm, for a single strange moment without hurry or engines.

Part 2 – Effects of stillness

Fishermen would stop hurting whales; the salt-gatherer would see his injured hands. Warmongers preparing wars of gas and fire, whose victory would leave no survivors, would put on clean clothes and walk with their brothers in the shade.

Part 3 – Clarification

The poet says this is not inactivity or death. Life is the point. He wants nothing to do with death.

Part 4 – Self-understanding

Because we are single-minded and always moving, we never understand ourselves and threaten our own lives. Perhaps one big silence could end this sadness.

Part 5 – Lesson from earth

Earth teaches when all seems dead, yet later proves alive. So the poet counts to twelve, asks us to be silent, and quietly leaves.

4. Animated Diagram (Count, Earth, Renewal)

Explanation: The clock hand ticks to twelve, the call to pause. The Earth in the centre stands for all of humanity and nature, united in one moment. The plant on the right grows, like spring following winter: silence leads to renewal.

7. Theme, Tone, Message

Themes: peace, introspection, unity, harmony with nature. Tone: calm, gentle, persuasive. Message: A pause to reflect can end violence and restore life.

8. Short and Long Q&A (click to open)

Q1. What does the poet ask us to do at the start of the poem?

To count up to twelve and then keep absolutely still and silent, without speaking in any language.

Q2. Why twelve?

Twelve is the number of hours on a clock and months in a year; it is a short, symbolic pause in time.

Q3. What does the poet mean by 'for once on the face of the earth'?

That never in history have all humans stopped together; he wants one united, worldwide moment of stillness.

Q4. What is the 'strange moment' described?

A moment without hurry, engines or rush, when people pause their constant activity.

Q5. What would fishermen do during this stillness?

They would stop harming whales in the cold sea and leave the creatures in peace.

Q6. What would the man gathering salt do?

He would look at his hurt, wounded hands, realising the pain of his labour.

Q7. Who are the people 'preparing green wars'?

Leaders and soldiers planning wars (with gas, fire, ‘victory with no survivors’); they would stop.

Q8. What would these warmongers do in the silence?

They would put on clean clothes and walk with their brothers in the shade, doing nothing.

Q9. What is meant by 'victory with no survivors'?

A war so destructive that even the winner is wiped out; total futility of war.

Q10. Does the poet mean total inactivity?

No. He clarifies it should not be confused with total inactivity or death; life continues, and he wants nothing to do with death.

Q11. What does 'Life is what it is about' mean?

Life is the main concern; stillness is meant to value and protect life, not to end it.

Q12. What does the poet say about people being single-minded?

We are so busy and rushing that we do not understand ourselves; if we paused, a huge silence might interrupt our sadness.

Q13. What is 'sadness of never understanding ourselves'?

Constant hurry keeps us from self-knowledge; we do not know who we truly are or what we want.

Q14. What is 'threatening ourselves with death'?

Through wars, pollution and destruction we endanger human existence.

Q15. What can the earth teach us?

When everything seems dead in winter, life later proves to be alive in spring; dormancy leads to renewal.

Q16. What is the symbolism of winter and spring?

Winter: apparent death, stillness; spring: rebirth. Silence is not an end but a source of revival.

Q17. What is the last line’s meaning?

The poet says he will count to twelve; now you keep quiet, and I will go: he invites us to reflect on our own.

Q18. What is the central message?

Pause, reflect, give up violence, and live in harmony with each other and nature.

Q19. Name the poetic devices.

Imagery, symbolism, repetition, paradox, personification (earth teaching), alliteration, free verse, enjambment.

Q20. What is the tone of the poem?

Calm, gentle, persuasive, hopeful and reflective.

Q21. What is the paradox in the poem?

Keeping quiet and doing nothing leads to the deepest understanding and brings peace and renewal.

Q22. What is the form of the poem?

A free verse lyric, addressed to the reader; conversational and gentle.

Q23. What is the relationship between silence and brotherhood?

In silence, enemies would become brothers and walk together; silence erases hatred.

Q24. Who is Pablo Neruda?

A Chilean poet and diplomat (1904–1973), Nobel Prize winner for literature in 1971; known for political and love poetry.

9. Long Answer Questions

Q1. What does Neruda mean by keeping quiet? (120 words)

He means a short, shared pause in activity so humans can reflect, not total inactivity or death. If all stopped hurrying, wars would end, nature would be spared and people would understand themselves. Silence would unite people as brothers. Like winter before spring, stillness leads to renewal and new life.

Q2. How does the poet show the harm done by humans to nature and each other?

Fishermen harm whales, salt-gatherers suffer wounded hands, and rulers prepare gas and fire wars with 'victory with no survivors'. These show exploitation, labour pain and destruction. Stillness would stop these.

Q3. What lesson does the earth teach?

The earth shows that apparent death in winter is followed by life in spring. Likewise, silence and pause are not endings but beginnings of renewal and understanding.
