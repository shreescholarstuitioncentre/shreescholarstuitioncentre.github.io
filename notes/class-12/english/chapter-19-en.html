✎ On the Face of It — Susan Hill

Class 12 English • Detailed Notes (Theme • Characters • Summary • Q&A • MCQs)

1. About the Author

Susan Hill (born 1942) is an English novelist and playwright, famous for The Woman in Black. On the Face of It is a one-act play. It deals with loneliness, prejudice, physical disfigurement, and acceptance.

2. Setting & Central Idea

Setting: Mr Lamb's quiet, beautiful garden, full of flowers, trees, bees and sunlight.

Central Idea: People judge others by outward appearance, but real worth lies within. Derry, a boy with a burnt face, learns from old Mr Lamb, who has a tin leg, that life is worth living and that society's rejection should not make one hide from the world.

3. Characters

CharacterDescription
DerryA teenage boy; one side of his face is badly burnt. Lonely, bitter, defensive, hurt by people's reactions. Gradually opens up.
Mr LambAn old, kind man with a tin (artificial) leg lost in the war. Wise, cheerful, loves nature, treats Derry as a normal person.
Mrs DawkinsDerry's mother; over-protective, sad; her fear affects Derry.
Other childrenMock Derry; represent society's cruelty.

4. Summary (Step by Step)

Step 1: Derry comes to Mr Lamb's garden and stands at the gate. He expects to be laughed at, so he speaks roughly and says he is only there because of the garden's reputation.

Step 2: Mr Lamb welcomes him without any reaction to his face. Derry is surprised, because everyone else has either stared or run.

Step 3: Derry says he is "different" and describes his sufferings: children call him names, his mother worries and hides, and he feels no one wants him around.

Step 4: Mr Lamb shows his tin leg and tells Derry that he too is "different", yet he lives happily. He says that the only way to face the world is to face it, not hide from it.

Step 5: Mr Lamb talks about the garden, the bees, and his love of life. He wants Derry to come back. Derry slowly relaxes.

Step 6: Mr Lamb offers him a job and asks him to come to the garden whenever he likes. Derry, however, hears his mother's cries and the world's pressures again, so the ending is open but hopeful.

Step 7: In the end Mr Lamb dies in an accident while picking apples (the ladder falls). Derry runs to get help but is shattered. Even so he decides not to withdraw into his shell, and the lesson he carried is that one should live life fully.

6. Themes

Appearance vs. reality: The title means "judging by outward looks". Loneliness: both Derry and Lamb are lonely. Prejudice: society mocks the different. Acceptance & courage: face life openly. Nature as healer: the garden brings peace.

7. Symbols & Literary Devices

Garden = a world of acceptance. Tin leg = shared suffering. Bees = life, work and sweetness. Burnt face = outward flaw. Irony: a garden of beauty hosts two "disfigured" people. Dialogue carries emotion; stage directions show mood.

8. Short & Long Q&A

Q1. Who is Derry? Why is he unhappy?

Derry is a teenage boy with half his face burnt. He is unhappy as people stare, laugh or avoid him, and he feels unwanted and lonely.

Q2. How does Mr Lamb treat Derry?

He treats him as a normal person, without pity or fear, which is why Derry opens up.

Q3. What does Mr Lamb mean by "Only the outside is damaged"? (idea)

He means that physical scars do not reduce one's worth, since a person's true self is inside.

Q4. Why does Mr Lamb show his tin leg?

To show Derry that he too is "different" and that suffering can be borne with a positive attitude.

Q5. What is the role of the garden?

It is a refuge from a judging world, a symbol of life, growth and acceptance.

Q6. Why does Derry's mother worry about him?

She fears he will be hurt; her over-protection ends up isolating him.

Q7. Explain the title.

"On the face of it" means "at first glance". The play shows that first impressions based on looks are misleading.

Q8. Why does Derry visit the garden at first?

He is curious and defiant, partly to test whether the old man will reject him too.

Q9 (Long). Bring out the message of the play.

The play teaches us not to judge by looks, to treat everyone with respect, and to face life with courage. Derry, hurt by society, finds in Mr Lamb a friend whose own loss makes him empathetic. Through Lamb's words and death, Derry learns that hiding does not help; one must live fully despite flaws.

Q10 (Long). Character sketch of Mr Lamb.

Kind, wise, cheerful, nature-loving, empathetic, accepting and brave. He lost a leg in the war but never lost hope. He is a father-like guide for Derry.

Q11 (Long). Compare Derry and Mr Lamb.

Both have physical differences and are lonely. Derry is bitter and withdrawn; Lamb is cheerful and open. Lamb's attitude shows the way Derry could take.
