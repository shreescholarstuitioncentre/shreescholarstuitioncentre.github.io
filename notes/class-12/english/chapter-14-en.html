Aunt Jennifer’s Tigers – Adrienne Rich (Notes)

Poem: Aunt Jennifer’s Tigers  |  Poet: Adrienne Rich (1929–2012)  |  Book: Flamingo, Class 12 English (Poetry, Ch.6)

1. About the Poet

Adrienne Rich was an American poet, essayist and feminist. Her poetry deals with women’s experience, identity, freedom and politics. Famous works: A Change of World (1951, which contains this poem), Diving into the Wreck, Of Woman Born. She is known as one of the most influential voices of feminist poetry.

2. Central Idea (≈150 words)

'Aunt Jennifer’s Tigers' tells the story of an oppressed woman who finds freedom through art. Aunt Jennifer, an elderly woman dominated by her husband, embroiders bold tigers on a panel. The tigers are bright, fearless and free, unlike the aunt, who is timid and trembling. Her fingers can barely pull the needle because of the heavy burden of her wedding band, a symbol of marriage and male authority. The poem shows the contrast between the tigers and the aunt: she cannot be free in life, so she creates freedom through her art. The poet says that even after Aunt Jennifer’s death, when her terrified hands lie still, marked by her ordeals, the tigers she made will continue prancing proudly and fearlessly. The poem is a protest against patriarchal oppression and a tribute to women’s creativity and spirit, which can survive suffering and inspire freedom.

3. Stanza-wise Explanation

Stanza 1 – The tigers

Aunt Jennifer’s tigers stride across a panel, bright topaz-coloured animals in a green world. They are not afraid of the men beneath the trees. They walk with graceful, noble confidence. They represent freedom and courage.

Stanza 2 – Aunt Jennifer

Aunt Jennifer’s fingers tremble while she embroiders; the ivory needle is hard for her to pull. The massive wedding band of her husband weighs heavily on her hand, a symbol of the burden of marriage and her lack of freedom.

Stanza 3 – After death

When she dies, her hands will lie still, with the marks of her suffering. But the tigers she created will go on prancing, proud and unafraid, showing that her spirit and desire for freedom live on in art.

4. Animated Diagram (Tigers vs. Aunt)

Explanation: On the left, the embroidered tiger (moving) strides freely in a green panel: courage and freedom. On the right, Aunt Jennifer’s trembling hand with the heavy ring and the needle: fear and oppression. The contrast is the heart of the poem.

7. Theme, Tone, Message

Themes: women’s oppression, freedom, art as escape. Tone: sympathetic, critical, defiant. Message: A woman’s spirit cannot be fully suppressed; art keeps it alive.

8. Short and Long Q&A (click to open)

Q1. Who is Aunt Jennifer?

An elderly woman who embroiders tigers on a panel; she lives under the control of her dominating husband.

Q2. What are the tigers in the poem?

They are embroidered on a panel by Aunt Jennifer. They are symbols of freedom, strength, courage and her suppressed desires.

Q3. How are the tigers described?

Bright, topaz-coloured, fearless, striding proudly in a green world, sleek and confident.

Q4. Why do the tigers not fear the men?

Because they are fearless, strong and self-assured; they represent the courage Aunt Jennifer herself lacks.

Q5. What does 'chivalric certainty' suggest?

The tigers move with the noble, confident bearing of a knight; they are free from doubt or fear.

Q6. Why do Aunt Jennifer’s fingers flutter?

Because she is old, nervous and afraid; her fingers tremble and she struggles to hold the needle.

Q7. What is 'ivory needle'?

Her needle, smooth and pale; even this light thing feels hard to pull because of her weakness and fear.

Q8. What does the 'massive weight of Uncle’s wedding band' symbolise?

The heavy burden of marriage: oppression, domination and lack of freedom in her married life.

Q9. Why is the wedding band called 'massive'?

It is not a physical weight but an emotional burden, the load of duty, authority and suffering.

Q10. What contrast is shown between Aunt and the tigers?

Aunt is weak, timid, trapped; the tigers are bold, free, fearless. She creates what she cannot be.

Q11. Why does Aunt Jennifer create tigers?

Embroidery is her escape: through the tigers, she expresses her longing for freedom, strength and courage.

Q12. What will happen to her hands after death?

Her hands, once terrified, will lie still, still marked by the ordeals and suffering of her marriage.

Q13. What are 'the ordeals she was mastered by'?

The hardships, fear and suffering of a life dominated by her husband.

Q14. What will happen to the tigers after her death?

They will go on 'prancing' proudly, unafraid, keeping her wish for freedom alive forever.

Q15. What is the symbolism of the tigers?

Courage, freedom, rebellion, creativity and a woman’s suppressed desires.

Q16. What is the symbolism of the wedding band?

Patriarchal control and the burden of marriage.

Q17. What is the theme of the poem?

Women’s oppression in a patriarchal society and the power of art as an escape and a rebellion.

Q18. What is the tone of the poem?

Sympathetic, sad, critical and at the end defiant and hopeful.

Q19. Name the poetic devices.

Symbolism, contrast, imagery, alliteration, enjambment, irony, rhyming couplets, personification.

Q20. What is the rhyme scheme?

aabb (rhyming couplets) in three four-line stanzas.

Q21. Which movement does the poem relate to?

Feminism, it highlights the struggle of women for freedom and equality.

Q22. What is the irony of the poem?

Aunt Jennifer is a prisoner, but her creations are free; her art outlives her suffering.

Q23. What does the poet’s message say?

Women must be free from oppression; creativity can express and preserve their spirit even if life is limited.

Q24. Who is Adrienne Rich?

An American poet and feminist (1929–2012), known for poetry about women’s experience, politics and identity.

9. Long Answer Questions

Q1. How does the poet contrast Aunt Jennifer and her tigers? (120 words)

Aunt Jennifer is old, timid and under her husband’s control, her fingers trembling and burdened by her wedding band. Her tigers are bold, bright, proud and fearless. They are everything she cannot be. Through the contrast, the poet shows her suppressed desire for freedom and how art lets her express it.

Q2. What does the wedding band symbolise?

The 'massive' wedding band symbolises the burden of marriage and her husband’s power. It makes even a light needle hard to pull, showing how oppression weighs on her body and spirit.

Q3. What is the significance of the last stanza?

Even after her death, her terrified hands lie still, marked by suffering, but her tigers continue to prance proudly. This shows that art outlives the artist and that women’s spirit and desire for freedom cannot be killed.
