Indigo

Louis Fischer - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.5)

Chapter Summary

In 1916 Rajkumar Shukla, a poor peasant of Champaran, persuaded Gandhi to visit Bihar and help the sharecroppers forced to grow indigo under the tinkathia system. Gandhi gathered facts, was ordered to leave and refused, and went to court at Motihari. Thousands of peasants supported him. The case was dropped, and after an inquiry the landlords agreed to refund 25 per cent. Gandhi also started schools and health work, teaching self-reliance and fearlessness.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Indigo'?

Louis Fischer, an American journalist, who wrote it from his biography The Life of Mahatma Gandhi.

Q2. Who was Rajkumar Shukla?

A poor, illiterate peasant of Champaran who was determined to get Gandhi to visit his district and help the sharecroppers.

Q3. Where did Shukla first meet Gandhi?

At the 1916 Congress session in Lucknow, and later followed Gandhi to Cawnpore and Ahmedabad.

Q4. What was the tinkathia system?

Peasants had to plant three twentieths (about 15%) of their land with indigo for the landlord as rent.

Q5. Why did the landlords want compensation?

When synthetic indigo was developed in Germany, the landlords no longer needed natural indigo, so they demanded compensation for releasing the peasants from the arrangement.

Q6. What happened at Motihari court?

Gandhi was summoned for defying the order to leave. He pleaded guilty. The magistrate postponed the verdict and finally the case was dropped.

Q7. What did the landlords finally agree to?

To refund 25 per cent of the money they had illegally taken from the peasants.

Q8. What did Gandhi teach the peasants?

To be free from fear and not depend on outside help; he asked C. F. Andrews not to join, saying they must learn self-reliance.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe how Gandhi's Champaran campaign began and developed.

Rajkumar Shukla begged Gandhi to visit Champaran. Gandhi went to Muzaffarpur and then Motihari, gathering facts about sharecroppers. The authorities ordered him to leave but he defied it and prepared to face jail. Thousands of peasants gathered at the court. The magistrate and the government retreated. An official inquiry was ordered, a commission was set, and a 25 per cent refund was agreed. It was the first act of civil disobedience in India.

Q10. How did Gandhi establish self-reliance and social reform in Champaran?

He opened primary schools in six villages with volunteers like Kasturba, Devdas, Mahadev Desai and Narhari Parikh. He also appointed a doctor to give medical aid and taught villagers hygiene and cleanliness. He believed that India's freedom needed self-reliant, fearless and healthy people. He did not want outside lawyers, and said they must not depend on C. F. Andrews.

Q11. What is the central message of 'Indigo'?

Moral courage and non-violent resistance can defeat injustice. Gandhi shows that leadership means serving the poor and removing fear. The Champaran victory taught Indians that British power could be challenged and prepared them for the freedom movement.
