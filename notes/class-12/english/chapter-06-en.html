Poets and Pancakes

Asokamitran - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.6)

Chapter Summary

Asokamitran recalls his years at Gemini Studios in Madras, where he worked as a clerk in the Story Department. He describes the busy make-up department, where actors used Pancake make-up, and the odd staff members: the Legal Adviser, the poet Kothamangalam Subbu and others. The film world was a mix of glamour, humour and wasted talent. The visit of the English poet Stephen Spender, editor of Encounter, highlights the gap between the studio staff and world literature.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Poets and Pancakes'?

Asokamitran, a Tamil writer, whose real name was Jagadisa Thyagarajan. The chapter is translated from Tamil, and it describes his years at Gemini Studios, Madras.

Q2. What was Gemini Studios?

A famous film studio in Madras founded by S. S. Vasan. It made well-known films, including Chandralekha.

Q3. Why does the title mention pancakes?

'Pancake' is the make-up brand used at Gemini Studios. The make-up department used it to prepare the actors.

Q4. What was the author's job at Gemini Studios?

He worked in the Story Department as a clerk, cutting out newspaper clippings and filing them, though he never found it very purposeful.

Q5. Who was Kothamangalam Subbu?

A poet and a staff member at Gemini Studios, loved for his humour and talent, who headed the story department.

Q6. Who was the Legal Adviser?

A lawyer in the studio, who was treated like a special member of the staff and hardly ever visited the studio premises.

Q7. Who was Stephen Spender?

An English poet and editor of the magazine Encounter, who visited the studio, and nobody in the studio could understand why.

Q8. What did the author discover about Encounter?

Years later it emerged that Encounter had been funded by the CIA; this made the author reflect on how poets could be used.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the working of the make-up department at Gemini Studios.

The make-up department was a large hall with many mirrors. All actors and extras used make-up in the same room, from the star to the crowd. Powders, lipsticks and pancake, a brand of make-up, were used. The make-up man was fussy and artists were treated in order of importance. It was an interesting place full of small drama, jokes and fun, and ordinary people turned into characters.

Q10. Describe the atmosphere in the Story Department.

The Story Department had talented men like Kothamangalam Subbu. They wrote songs, poems and plays, but there was little work. They spent time in lively chats and humour. Poets were respected in name, but they had no clear role. The author as a clerk found it a place of lazy brilliance and also of unfulfilled talent, and he was frustrated by the lack of purpose.

Q11. What is the central message of 'Poets and Pancakes'?

The chapter gently satirises the film world, where poets and artists have to work in a business, and where talent often goes unused. It humorously shows how people at Gemini lived and how England's poet's visit shows the gap between literature and ordinary people. It is also a nostalgic memory of Gemini Studios.
