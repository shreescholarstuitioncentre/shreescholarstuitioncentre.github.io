Deep Water

William O. Douglas - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.3)

Chapter Summary

Douglas recalls his childhood terror of water. At three or four he was knocked down by waves on a California beach. At ten or eleven he decided to learn swimming at the Y.M.C.A. pool in Yakima, but a bully threw him into the deep end. He nearly drowned and lost consciousness. Haunted by fear for years, he hired an instructor who trained him patiently with a rope and pulley. He finally swam in lakes and rivers and learned that fear is the real enemy.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Deep Water'?

William O. Douglas, an American Supreme Court Justice. The chapter is from his autobiography Of Men and Mountains.

Q2. Why did Douglas want to learn swimming?

He wanted to enjoy water sports with friends and to overcome his terrible fear of water after the pool accident.

Q3. How did his fear of water begin?

At three or four he was knocked down by waves on a California beach. Terrified, he nearly drowned and the fear stayed with him.

Q4. What happened at the Y.M.C.A. pool in Yakima?

A big boy threw him into the deep end. He sank, swallowed water, panicked, and lost consciousness before being rescued.

Q5. What plan did he make at the bottom of the pool?

He planned to touch the bottom, spring up like a cork and paddle to the edge. His plan failed because panic paralysed his legs.

Q6. How did the instructor teach him to swim?

He put a belt around Douglas with a rope through a pulley and trained him for many weeks, one limb at a time.

Q7. Where did he test his courage later?

He swam in Lake Wentworth in New Hampshire and in rivers; he found the fear sometimes returned.

Q8. What is the message of the lesson?

Fear can be conquered by will and practice; as the story ends he says that fear is the only thing we have to fear.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe Douglas's near-drowning experience in the pool.

He went to the Y.M.C.A. pool in Yakima to practise alone. A big bruiser of a boy picked him up and threw him in the deep end. He sank nine feet, terrified, with a plan to touch the bottom and spring up like a cork. His lungs ached and he rose, but water filled him and he sank again and cried. Legs paralysed, he became sleepy and lost consciousness. After being pulled out and revived, he was shaken and carried the fear for years.

Q10. How did Douglas conquer his fear of water?

He hired an instructor who trained him patiently for several months with a rope and pulley. First he taught him to put face in water and blow bubbles, then to kick and move arms one by one, then the full stroke. Then Douglas practised alone and swam in a lake. When fear returned he swam harder until it vanished. He thus learned that to overcome fear one needs will, practice and a guide.

Q11. What does 'Deep Water' teach us?

That fear is the real enemy. Roosevelt's words, 'the only thing we have to fear is fear itself', fit perfectly. Success needs patience, trust and a good teacher. Facing fear step by step makes one confident and free to enjoy life.
