Going Places

A. R. Barton - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.8)

Chapter Summary

Sophie, a teenage girl from a modest family, dreams of becoming a famous person: an actress or the owner of a boutique where she would meet the young footballer Danny Casey. Her practical friend Jansie and her brother Geoff doubt her stories. Sophie even tells fantasies as if they were true. In the end, reality returns, and she is left with quiet disappointment. The story shows the clash between dreams and reality in adolescence.

Characters

Themes

Dreams vs reality; adolescent fantasy; hero worship; class and ambition.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Going Places'?

A. R. Barton, a writer who shows the dreams of young people in a small working-class town.

Q2. Who is Sophie?

A teenage girl who dreams of becoming a famous actress or owning a boutique, and who imagines meeting the footballer Danny Casey.

Q3. Who is Jansie?

Sophie's practical friend, who is level-headed and tries to keep Sophie's feet on the ground.

Q4. Who is Geoff?

Sophie's elder brother who works hard and is sceptical of her wild dreams; he teases her about them.

Q5. Who is Danny Casey?

A famous young footballer, Sophie's hero, who plays for a leading club and is admired by the whole town.

Q6. What does Sophie dream of?

She imagines meeting Danny Casey at the boutique where she works and becoming a successful and famous person.

Q7. Did Sophie meet Danny Casey?

No. It is her fantasy, and she builds stories about him, which her brother and friend see as false.

Q8. What is the theme of the story?

The clash between dreams and reality, and the way adolescents use imagination to escape ordinary life.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe Sophie's character.

Sophie is imaginative, restless and ambitious. She hates her dull life and wants to be famous. She builds castles in the air and believes her fantasies, even telling people she met Danny Casey. She is also sensitive and hurt when Geoff mocks her. At the end she faces reality with a sad silence, which shows she is growing up. Her character shows the dreamy mind of an adolescent.

Q10. Compare Sophie and Jansie.

Sophie is a dreamer, Jansie is practical. Sophie wants glamour and success, while Jansie accepts her simple life and thinks of marriage and work. Sophie lies and fantasises, while Jansie speaks honestly. Both are friends, and Jansie's reality check reveals Sophie's fancy. Their contrast shows the two attitudes of youth: hope and caution.

Q11. What does 'Going Places' teach us?

Dreams give hope and energy but must be joined with effort and realism. Escape into fantasy leaves one disappointed. Young people should aim high, but must also accept reality and work to achieve their goals. Parents and friends can guide them, and family bonds matter.
