The Rattrap

Selma Lagerlöf - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.4)

Chapter Summary

A poor peddler sells rattraps and believes the world is a big rattrap. A kind old crofter shelters him and trusts him, but the peddler steals his thirty kronor. Lost in a forest, he feels caught in the rattrap himself. At Ramsjö ironworks the ironmaster mistakes him for an old comrade, Captain von Stahle. The ironmaster's daughter Edla treats him with kindness. He leaves the stolen money and a rattrap as her Christmas gift, with a letter of thanks.

Themes

Kindness vs temptation; redemption through trust; loneliness; world as a trap.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'The Rattrap'?

Selma Lagerlöf, a Swedish writer and the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1909).

Q2. What was the peddler's trade?

He sold small rattraps made of wire, which he made from material begged from stores. This did not earn enough, so he also begged and stole.

Q3. What idea struck him about the world?

He thought the whole world is a big rattrap, offering riches and joys as bait, but whoever touches the bait is caught.

Q4. How did the crofter treat the peddler?

He was kind and shared porridge, tobacco and a game of cards, and told him about the thirty kronor he had saved from selling his cow's milk.

Q5. What did the peddler do the next morning?

He took the thirty kronor from the leather pouch hanging on the window frame and left with it.

Q6. Why did he get lost in the forest?

He left the highway to avoid being caught and walked into the forest, which seemed a maze with no way out, like a rattrap.

Q7. Why did the ironmaster invite him home?

He mistook the peddler for an old regimental comrade, Captain von Stahle, and wanted to help him.

Q8. What did the peddler leave for Edla?

A small rattrap as a Christmas gift, with the stolen money and a letter signed Captain von Stahle.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the character of the peddler.

The peddler is poor, lonely and cunning. He steals from the kind crofter, showing weakness for temptation. Yet he is philosophical and sensitive; he sees the world as a rattrap. When treated with kindness by Edla, his goodness is reawakened. He returns the money and gives her the rattrap, thus showing he has changed.

Q10. How did Edla Willmansson's kindness change the peddler?

Edla invited him to spend Christmas with them, said he need not fear and treated him as a guest. She did not judge him, and she was sorry for him. Her gentleness made him realise she trusted him. So he left the stolen money and a letter, calling himself the captain, who was raised to a better life by the Christmas gift.

Q11. What is the central message of 'The Rattrap'?

Human goodness can awaken through love and trust, not punishment. The world is a trap of temptation, but kindness can set us free. It also shows that outward appearance and past mistakes do not define a person.

Example 1 (Easy)
Given: The peddler visited the crofter. To find: What he received. Solution: Step 1: The old man welcomed him. Step 2: He served porridge. Step 3: He gave tobacco. Step 4: They played cards. Step 5: He offered a place to sleep.
Example 2 (Intermediate)
Given: The peddler stole thirty kronor. To find: Why was he trapped? Solution: Step 1: He feared being caught. Step 2: He left the road for the forest. Step 3: He wandered in circles. Step 4: He realised he fell into the rattrap by greed.
Example 3 (Hard)
Given: 'The world is a rattrap.' To find: Explain with irony. Solution: Step 1: The peddler believes the world traps everyone with bait. Step 2: He himself is caught by the crofter's money. Step 3: Edla's kindness frees him. Step 4: So the same world can offer freedom through love.
