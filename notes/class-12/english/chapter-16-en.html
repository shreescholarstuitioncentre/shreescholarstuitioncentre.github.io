The Tiger King

Kalki - Class 12 English (Vistas, Ch.2)

Chapter Summary

At the birth of Maharaja Sir Jilani Jung Jung Bahadur, an astrologer predicted that a tiger would kill him. The Maharaja grew up determined to defy fate by killing a hundred tigers. He banned others from hunting tigers, and the tigers of Pratibandhapuram were nearly wiped out. In the end, a wooden toy tiger bought for his son pricked his hand, the wound became infected, and he died. The story is a satire on foolish pride and power.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'The Tiger King'?

Kalki (R. Krishnamurthy), a Tamil writer. The story is a satire on the power and foolishness of a ruler.

Q2. Who was the Tiger King?

Maharaja Sir Jilani Jung Jung Bahadur of Pratibandhapuram, nicknamed the Tiger King because of his passion for hunting tigers.

Q3. What was predicted at the Maharaja's birth?

A wise astrologer said that the child, a ten-day-old prince, would one day be killed by a tiger.

Q4. How did the Maharaja react to the prophecy?

He grew up determined to kill tigers; he vowed to hunt a hundred and so escape the prophecy.

Q5. What did the Maharaja do to protect his hunting?

He banned others from hunting tigers, and threatened officials who failed to find tigers.

Q6. What happened to the tigers in the kingdom?

Because of heavy hunting, they were nearly wiped out and the Maharaja got a hard time finding them.

Q7. How did the Maharaja die?

On his son's birthday he bought a wooden toy tiger; a splinter pricked his hand, it became infected and he died.

Q8. What does the story satirise?

The foolish pride of kings, flatterers, and the way human arrogance cannot defeat fate.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the character of the Maharaja.

The Maharaja is proud, stubborn, cruel and obsessed with power. He thinks he can defy fate by killing tigers. He rules harshly and punishes anyone who opposes him. He is also gullible and misled by flatterers. His end by a toy tiger shows irony, since he escaped all real tigers but fell to a small wooden one.

Q10. How is the story a satire?

Kalki mocks absolute rulers who think they are above fate and law. The Maharaja's rule is shown as absurd: he orders a ban on tiger hunting for others but hunts himself. The officials and the Dewan flatter him. The tiny wooden toy that kills him is a joke on his hunting pride. It teaches that arrogance and cruelty bring downfall.

Q11. What lesson does 'The Tiger King' teach?

No one can escape fate by power or wealth. Pride and cruelty to animals lead to ruin. Rulers must act wisely and kindly. Nature should be protected, and flattering people mislead us.

Solved Examples (Given / To find / Solution)

Example 1 (Easy)
Given: The astrologer predicted the prince's death. To find: What was predicted? Solution: Step 1: The prince was ten days old. Step 2: The astrologer studied his horoscope. Step 3: He said a tiger would kill him. Step 4: The Maharaja later hunted tigers to avoid this.
Example 2 (Intermediate)
Given: The Maharaja banned tiger hunting for others. To find: Why? Solution: Step 1: He wanted all tigers for himself. Step 2: He wanted to reach a hundred. Step 3: Other hunters would reduce the tigers. Step 4: So he forbade everyone but himself.
Example 3 (Hard)
Given: A toy tiger killed the Maharaja. To find: What is the irony? Solution: Step 1: He killed many real tigers. Step 2: He feared the prophecy. Step 3: A small wooden tiger's splinter infected him. Step 4: Thus fate defeated him in an unexpected way.
