✎ Evans Tries an O-Level — Colin Dexter

Class 12 English (Vistas) • Detailed Notes

1. About the Author

Colin Dexter (1930–2017) was a British crime writer, best known for the Inspector Morse novels. His stories have clever plots and surprise endings, and this story is a comic-crime tale of cunning.

2. Central Idea

A clever convict, James Roderick Evans ("Evans the Break"), uses the harmless-looking idea of sitting an O-Level German exam in prison to plan a perfect escape. The story shows that appearances are deceptive, and that over-confident authority can be outwitted by careful planning.

4. Summary (Step by Step)

Step 1: Evans, a famous escaper, is held at Oxford prison. He asks to take O-Level German. The Governor, suspicious but not wishing to appear fearful, agrees and writes to the Examinations Board.

Step 2: The Board sends a proctor, Rev. McLeery. Strict arrangements are made: Evans's razor and nail scissors are taken, and a prison officer stays in the cell.

Step 3: On the exam day Jackson and Stephens bring Evans in. Evans pleads that he needs his old hat for luck, and Jackson lets him keep it, though the hat actually hides his new haircut.

Step 4: During the exam a phone call from the "Assistant Secretary" gives "corrections" to the paper. These are a hidden signal about where to go after the escape (a hotel). He also finds out the exam's start time.

Step 5: About three minutes before the end, another call distracts Jackson and Stephens. In that gap Evans's plan is carried out, and a scene of blood and confusion is created.

Step 6: Evans, disguised as the proctor, leaves the prison, escorted by an officer who is also his accomplice. Stephens later finds the man left in the cell covered in blood, and the prison is thrown into panic.

Step 7: The Governor puts the clues together and traces Evans to the Golden Lion Hotel, Chipping Norton. Evans explains how he planned it, and he has the last laugh.

6. Themes

Intelligence and deception: Evans outthinks everyone. Appearance vs. reality: fake proctor, fake "corrections", fake accident. Over-confidence of authority: the Governor never checks the German teacher or the callers. Humour and irony: the title suggests a study task, but it is a plan to escape.

7. Symbols & Devices

Hat = hides the haircut/disguise. Phone calls = tools of misdirection. Time details = hints of the plan. Irony is used all through. Suspense and a surprise ending are typical of crime fiction.

8. Q&A

Q1. Why was Evans called "Evans the Break"?

Because he had escaped from prison three times and was famous for breaking out.

Q2. Why did Evans want to take the O-Level German exam?

Officially to improve himself, but really to use the exam as cover for a carefully planned escape.

Q3. Why did the Governor agree?

He did not want to appear afraid of Evans and also thought there was a slight possibility that Evans was sincere.

Q4. What security arrangements were made?

Razor and nail scissors were taken away, a prison officer stayed with Evans, and the cell was checked through the peephole.

Q5. What was the role of the telephone calls?

The first call passed hidden information about where to go, and a second call distracted the officers just before the exam ended.

Q6. Why did Evans insist on wearing his hat?

It was supposedly for luck, but it hid his changed haircut and helped his disguise.

Q7. Where was Evans finally found?

The Governor traced him to the Golden Lion Hotel in Chipping Norton.

Q8 (Long). How does the story show that appearances can be deceptive?

The exam looks like self-improvement, the proctor looks genuine, the phone calls seem routine, and the bloody scene looks like an accident. All of these are parts of Evans's plan, so everyone is fooled.

Q9 (Long). Character sketch of Evans.

Evans is intelligent, patient, humorous, a master planner and a good judge of human nature. He plays on the officers' small weaknesses and uses friends outside the prison. He is also cheerful and well-mannered.

Q10 (Long). Why is the Governor partly responsible for the escape?

He never verified the callers and the "teacher" and relied on assumptions. His wish to avoid looking worried also made him loosen the security.
