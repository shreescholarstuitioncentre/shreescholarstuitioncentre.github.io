The Third Level

Jack Finney - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.9)

Chapter Summary

Charley, a New Yorker, tells how he discovered a third level at Grand Central Station that leads to the year 1894. His psychiatrist friend Sam Weiner calls it a waking dream, an escape from the tension of modern life. Charley sees gas lamps and old clothes and tries to buy tickets to Galesburg, but his modern money is refused. He later cannot find the level again. Then Sam disappears, and Charley finds an old letter dated 1894 from Galesburg, suggesting the third level was real.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'The Third Level'?

Jack Finney, an American writer of science fiction and fantasy.

Q2. Where does the story take place?

In Grand Central Station, New York, which has two levels, yet the narrator Charley finds a mysterious third level.

Q3. What did Charley's psychiatrist friend say about the third level?

Sam Weiner said it was a waking dream and a way of escaping the stress and worries of modern life.

Q4. What did Charley notice on the third level?

The station had gas lamps, old-fashioned clothes, a wooden-looking ticket window and a newspaper dated 1894.

Q5. Why could he not buy tickets?

The ticket clerk refused to accept his modern currency, as the bills were of a different size and he needed old-style money.

Q6. How did Charley get old currency?

He went to a coin dealer, sold him $800 and got about $200 of old bills.

Q7. What did Charley's friend Sam do?

Sam disappeared, and later Charley found that he had gone to Galesburg, Illinois, in 1894.

Q8. What was in Sam's letter?

It was an old first-day cover (an envelope) posted from Galesburg in 1894, asking Charley to join him in the peaceful past.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe Charley's visit to the third level.

Charley, rushing home, takes a wrong turn at Grand Central. He finds a place with old-fashioned clothes, gas lamps and an 1894 newspaper. Everything is calm and old. The clerk will not accept his modern money and the date on the paper shows 1894. He goes back to the present to get old bills but cannot find the third level again. This shows he longs for the peaceful past.

Q10. What does the third level symbolise?

The third level symbolises escape into a peaceful past from the tension, hurry and fear of modern life. People in 1894 were not worried about wars and the fast pace of life. Charley and Sam wish for a safe, small-town world like Galesburg. It reveals the human craving for peace and a simpler life, and is also a fantasy world.

Q11. How does the story blend fantasy and reality?

Finney starts with an everyday setting of Grand Central and adds a realistic psychiatrist's explanation. The old newspapers and tickets add proof of the past, and Sam's 1894 letter confirms the fantasy. The ending leaves the reader uncertain: it may be a dream, or it may be time travel. This ambiguity is the charm of the story.

Example 2 (Intermediate)
Given: The clerk refused Charley's money. To find: Why? Solution: Step 1: Modern bills were larger and different. Step 2: Old-style bills were used in 1894. Step 3: The clerk saw them as strange. Step 4: So Charley had to get old currency.
Example 3 (Hard)
Given: Sam's letter from 1894. To find: What does it prove? Solution: Step 1: The letter came in an old envelope. Step 2: It was dated 1894. Step 3: It was from Galesburg. Step 4: It suggests the third level was real and Sam found peace there.
