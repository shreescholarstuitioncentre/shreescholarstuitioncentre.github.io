Journey to the End of the Earth

Tishani Doshi - Class 12 English (Vistas, Ch.3)

Chapter Summary

Tishani Doshi describes her voyage to Antarctica on a student expedition. She crosses the Antarctic Convergence, sees the white continent of ice and learns about Gondwana, the ancient supercontinent. Scientists study its ice and fossils to understand earth's climate history. She is awed by the pure nature, but warns that climate change threatens it. The chapter urges care for the earth.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Journey to the End of the Earth'?

Tishani Doshi, an Indian poet and writer who describes her visit to Antarctica.

Q2. Why did the author go to Antarctica?

She joined a student expedition to learn about the continent, its ice and the signs of climate change.

Q3. What is Antarctica called and why?

The 'end of the earth' or the white continent, because it is the southernmost, coldest and most remote place, covered with ice.

Q4. What is the Antarctic Convergence?

The zone where the cold polar waters meet the warmer waters of the oceans, which are rich in marine life.

Q5. What is Gondwana?

An ancient supercontinent, about 650 million years ago, of which India, Africa, Australia, South America and Antarctica were parts.

Q6. What does Antarctica's ice preserve?

It stores earth's climate history; ice cores and fossils show how the planet changed over millions of years.

Q7. How did the author feel about the trip?

She felt awe at the pristine nature and also fear for its future; the trip made her realise how small human beings are.

Q8. What is the main message of the chapter?

We must protect the earth, since climate change affects even the remote poles, and our actions shape the future.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the author's journey and the significance of the Antarctic Convergence.

The author travelled by ship on a student expedition across the stormy southern seas to Antarctica. On the way the ship crosses the Antarctic Convergence, where cold waters of the pole meet warmer waters. This zone is full of plankton and fish, and a boundary of two worlds. It shows how nature is connected and why the ocean around Antarctica is important for the earth's climate.

Q10. What does Antarctica teach us about the earth's past and future?

Antarctica was once part of Gondwana with warm climate and forests, as fossils show. Its ice holds records of ancient air and climate. Scientists study ice cores to understand climate change. Melting of its ice would raise sea levels worldwide. So protecting Antarctica means protecting the earth's future.

Q11. Why is Antarctica important to the whole world?

It controls ocean currents and the climate, stores most of earth's fresh water as ice, and is a living laboratory for scientists. It belongs to no country and is used for peaceful research under the Antarctic Treaty. Its health is a warning for all mankind.
