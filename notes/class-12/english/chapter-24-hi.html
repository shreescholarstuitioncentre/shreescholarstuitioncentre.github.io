कक्षा 12 अंग्रेज़ी – Unseen Passages (हिंदी नोट्स)

1. अपठित गद्यांश क्या है?

ऐसा गद्यांश जो आपने पहले पढ़ा नहीं होता। इससे बोध-क्षमता, शब्द-ज्ञान और लेखन-कौशल की परीक्षा होती है। सभी प्रश्न केवल गद्यांश से ही पूछे जाते हैं।

2. गद्यांश के प्रकार

  • तथ्यात्मक – समाचार, विज्ञान, रिपोर्ट।
  • वर्णनात्मक – व्यक्ति, स्थान या घटना का वर्णन।
  • विचारात्मक – लेखक के तर्क व मत।
  • साहित्यिक – कहानी या भावप्रधान लेखन।

3. हल करने की विधि

  • पहले एक बार पूरा पढ़कर मुख्य विचार समझें।
  • फिर प्रश्न पढ़ें और मुख्य शब्द चुनें।
  • दोबारा पढ़कर उत्तर वाली पंक्तियाँ रेखांकित करें।
  • उत्तर अपने शब्दों में पूरे वाक्य में लिखें।
  • अंत में वर्तनी, काल और व्याकरण जाँचें।

4. प्रश्नों के प्रकार

  • बहुविकल्पीय / रिक्त स्थान – सबसे उपयुक्त अर्थ चुनें।
  • लघु उत्तर – 1–2 पूरे वाक्य।
  • शब्दावली – पर्यायवाची, विलोम, संदर्भ में अर्थ।
  • शीर्षक / सारांश – संक्षिप्त और मुख्य भाव वाला।
  • अनुमान-आधारित – पंक्तियों के बीच का अर्थ।

5. शब्द-ज्ञान के सुझाव

अनजाने शब्द का अर्थ संदर्भ से समझें। उपसर्ग/प्रत्यय देखें: un-, dis- = उल्टा अर्थ; -ful = से भरा। पर्यायवाची उसी शब्द-भेद (noun/verb) का हो।

6. क्या करें, क्या न करें

  • ✔ पूरे वाक्य में उत्तर दें।
  • ✔ समय का ध्यान रखें (लगभग 15 मिनट)।
  • ✘ अपना मत न जोड़ें।
  • ✘ पूरा अनुच्छेद न उतारें।

7. शीर्षक कैसे दें

शीर्षक 3–5 शब्द का हो और विषय को दर्शाए, जैसे 'Save Water, Save Life'। पूरा वाक्य न लिखें।

हल किए गए Unseen Passages

Passage 1

Water is the most precious gift of nature. Yet we waste it carelessly. Rivers are drying, and groundwater is falling every year. If we do not save water today, our children may have to fight for a single glass tomorrow. Simple habits like closing taps and harvesting rainwater can make a big difference.

Q1. What does the writer call water?
Q2. What can happen to our children?
Q3. Find a word meaning 'valuable'.
Q4. Give a suitable title.

A1. The writer calls water the most precious gift of nature. (लेखक जल को प्रकृति का सबसे अनमोल उपहार कहता है।)
A2. They may have to struggle even for a glass of water. (उन्हें एक गिलास पानी के लिए भी संघर्ष करना पड़ सकता है।)
A3. Precious (अनमोल)।
A4. Save Water, Save Life.

Passage 2

Reading is a habit that shapes the mind. A person who reads daily gains knowledge, improves language and learns to think deeply. Books are silent friends who never complain. A good book can change a whole life, so every student should read at least a few pages every day.

Q1. How does reading help a person?
Q2. Why are books called silent friends?
Q3. Antonym of 'deeply'?
Q4. Title?

A1. It gives knowledge, improves language and develops deep thinking. (ज्ञान, भाषा और गहरी सोच बढ़ाता है।)
A2. Because they stay with us quietly and never complain. (वे चुपचाप साथ रहती हैं, शिकायत नहीं करतीं।)
A3. Superficially (सतही रूप से)।
A4. The Joy of Reading.

Passage 3

Punctuality means doing work at the right time. Great people respect time because lost time never returns. A punctual student finishes tasks calmly, while a late one is always in a hurry and makes mistakes.

Q1. What is punctuality?
Q2. Why do great people respect time?
Q3. Synonym of 'calmly'?

A1. Doing work at the right time. (सही समय पर काम करना।)
A2. Because lost time never returns. (क्योंकि बीता समय लौटकर नहीं आता।)
A3. Peacefully (शांति से)।

और Solved Passages (Class 12 Level)

Passage 4: Technology and Learning

The digital revolution has transformed classrooms from chalk-and-talk spaces into interactive hubs of knowledge. Students now access lectures, simulations and libraries with a single click. However, this abundance is a double-edged sword: unfiltered information breeds distraction and shallow understanding. Technology, therefore, can only supplement, never replace, the guidance of a thoughtful teacher who teaches learners to question, verify and reflect.

Q1. What has the digital revolution done to classrooms?
Q2. Why is technology called a 'double-edged sword'?
Q3. Find a word from the passage meaning 'to add to'.
Q4. Give a suitable title.

A1. It has turned them into interactive centres of knowledge.
A2. It gives abundant information but also causes distraction and shallow understanding.
A3. Supplement.
A4. Technology: A Tool, Not a Teacher.

सार: तकनीक ने कक्षाओं को बदला है, पर वह शिक्षक का स्थान नहीं ले सकती।

Passage 5: Climate Change

Climate change is no longer a distant threat; it is an unfolding reality. Melting glaciers, erratic monsoons and rising sea levels bear witness to the damage caused by unchecked industrialisation and deforestation. Developed nations, having emitted the most greenhouse gases historically, bear a greater responsibility, yet the burden falls heaviest on the poor, who have contributed least. Only collective, equitable action can avert catastrophe.

Q1. What evidence shows that climate change is real?
Q2. Who should bear greater responsibility and why?
Q3. Who suffers most?
Q4. Antonym of 'unchecked'?

A1. Melting glaciers, erratic monsoons and rising sea levels.
A2. Developed nations, because they have emitted the most greenhouse gases.
A3. The poor, who have contributed least.
A4. Controlled / regulated.

सार: जलवायु परिवर्तन वास्तविकता है; विकसित देशों की ज़िम्मेदारी अधिक है पर सबसे ज़्यादा नुकसान गरीबों को होता है।

Passage 6: Social Media and Youth

Social media connects millions, giving young people a platform to voice opinions and discover talent. Yet the constant comparison with curated, filtered lives breeds anxiety and low self-esteem. Many adolescents measure their worth in likes and followers. Experts urge a balanced approach: use these platforms purposefully, limit screen time and remember that online perfection is rarely real.

Q1. Mention one benefit of social media.
Q2. How does it harm adolescents?
Q3. What do experts advise?
Q4. Synonym of 'curated'?

A1. It gives youth a platform to express views and show talent.
A2. Comparison with filtered lives causes anxiety and low self-esteem.
A3. Purposeful use, limited screen time and awareness that online perfection is unreal.
A4. Carefully selected.

सार: सोशल मीडिया लाभ भी देता है और चिंता भी; संतुलित उपयोग ज़रूरी है।

Passage 7: The Power of Truth

Mahatma Gandhi believed that truth was not merely a moral virtue but a force capable of moving nations. His satyagraha demanded courage, for it required one to face injustice without violence and to suffer without bitterness. He argued that a cause built on falsehood might triumph briefly but must eventually crumble. For him, means were as important as ends, and impure means could never produce a pure result.

Q1. How did Gandhi view truth?
Q2. Why does satyagraha need courage?
Q3. What happens to a cause based on falsehood?
Q4. Give a title.

A1. As a force capable of moving nations, not just a virtue.
A2. Because it means facing injustice nonviolently and suffering without bitterness.
A3. It may win briefly but will finally collapse.
A4. Truth: Gandhi's Weapon.

सार: गांधीजी के लिए सत्य नैतिक गुण ही नहीं, शक्ति था; साधन भी साध्य जितने शुद्ध होने चाहिए।

Passage 8: Yoga and Wellbeing

Originating in ancient India, yoga is a discipline that harmonises body, mind and spirit. Regular practice improves flexibility and strength, but its deeper gift is mental calm. In a world beset by stress, millions are turning to breathing exercises and meditation to restore balance. The United Nations' recognition of International Yoga Day reflects the universal appeal of this age-old tradition.

Q1. What does yoga harmonise?
Q2. What is its deeper benefit?
Q3. What shows yoga's universal appeal?
Q4. Antonym of 'ancient'?

A1. Body, mind and spirit.
A2. Mental calm.
A3. The UN recognition of International Yoga Day.
A4. Modern.

सार: योग शरीर, मन और आत्मा में संतुलन लाता है और इसकी अपील सार्वभौमिक है।

Passage 9: Women Empowerment

A nation cannot soar if half its population is grounded. Education and economic independence are the twin wings of women's empowerment. When a girl is educated, she delays marriage, raises healthier children and contributes to growth. Yet deep-rooted prejudice continues to deny many women their rightful opportunities. Change must begin in the mind, for laws alone cannot uproot mindsets.

Q1. What are the 'twin wings' of empowerment?
Q2. How does an educated girl benefit society?
Q3. Why are laws insufficient?
Q4. Synonym of 'prejudice'?

A1. Education and economic independence.
A2. She delays marriage, raises healthier children and adds to growth.
A3. Laws cannot uproot deep-rooted mindsets.
A4. Bias.

सार: महिलाओं की शिक्षा और आर्थिक स्वतंत्रता राष्ट्र की प्रगति के लिए ज़रूरी हैं; सोच बदलना सबसे ज़रूरी है।

Passage 10: Skills and Employment

Unemployment in India is paradoxical: millions of graduates remain jobless while industries complain of a shortage of skilled workers. The gap lies in an education system that rewards memorisation over practical ability. Vocational training, internships and entrepreneurship must be integrated into curricula so that degrees translate into livelihoods rather than remain mere certificates.

Q1. What is paradoxical about unemployment?
Q2. What is wrong with the education system?
Q3. What remedies are suggested?
Q4. Meaning of 'livelihoods'?

A1. Graduates are jobless while industry lacks skilled workers.
A2. It rewards memorisation over practical ability.
A3. Vocational training, internships and entrepreneurship in curricula.
A4. Means of earning a living.

सार: डिग्रियाँ कौशल के बिना बेकार हैं; व्यावसायिक प्रशिक्षण पाठ्यक्रम का हिस्सा बने।

Passage 11: Forests and Biodiversity

Forests are the lungs of the earth, absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen. They shelter nearly eighty per cent of terrestrial species and regulate rainfall. Yet every year, vast tracts fall to the axe for agriculture and urban expansion. Once a species vanishes, no technology can resurrect it. Preserving forests is thus not charity toward nature but insurance for our own survival.

Q1. Why are forests called the 'lungs of the earth'?
Q2. What threatens forests?
Q3. Why is conservation 'insurance'?
Q4. Synonym of 'resurrect'?

A1. They absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen.
A2. Clearing for agriculture and urban expansion.
A3. Our survival depends on it.
A4. Revive.

सार: वन पृथ्वी के फेफड़े हैं; इनका संरक्षण हमारे अपने अस्तित्व का बीमा है।

Passage 12: India in Space

From launching rockets carried on bicycles to landing on the Moon's south pole, India's space journey is a saga of ingenuity on a modest budget. ISRO's frugal engineering has made missions cost a fraction of those elsewhere. Space science is not a luxury for a developing nation; satellites guide farmers, forecast cyclones and connect remote villages, repaying the investment many times over.

Q1. What makes ISRO's missions distinctive?
Q2. How do satellites help ordinary people?
Q3. Is space science a luxury? Why?
Q4. Antonym of 'modest'?

A1. Frugal engineering with very low costs.
A2. They guide farmers, forecast cyclones and connect villages.
A3. No, because its returns outweigh the investment.
A4. Extravagant.

सार: इसरो कम खर्च में बड़ी उपलब्धियाँ देता है और उपग्रह आम जनता के काम आते हैं।

Passage 13: The Joy of Giving

True generosity is measured not by the size of the gift but by the spirit behind it. A poor man who shares his meal gives more than a rich man who donates from surplus. Giving also enriches the giver, awakening empathy and dissolving selfishness. Societies bound by mutual kindness, rather than mere contracts, are those that endure the hardest times.

Q1. How is true generosity measured?
Q2. Who gives more: the poor man or the rich?
Q3. How does giving benefit the giver?
Q4. Suggest a title.

A1. By the spirit behind the gift, not its size.
A2. The poor man sharing his meal, as he gives not from surplus.
A3. It awakens empathy and dissolves selfishness.
A4. The Spirit of Giving.

सार: दान की महत्ता भावना में है, मात्रा में नहीं; इससे दाता भी समृद्ध होता है।
