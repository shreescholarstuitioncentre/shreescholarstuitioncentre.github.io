Lost Spring

Anees Jung - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.2)

Chapter Summary

The chapter has two parts. In 'Stolen Childhood' the author meets Saheb-e-Alam, a ragpicker from Dhaka living in Seemapuri, Delhi. He walks barefoot, dreaming of tennis shoes, and later takes a tea-stall job but loses his freedom. In 'I Want to Drive a Car' she visits Firozabad, where families make glass bangles in dark furnaces. Mukesh breaks the pattern by wanting to be a motor mechanic. Both stories show how poverty, tradition and a cruel system rob children of childhood.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'Lost Spring'?

Anees Jung, an Indian writer. The chapter is from her book Lost Spring: Stories of Stolen Childhood.

Q2. What does 'Saheb-e-Alam' mean?

It means 'Lord of the Universe'. Ironically, Saheb is a poor ragpicker who does not know the meaning of his name.

Q3. Where did Saheb come from?

From Dhaka, Bangladesh. Storms swept away their fields and homes, so the family came to Delhi and settled in Seemapuri.

Q4. Why is garbage 'gold' to ragpickers?

It gives them daily bread. For children it is wrapped in wonder: sometimes they find a rupee or even a ten-rupee note.

Q5. Why does Saheb wear tennis shoes?

He got them from a rubbish heap. He longs to play tennis but is happy to just wear them; the shoes are his dream.

Q6. What job did Saheb take at the tea stall?

He got Rs 800 a month and all meals. But the steel canister he carries seems heavier than his bag of garbage; he is no longer his own master.

Q7. Who is Mukesh?

A boy of Firozabad from a bangle-making family. He dreams of becoming a motor mechanic.

Q8. Why do bangle makers of Firozabad lose their eyesight?

They work in dim, dark hutments near hot furnaces without proper light or air. The glass dust makes them blind before they see adulthood.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the life of ragpickers in Seemapuri.

Seemapuri is on the outskirts of Delhi, a place of squatters from Bangladesh who came in 1971. About 10,000 ragpickers live in structures of mud with roofs of tin and tarpaulin, with no drainage, sanitation or running water. They have no identity papers but ration cards that get them food. For them food is more important than identity. Ragpicking is their only livelihood, and for children it is a means of survival and sometimes a hope of finding something valuable.

Q10. What is the 'vicious circle' that traps the bangle makers?

The families are trapped by the sahukars, middlemen, policemen, bureaucrats and politicians. If they try to organise themselves into a cooperative, the police arrest them as law-breakers and beat them. Their caste and class keep them poor; a family is born in the 'god-given lineage' of bangle-making, so Mukesh's grandmother asks if it can ever be broken. Thus there is no escape from poverty, debt and exploitation, and children are denied school and a free childhood.

Q11. What is the central theme of 'Lost Spring'?

The chapter exposes child labour and the loss of childhood among the poor. It shows how poverty, tradition and a corrupt system steal their 'spring' - the freedom to learn and play. Yet it ends with hope in Mukesh's willingness to dream and walk the long path to a garage.

Solved Examples (Given / To find / Solution)

Example 1 (Easy)
Given: Saheb has no school in his neighbourhood. To find: Author's offer. Solution: Step 1: The author asks why he is not in school. Step 2: He says there is none. Step 3: She says she will start one. Step 4: He asks if he will study there. Step 5: Later she finds that the promise was careless, as the school never existed.
Example 2 (Intermediate)
Given: Saheb's tea-stall job. To find: Is he better off? Solution: Step 1: He earns Rs 800 and meals. Step 2: He is no longer free like a bird. Step 3: The canister is heavier than his sack. Step 4: So he has lost his freedom and remains a child labourer.
Example 3 (Hard)
Given: 'Dreams are not for those who live in Firozabad.' To find: Explain. Solution: Step 1: Poverty and generations of toil limit them. Step 2: Elders accept it as fate. Step 3: Mukesh is different; he says he will be a motor mechanic. Step 4: He plans to walk to the garage. Step 5: Thus he is a ray of hope.
