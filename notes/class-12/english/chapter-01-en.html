The Last Lesson

Alphonse Daudet - Class 12 English (Flamingo, Prose Ch.1)

Chapter Summary

Franz, a lazy schoolboy, runs late to school in a village of Alsace, expecting a scolding about participles. He sees a crowd at the town hall notice board. In school all is calm, and M. Hamel wears his best clothes. Villagers sit at the back. Franz learns that Berlin has ordered only German to be taught, and this is the last French lesson. Franz is shocked and regrets his idleness. M. Hamel teaches grammar, writing and reading with great love, praises French and blames everyone for neglect. At twelve the church clock strikes and Prussian trumpets sound. He writes Vive la France! and dismisses the class.

Characters

  • Franz - young, lazy boy who learns too late.
  • M. Hamel - dedicated, patriotic teacher of 40 years.
  • Old Hauser - elderly villager learning to read the primer.
  • Wachter - blacksmith at the notice board.
  • Villagers - ex-mayor, postmaster, old soldiers.

Themes

Love for language and country; value of time and education; loss of freedom under imperial rule; regret and change.

Short Answer Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Who is the author of 'The Last Lesson'?

Alphonse Daudet, a French writer. The story comes from his collection Monday Tales.

Q2. Why was Franz late for school?

He had not studied participles and feared a scolding. The warm day and birds tempted him to stay out.

Q3. What was on the bulletin board?

The notice from Berlin: only German would be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine.

Q4. Why was M. Hamel dressed specially?

He wore his green coat, frilled shirt and black silk cap, worn only on inspection or prize days, to honour his last lesson.

Q5. Why were the village elders sitting in class?

They wanted to thank M. Hamel for forty years of service and to show respect to their lost country.

Q6. What did Franz regret?

He had wasted time in play and skipped lessons. Books once heavy now felt like old friends.

Q7. What did M. Hamel say about French?

It is the most beautiful, clearest, most logical language; a people enslaved hold the key to their prison while they keep their language.

Q8. How did the story end?

The clock struck twelve, Prussian trumpets sounded, M. Hamel turned pale and wrote 'Vive la France!' on the blackboard.

Long Answer Q&A

Q9. Describe the character of M. Hamel.

M. Hamel is patient, devoted and patriotic. Though strict earlier, he now blames himself, not the children, for neglect. He confesses that he often sent students to water his plants and gave holidays to fish. He teaches with all his heart on the last day. Overcome by emotion, he chokes, yet writes 'Vive la France!' - an act of love for his country.

Q10. What is the central theme of the story?

Love for one's mother tongue and nation. Language is identity and freedom. Loss is realised only when it is about to be lost. The story also warns us not to postpone learning and not to take what we have for granted.

Q11. How does Franz change during the story?

At first he is lazy and wants to play. After hearing the news he feels guilty and ashamed; he listens intently, understands everything easily, and sees his teacher and books with new respect. He even wonders whether pigeons will be forced to coo in German.

MCQs

(a). M. Hamel.

(b). Alsace, a French region taken by Prussia.

Solved Examples (Given / To find / Solution)

Example 1 (Easy)
Given: Franz saw people reading a notice. To find: What was the notice? Solution: Step 1: He passed the town hall. Step 2: The blacksmith, Wachter, was reading the bulletin board. Step 3: This was the bad news for two years: lost battles, orders of the Kommandantur. Step 4: Now it says German only will be taught.
Example 2 (Intermediate)
Given: M. Hamel blames parents and himself. To find: Why? Solution: Step 1: Parents wanted children to work on farms and mills for more money. Step 2: He himself used students to water flowers and gave holidays. Step 3: So all share blame for neglecting French.
Example 3 (Hard)
Given: 'When people are enslaved, as long as they hold fast to their language, it is as if they had the key to their prison.' To find: Explain. Solution: Step 1: Enslaved people lose land and rights. Step 2: Language holds culture, memory and identity. Step 3: Holding it keeps hope and the way to freedom alive. Step 4: So language is a symbol of resistance.
