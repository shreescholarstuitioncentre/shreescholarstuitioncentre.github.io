1. Elements of a Communication System

Communication is the transfer of information from one point to another.

  • Information source and transducer: message (speech, music, picture) converted to an electrical signal.
  • Transmitter: amplifies, modulates the signal and radiates it through a transmitting antenna.
  • Channel (medium): link between transmitter and receiver — wire pair, coaxial cable, optical fibre (guided) or free space (unguided).
  • Receiver: picks the signal with an antenna, amplifies, demodulates it.
  • Destination: output transducer (speaker, screen).
  • Along the way, noise adds unwanted random signal, and attenuation weakens the signal; repeaters restore it.

2. Analog and Digital Communication

Analog: the signal varies continuously in amplitude/frequency (AM, FM radio). Digital: the signal is a series of discrete pulses (binary 0 and 1) — e.g. PCM, computers, mobile phones. Digital communication is less affected by noise, allows error correction, coding and security, and is easy to multiplex; analog is simpler and needs less bandwidth.

Base-band signal = original signal: speech 300–3100 Hz, audio 20 Hz–20 kHz, video ≈ 4.2 MHz, TV channel ≈ 6 MHz.

3. Modulation — Need and Types

Modulation superimposes the low-frequency message on a high-frequency carrier wave. Need:

  1. Practical antenna length: efficient antenna needs l ≈ λ/4. For 1 kHz, λ = 300 km — impossible; for 1 MHz, l ≈ 75 m.
  2. Effective power radiated ∝ (l/λ)², so high frequency radiates much more power.
  3. No mixing of signals: different stations use different carrier frequencies.

Types: amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), phase modulation (PM).

Amplitude modulation

Carrier c(t) = Ac sin ωct; message m(t) = Am sin ωmt.
Modulation index μ = Am/Ac (μ ≤ 1; μ > 1 gives distortion). Also μ = (Amax − Amin)/(Amax + Amin).
cm(t) = Ac sin ωct + (μAc/2) cos(ωc − ωm)t − (μAc/2) cos(ωc + ωm)t
Frequencies: fc, fc − fm (LSB), fc + fm (USB); bandwidth = 2fm.
Power: Pt = Pc(1 + μ²/2).

4. Demodulation (Detection)

Recovering the message from the modulated carrier. In an AM receiver, the signal passes through a rectifier (diode) that removes one half; then an envelope detector (RC low-pass filter) smooths the carrier ripples and leaves the message envelope; a capacitor blocks DC. Time constant RC must satisfy 1/fc ≪ RC ≪ 1/fm.

5. Propagation of Electromagnetic Waves

  • Ground (surface) wave: follows earth’s surface, for frequencies up to ~1.5 MHz (AM broadcast); absorbed by ground, so the range is small and falls with frequency.
  • Sky wave: 3–30 MHz (short wave) is reflected back by the ionosphere (F-layer) enabling long-distance communication. Critical frequency fc = 9√Nmax (N in m⁻³).
  • Space wave: > 30 MHz (VHF, UHF, microwaves, TV, FM) travels in line-of-sight. Range of one tower of height h: d = √(2Rh) (R = 6.4×10⁶ m); area covered A = πd² = 2πRh; between two towers: dM = √(2RhT) + √(2RhR). Range is extended by tall towers, repeaters or satellites.

6. Bandwidth and Noise

Bandwidth is the range of frequencies occupied by a signal or passed by a channel; AM needs 2fm (≈ 10 kHz per station), FM ≈ 200 kHz, TV ≈ 6 MHz. Channel capacity rises with bandwidth. Noise is unwanted random disturbance: internal (thermal, shot noise in devices) and external (atmospheric static, man-made, cosmic). Quality is judged by signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) = signal power/noise power.

7. Antenna

A conductor/structure that converts electrical signal into electromagnetic waves (transmitting) and vice versa (receiving). Common types: dipole (half-wave, length λ/2), quarter-wave monopole, parabolic dish for microwave/satellite signals (concentrates waves, high gain and directivity). A short antenna radiates poorly, hence l ≈ λ/4 or λ/2 is used.

8. Satellite Communication

Microwaves (GHz) pass through the ionosphere, so a satellite relays signals. A geostationary satellite orbits above the equator at ≈ 36,000 km, period 24 h, remaining fixed over a point. A transponder receives the uplink (≈ 6 GHz), amplifies, shifts frequency and sends the downlink (≈ 4 GHz) to avoid interference. Three satellites cover the whole Earth. Uses: TV, telephony, internet, weather forecasting, navigation (GPS/NavIC), remote sensing. Indian satellites: INSAT, GSAT.

Short / Long Questions with Answers

Q1. Name the elements of a communication system.

Information source, transmitter, channel, receiver and destination.

Q2. What is modulation?

Superimposing a low-frequency message on a high-frequency carrier wave.

Q3. Why is modulation necessary?

For practical antenna size, efficient power radiation and to avoid mixing of signals.

Q4. Define modulation index.

μ = Am/Ac, the ratio of message amplitude to carrier amplitude.

Q5. What is the bandwidth of an AM wave?

2fm.

Q6. What is demodulation?

The process of recovering the message signal from a modulated wave.

Q7. Which wave is reflected by the ionosphere?

Sky waves (3–30 MHz).

Q8. Give the formula for the range of a TV tower.

d = √(2Rh).

Q9. What is noise?

Unwanted random signals that disturb the message.

Q10. Why is a geostationary satellite stationary?

Its period equals Earth’s rotation period (24 h) and it orbits in the equatorial plane.

Solved Numericals (Step by Step)

Ex.1 (Easy) A carrier of amplitude 10 V is modulated by a 6 V message. Find μ.
μ = Am/Ac = 6/10 = 0.6.
Ex.2 (Easy) fc = 1 MHz and fm = 5 kHz. Find sidebands and bandwidth.
USB = 1000 + 5 = 1005 kHz; LSB = 1000 − 5 = 995 kHz; bandwidth = 2×5 = 10 kHz.
Ex.3 (Medium) Pc = 100 W, μ = 0.5. Find total and sideband power.
Pt = 100(1 + 0.25/2) = 100×1.125 = 112.5 W; sidebands = 12.5 W.
Ex.4 (Medium) Find quarter-wave antenna length for 1 MHz.
λ = c/f = 3×10⁸/10⁶ = 300 m; l = λ/4 = 75 m.
Ex.5 (Medium) TV tower is 100 m high. Find range and area (R = 6.4×10⁶ m).
d = √(2×6.4×10⁶×100) = √(1.28×10⁹) = 3.58×10⁴ m = 35.8 km. A = πd² = 4.0×10⁹ m² ≈ 4000 km².
Ex.6 (Hard) Critical frequency for Nmax = 10¹² m⁻³; and LOS range between towers of 100 m and 80 m.
fc = 9√(10¹²) = 9×10⁶ Hz = 9 MHz. dM = √(2R×100) + √(2R×80) = 35.8 + 32.0 = 67.8 km.
Ex.7 (Hard) Prove μ = (Amax − Amin)/(Amax + Amin).
Envelope maximum Amax = Ac + Am; minimum Amin = Ac − Am. Difference = 2Am, sum = 2Ac, so ratio = Am/Ac = μ. Hence proved (e.g. max 14 V, min 6 V gives μ = 8/20 = 0.4).
