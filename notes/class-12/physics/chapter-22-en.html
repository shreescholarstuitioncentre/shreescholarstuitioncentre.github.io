Chapter 22: Polarisation of Light — Class 12 Physics (UP Board)

Animated figure: unpolarised light vibrates in all directions; the polariser passes only one direction (red); the analyser turned by θ passes the component along its axis (green).

1. Polarisation — Meaning

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave: the electric vector E vibrates perpendicular to the direction of travel. In ordinary (unpolarised) light, E vibrates in all directions in the plane perpendicular to the ray. Restricting the vibrations to a single direction is called polarisation. It proves that light is a transverse wave (longitudinal waves such as sound cannot be polarised).

2. Plane Polarised Light

Plane of vibration: plane containing the vibrations and the ray.
Plane of polarisation: plane perpendicular to the plane of vibration, containing the ray.

Light whose vibrations are confined to one plane is plane (linearly) polarised. Unpolarised light is shown by dots and double arrows; polarised light by only dots or only arrows. Intensity of unpolarised light = I₀; after a perfect polariser it becomes I₀/2.

3. Methods of Polarisation

  1. Reflection: light reflected from glass/water at the polarising angle is completely plane polarised.
  2. Refraction: a pile of glass plates polarises the transmitted beam more with each plate.
  3. Double refraction: calcite or quartz splits a ray into the ordinary (O) and extraordinary (E) rays, both plane polarised at right angles (Nicol prism).
  4. Selective absorption (dichroism): tourmaline crystal or Polaroid absorbs one vibration direction.
  5. Scattering: light from the clear sky is partly polarised.

4. Brewster’s Law

When unpolarised light strikes a transparent surface at the polarising (Brewster) angle ip, the reflected light is completely plane polarised, with vibrations perpendicular to the plane of incidence.

μ = tan ip
At ip, reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular: ip + rp = 90°.

Proof: Snell: μ = sin ip/sin rp. With rp = 90° − ip, sin rp = cos ip, so μ = tan ip. For glass (μ = 1.5) ip ≈ 56.3°; for water (μ = 4/3) ≈ 53.1°. μ depends on λ, so ip differs for colours.

5. Polaroids

A Polaroid is a thin film of long-chain molecules (e.g. iodoquinine sulphate) aligned in one direction, which absorbs vibrations parallel to the chains and transmits those perpendicular. Uses: sunglasses and car headlight glare reduction, 3-D cinema, LCD displays, photography filters, stress analysis.

6. Malus’ Law

Plane polarised light of intensity I₀ falling on an analyser whose axis makes angle θ with the polarisation direction transmits I = I₀cos²θ.

Proof: amplitude A along polariser; component along analyser = A cosθ; intensity ∝ (amplitude)², so I = I₀cos²θ. At θ = 0° I = I₀ (maximum); at 90° I = 0 (crossed); at 45° I = I₀/2. Rotating the analyser through 360° gives two maxima and two zeros.

Short / Long Questions with Answers

Q1. What is polarisation?

Restriction of the vibrations of light to a single plane.

Q2. What does polarisation prove?

That light is a transverse wave.

Q3. Can sound be polarised? Why?

No, sound is longitudinal: vibrations already lie along the direction of travel.

Q4. Define plane of polarisation.

Plane perpendicular to the plane of vibration, containing the ray.

Q5. State Brewster’s law.

μ = tan ip: refractive index equals tangent of the polarising angle.

Q6. What is the angle between reflected and refracted rays at ip?

90°.

Q7. State Malus’ law.

I = I₀cos²θ.

Q8. What is the intensity from a polaroid in unpolarised light?

Half the incident intensity.

Q9. Why do sunglasses use Polaroids?

Glare from roads/water is partly polarised horizontally; vertical-axis Polaroids block it.

Q10. Name the methods of polarisation.

Reflection, refraction, double refraction, selective absorption, scattering.

MCQs

1. Polarisation proves light is: (a) longitudinal (b) transverse (c) particle (d) none

Answer: (b)

2. Brewster’s law: (a) μ = sin ip (b) μ = tan ip (c) μ = cos ip (d) μ = cot ip

Answer: (b)

3. Crossed polaroids transmit: (a) I₀ (b) I₀/2 (c) zero (d) I₀/4

Answer: (c) zero

4. Unpolarised light through a polaroid: (a) I₀ (b) I₀/2 (c) 2I₀ (d) 0

Answer: (b)

5. At θ = 45°, Malus’ law gives: (a) I₀ (b) I₀/2 (c) I₀/4 (d) 0

Answer: (b)

Solved Numericals (Step by Step)

Ex.1 (Easy) Find the Brewster angle for glass, μ = 1.5.
tan ip = 1.5 ⇒ ip = tan⁻¹(1.5) = 56.3°.
Ex.2 (Easy) Brewster angle is 60°. Find μ.
μ = tan 60° = √3 ≈ 1.73.
Ex.3 (Medium) Unpolarised light I₀ passes through two polaroids with axes at 60°. Find the final intensity.
Step 1: After first polaroid I₁ = I₀/2.
Step 2: Malus: I₂ = I₁cos²60° = (I₀/2)(1/4).
Step 3: I₂ = I₀/8.
Ex.4 (Hard) At what θ does the transmitted intensity become 25% of the polarised incident intensity?
cos²θ = 1/4 ⇒ cosθ = 1/2 ⇒ θ = 60°.
Ex.5 (Hard) Prove that at the polarising angle the refracted angle is rp = 90° − ip.
Reflected and refracted rays are at 90°. Angles on a straight surface: ip + 90° + rp = 180° ⇒ rp = 90° − ip. Hence proved.
