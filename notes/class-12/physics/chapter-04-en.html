Class 12 Physics – Capacitance (Notes)

1. Capacitor and Capacitance

A capacitor is an arrangement of two conductors (plates) separated by an insulator or dielectric, used to store electric charge and energy. Charge +Q on one plate and −Q on the other.

Capacitance: the ratio of the charge on either plate to the potential difference between them.

C = Q / V

SI unit: farad (F) = coulomb/volt. 1 F is the capacitance when 1 C of charge raises the potential difference by 1 V. 1 µF = 10⁻⁶ F, 1 nF = 10⁻⁹ F, 1 pF = 10⁻¹² F. Dimensions [M⁻¹ L⁻² T⁴ A²]. It depends on shape, size, separation and the medium, not on Q or V.

Isolated sphere: V = kQ/R so C = 4πε₀R. Principle: bringing an earthed conductor near a charged conductor lowers its potential and so increases its capacitance, so more charge can be stored.

2. Parallel Plate Capacitor

Two plates, each of area A, separated by distance d (d much smaller than plate size), in vacuum.

Surface charge density σ = Q/A. The field between the plates is uniform: E = σ/ε₀ = Q/(ε₀A) (outside, E = 0). Potential difference V = Ed = Qd/(ε₀A).

C = Q/V = ε₀ A / d

With a dielectric completely filling the gap: C = K ε₀ A / d = K C₀. To get large C: large A, small d, large K.

3. Combination of Capacitors

Series

Same charge Q on each (inner plates only get induced charge). Potentials add: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ = Q/C₁ + Q/C₂ + Q/C₃. With V = Q/C:

1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃

Equivalent C is smaller than the smallest. Two capacitors: C = C₁C₂/(C₁+C₂). n equal: C/n. The smallest capacitor gets the largest voltage (V ∝ 1/C).

Parallel

Same V across each. Charge divides: Q = Q₁ + Q₂ + Q₃ = (C₁ + C₂ + C₃)V.

C = C₁ + C₂ + C₃

Equivalent C is larger than the largest. The largest capacitor stores the most charge (Q ∝ C).

4. Energy Stored in a Capacitor

When charge q is on the plates, potential is q/C. Work to add dq: dW = (q/C)dq. Total work to charge to Q:

W = ∫₀^Q (q/C)dq = Q²/2C

U = ½ C V² = Q² / 2C = ½ Q V

This energy is stored in the electric field between the plates (it is not lost). Graph of V against Q is a straight line; the area under it is the energy.

Energy density

Energy per unit volume. Volume = Ad, V = Ed, C = ε₀A/d: U = ½(ε₀A/d)(E²d²) = ½ ε₀ E² A d.

u = U / (A d) = ½ ε₀ E²

In a dielectric medium: u = ½ ε₀ K E² = ½ ε E². Unit J/m³.

Sharing of charge

Capacitors C₁ (V₁), C₂ (V₂) joined with like plates together: common potential V = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂)/(C₁ + C₂). Energy lost (as heat/sparks): ΔU = C₁C₂(V₁ − V₂)² / 2(C₁ + C₂).

5. Dielectric Constant and Its Effect

Dielectric constant K = ε/ε₀ = C/C₀ = E₀/E, the ratio of capacitance with the dielectric to that without it (same dimensions). K ≥ 1 (vacuum 1, air ≈ 1.0006, mica 6, water 80, metal ∞).

Reason: the dielectric polarises and bound charges set up E_p opposite to E₀, so E = E₀ − E_p = E₀/K, and V = Ed falls, so C = Q/V rises K times.

6. Capacitor Partially Filled with Dielectric

(a) Slab of thickness t, full plate area

Field in air gap (d − t) is E₀ = σ/ε₀; in the slab E = E₀/K. V = E₀(d − t) + E₀t/K = (σ/ε₀)[d − t + t/K].

C = ε₀A / (d − t + t/K)

Conducting slab (K → ∞): C = ε₀A/(d − t). If t = d: C = Kε₀A/d.

(b) Slab covers part of the area

Two capacitors in parallel: C = ε₀(KA₁ + A₂)/d, A₁ with slab, A₂ with air.

(c) Two dielectrics in layers (thickness d₁, d₂)

Series: C = ε₀A / (d₁/K₁ + d₂/K₂).

Questions & Answers (Theory and MCQs)

Q1. What is a capacitor? Define capacitance and its SI unit. (2)

Two conductors separated by a dielectric to store charge/energy. C = Q/V; unit farad = coulomb/volt.

Q2. Derive the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor. (5)

E = σ/ε₀ = Q/ε₀A, V = Ed = Qd/ε₀A, C = Q/V = ε₀A/d.

Q3. Derive the equivalent capacitance in series. (3)

Same Q. V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ = Q(1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃) = Q/C, hence 1/C = Σ1/Cᵢ.

Q4. Derive the equivalent capacitance in parallel. (3)

Same V. Q = Q₁ + Q₂ + Q₃ = (C₁ + C₂ + C₃)V = CV, so C = ΣCᵢ.

Q5. Derive the energy stored in a capacitor. (3)

dW = (q/C)dq, W = ∫₀^Q q dq/C = Q²/2C = ½CV² = ½QV.

Q6. Derive the energy density of an electric field. (3)

U = ½CV² = ½(ε₀A/d)(Ed)² = ½ε₀E²(Ad). u = U/Ad = ½ε₀E².

Q7. Explain the effect of a dielectric slab on C, V, E, U when the battery is disconnected. (5)

Q is fixed. C′ = KC₀. V′ = Q/C′ = V₀/K. E′ = E₀/K. U′ = Q²/2C′ = U₀/K (reduces).

Q8. A slab of thickness t is placed in a capacitor. Derive C. (5)

See Section 6(a): V = E₀(d − t) + E₀t/K, C = ε₀A/(d − t + t/K).

Q9. Why is energy lost when two charged capacitors are connected? (2)

Charge flows to equalise potentials; the current heats the wires and appears as sparks, so ΔU = C₁C₂(V₁ − V₂)²/2(C₁+C₂) > 0.

Q10. Why does a capacitor block DC but pass AC? (2)

In DC, once charged there is no current (gap is insulating). In AC it charges and discharges continuously, so current flows.

Solved Numericals (Easy → Hard)

Easy 1. A capacitor holds 6 µC at 12 V. Find C.
C = Q/V = 6×10⁻⁶/12 = 0.5×10⁻⁶ F = 0.5 µF.
Easy 2. Plates of area 0.01 m², gap 1 mm, in air. Find C.
C = ε₀A/d = 8.85×10⁻¹² × 0.01 / 10⁻³ = 8.85×10⁻¹¹ F = 88.5 pF.
Medium 1. 2 µF, 3 µF, 6 µF in (i) series (ii) parallel.
(i) 1/C = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = (3+2+1)/6 = 1, so C = 1 µF. (ii) C = 2 + 3 + 6 = 11 µF.
Medium 2. Energy stored in 10 µF at 100 V.
U = ½CV² = ½ × 10×10⁻⁶ × 10⁴ = 0.05 J.
Medium 3. 4 µF charged to 200 V is joined to an uncharged 6 µF. Find common V and energy loss.
V = C₁V₁/(C₁+C₂) = 4×200/10 = 80 V. U_i = ½×4×10⁻⁶×4×10⁴ = 0.08 J. U_f = ½×10×10⁻⁶×6400 = 0.032 J. Loss = 0.048 J.
Hard 1. C₀ = 10 pF. A slab of K = 5 and t = d/2 is inserted. Find new C.
d − t + t/K = d/2 + d/10 = 0.6d. C = ε₀A/0.6d = C₀/0.6 = 16.7 pF.
Hard 2. 5 µF charged to 100 V, battery removed, dielectric K = 4 fully inserted. Find V and energy change.
C′ = 20 µF. Q = 500 µC fixed, V′ = 500/20 = 25 V. U₀ = ½×5×10⁻⁶×10⁴ = 0.025 J. U′ = ½×20×10⁻⁶×625 = 0.00625 J. Decrease 0.01875 J (slab is pulled in).
Hard 3. 2 µF is in parallel with a series pair of 3 µF and 6 µF. Connected to 12 V. Find charge on each.
Series pair: 3×6/9 = 2 µF. Total = 2 + 2 = 4 µF. Q_total = 4×12 = 48 µC. Branch 1 (2 µF): 24 µC. Branch 2: 2×12 = 24 µC on both 3 µF and 6 µF. V(3 µF) = 24/3 = 8 V; V(6 µF) = 24/6 = 4 V (total 12 V).
Hard 4. Energy density where E = 10⁵ N/C.
u = ½ε₀E² = 0.5 × 8.85×10⁻¹² × 10¹⁰ = 4.4×10⁻² J/m³.
