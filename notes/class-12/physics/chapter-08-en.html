Torque on Current Loop and Moving Coil Galvanometer

Class 12 Physics · Unit 3 · Chapter 8

1. Torque on a Current Loop

A rectangular loop (N turns, sides a and b, area A=ab) carries current I in uniform field B. Forces on the two sides parallel to B are zero. The other two sides (length a) feel equal and opposite forces F = BIa, forming a couple. Arm of couple = b sinθ, where θ is the angle between B and the normal to the loop.

τ = NIAB sinθ
Net force = 0, so the loop only rotates. Max torque (θ=90°) when the plane is parallel to B; zero when the plane is perpendicular to B (stable equilibrium).

2. Magnetic Dipole Moment

A current loop behaves like a bar magnet.

M = NIA (vector, along the normal)
Unit: A·m². Direction by right-hand rule (curl fingers along current, thumb gives M). In vector form τ = M × B, and potential energy U = −M·B (minimum at θ=0, maximum at θ=180°). An orbiting electron has μ = evr/2 = (e/2m)L.

3. Moving Coil Galvanometer: Construction

It has a rectangular coil of many turns of insulated copper wire wound on a soft-iron cylindrical core. The coil is suspended between concave pole pieces of a strong horseshoe magnet by a phosphor-bronze strip (or pivoted with a hair spring). A pointer or a lamp-and-scale arrangement with a mirror shows the deflection.

4. Principle

A current-carrying coil placed in a uniform magnetic field experiences a torque, τ = NIAB sinθ. The coil rotates until this torque is balanced by the restoring torque of the spring.

5. Working, Radial Field, Soft-Iron Core

Concave poles and the soft-iron core make the field radial, so the plane of the coil is always parallel to B (θ=90°) at every deflection. Deflecting torque = NIAB (constant). Restoring torque = kφ (k = torsional constant). At equilibrium:

NIAB = kφ  ⟹  φ = (NAB/k) I
So φ ∝ I and the scale is linear and uniform. The soft-iron core strengthens the field and makes it radial. Damping by the metal frame makes the needle settle quickly.

6. Current Sensitivity

Deflection per unit current:

Is = φ/I = NAB/k
Unit: rad/A (or div/A). Increase by raising N, A, B or using a weaker spring (smaller k).

7. Voltage Sensitivity

Deflection per unit voltage:

Vs = φ/V = NAB/(kG) = Is/G
G = coil resistance. If N is doubled, Is doubles but G also doubles, so Vs stays the same. Hence increasing N does not raise voltage sensitivity.

8. Conversion into Ammeter

Connect a small shunt resistance S in parallel with the galvanometer (resistance G, full-scale current Ig). To measure current up to I:

S = IgG / (I − Ig)
Ammeter resistance RA = GS/(G+S), very small. It is connected in series. An ideal ammeter has zero resistance.

9. Conversion into Voltmeter

Connect a large resistance R in series with the galvanometer. To read up to V:

R = V/Ig − G
Voltmeter resistance RV = G + R, very large. It is connected in parallel across the component. An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance.

Questions and Answers

Q1. Why is the field made radial in a moving coil galvanometer?
Ans: So the coil's plane stays parallel to B at every position; torque NIAB is then constant and deflection ∝ current, giving a linear scale.
Q2. Why is a shunt connected in parallel to make an ammeter?
Ans: Most current bypasses the galvanometer through the low-resistance shunt, protecting the coil and extending the range; the combined resistance becomes very small.
Q3. Why should an ideal voltmeter have infinite resistance?
Ans: It must draw no current from the circuit, so that it does not alter the potential difference being measured.
Q4. Define current sensitivity and voltage sensitivity.
Ans: Current sensitivity = deflection per unit current (NAB/k). Voltage sensitivity = deflection per unit voltage (NAB/kG).
Q5. MCQ: Torque on a loop is maximum when its plane makes what angle with B? (a) 0° (b) 45° (c) 90° (d) 180°
Ans: (a) 0°. The plane parallel to B means the normal is at 90° to B, so sinθ = 1.
Q6. MCQ: Ammeter resistance is (a) very high (b) very low (c) infinite (d) equal to G
Ans: (b) very low, since GS/(G+S) < S.
Q7. Why does doubling the turns not increase voltage sensitivity?
Ans: Is doubles but the coil resistance G also doubles, so Vs = Is/G is unchanged.
Q8. What is the net force on a current loop in a uniform field?
Ans: Zero, because the forces on opposite sides are equal and opposite; only a torque acts.

Numericals (step by step)

Easy A coil has 100 turns, area 0.02 m², current 2 A, in B = 0.5 T with its plane parallel to B. Find M and τ.
Formula: M = NIA, τ = NIAB sinθ (θ = 90°).
M = 100 × 2 × 0.02 = 4 A·m².
τ = 4 × 0.5 × 1 = 2 N·m.
Easy A galvanometer has G = 50 Ω, Ig = 5 mA. Find the shunt to read 0–1 A.
S = IgG/(I − Ig) = (0.005 × 50)/(1 − 0.005) = 0.25/0.995 = 0.25 Ω.
Intermediate Convert G = 100 Ω, Ig = 5 mA into a 0–10 V voltmeter.
R = V/Ig − G = 10/0.005 − 100 = 2000 − 100 = 1900 Ω in series. RV = 2000 Ω.
Intermediate A dipole M = 0.4 A·m² makes 30° with B = 0.25 T. Find τ and U.
τ = MB sinθ = 0.4 × 0.25 × 0.5 = 0.05 N·m.
U = −MB cosθ = −0.1 × 0.866 = −0.087 J.
Hard G = 12 Ω, Ig = 3 mA. Make a 0–6 A ammeter. Find S and RA.
S = (0.003 × 12)/(6 − 0.003) = 0.036/5.997 = 6.0×10⁻³ Ω.
RA = GS/(G+S) = 12 × 0.006/12.006 = 6.0×10⁻³ Ω (≈ S).
Hard A galvanometer gives 10 div/mA and G = 50 Ω. Find its voltage sensitivity.
Is = 10 div/mA = 10⁴ div/A.
Vs = Is/G = 10⁴/50 = 200 div/V.
