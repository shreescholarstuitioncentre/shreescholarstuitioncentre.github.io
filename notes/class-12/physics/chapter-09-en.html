Magnetic Field and Earth's Magnetism

Class 12 Physics · Unit 3 · Chapter 9

1. Magnetic Field

The region around a magnet or a current-carrying conductor in which a magnetic force is felt. A moving charge q with velocity v feels F = q(v × B). SI unit of B: tesla (T) = N/(A·m). 1 gauss = 10⁻⁴ T. Earth's field is about 3×10⁻⁵ to 6×10⁻⁵ T (0.3 to 0.6 G).

2. Magnetic Flux

Flux through area A in field B:

φ = B·A = BA cosθ
θ is the angle between B and the area vector (the normal). Unit: weber (Wb) = T·m². Gauss's law for magnetism: ∮B·dA = 0, i.e. net flux through any closed surface is zero because magnetic monopoles do not exist.

3. Magnetic Field Lines

Properties: (i) they form closed loops, going from N to S outside the magnet and S to N inside; (ii) the tangent at a point gives the direction of B; (iii) closer lines mean a stronger field; (iv) two lines never intersect, otherwise B would have two directions at one point; (v) they have no beginning or end, showing there are no monopoles.

4. Magnetic Dipole

A pair of equal and opposite poles ±m separated by 2l, e.g. a bar magnet.

M = m × 2l (from S to N)
Unit: A·m². A current loop is also a dipole, M = NIA. In a field B: torque τ = MB sinθ and potential energy U = −MB cosθ. A bar magnet cut in two gives two smaller magnets, never isolated poles.

5. Axial Field

At distance r from the centre on the axis (end-on position):

B = (μ₀/4π) · 2Mr / (r² − l²)²
For a short magnet (r ≫ l):
Baxial = μ₀2M / 4πr³
Direction is along M (the same direction as the dipole moment).

6. Equatorial Field

At distance r on the perpendicular bisector (broadside position):

B = (μ₀/4π) · M / (r² + l²)^(3/2)
For a short magnet:
Beq = μ₀M / 4πr³
Direction is opposite to M. So Baxial : Beq = 2 : 1 at the same distance.

7. Earth's Magnetic Field

Earth acts like a giant bar magnet with M ≈ 8×10²² A·m². Its magnetic south pole lies near the geographic north (that is why a compass N-end points north). The magnetic axis is tilted about 11.5° from the rotation axis. Origin: convection currents of molten iron-nickel in the outer core (dynamo effect). The field also reverses over geological time.

8. Declination (θ)

The angle between the magnetic meridian and the geographic meridian at a place. It is measured with a compass and varies from place to place and with time. It is small near the equator in India.

9. Inclination or Dip (δ)

The angle that the total field Be makes with the horizontal, measured with a dip circle in the magnetic meridian. δ = 0° at the magnetic equator and 90° at the magnetic poles.

tanδ = V/H

10. Horizontal Component (H)

H = Be cosδ,   V = Be sinδ,   Be = √(H² + V²)
H is maximum at the equator (H = Be) and zero at the poles. A compass needle settles along H. Typical H in India ≈ 0.3 to 0.4 G (about 3.5×10⁻⁵ T). A neutral point is where the magnet's field cancels H.

Questions and Answers

Q1. Why can magnetic monopoles not be separated?
Ans: Cutting a magnet always gives two smaller dipoles. Field lines are closed loops, so ∮B·dA = 0.
Q2. Why do two field lines never cross?
Ans: At the crossing point B would have two directions, which is impossible.
Q3. Compare axial and equatorial fields of a short magnet.
Ans: Baxial = μ₀2M/4πr³ along M; Beq = μ₀M/4πr³ opposite to M. Ratio 2 : 1.
Q4. Define declination and dip.
Ans: Declination: angle between magnetic and geographic meridians. Dip: angle of the total field with the horizontal.
Q5. MCQ: At the magnetic poles the dip is (a) 0° (b) 45° (c) 90° (d) 180°
Ans: (c) 90°, since H = 0 and the field is vertical.
Q6. MCQ: Unit of magnetic flux is (a) tesla (b) weber (c) gauss (d) henry
Ans: (b) weber (Wb = T·m²).
Q7. How is the Earth's field produced?
Ans: By convection currents of molten iron-nickel in the outer core, acting as a dynamo.
Q8. What is a neutral point?
Ans: A point where the net magnetic field is zero, as the magnet's field equals H and is opposite to it.

Numericals (step by step)

Easy Flux through a 0.2 m² coil in B = 0.5 T, when B makes 60° with the normal.
φ = BA cosθ = 0.5 × 0.2 × cos60° = 0.1 × 0.5 = 0.05 Wb.
Easy H = 0.3 G, dip = 60°. Find V and Be.
V = H tanδ = 0.3 × 1.732 = 0.52 G.
Be = H/cosδ = 0.3/0.5 = 0.6 G.
Intermediate A short magnet M = 1.2 A·m². Find B on the axis and on the equator at r = 0.3 m.
Baxial = 10⁻⁷ × 2M/r³ = (2.4×10⁻⁷)/0.027 = 8.9×10⁻⁶ T.
Beq = half = 4.4×10⁻⁶ T.
Intermediate Be = 0.4 G and dip = 30°. Find H and V.
H = Be cos30° = 0.4 × 0.866 = 0.35 G.
V = Be sin30° = 0.2 G.
Hard A short magnet M = 5 A·m². At what axial distance is B = 3.2×10⁻⁵ T?
B = 2×10⁻⁷M/r³ ⟹ r³ = (2×10⁻⁷ × 5)/(3.2×10⁻⁵) = 10⁻⁶/3.2×10⁻⁵ = 0.03125.
r = 0.315 m.
Hard A short magnet M = 0.8 A·m² has its N pole pointing geographic north. Find the distance of the neutral points on the equatorial line. H = 3.2×10⁻⁵ T.
Equatorial field points south, H points north, so they cancel when Beq = H.
10⁻⁷M/r³ = H ⟹ r³ = (10⁻⁷ × 0.8)/(3.2×10⁻⁵) = 2.5×10⁻³.
r = 0.136 m.
