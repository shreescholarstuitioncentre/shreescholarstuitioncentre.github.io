Class 12 Physics – Unit 4
Ch 11: Electromagnetic Induction

1. Magnetic Flux

Flux Φ = B·A·cosθ, where θ is the angle between B and the area vector (normal to the coil). Unit: weber (Wb) = T·m². Flux can change by changing B, A or θ.

2. Faraday's Laws

  • First law: Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an emf is induced in it. The emf lasts only while the flux is changing.
  • Second law: The induced emf is equal to the rate of change of flux linkage.
ε = −dΦ/dt  |  ε = −N dΦ/dt (N turns)  |  I = ε/R  |  Charge q = ΔΦ/R

Induced emf can be increased by more turns, faster motion, a stronger magnet or a soft-iron core.

3. Lenz's Law

The direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the change (cause) producing it. The negative sign in ε = −dΦ/dt represents this.

  • North pole moving toward a coil: the near face becomes North (repels), so the current is anticlockwise as seen from the magnet side.
  • North pole moving away: the near face becomes South (attracts), so the current is clockwise.
  • Conservation of energy: work done against the opposing force is converted to electrical energy. If the induced effect aided the cause, energy would be created from nothing, which is impossible.

5. Motional EMF

A rod of length l moves with velocity v perpendicular to uniform field B. Free electrons feel the force F = qvB, so charges pile up at the ends until the electric force balances it.

Derivation: Area swept in time dt = l·v·dt. dΦ = B·l·v·dt. So |ε| = dΦ/dt = Blv.

ε = Blv  |  I = Blv/R  |  F = B²l²v/R  |  Power P = B²l²v²/R = F·v

The mechanical power spent to pull the rod equals the electrical power dissipated as heat, which confirms conservation of energy.

6. Eddy Currents

  • Circulating induced currents in a bulk conductor when the flux through it changes (Foucault currents).
  • Harmful: heating and energy loss in transformer cores and motors. Reduced by laminating the core (thin insulated sheets).
  • Uses: induction furnace, electromagnetic braking in trains, dead-beat galvanometer (damping), induction cooktop, energy meter.

7. Self Induction and Self Inductance

A changing current in a coil changes its own flux and induces an emf in the same coil that opposes the change.

Φ = LI  |  ε = −L dI/dt  |  L (solenoid) = μ₀n²Al = μ₀N²A/l  |  U = ½LI²
  • Unit of L: henry (H) = Wb/A. 1 H means 1 V induced when the current changes at 1 A/s.
  • L depends on turns, area, length and core material (μr). It is the electrical "inertia".

8. Mutual Induction and Mutual Inductance

A changing current in one coil induces emf in a neighbouring coil.

Φ₂ = M I₁  |  ε₂ = −M dI₁/dt  |  M = μ₀N₁N₂A/l (coaxial solenoids)  |  M = k√(L₁L₂)
  • Unit: henry. M depends on coil geometry, distance, orientation, turns and core.

9. Transformer

  • Principle: mutual induction.
  • Working: AC in the primary produces an alternating flux in the soft-iron core. The same flux links the secondary and induces an alternating emf of the same frequency.
Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is (ideal) = k (turns ratio)
Step-up: Ns > Np (Vs > Vp, Is < Ip). Step-down: Ns < Np.

10. Solved Numericals (step by step)

Easy 1. A coil of 100 turns: flux changes from 0.05 Wb to 0.01 Wb in 0.2 s. Find emf.

ε = N|ΔΦ|/Δt = 100 × 0.04/0.2 = 20 V.

Easy 2. A rod of l = 0.4 m moves at 10 m/s perpendicular to B = 0.5 T. Find emf.

ε = Blv = 0.5 × 0.4 × 10 = 2 V.

Intermediate 3. L = 2 H, current falls from 3 A to 0 in 0.1 s. Find average emf and initial energy.

ε = L|ΔI|/Δt = 2 × 3/0.1 = 60 V. U = ½LI² = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.

Intermediate 4. A transformer has Np = 1000, Ns = 50, Vp = 220 V. Find Vs.

Vs = Vp × Ns/Np = 220 × 50/1000 = 11 V (step-down).

Hard 5. Rod l = 0.5 m, v = 4 m/s, B = 0.2 T, circuit R = 0.1 Ω. Find emf, current, force and power.

ε = Blv = 0.2 × 0.5 × 4 = 0.4 V. I = ε/R = 4 A. F = BIl = 0.2 × 4 × 0.5 = 0.4 N. P = Fv = 1.6 W. Check: I²R = 16 × 0.1 = 1.6 W ✓.

Hard 6. A transformer (90% efficient) gives 220 V, 10 A from a 2200 V mains. Find input current.

Pout = 220 × 10 = 2200 W. Pin = 2200/0.9 = 2444 W. Ip = Pin/Vp = 2444/2200 ≈ 1.11 A.

11. Q & A

Q1 (Short). State Lenz's law.

The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change in flux that produces it.

Q2 (Short). Why are transformer cores laminated?

To cut the path of eddy currents and reduce I²R loss.

Q3 (Short). Define the henry.

The inductance of a coil in which a current changing at 1 A/s induces 1 V.

Q4 (Short). Can a transformer work on DC? Why?

No. DC gives constant flux, so there is no change in flux and no induced emf in the secondary.

Q5 (Long). Derive the motional emf ε = Blv.

See section 5: dΦ = B·l·v·dt, so ε = dΦ/dt = Blv.

Q6 (Long). Explain principle, working, efficiency and losses of a transformer.

See section 9.

Q7 (Long). Show Lenz's law agrees with conservation of energy.

If the induced current aided the motion, the magnet would speed up, giving more energy with no input. Since that is impossible, the induced effect must oppose the motion, and the work done against it appears as electrical energy.

Q8 (MCQ). Unit of self inductance is: (a) Wb (b) H (c) T (d) V

(b) henry.

Q9 (MCQ). Eddy currents are used in: (a) induction furnace (b) fuse (c) battery (d) lamp

(a) induction furnace.

Q10 (MCQ). Energy stored in an inductor is: (a) LI (b) ½LI² (c) L²I (d) LI²

(b) ½LI².
