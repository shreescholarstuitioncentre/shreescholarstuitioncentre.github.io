Class 12 Physics – Unit 4
Ch 12: Alternating Current

1. AC Voltage and Current

An alternating current changes its magnitude continuously and reverses direction periodically. Mains AC in India: 50 Hz, 220 V (rms).

V = V₀ sin ωt  |  I = I₀ sin(ωt ± φ)  |  ω = 2πf = 2π/T

V₀, I₀ are peak values (amplitudes), f is frequency, T is time period, φ is phase difference between V and I.

3. Peak, RMS and Average Values

  • Peak value: maximum value I₀, V₀.
  • Average value: over a full cycle it is zero. Over a half cycle, Iav = 2I₀/π = 0.637 I₀.
  • RMS value: the steady DC value that produces the same heat in a resistor. Irms = I₀/√2 = 0.707 I₀.
  • Derivation: mean of I₀² sin²ωt over a cycle = I₀²/2, since ⟨sin²⟩ = ½. So Irms = √(I₀²/2) = I₀/√2.
  • AC meters read rms values. Form factor = rms/average = 1.11.
Irms = 0.707 I₀  |  Vrms = 0.707 V₀  |  V₀ = 220√2 ≈ 311 V

4. AC through a Resistor

V = V₀ sin ωt, so I = (V₀/R) sin ωt. Current and voltage are in phase (φ = 0). I₀ = V₀/R. Average power = VrmsIrms = I²rmsR, and it is all dissipated as heat.

5. AC through an Inductor

The induced emf L dI/dt opposes the change. Solving gives I = I₀ sin(ωt − π/2), with I₀ = V₀/ωL.

  • Current lags voltage by 90°.
  • Inductive reactance XL = ωL = 2πfL (ohm). It grows with frequency. For DC (f = 0), XL = 0.
  • Average power over a cycle = 0. Energy is stored in the magnetic field and returned.

6. AC through a Capacitor

q = CV₀ sin ωt, so I = dq/dt = CωV₀ cos ωt = I₀ sin(ωt + π/2), with I₀ = V₀ωC.

  • Current leads voltage by 90°.
  • Capacitive reactance XC = 1/ωC = 1/(2πfC). It falls with frequency. For DC, XC → ∞ (blocks DC).
  • Average power over a cycle = 0.

Memory trick: CIVIL – in a Capacitor I leads V; in an inductor (L) V leads I.

7. Series LCR Circuit, Phasor Diagram, Impedance

V² = VR² + (VL − VC)²  ⇒  Z = √[R² + (XL − XC)²]
tan φ = (XL − XC)/R  |  I₀ = V₀/Z
  • XL > XC: inductive, current lags. XC > XL: capacitive, current leads. XL = XC: resistive, φ = 0.
  • Phase difference φ is the angle between voltage and current. Impedance Z is the total opposition (ohm).

8. Resonance and Q-Factor

  • Resonance occurs when XL = XC. Then Z = R (minimum), current is maximum and φ = 0.
  • ω₀L = 1/ω₀C gives ω₀ = 1/√(LC), f₀ = 1/(2π√LC).
  • Q-factor measures sharpness of resonance: Q = ω₀L/R = 1/(ω₀CR) = (1/R)√(L/C) = ω₀/(2Δω) where 2Δω is the bandwidth. A smaller R gives a larger Q and sharper tuning (radio tuning).
  • At resonance VL = VC = Q·V, so voltages across L and C can exceed the source voltage.

9. Power, Power Factor, Wattless Current

Pav = Vrms Irms cos φ  |  Power factor cos φ = R/Z
  • Power factor is 1 for a pure resistor or at resonance. It is 0 for a pure L or pure C.
  • Wattless (idle) current is the component Irms sin φ, which is 90° out of phase with voltage and consumes no power.
  • A low power factor means more current for the same power, so more line loss. Industry improves it with capacitors.

10. LC Oscillations

A charged capacitor discharges through L. Energy swaps between the electric field (q²/2C) and the magnetic field (½LI²). Without resistance, total energy stays constant.

d²q/dt² + q/LC = 0  ⇒  q = q₀ cos ωt,   ω = 1/√(LC)

This is analogous to a mass-spring system (L ↔ m, 1/C ↔ k). With resistance the oscillations are damped.

11. AC Generator (Dynamo)

  • Principle: electromagnetic induction.
  • Parts: armature (coil of N turns), strong field magnet, slip rings, brushes.
  • Working: the coil rotates at angular speed ω in field B, so flux Φ = NBA cos ωt changes and ε = −dΦ/dt.
ε = NBAω sin ωt  |  ε₀ = NBAω

12. Solved Numericals (step by step)

Easy 1. Mains is 220 V rms. Find the peak voltage.

V₀ = √2 × Vrms = 1.414 × 220 ≈ 311 V.

Easy 2. Find XL of a 0.1 H inductor at 50 Hz.

XL = 2πfL = 2 × 3.14 × 50 × 0.1 = 31.4 Ω.

Intermediate 3. A 100 μF capacitor is connected to 220 V, 50 Hz. Find the rms current.

XC = 1/(2π × 50 × 100×10⁻⁶) = 31.8 Ω. I = 220/31.8 ≈ 6.9 A.

Intermediate 4. Find resonant frequency for L = 1 mH, C = 1 μF.

f₀ = 1/(2π√(10⁻³ × 10⁻⁶)) = 1/(2π × 3.16×10⁻⁵) ≈ 5030 Hz (ω₀ ≈ 31623 rad/s).

Hard 5. L = 5 H, C = 80 μF, R = 40 Ω. Find ω₀ and Q. If the source is 200 V, find current at resonance.

ω₀ = 1/√(5 × 80×10⁻⁶) = 1/√(4×10⁻⁴) = 50 rad/s. Q = ω₀L/R = 50 × 5/40 = 6.25. At resonance Z = R, so I = 200/40 = 5 A.

Hard 6. R = 30 Ω, XL = 70 Ω, XC = 30 Ω, V = 220 V. Find Z, I, power factor, power and wattless current.

Z = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω. I = 220/50 = 4.4 A. cos φ = 30/50 = 0.6. P = 220 × 4.4 × 0.6 = 580.8 W (check I²R = 19.36 × 30 = 580.8 ✓). sin φ = 40/50 = 0.8, so wattless current = 4.4 × 0.8 = 3.52 A.

13. Q & A

Q1 (Short). Define rms value of AC.

The value of steady DC that produces the same heat in a resistor in the same time. Irms = I₀/√2.

Q2 (Short). Why does a capacitor block DC but pass AC?

For DC, XC = 1/ωC is infinite since ω = 0. For AC, charging and discharging continue, so current flows.

Q3 (Short). What is wattless current?

The current component Irms sin φ, which does no net work because it is 90° out of phase with the voltage.

Q4 (Short). Why is the average power zero for pure L or C?

Current and voltage are 90° apart, so cos φ = 0. Energy is stored and returned each quarter cycle.

Q5 (Long). Derive the impedance of a series LCR circuit using phasors.

See section 7: V² = V_R² + (V_L − V_C)², giving Z = √[R² + (X_L − X_C)²].

See section 8.

Q8 (MCQ). In a pure inductor, current: (a) leads V by 90° (b) lags V by 90° (c) is in phase (d) leads by 45°

Q9 (MCQ). At resonance in LCR, the power factor is: (a) 0 (b) 0.5 (c) 1 (d) ∞
