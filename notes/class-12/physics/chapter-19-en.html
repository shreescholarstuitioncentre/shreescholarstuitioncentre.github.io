1. Wavefront

The locus of all points of a medium which are vibrating in the same phase at a given instant is called a wavefront. A line perpendicular to the wavefront at any point shows the direction of propagation of light and is called a ray.

Types of wavefront

(i) Spherical: from a point source in a uniform medium. Intensity I ∝ 1/r², amplitude ∝ 1/r.
(ii) Cylindrical: from a linear (slit) source. I ∝ 1/r, amplitude ∝ 1/√r.
(iii) Plane: from a very distant source (a small part of a huge sphere) or from a lens. The intensity and amplitude are constant.

Properties

1) The wavefront is perpendicular to the rays. 2) All points on it have the same phase. 3) Energy flows along the rays, perpendicular to the wavefront. 4) The wavefront moves forward with the speed of the wave (v = c/μ). 5) Wavefront and wave speed are not the same thing as the speed of a particle.

2. Huygens Principle

Statements: (1) Every point on a wavefront acts as a new source of secondary wavelets that spread out in all directions with the speed of light in that medium. (2) The new wavefront after time t is the forward envelope (common tangent) of these secondary wavelets.

Construction: draw spheres of radius vt about points of the wavefront AB; draw the tangent surface A′B′ in the forward direction – this is the wavefront at time t.

Limitations: it cannot explain why there is no backward wave (Fresnel's obliquity factor was needed), and it cannot explain polarisation. Both are removed by the modern wave theory.

3. Reflection by Huygens Theory

A plane wavefront AB meets a reflecting surface MN at angle i. Point A touches the surface first, while B takes time t to reach C: BC = vt. Meanwhile the wavelet from A spreads to radius AE = vt in the same medium. The tangent from C to this circle gives the reflected wavefront CE.

Proof of ∠i = ∠r. In right triangles ABC and AEC: 1) AC is common; 2) BC = AE = vt; 3) ∠ABC = ∠AEC = 90°. So △ABC ≅ △AEC (RHS). Hence ∠BAC = ∠ECA, which gives ∠i = ∠r. The incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane. ∎

4. Refraction by Huygens Theory

A plane wavefront AB travels in medium 1 (speed v₁) and meets the surface at angle i. While B moves to C (BC = v₁t), the wavelet from A spreads in medium 2 up to AE = v₂t. The tangent CE from C to the wavelet is the refracted wavefront.

Proof of Snell's law. In △ABC: sin i = BC/AC = v₁t/AC. In △AEC: sin r = AE/AC = v₂t/AC. Dividing,

sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂ = μ₂/μ₁ = ₁μ₂ (constant)

Results: 1) For a denser medium (v₂ < v₁) r < i, so the ray bends towards the normal. 2) The frequency stays the same, but λ₂ = λ₁ v₂/v₁ = λ₁/ ₁μ₂, so the wavelength decreases in a denser medium. 3) Substituting v = c/μ gives μ = c/v.

Wavefronts after optical devices

Concave mirror: plane → converging spherical. Convex lens: plane → converging spherical (centre thicker, so it lags more). Prism: the plane wavefront emerges tilted. Convex mirror: plane → diverging spherical.

Solved Numericals (step by step)

Ex.1 Find the speed of light in glass of μ = 1.5 (c = 3×10⁸ m/s).
v = c/μ = 3×10⁸/1.5 = 2×10⁸ m/s.
Ex.2 Light of λ = 6000 Å enters water (μ = 4/3). Find λ, ν and v in water.
ν = c/λ = 3×10⁸/6×10⁻⁷ = 5×10¹⁴ Hz (unchanged). λ′ = λ/μ = 6000 × 3/4 = 4500 Å. v = c/μ = 2.25×10⁸ m/s.
Ex.3 A ray enters a medium of μ = √2 at i = 45°. Find r.
sin r = sin i/μ = 0.7071/1.414 = 0.5 ⇒ r = 30°.
Ex.4 The intensity of a point source at 1 m is 100 W/m². Find it at 5 m and the amplitude ratio.
I ∝ 1/r² ⇒ I = 100/25 = 4 W/m². Amplitude ∝ 1/r ⇒ ratio a₅/a₁ = 1/5.
Ex.5 Light crosses a glass slab (μ = 1.5) 3 mm thick. Find the extra time compared with air.
t(glass) = μd/c = 1.5×3×10⁻³/3×10⁸ = 1.5×10⁻¹¹ s; t(air) = 10⁻¹¹ s. Extra = 5×10⁻¹² s.
Ex.6 Light of λ = 5000 Å (in air) enters glass of μ = 1.5. Find ν, λ and v in glass.
ν = 3×10⁸/5×10⁻⁷ = 6×10¹⁴ Hz; λ′ = 5000/1.5 = 3333 Å; v = 2×10⁸ m/s.

Q & A

Q1. Define wavefront.
The locus of points vibrating in the same phase at an instant.
Q2. What is the shape of the wavefront from a point source? From a distant source?
Spherical, and plane respectively.
Q3. State Huygens' principle.
Each point of a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets, and the forward envelope of these wavelets is the new wavefront.
Q4. Why does the wavelength change on refraction but the frequency does not?
Frequency is fixed by the source; the speed changes in the medium, so λ = v/ν changes.
Q5. Is the speed of light greater in a denser medium?
No – v = c/μ, so it is smaller.
Q6. What does Huygens' theory say about the speed of light in water compared with air?
It is smaller in water, which agrees with the experiment (Foucault); Newton's corpuscular theory predicted the reverse.
Q7. MCQ: Wavefront from a linear source is (a) plane (b) spherical (c) cylindrical (d) elliptical → (c).
Q8. MCQ: Which stays unchanged on refraction? (a) speed (b) wavelength (c) frequency (d) amplitude → (c).
Q9. MCQ: In a spherical wavefront, I ∝ (a) r (b) 1/r (c) 1/r² (d) r² → (c).
Q10. Name one limitation of Huygens' principle.
It cannot explain the absence of a backward wave.
