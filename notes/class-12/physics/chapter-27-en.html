1. Nuclear Composition

Rutherford showed the atom has a tiny nucleus. It contains protons (charge +e, mass 1.00728 u) and neutrons (no charge, mass 1.00867 u), together called nucleons. Electron mass = 0.00055 u.

Atomic number Z = number of protons
Mass number A = protons + neutrons
Neutron number N = A − Z
Notation: AZX, e.g. 23892U has 92 p and 146 n.

Size and density

Nuclear radius R = R₀A^{1/3}, R₀ = 1.2 fm (1 fm = 10⁻¹⁵ m). Volume ∝ A, so density is the same for all nuclei ≈ 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³.

2. Atomic Mass Unit (u)

1 u = 1/12 of the mass of one ¹²C atom = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg. Using E = mc²: 1 u = 931.5 MeV. 1 MeV = 1.6×10⁻¹³ J.

3. Nuclear Forces

Strong attractive forces hold nucleons despite proton–proton repulsion. They are short range (~2 fm), charge independent (p–p, n–n, p–n equal), saturated, spin dependent, non-central and arise by exchange of π-mesons (Yukawa). Stability: light nuclei have N ≈ Z; heavy nuclei need N/Z up to ≈ 1.5.

4. Mass Defect

Mass of a nucleus is always less than the sum of masses of its nucleons.

Δm = [Zmp + (A − Z)mn] − M
(with atomic masses, use mH instead of mp for Z hydrogen atoms)

5. Binding Energy

Energy equivalent of mass defect = minimum energy needed to break the nucleus into free nucleons.

B.E. = Δm × 931.5 MeV (Δm in u)
B.E. per nucleon = B.E./A

Higher B.E. per nucleon means a more stable nucleus.

6. Binding Energy Curve

  • B.E./A is small for light nuclei (H-2: 1.1 MeV) but rises rapidly; He-4 has a high peak (7.07 MeV) — very stable.
  • From A ≈ 30 to 120 the curve is nearly flat at ≈ 8.5 MeV; maximum ≈ 8.8 MeV at Fe-56, the most stable nucleus.
  • For heavy nuclei it falls slowly to ≈ 7.6 MeV at U-238.

Fusion: light nuclei join into heavier ones, moving up the curve → energy released. Fission: heavy nucleus splits into medium ones, moving up the curve → energy released.

7. Isotopes, Isobars, Isotones

Isotopes: same Z, different A (¹H, ²H, ³H; ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl). Same chemical properties.
Isobars: same A, different Z (¹⁴C, ¹⁴N). Different chemical properties.
Isotones: same N, different Z (¹³C and ¹⁴N, both N = 7).

Short / Long Questions with Answers

Q1. What are nucleons?

Protons and neutrons inside the nucleus.

Q2. Write the formula for nuclear radius.

R = R₀A^{1/3}, R₀ = 1.2 fm.

Q3. Why is nuclear density independent of mass number?

Volume ∝ A and mass ∝ A, so their ratio is constant (≈ 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³).

Q4. Define 1 atomic mass unit.

One-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom; 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c².

Q5. Define mass defect.

Difference between the total mass of nucleons and the actual nuclear mass.

Q6. Define binding energy.

Energy required to separate a nucleus into its free nucleons.

Q7. Which nucleus is most stable?

Fe-56, with the highest B.E. per nucleon ≈ 8.8 MeV.

Q8. Why does fusion release energy?

Light nuclei have lower B.E./A; fusing raises it, so extra energy is released.

Q9. Define isotopes with an example.

Atoms with the same Z but different A, e.g. ¹H, ²H, ³H.

Q10. State two properties of nuclear force.

Short range and charge independent.

Solved Numericals (Step by Step)

Ex.1 (Easy) Find nucleons of 23892U.
Protons = 92; neutrons = 238 − 92 = 146.
Ex.2 (Easy) Radius of a nucleus with A = 64.
R = 1.2×(64)^{1/3} = 1.2×4 = 4.8 fm.
Ex.3 (Medium) Find B.E. and B.E./A of He-4 (M = 4.002603 u; mH = 1.007825 u; mn = 1.008665 u).
Δm = 2(1.007825) + 2(1.008665) − 4.002603 = 2.015650 + 2.017330 − 4.002603 = 0.030377 u.
B.E. = 0.030377×931.5 = 28.3 MeV; B.E./A = 28.3/4 = 7.07 MeV.
Ex.4 (Hard) Find B.E./A of Li-7 (M = 7.016 u).
Δm = 3(1.007825) + 4(1.008665) − 7.016 = 3.023475 + 4.034660 − 7.016 = 0.042135 u. B.E. = 0.042135×931.5 = 39.25 MeV ⇒ B.E./A = 5.6 MeV.
Ex.5 (Hard) Prove nuclear density is independent of A and evaluate it.
M ≈ A u; V = (4/3)πR³ = (4/3)πR₀³A. ρ = A u/[(4/3)πR₀³A] = 3u/(4πR₀³) = 3×1.66×10⁻²⁷/(4π×1.728×10⁻⁴⁵) ≈ 2.3×10¹⁷ kg/m³, independent of A.
