Physics Class 12 – Optics – Chapter 16: Refraction at Spherical Surfaces (Lens)

Key formulas: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f  |  1/f = (n−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)  |  P = 1/f  |  m = v/u

1. Refraction at a Spherical Surface

Let a spherical surface of radius of curvature R separate two media of refractive indices n₁ and n₂. The object is at distance u from the pole P and the image forms at v. For a small aperture and paraxial rays, Snell’s law gives:

n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R

Sign convention is the same as for mirrors: distances from the pole, positive along the incident light. R is positive for a convex surface (bulging towards the incident light) and negative for a concave surface.

2. Lens

A transparent medium bounded by two spherical surfaces is a lens. A convex (converging) lens is thicker in the middle; a concave (diverging) lens is thinner in the middle. A ray through the optical centre O passes undeviated. A lens has two principal foci (F₁, F₂) with equal focal length f on both sides.

3. Sign Convention for Lenses

All distances from the optical centre O, positive along the incident light. The object is on the left, so u is negative. Convex lens: f positive; concave lens: f negative. Heights above the axis are positive, below negative.

4. Thin Lens Formula

1/v − 1/u = 1/f

(Positive v: image on the other side, real. Negative v: on the object side, virtual.) Note: the mirror formula has + 1/u; the lens formula has − 1/u.

5. Magnification

m = h′/h = v/u. Negative m: real, inverted. Positive m: virtual, erect. (For mirrors it was m = −v/u.)

8. Lens Maker’s Formula

1/f = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)

n = refractive index of the lens material (in air), R₁ and R₂ = radii of the first and second surfaces (with sign). Derivation: refraction at the first surface gives n/v′ − 1/u = (n−1)/R₁; at the second, 1/v − n/v′ = (1−n)/R₂. Adding: 1/v − 1/u = (n−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂), and v = f when u = −∞. In a medium of index n₁: 1/f = (n/n₁ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂); if n < n₁ a convex lens becomes diverging. For an equiconvex lens R₁ = +R, R₂ = −R.

9. Power of a Lens

P = 1/f (f in metres). Unit: dioptre (D). Convex lens: positive power; concave: negative. f = 25 cm ⇒ P = +4 D.

10. Combination of Lenses

In contact: 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ ⇒ P = P₁ + P₂; magnification m = m₁ × m₂. Separated by d: 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂ − d/(f₁f₂). The total power of lenses in contact is the algebraic sum of their powers.

11. Combination of Lens and Mirror

If the back surface of a thin lens is silvered, light passes through the lens twice and reflects once. Equivalent focal length: 1/F = 2/f_L + 1/f_M (plane mirror: 1/f_M = 0, so F = f_L/2) and P = 2P_L + P_M. If a mirror stands at some distance behind the lens, find the image from the lens, treat it as the object for the mirror, then treat the mirror’s image as the object for the lens again to get the final image.

12. Solved Numericals

Q1 (Easy): Convex lens f = 20 cm, object at 30 cm. Find v and m.
f = +20, u = −30. 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60 ⇒ v = +60 cm (real). m = v/u = 60/(−30) = −2 (inverted, twice the size).
Q2 (Easy): Power of a convex lens f = 25 cm and a concave lens f = −50 cm?
P₁ = 1/0.25 = +4 D. P₂ = 1/(−0.5) = −2 D.
Q3 (Medium): An equiconvex lens of n = 1.5 has f = 20 cm. Find the radius of curvature.
1/f = (n−1)(1/R − 1/(−R)) = 0.5 × 2/R = 1/R ⇒ R = f = 20 cm.
Q4 (Medium): Lenses f₁ = +30 cm and f₂ = −20 cm are in contact. Find F and power.
P = 100/30 − 100/20 = 3.33 − 5 = −1.67 D. F = 1/P = −0.6 m = −60 cm (diverging).
Q5 (Medium): Convex surface R = +20 cm, n₁ = 1, n₂ = 1.5, object at 60 cm. Find v.
n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂−n₁)/R ⇒ 1.5/v + 1/60 = 0.5/20 = 1/40 ⇒ 1.5/v = 1/120 ⇒ v = +180 cm.
Q6 (Hard): The back of a thin convex lens (f = 20 cm) is silvered as a plane mirror. Find the equivalent focal length.
1/F = 2/20 + 0 = 1/10 ⇒ F = 10 cm. The system acts like a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm.

13. Important Questions & Answers

Why does a lens’s focal length depend on colour?
The refractive index depends on wavelength: higher for violet (smaller f), lower for red (larger f). This causes chromatic aberration.
What kind of lens is an air bubble in water?
A concave (diverging) lens, because the bubble’s refractive index is lower than that of the surrounding water.
MCQ: SI unit of lens power? (a) m (b) dioptre (c) cm (d) N
Answer: (b) dioptre (m⁻¹).
MCQ: Two thin lenses in contact have P₁ = +3 D, P₂ = +2 D. Total power? (a) 1 D (b) 5 D (c) 6 D (d) 0.2 D
Answer: (b) 5 D.
