Physics Class 12 – Optics – Chapter 14: Reflection of Light at Spherical Mirrors

Key formulas: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f  |  f = R/2  |  m = h′/h = −v/u

1. Spherical Mirror

A part cut from a hollow sphere, with one surface polished, is a spherical mirror. If the inner surface reflects it is a concave (converging) mirror; if the outer surface reflects it is a convex (diverging) mirror.

2. Important Terms

Pole (P): centre of the mirror. Centre of curvature (C): centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part. Radius of curvature (R): distance PC. Principal axis: line through P and C. Principal focus (F): point where rays parallel to the axis meet (or appear to meet) after reflection. Focal length (f): distance PF. Aperture: width of the reflecting surface.

3. Relation between R and f

For a small aperture: a parallel ray strikes at M, CM is the normal, ∠i = ∠r = θ. In triangle FMC, ∠FCM = θ and ∠FMC = θ, so FC = FM ≈ FP. Hence PC = PF + FC = 2PF, i.e. R = 2f or f = R/2.

4. Sign Convention (Cartesian)

All distances are measured from the pole P. Distances along the direction of incident light are positive; against it, negative. Heights above the axis are positive, below are negative. Concave: f and R negative. Convex: f and R positive. The object is in front, so u is always negative.

5. Mirror Formula

1/v + 1/u = 1/f

u = object distance, v = image distance, f = focal length. (Positive v = behind the mirror, virtual image; negative v = in front, real image.)

6. Magnification

m = image height / object height = h′/h = −v/u. Negative m → real, inverted; positive m → virtual, erect. |m| > 1 enlarged, |m| < 1 diminished, |m| = 1 same size.

8. Solved Numericals (step by step)

Q1 (Easy): Concave mirror f = 15 cm, object at 30 cm. Find v and m.
Given: f = −15, u = −30. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/30 = −1/30 ⇒ v = −30 cm (in front, real). m = −v/u = −(−30)/(−30) = −1 (inverted, same size).
Q2 (Easy): If R = 40 cm, find f.
f = R/2 = 20 cm (−20 cm for a concave mirror).
Q3 (Medium): Convex mirror f = 20 cm, object at 30 cm. Find the image.
f = +20, u = −30. 1/v = 1/20 + 1/30 = 5/60 ⇒ v = +12 cm (behind, virtual). m = −12/(−30) = +0.4 (erect, diminished).
Q4 (Hard): A concave mirror forms a real image 3 times the object; object at 10 cm. Find f.
m = −3 = −v/u ⇒ v = 3u = −30. 1/f = 1/v + 1/u = −1/30 − 1/10 = −4/30 ⇒ f = −7.5 cm.

9. Important Questions & Answers

Which mirror is used for shaving? Why?
Concave, because with the face between P and F it gives an enlarged, erect, virtual image.
Why are convex mirrors used in vehicles?
They always give an erect, diminished image and a wide field of view, showing more of the road behind.
Derive the mirror formula briefly.
From similar triangles A′B′F and MPF: A′B′/PM = (v−f)/f. Since PM = AB, −v/u = (v−f)/f. Simplifying gives 1/v + 1/u = 1/f.
MCQ: Focal length of a plane mirror? (a) 0 (b) infinity (c) R (d) R/2
Answer: (b) infinity, since R = ∞.
MCQ: If m = +2, the image is? (a) real inverted (b) virtual erect enlarged (c) virtual diminished (d) real erect
Answer: (b).
