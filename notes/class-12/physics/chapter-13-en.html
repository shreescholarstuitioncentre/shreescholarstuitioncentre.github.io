Ch 13: Electromagnetic Waves

1. Displacement Current

Problem with Ampere’s law. Ampere’s law says ∮B·dl = μ₀I. While a capacitor charges, current I flows in the wires, but no charge crosses the gap between the plates. Take loop C around the wire. A flat surface cutting the wire gives I, but a bulging surface passing between the plates gives 0. Both surfaces have the same boundary, so the law is inconsistent.

Maxwell’s fix. A changing electric field between the plates acts like a current. This is the displacement current:

Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt  |  ∮B·dl = μ₀(I + Id) = μ₀I + μ₀ε₀ dΦE/dt

Check: between the plates, ΦE = EA = (q/ε₀A)·A = q/ε₀. So Id = ε₀ d(q/ε₀)/dt = dq/dt = I. The displacement current in the gap equals the conduction current in the wire, so the total current is continuous.

  • It is not a flow of charge. It arises from a changing electric flux.
  • It exists in vacuum too, and it produces a magnetic field like a normal current.

2. Electromagnetic Waves

  • A changing E produces B (displacement current), and a changing B produces E (Faraday). These two sustain each other and travel as an electromagnetic wave.
  • Source: accelerated (oscillating) charges, e.g. current in an antenna.
  • They need no medium and travel in vacuum at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = 3×10⁸ m/s.
E = E₀ sin(kx − ωt)  |  B = B₀ sin(kx − ωt)  |  E₀/B₀ = E/B = c
c = fλ  |  k = 2π/λ  |  ω = 2πf  |  in a medium v = 1/√(με) = c/n

3. Characteristics and Transverse Nature

  • E and B oscillate in phase, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. So EM waves are transverse.
  • Transverse nature is proved by polarization: light passes through a polaroid only in one orientation. Longitudinal waves cannot be polarized.
  • Not deflected by electric or magnetic fields (uncharged).
  • Energy is shared equally: uE = ½ε₀E² = uB = B²/2μ₀. Intensity I = ½ε₀E₀²c.
  • They carry momentum p = U/c and exert radiation pressure: P = I/c (absorbed), 2I/c (reflected).
  • Direction of travel is along E × B.

5. Solved Numericals

Easy 1. Find c from μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ and ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹².

μ₀ε₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 8.85×10⁻¹² = 1.112×10⁻¹⁷. √ = 3.335×10⁻⁹. c = 1/(3.335×10⁻⁹) = 3×10⁸ m/s.

Easy 2. Wavelength of 3 GHz microwaves?

λ = c/f = 3×10⁸/3×10⁹ = 0.1 m.

Intermediate 3. E₀ = 60 V/m. Find B₀.

B₀ = E₀/c = 60/(3×10⁸) = 2×10⁻⁷ T.

Intermediate 4. A capacitor of 2 μF has dV/dt = 10 V/s. Find the displacement current.

Id = I = C dV/dt = 2×10⁻⁶ × 10 = 2×10⁻⁵ A = 20 μA.

Hard 5. Sunlight intensity at Earth is 1400 W/m². Find E₀ and B₀.

I = ½ε₀cE₀² ⇒ E₀ = √(2I/ε₀c) = √(2800/(8.85×10⁻¹²×3×10⁸)) = √(1.055×10⁶) ≈ 1.03×10³ V/m. B₀ = E₀/c ≈ 3.4×10⁻⁶ T.

Hard 6. Radiation pressure of this sunlight on a perfectly absorbing surface, and on a perfect reflector?

Absorbing: P = I/c = 1400/(3×10⁸) = 4.7×10⁻⁶ N/m². Reflecting: 2I/c = 9.3×10⁻⁶ N/m².

6. Q & A

Q1 (Short). What is displacement current?

The current Id = ε₀ dΦE/dt due to a changing electric flux. It is not due to moving charge.

Q2 (Short). Why did Maxwell add it?

To make Ampere’s law consistent for charging capacitors and to make current continuous.

Q3 (Short). Which waves are produced by accelerated charges?

All EM waves.

Q4 (Short). Why are EM waves not deflected by fields?

They carry no charge.

Q5 (Short). Name the rays used for (a) sterilizing water (b) satellite TV (c) bone fractures.

(a) ultraviolet (b) microwave/radio (c) X‑rays.

Q6 (Short). Why do microwave ovens heat food?

Microwave frequency matches the natural frequency of water molecules, so they absorb energy resonantly and heat up.
