Moving Charges and Magnetism

Class 12 Physics · Unit 3 · Chapter 7

1. Oersted Experiment

In 1820 Oersted saw a compass needle deflect when placed near a current-carrying wire. Conclusion: electric current produces a magnetic field. Reverse the current and the deflection reverses. Field lines around a straight wire are concentric circles. Right-hand thumb rule: thumb along current, curled fingers show the field direction.

2. Biot-Savart Law

A current element I dl produces at distance r:

dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl sinθ / r²
Vector form: dB = (μ₀/4π) I (dl × r̂)/r². Here μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A. Direction is along dl × r. B is zero along the line of the element (θ=0) and maximum when θ=90°.

3. Straight Wire

Infinite wire:

B = μ₀I / 2πr
Finite wire: B = (μ₀I/4πr)(sinφ₁ + sinφ₂). Field falls as 1/r. Direction by right-hand thumb rule.

4. Circular Loop

At centre:

B = μ₀NI / 2R
On axis at distance x: B = μ₀NIR² / 2(R²+x²)^(3/2). Arc subtending θ at centre: B = μ₀Iθ/4πR. Direction: curl fingers along current, thumb gives B (face with anticlockwise current is a North pole).

5. Ampere's Circuital Law

∮ B·dl = μ₀ Ienclosed
Line integral of B around any closed loop equals μ₀ times net current through it. It is useful for symmetric cases (long wire, solenoid, toroid), like Gauss's law in electrostatics.

6. Solenoid

Inside a long solenoid the field is uniform and axial:

B = μ₀nI
n = turns per unit length. At the end, B = μ₀nI/2. Outside, B ≈ 0. With a soft-iron core: B = μ₀μᵣnI.

7. Toroid

A solenoid bent into a ring. Inside the turns:

B = μ₀NI / 2πr
Outside and in the hollow centre, B = 0. The field is not uniform over the cross-section.

8. Lorentz Force

F = q(E + v × B)
Magnetic part: F = qvB sinθ, perpendicular to v, so it does no work and speed stays constant. For v ⊥ B: radius r = mv/qB, period T = 2πm/qB (independent of v). For oblique entry: helical path, r = mv sinθ/qB, pitch = v cosθ · T.

9. Force on Current-Carrying Conductor

F = I (L × B) = BIL sinθ
Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule (forefinger = B, middle finger = I, thumb = force). Torque on a loop: τ = NIAB sinθ = M × B.

10. Cyclotron

Accelerates charged particles using an alternating electric field in the gap between two D-shaped dees, with a perpendicular magnetic field. Frequency:

f = qB / 2πm
Max KE = q²B²r²/2m. Limits: not for electrons (small mass, relativistic effects) or neutrons (no charge).

11. Force between Parallel Conductors

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂ / 2πd
Same-direction currents attract; opposite-direction currents repel. 1 ampere is the current which, in two infinite parallel wires 1 m apart, produces a force of 2×10⁻⁷ N per metre.

Questions and Answers

Q1. Why does the magnetic force do no work?
Ans: F ⊥ v always, so F·v = 0. Power is zero, hence KE and speed stay constant; only direction changes.
Q2. State Ampere's circuital law.
Ans: The line integral of B around a closed path equals μ₀ times the net current enclosed: ∮B·dl = μ₀I.
Q3. Derive B inside a long solenoid.
Ans: Take a rectangle with side h inside. Only the inner side contributes: B·h = μ₀(nh)I, so B = μ₀nI.
Q4. Why is a cyclotron not suitable for electrons?
Ans: Electrons have very small mass, gain speed quickly and become relativistic, so their frequency changes and they fall out of resonance.
Q5. MCQ: Field at centre of a loop of radius R carrying I is (a) μ₀I/2πR (b) μ₀I/2R (c) μ₀I/4πR (d) 0
Ans: (b) μ₀I/2R
Q6. MCQ: A charge moving parallel to B experiences force (a) maximum (b) qvB/2 (c) zero (d) infinite
Ans: (c) zero, since sin0° = 0.
Q7. Two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. What happens?
Ans: They attract each other, with force per length μ₀I₁I₂/2πd.
Q8. Why is B zero inside a toroid's hollow and outside?
Ans: An Amperian loop there encloses zero net current (inward and outward currents cancel), so B = 0.

Numericals (step by step)

Easy A straight wire carries 5 A. Find B at 10 cm.
Given: I = 5 A, r = 0.1 m.
Formula: B = μ₀I/2πr.
B = (2×10⁻⁷ × 5)/0.1 = 1×10⁻⁵ T.
Easy A circular coil of 100 turns, radius 10 cm, carries 2 A. Find B at the centre.
B = μ₀NI/2R = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100 × 2)/(2×0.1) = 1.26×10⁻³ T.
Intermediate An electron (v = 2×10⁷ m/s) enters a field B = 0.5 mT perpendicularly. Find the radius.
Formula: r = mv/eB.
r = (9.1×10⁻³¹ × 2×10⁷)/(1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 5×10⁻⁴) = 1.82×10⁻²³/8×10⁻²³ = 0.23 m.
Intermediate Two wires 5 cm apart carry 10 A and 20 A in the same direction. Find force per metre.
F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd = (2×10⁻⁷ × 10 × 20)/0.05 = 8×10⁻⁴ N/m, attractive.
Hard Find the cyclotron frequency for a proton in B = 1 T.
f = qB/2πm = (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 1)/(2π × 1.67×10⁻²⁷) = 1.53×10⁷ Hz ≈ 15.3 MHz.
