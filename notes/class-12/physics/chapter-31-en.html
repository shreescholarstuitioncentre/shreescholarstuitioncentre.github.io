1. PN Junction Diode

A p-type and n-type crystal joined together form a p–n junction. Holes diffuse from p to n and electrons from n to p; they recombine near the junction, leaving immobile negative acceptor ions on the p-side and positive donor ions on the n-side. This charge-free but ion-filled zone is the depletion region (~1 μm). Its electric field creates a barrier potential (Si 0.7 V, Ge 0.3 V) that stops further diffusion. Symbol: arrow (p→n) pointing in the direction of conventional forward current.

2. Forward and Reverse Bias

Forward bias: p to battery +, n to −. Applied voltage opposes the barrier; depletion region narrows; majority carriers cross, so current rises exponentially after the knee (threshold) voltage (0.7 V Si; 0.3 V Ge) — current in mA. Dynamic resistance r = ΔV/ΔI is small.
Reverse bias: p to −, n to +. Barrier increases; depletion region widens; only minority carriers give a tiny reverse saturation current (μA for Ge, nA for Si), almost independent of voltage until breakdown. Hence a diode conducts one way — a one-way valve.

3. Rectifiers

Conversion of AC to pulsating DC using diodes.

  • Half-wave: one diode conducts during positive half cycles; output frequency = input f; Vdc = Vm/π; efficiency 40.6%.
  • Full-wave (centre-tapped, two diodes) / bridge (four diodes): both half cycles used; output frequency = 2f; Vdc = 2Vm/π; efficiency 81.2%.
  • A filter (capacitor/inductor) smooths the pulsations into steady DC.

4. Zener Diode

A heavily doped p–n diode designed to work in reverse breakdown (Zener or avalanche effect). In breakdown, voltage across it stays nearly constant (VZ) while the current varies widely — used as a voltage regulator: connected in reverse across the load with a series resistor R. If input voltage or load changes, extra current flows through the Zener, keeping Vload = VZ.

5. LED, Photodiode and Solar Cell

LED: forward-biased p–n junction of GaAsP/GaP; electron–hole recombination releases photons with hν ≈ Eg. Low power, fast, long-lived, coloured light (Eg 1.8–3 eV).
Photodiode: operated in reverse bias; light (hν ≥ Eg) creates electron–hole pairs, so reverse current rises with intensity; used as light detector, in optical switches and remotes.
Solar cell: unbiased p–n junction (Si/GaAs) with a thin top layer; photons make pairs which the junction field separates, giving photovoltage 0.5–1 V; used in satellites, calculators, power panels.

6. Junction Transistor and CE Configuration

Three-layer device: npn or pnp. Emitter (heavily doped, supplies carriers), base (very thin, lightly doped), collector (wider, moderately doped, collects carriers). Emitter–base is forward biased, collector–base reverse biased. IE = IB + IC.

Common emitter: input between base and emitter, output between collector and emitter.
Current gain β = ΔIC/ΔIB (VCE const.), α = IC/IE, β = α/(1 − α).
Voltage gain AV = ΔVout/ΔVin = −β RL/Rin (minus sign: output is 180° out of phase).
Power gain = β·AV

Regions: cut-off (switch OFF), active (amplifier), saturation (switch ON). As amplifier, a small base-current change produces a large collector-current change and a large output voltage across RL.

7. Analog and Digital Signals

Analog signals vary continuously (sound, temperature). Digital signals have only two levels: 1 (HIGH, e.g. 5 V) and 0 (LOW, 0 V). Digital circuits are less noisy and easier to store/process.

8. Logic Gates, Boolean Algebra and Truth Tables

A logic gate is a digital circuit with one or more inputs and one output following a logical rule. Boolean algebra uses variables 0 and 1: OR (+), AND (·), NOT (bar).

NOT: A = 0 → Y = 1; A = 1 → Y = 0.

Laws: A + 0 = A; A·1 = A; A + 1 = 1; A·0 = 0; A + A = A; A·A = A; A + A′ = 1; A·A′ = 0; A + AB = A.

NAND and NOR are universal gates: any gate can be made from them (NOT: tie inputs together).

Short / Long Questions with Answers

Q1. What is the depletion region?

Region near a p–n junction depleted of free charge carriers, containing only immobile ions.

Q2. What is barrier potential for Si and Ge?

0.7 V and 0.3 V.

Q3. Why does a diode conduct in forward bias only?

Forward bias lowers the barrier so majority carriers flow; reverse bias raises it, leaving only a tiny minority-carrier current.

Q4. What is the output frequency of a full-wave rectifier?

Twice the input frequency.

Q5. What is the role of a Zener diode?

Voltage regulation in reverse breakdown.

Q6. Why is a photodiode reverse biased?

Fractional change in the small reverse current by light is easily measured.

Q7. Why is the base thin and lightly doped?

So that most carriers from the emitter reach the collector without recombining in the base.

Q8. Define current gain β.

β = ΔIC/ΔIB at constant VCE.

Q9. Which gates are universal?

NAND and NOR.

Q10. State De Morgan’s theorems.

(A + B)′ = A′·B′ and (A·B)′ = A′ + B′.

Solved Numericals (Step by Step)

Ex.1 (Easy) Si diode (0.7 V drop) with 5 V battery and R = 430 Ω in series. Find the current.
I = (5 − 0.7)/430 = 4.3/430 = 10 mA.
Ex.2 (Easy) α = 0.98. Find β.
β = α/(1 − α) = 0.98/0.02 = 49.
Ex.3 (Medium) β = 100, IB = 40 μA. Find IC and IE.
IC = βIB = 100×40 μA = 4 mA; IE = IB + IC = 4.04 mA.
Ex.4 (Medium) β = 100, RL = 4 kΩ, Rin = 500 Ω. Find voltage and power gain.
AV = βRL/Rin = 100×4000/500 = 800; power gain = β×AV = 8×10⁴.
Ex.5 (Medium) Full-wave rectifier with Vm = 10 V. Find Vdc.
Vdc = 2Vm/π = 20/3.14 = 6.37 V.
Ex.6 (Hard) Zener regulator: Vin = 15 V, VZ = 9 V, R = 300 Ω, load 1 kΩ. Find Zener current.
Itotal = (15 − 9)/300 = 20 mA; Iload = 9/1000 = 9 mA; IZ = 20 − 9 = 11 mA.
Ex.7 (Hard) Prove A + AB = A and show NAND gives NOT.
A + AB = A(1 + B) = A·1 = A. NAND with both inputs A: Y = (A·A)′ = A′, which is NOT A. Hence proved.
