Class 12 Physics – Ch.17 Refraction & Dispersion through Prism

1. Prism

A prism is a transparent medium (glass) bounded by two plane refracting surfaces inclined at an angle. This angle is the angle of prism (A). The two faces are the refracting surfaces; the third is the base. A ray bends towards the base.

Refraction through a prism (step by step)

1) The ray enters face AB at angle of incidence i and refracts towards the normal (angle r₁). 2) Inside, it strikes face AC at angle r₂. 3) It leaves at angle of emergence e, bending away from the normal.

r₁ + r₂ = A    (geometry of the quadrilateral)
δ = i + e − A    (angle of deviation)

i–δ graph

As i increases, δ first decreases, reaches a minimum value δm, then increases. The same δ occurs for two values of i (i and e interchanged, by reversibility of light).

2. Minimum Deviation

At minimum deviation: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = r = A/2, and the ray inside the prism runs parallel to the base. δm = 2i − A, so i = (A+δm)/2.
μ = sin[(A+δm)/2] / sin(A/2)

Thin prism (A small, ≤10°): δ = (μ − 1)A – independent of angle of incidence for small i.

3. Dispersion

The splitting of white light into its constituent colours (VIBGYOR) on passing through a prism is called dispersion. It happens because μ depends on wavelength: μ = a + b/λ² (Cauchy). Violet (shortest λ) has the largest μ, so deviates most; red deviates least. In vacuum all colours travel at the same speed, but in glass red is fastest.

4. Angular Dispersion

The angle between the emergent violet and red rays:

θ = δv − δr = (μv − μr)A

5. Dispersive Power (ω)

It is a property of the material, with no unit and no dimensions.

ω = (δv − δr)/δy = (μv − μr)/(μy − 1)

Mean deviation δy = (μy − 1)A, with μy ≈ (μv + μr)/2. Flint glass has higher ω than crown glass. Achromatic combination: θ₁ + θ₂ = 0 gives ω₁/ω₂ = −(μ₂−1)A₂/… i.e. two prisms of different materials placed opposite give dispersion without deviation or deviation without dispersion.

6. Rainbow

Formed by refraction, internal reflection and dispersion of sunlight in raindrops; the observer must have the Sun behind.
Primary rainbow: one internal reflection; red outside (≈42°), violet inside (≈40°).
Secondary rainbow: two internal reflections; fainter, red inside (≈51°), violet outside (≈54°), colours reversed.

7. Rayleigh Scattering

Scattering of light by particles much smaller than the wavelength (λ ≫ size): I ∝ 1/λ⁴. Blue (≈450 nm) scatters about 5–6 times more than red (≈700 nm).
• Blue sky: scattered blue light reaches the eye from all directions.
• Red sun at sunrise/sunset: light travels a longer path, blue is scattered out, red remains.
• Danger signals are red: they scatter least and are visible from far away.

Solved Numericals (step by step)

Ex.1 A prism with A = 60° shows δm = 30°. Find μ.
Given: A=60°, δm=30°. Formula: μ = sin[(A+δm)/2]/sin(A/2).
μ = sin45°/sin30° = 0.7071/0.5 = 1.414 (√2).
Ex.2 A = 60°, μ = √3. Find δm.
sin[(60+δm)/2] = √3 × sin30° = √3/2 ⇒ (60+δm)/2 = 60° ⇒ δm = 60°.
Ex.3 Thin prism A = 4°, μ = 1.5. Find δ.
δ = (μ−1)A = 0.5 × 4° = 2°.
Ex.4 μv=1.68, μr=1.60, μy=1.64. Find ω.
ω = (1.68−1.60)/(1.64−1) = 0.08/0.64 = 0.125.
Proof δm formula: at minimum deviation r₁=r₂=A/2 and i=e=(A+δm)/2. Snell's law: μ = sin i / sin r₁ = sin[(A+δm)/2]/sin(A/2). ∎

Q & A

Q1. What is the angle of prism?
The angle between the two refracting faces.
Q2. Why does violet deviate more than red?
Because μ is larger for shorter wavelength (μ = a + b/λ²).
Q3. What is the path of the ray inside the prism at minimum deviation?
Parallel to the base (symmetric).
Q4. Define dispersive power. Does it have a unit?
ω = (μv−μr)/(μy−1); it is a pure number with no unit.
Q5. Why is the sky blue?
Rayleigh scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴, so blue is scattered most.
Q6. Why is the rainbow seen only with the Sun behind the observer?
Light must be refracted, reflected and refracted back towards the observer, at about 42° from the antisolar point.
Q7. Why does the secondary rainbow have reversed colours?
It has two internal reflections, which reverse the order.
Q8. MCQ: Which colour has the least deviation? (a) violet (b) green (c) red (d) blue → (c) red.
Q9. MCQ: In a thin prism δ = ? (a) (μ+1)A (b) (μ−1)A (c) μA (d) A/μ → (b).
Q10. Why do danger signals use red?
It is scattered the least, so it travels farthest.
