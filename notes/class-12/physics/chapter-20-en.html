Class 12 Physics – Ch.20 Interference of Light

(Diagram: Young's double slit experiment – animated: rays reach P, then fringes appear)

1. Coherent Sources

Interference is the redistribution of energy when two light waves of the same frequency overlap: it is maximum at some points and minimum at others. Coherent sources have a constant phase difference (and the same frequency) with time.
Two ordinary sources are not coherent, as the phase of an atom's emission changes randomly about every 10⁻¹⁰ s. So coherent sources are obtained from a single source: by division of wavefront (Young's slits, Fresnel's biprism) or by division of amplitude (thin films).

Superposition and intensity

Two waves with amplitudes a₁, a₂ and phase difference φ:

I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂) cosφ   (I ∝ a²)

Constructive: φ = 2nπ (Δ = nλ), Imax = (a₁+a₂)². Destructive: φ = (2n−1)π (Δ = (2n−1)λ/2), Imin = (a₁−a₂)².
Relation: φ = (2π/λ)Δ. Also Imax/Imin = [(a₁+a₂)/(a₁−a₂)]². For equal amplitudes I = 4I₀cos²(φ/2).

2. Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE)

Setup: monochromatic light falls on a narrow slit S, which illuminates two narrow slits S₁ and S₂ (distance d) placed equally far from S. The waves from S₁ and S₂ are coherent. They overlap, and alternate bright and dark fringes appear on a screen at distance D (D ≫ d).
The fringes are straight, parallel to the slits, and equally spaced.

3. Path Difference

Take a point P at distance y from the central point O. Then S₂P² − S₁P² = (y + d/2)² + D² − (y − d/2)² − D² = 2yd. So S₂P − S₁P = 2yd/(S₂P + S₁P). Since S₂P + S₁P ≈ 2D,

Δ = yd/D   (= d sinθ ≈ dθ)

4. Bright Fringe

Condition: Δ = nλ, so yd/D = nλ:

yₙ = nλD/d   (n = 0, 1, 2…)

n = 0 gives the central bright fringe at O.

5. Dark Fringe

Condition: Δ = (2n−1)λ/2:

yₙ = (2n−1)λD/(2d)   (n = 1, 2, 3…)

6. Fringe Width

The distance between two consecutive bright (or two dark) fringes: β = yₙ₊₁ − yₙ.
β = λD/d    Angular fringe width = λ/d

β is the same for bright and dark fringes. β ∝ λ, β ∝ D, β ∝ 1/d. So red fringes are wider than blue. If the whole set-up is immersed in a liquid, β′ = β/μ. With white light, the central fringe is white and the fringes on its sides are coloured (violet inner, red outer), disappearing after a few fringes.
Shift on introducing a thin sheet (thickness t, index μ) before one slit: the whole pattern shifts by (μ−1)tD/d towards that slit.

7. Intensity Distribution

With equal slit intensities I₀: I = 4I₀cos²(φ/2), where φ = 2πyd/λD.
• Bright centres: I = 4I₀ (φ = 0, 2π, …).
• Dark centres: I = 0 (φ = π, 3π, …).
• The average intensity is 2I₀ = I₀ + I₀: energy is not destroyed – it is only redistributed from dark to bright places.

Solved Numericals (step by step)

Ex.1 d = 1 mm, D = 1 m, λ = 5000 Å. Find β.
β = λD/d = 5×10⁻⁷ × 1/10⁻³ = 5×10⁻⁴ m = 0.5 mm.
Ex.2 Using Ex.1 data, find the position of the 3rd bright and 3rd dark fringes.
y₃(bright) = 3β = 1.5 mm. y₃(dark) = (2×3−1)β/2 = 2.5β = 1.25 mm.
Ex.3 β = 0.45 mm, d = 1 mm, D = 1 m. Find λ.
λ = βd/D = 0.45×10⁻³ × 10⁻³ / 1 = 4.5×10⁻⁷ m = 4500 Å.
Ex.4 The set-up of Ex.1 is immersed in water (μ = 4/3). Find β′.
β′ = β/μ = 0.5 × 3/4 = 0.375 mm.
Ex.5 The intensity ratio of two sources is 9 : 1. Find Imax/Imin.
a₁/a₂ = √9 = 3. Imax/Imin = (3+1)²/(3−1)² = 16/4 = 4.
Ex.6 In Ex.1 a sheet (μ = 1.5, t = 2 µm) covers one slit. Find the shift.
Shift = (μ−1)tD/d = 0.5 × 2×10⁻⁶ × 1/10⁻³ = 10⁻³ m = 1 mm, i.e. 2 fringe widths.
Ex.7 Path difference = λ/3. Find I in terms of I₀ (equal slits).
φ = 2π/3. I = 4I₀cos²(π/3) = 4I₀ × ¼ = I₀.
Proof β = λD/d: yₙ₊₁ − yₙ = (n+1)λD/d − nλD/d = λD/d. ∎

Q & A

Q1. What are coherent sources?
Sources with a constant phase difference and the same frequency.
Q2. Why can't two sodium lamps produce interference?
They are independent, so the phase difference changes randomly and quickly – the sources are not coherent.
Q3. Why must the slits be narrow and close?
Narrow slits diffract light so the waves overlap; a small d gives a wide, visible β = λD/d.
Q4. What happens to the fringes if d is doubled?
β is halved.
Q5. Does interference violate energy conservation?
No – energy is only redistributed; the average intensity is unchanged.
Q6. What happens with white light in YDSE?
A white central fringe with coloured fringes at the sides, soon merging into uniform white.
Q7. MCQ: Condition for the 2nd dark fringe: Δ = (a) λ (b) λ/2 (c) 3λ/2 (d) 2λ → (c).
Q8. MCQ: β ∝ (a) d (b) 1/d (c) 1/λ (d) 1/D → (b).
Q9. MCQ: Imax/Imin for amplitudes 2 : 1 = (a) 3 (b) 9 (c) 4 (d) 1 → (b) 9.
Q10. How does the pattern change if one slit is covered?
Interference stops, and a single slit diffraction pattern remains.
