Class 12 Physics – Electric Charges and Fields (Notes)

1. Electric Charge

Charge is a basic property of matter that causes electric force. Two types: positive (proton) and negative (electron). Like charges repel, unlike attract. Unit: coulomb (C).

Frictional electricity

Rubbing glass with silk: electrons move glass → silk. Glass becomes +ve, silk −ve. Only electrons move, never protons.

Conductors & insulators

Conductors (metals) have free electrons so charge flows. Insulators (rubber, glass) have none, so charge stays where placed.

Conservation of charge

Total charge of an isolated system is always constant.

Charge can neither be created nor destroyed, only transferred. Example: pair production γ → e⁻ + e⁺ (net charge 0 before and after).

Quantisation of charge

q = ± n e,   e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

Charge exists only in whole-number multiples of e. Fractions of e (quarks) are never seen free. Charge is also additive (scalar).

2. Coulomb's Law

Force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance, along the line joining them.

F = k q₁q₂ / r²,   k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²

ε₀ (permittivity of free space) = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻². SI unit of charge: 1 coulomb is that charge which repels an equal charge 1 m away in vacuum with force 9 × 10⁹ N.

Vector form: F₁₂ = k q₁q₂ r̂₁₂ / r². F₁₂ = −F₂₁.

Dielectric medium

In a medium of relative permittivity (dielectric constant) K = ε/ε₀: F_medium = F_air / K. For water K = 80, so force reduces 80 times.

Superposition principle

Force on a charge due to many charges = vector sum of forces due to each one, as if the others were absent: F = F₁ + F₂ + F₃ + …

Continuous charge distribution

Linear λ = dq/dl (C/m); surface σ = dq/dA (C/m²); volume ρ = dq/dV (C/m³). Field is found by integrating dE = k dq / r².

3. Electric Field

Region around a charge where another charge feels force. Intensity: E = F / q₀ (q₀ = small positive test charge). Unit N/C = V/m. Force on charge q in field: F = qE.

Point charge: E = kq / r², directed away from +q, towards −q. Superposition: E = E₁ + E₂ + … (vector sum).

Electric field lines – properties

  • Start from +ve, end on −ve charge.
  • Tangent gives direction of E; closer lines mean stronger field.
  • Never intersect (else two directions of E at one point).
  • Never form closed loops (not a conservative-loop like magnetic).
  • Uniform field: parallel, equally spaced lines.
  • Lines are perpendicular to a conductor's surface.

4. Electric Dipole

Two equal and opposite charges ±q separated by a small distance 2a. Dipole moment p = q × 2a, a vector from −q to +q. Unit C m.

Axial position (on the line through the dipole)

E = 2kp / r³ (r ≫ a), along p

Equatorial position (perpendicular bisector)

E = kp / r³ (r ≫ a), opposite to p

So E_axial = 2 × E_equatorial. Dipole field falls as 1/r³ (faster than point charge).

Torque on dipole in uniform field

Forces qE on each charge are equal and opposite (net force 0) but form a couple: τ = qE × 2a sinθ.

τ = p E sinθ, vector form τ = p × E

Max torque at θ = 90°; zero at θ = 0° (stable) and 180° (unstable).

5. Electric Flux

Φ = E A cosθ = E · A

Measures number of field lines crossing a surface. Unit N m² C⁻¹. Scalar. Max when surface ⟂ E (θ = 0°), zero when parallel.

6. Gauss's Law

Φ = ∮ E · dA = q_enclosed / ε₀

Gaussian surface: an imaginary closed surface chosen with symmetry, so E is constant on it. Only enclosed charge counts; flux does not depend on surface shape or size. Charge outside gives net zero flux.

Applications

(a) Infinite line charge (λ). Use coaxial cylinder, radius r, length l. Flux only through curved surface: E(2πrl) = λl/ε₀.
E = λ / (2πε₀ r) = 2kλ/r
(b) Infinite plane sheet (σ). Use a pillbox crossing the sheet; flux through two ends: 2EA = σA/ε₀.
E = σ / 2ε₀, independent of distance.
(c) Thin spherical shell (R, charge q). Outside (r ≥ R): E = q/(4πε₀r²), acts as if charge is at the centre. Inside: E = 0 (no enclosed charge). On surface: E = σ/ε₀.

Questions & Answers (Theory, Numericals, MCQs)

Q1. State and explain the quantisation of charge. (2 marks)

Charge on any body is q = ne, n an integer, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. A body gaining/losing n electrons gets charge ∓ne. Smallest free charge is e.

Q2. State Coulomb's law and write its vector form. (3 marks)

F = kq₁q₂/r² along the line joining charges. Vector: F₁₂ = k q₁q₂ r̂₁₂ / r², where r̂₁₂ points from q₁ to q₂. Force is attractive for unlike, repulsive for like charges.

Q3. Why can two field lines never cross? (2 marks)

At the crossing point the field would have two directions (two tangents), which is impossible since E at a point has one direction.

Q4. Derive E on the axis of a dipole. (5 marks)

Take point P at distance r from the centre. E₊ = kq/(r−a)² (away), E₋ = kq/(r+a)² (towards). E = kq[1/(r−a)² − 1/(r+a)²] = kq·4ar/(r²−a²)². For r ≫ a: E = 4kqa/r³ = 2kp/r³ since p = 2qa. Direction along p.

Q5. Derive E on the equatorial line of a dipole. (5 marks)

Each charge is at distance √(r²+a²). |E₊| = |E₋| = kq/(r²+a²). Components along the line of the dipole axis add: 2E cosθ with cosθ = a/√(r²+a²). Perpendicular components cancel. E = 2kqa/(r²+a²)^{3/2} → for r ≫ a, E = kp/r³ opposite to p.

Q6. Derive the torque on a dipole in a uniform field. (3 marks)

Forces +qE and −qE act on the ends, net force zero. Perpendicular distance between them = 2a sinθ. τ = qE(2a sinθ) = pE sinθ.

Q7. State Gauss's law and prove Coulomb's law from it. (5 marks)

Φ = q/ε₀. Take a sphere of radius r around q: E·4πr² = q/ε₀ so E = q/(4πε₀r²). Force on q₀: F = q q₀/(4πε₀r²), which is Coulomb's law.

Q8. Find E due to infinite plane sheet using Gauss's law. (5 marks)

See section 6(b): pillbox of area A; flux = 2EA (no flux through the curved side); enclosed charge σA. So 2EA = σA/ε₀, E = σ/2ε₀.

Q9. Why is E = 0 inside a charged spherical shell? (2 marks)

A Gaussian sphere inside encloses no charge, so Φ = 0 and by symmetry E = 0.

Q10. Difference between electric flux and field? (2 marks)

Flux is a scalar (N m² C⁻¹) over a surface; field is a vector (N/C) at a point.

Solved Numericals (Easy → Hard)

Easy 1. Two charges 2 µC and 3 µC are 10 cm apart in air. Find the force.
Given: q₁ = 2×10⁻⁶, q₂ = 3×10⁻⁶ C, r = 0.1 m.
Formula: F = kq₁q₂/r².
Step 1: numerator = 9×10⁹ × 6×10⁻¹² = 5.4×10⁻².
Step 2: divide by r² = 10⁻²: F = 5.4 N (repulsive).
Easy 2. Find E at 30 cm from a 4 µC point charge.
E = kq/r² = 9×10⁹×4×10⁻⁶/(0.3)² = 3.6×10⁴/0.09 = 4×10⁵ N/C, away from the charge.
Medium 1. The same charges as Easy 1 are placed in water (K = 80). Find the force.
F_medium = F_air/K = 5.4/80 = 0.0675 N.
Medium 2. Dipole: q = 2 µC, separation 4 cm, in E = 5×10⁴ N/C at 30°. Find torque.
p = q×2a = 2×10⁻⁶×0.04 = 8×10⁻⁸ C m.
τ = pE sinθ = 8×10⁻⁸ × 5×10⁴ × 0.5 = 2×10⁻³ N m.
Medium 3. Field at 10 cm from infinite line charge λ = 2 µC/m.
E = 2kλ/r = 2×9×10⁹×2×10⁻⁶/0.1 = 3.6×10⁵ N/C.
Hard 1. Charges q and 4q are 30 cm apart. Where is E zero?
Zero lies between them (like charges). Let x from q. kq/x² = k4q/(0.3−x)² → (0.3−x)² = 4x² → 0.3−x = 2x → x = 0.1 m from q (20 cm from 4q).
Hard 2. Flux through each face of a cube with charge q at its centre.
Total Φ = q/ε₀ by Gauss. By symmetry 6 equal faces: q/6ε₀ each.
