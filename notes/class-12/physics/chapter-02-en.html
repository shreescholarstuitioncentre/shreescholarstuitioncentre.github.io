Class 12 Physics – Gauss's Theorem (Notes)

1. Electric Flux

Electric flux is a measure of the number of electric field lines passing through a surface. For a small area, define the area vector A: its magnitude is the area and its direction is the outward normal n̂ to the surface.

Φ = E · A = E A cosθ

θ = angle between E and the normal. SI unit: N m² C⁻¹ (or V m). It is a scalar. Dimensions: [M L³ T⁻³ A⁻¹].

  • θ = 0° (surface ⟂ field): Φ = EA, maximum.
  • θ = 90° (surface parallel to field): Φ = 0, no lines cross.
  • θ = 180°: Φ = −EA (lines enter the surface).

Non-uniform field / curved surface: divide into tiny areas dA, so Φ = ∫ E · dA. For a closed surface, the outward normal is taken as positive, so lines leaving give +ve flux and lines entering give −ve flux. Φ = ∮ E · dA.

2. Gauss's Law (Theorem)

Φ = ∮ E · dA = q_enclosed / ε₀

The total flux through any closed surface in vacuum equals 1/ε₀ times the net charge enclosed by it.

Proof for a point charge (sphere)

Put charge q at the centre of a sphere of radius r. E = q/(4πε₀r²) is the same everywhere on the sphere and parallel to dA (θ = 0°). So Φ = E × 4πr² = [q/(4πε₀r²)] × 4πr² = q/ε₀. Result is free of r, so any closed surface around q gives the same flux (field lines passing through one surface also pass through every other).

Important points

  • E on the surface is due to all charges (inside and outside), but q_enclosed counts only charges inside.
  • A charge outside the surface: lines go in and come out, so net flux 0.
  • Shape and size of the surface do not change the flux.
  • If q is not at the centre, flux is still q/ε₀ (only the distribution of lines changes).
  • Gauss's law is true always but is useful for finding E only when symmetry exists.

Gaussian surface

An imaginary closed surface chosen so that (i) E is either constant in magnitude and perpendicular to the surface, or (ii) E is parallel to the surface or zero. Charge distribution decides the choice: sphere (point charge / shell), cylinder (line charge), pillbox (plane sheet). It must not pass through a discrete charge.

3. Applications of Gauss's Theorem

(a) Infinite line charge, linear density λ

By symmetry E is radial, perpendicular to the wire, constant at distance r. Take a coaxial cylinder of radius r, length l.

  • Curved surface: E ∥ dA, so flux = E × 2πrl.
  • Two flat ends: E ⟂ dA (cos 90° = 0), flux = 0.
  • Charge enclosed q = λl.

Gauss: E(2πrl) = λl/ε₀

E = λ / (2π ε₀ r) = 2kλ/r

E ∝ 1/r. Directed outward for λ > 0.

(b) Infinite plane sheet, surface density σ

By symmetry E is perpendicular to the sheet, same magnitude at equal distances on both sides. Take a cylindrical pillbox of cross-section A cutting the sheet.

  • Two flat faces (each area A): flux EA each, total 2EA.
  • Curved side: E ∥ surface, flux 0.
  • Enclosed charge = σA.

2EA = σA/ε₀

E = σ / 2ε₀

Independent of distance. For a thick conducting plate (charge on one face outside): E = σ/ε₀. For two oppositely charged parallel plates: between plates E = σ/ε₀, outside E = 0.

(c) Uniformly charged thin spherical shell (radius R, charge q)

  • Outside (r > R): take a concentric sphere of radius r. E(4πr²) = q/ε₀, so E = q/(4πε₀r²). The shell behaves like a point charge at the centre.
  • On the surface (r = R): E = q/(4πε₀R²) = σ/ε₀.
  • Inside (r < R): a sphere inside encloses 0 charge, so E = 0.

(For a solid non-conducting sphere, inside E = ρr/3ε₀ ∝ r.)

4. Graphical Variation of Electric Field

  • Infinite line: hyperbola E ∝ 1/r, never touches either axis.
  • Infinite plane: horizontal straight line E = σ/2ε₀, independent of distance.
  • Shell: zero for r < R, jumps to q/(4πε₀R²) at r = R, then falls as 1/r².

Questions & Answers (Theory and MCQs)

Q1. Define electric flux. Give its SI unit and nature. (2)

Φ = E·A = EA cosθ, the number of field lines crossing a surface. Unit N m² C⁻¹; it is a scalar.

Q2. State Gauss's law. Is it valid for any closed surface? (2)

Φ = ∮E·dA = q/ε₀, q = net enclosed charge. Yes, valid for any closed surface of any shape/size, since flux depends only on enclosed charge.

Q3. Prove Gauss's law for a point charge. (3)

Sphere of radius r around q: E = q/4πε₀r², E ∥ dA. Φ = E·4πr² = q/ε₀. Independent of r, so true for all surfaces.

Q4. Using Gauss's law derive E due to infinite line charge. (5)

Coaxial cylinder radius r, length l. Flat ends give zero flux, curved gives E·2πrl. Enclosed charge λl. E·2πrl = λl/ε₀ so E = λ/(2πε₀r).

Q5. Derive E due to an infinite plane sheet. (5)

Pillbox of area A. Flux 2EA through the ends, 0 through the side. Enclosed charge σA. 2EA = σA/ε₀, E = σ/2ε₀, independent of distance.

Q6. Derive E due to a thin charged spherical shell at (i) outside (ii) surface (iii) inside. (5)

(i) E(4πr²) = q/ε₀, E = q/4πε₀r². (ii) At r = R, E = q/4πε₀R² = σ/ε₀. (iii) Gaussian sphere inside has no charge, E = 0.

Q7. A charge q is placed outside a closed surface. What is the net flux? (2)

Zero. Every line entering the surface also leaves it.

Q8. Why can't Gauss's law give E for a finite rod or a dipole easily? (2)

The field is not symmetric, so no Gaussian surface exists on which E is constant.

Q9. Sketch E versus r for a spherical shell and explain. (3)

See graph: E = 0 up to R, discontinuous jump at R to q/4πε₀R², then ∝ 1/r². Zero inside as no enclosed charge.

Q10. A point charge is at the centre of a cube. Why does flux change if the charge moves out of the cube? (2)

Net flux changes from q/ε₀ to 0 because enclosed charge becomes zero. Field lines now enter and leave.

Solved Numericals (Easy → Hard)

Easy 1. A uniform field 500 N/C is perpendicular to a 0.2 m² surface. Find flux.
θ = 0°. Φ = EA cosθ = 500 × 0.2 × 1 = 100 N m²/C.
Easy 2. E = 100 N/C, area 2 m², the angle between E and the normal is 60°. Find flux.
Φ = 100 × 2 × cos60° = 100 × 2 × 0.5 = 100 N m²/C.
Medium 1. Find flux through a closed surface enclosing 8.85 µC.
Φ = q/ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻⁶ / 8.85×10⁻¹² = 10⁶ N m²/C.
Medium 2. Plane sheet σ = 2 µC/m². Find E.
E = σ/2ε₀ = 2×10⁻⁶/(2×8.85×10⁻¹²) = 1.13×10⁵ N/C.
Medium 3. Shell R = 10 cm, q = 5 µC. Find E at 20 cm, on surface and at 5 cm.
At 20 cm: 9×10⁹×5×10⁻⁶/(0.2)² = 4.5×10⁴/0.04 = 1.125×10⁶ N/C.
Surface: 4.5×10⁴/0.01 = 4.5×10⁶ N/C.
At 5 cm (inside): 0.
Hard 1. E at 20 cm from an infinite line charge is 3.6×10⁴ N/C. Find λ.
E = 2kλ/r → λ = Er/2k = 3.6×10⁴ × 0.2 / (2×9×10⁹) = 7.2×10³/1.8×10¹⁰ = 4×10⁻⁷ C/m.
Hard 2. Two large parallel plates carry +σ and −σ, σ = 8.85 µC/m². Find E between and outside.
Each plate gives σ/2ε₀. Between them the fields add: E = σ/ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻⁶/8.85×10⁻¹² = 10⁶ N/C. Outside they cancel: 0.
Hard 3. Charge 2 µC at one corner of a cube. Find the flux through each of the three far faces.
Imagine 8 cubes meeting at that corner; total flux q/ε₀ shared equally, so this cube gets q/8ε₀. Three faces touching the corner have Φ = 0 (E parallel). Remaining 3 faces share equally: q/(24ε₀) = 2×10⁻⁶/(24×8.85×10⁻¹²) = 9.4×10³ N m²/C each.
