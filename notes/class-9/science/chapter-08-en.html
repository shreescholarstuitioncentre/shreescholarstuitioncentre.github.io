Class 9 Science – Chapter 8: Force and Laws of Motion (UP Board)

1. Force

Force is a push or pull on an object. It can change the speed, direction or shape of a body, or start/stop its motion. SI unit: newton (N), 1 N = 1 kg m/s². It is a vector.

Balanced forces: net force is zero, so the state of rest or motion does not change (tug of war with equal pull). Unbalanced forces: net force is not zero, so velocity changes, i.e. the body accelerates. Friction is an unbalanced force that slows moving bodies; without it a body would keep moving forever.

2. First Law of Motion (Law of Inertia)

A body remains at rest or in uniform motion along a straight line unless an external unbalanced force acts on it.

Inertia is the natural tendency of a body to resist a change in its state of rest or motion. Mass is the measure of inertia: heavier body, more inertia.

Types: inertia of rest (coin drops in glass when card is flicked; passengers fall backward when bus starts), inertia of motion (passengers lurch forward when bus brakes suddenly), inertia of direction (passengers slide outward when a vehicle takes a sharp turn; sparks fly off a grinding wheel along the tangent).

3. Momentum

Momentum p = mass × velocity = mv
SI unit: kg m/s. Vector, direction same as velocity.

A heavy truck and a light ball at the same speed have different momentum; a small bullet has large momentum due to high speed. Greater momentum needs greater force to stop in the same time.

4. Second Law of Motion

The rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction of the force.

Derivation: Mass m, initial velocity u, final velocity v after time t. Change in momentum = mv – mu. Rate = m(v–u)/t. So F ∝ m(v–u)/t. Since (v–u)/t = a, F ∝ ma, i.e. F = kma. Choosing the unit of force so that k = 1 gives F = ma.

1 newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². Same force gives smaller acceleration to a larger mass. The first law follows from it: if F = 0 then a = 0.

Fig 2: Newton's second law – effect of mass on acceleration

Cricketer pulls hands back while catching a fast ball: time of stopping increases, so force (F = change in momentum/time) decreases and hands are not hurt.

5. Third Law of Motion

To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Action and reaction act on different bodies, so they never cancel each other.

Examples: swimmer pushes water backward and water pushes him forward; rocket expels gas downward and moves up; a gun recoils backward when it fires a bullet; walking (foot pushes the ground back, ground pushes us forward); a boat moves backward when a person jumps ashore.

Fig 3: Newton's third law – recoil of a gun

6. Conservation of Momentum

Total momentum of an isolated system before collision = total momentum after collision (if no external unbalanced force acts).
m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

Derivation: Balls A and B collide for time t. Force on B by A: F₂ = m₂(v₂–u₂)/t. Force on A by B: F₁ = m₁(v₁–u₁)/t. By the third law F₁ = –F₂, so m₁(v₁–u₁) = –m₂(v₂–u₂), giving m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂.

Recoil of gun: momentum before firing is zero, so m(bullet)v(bullet) + M(gun)V(gun) = 0, V = –mv/M. The gun moves opposite to the bullet with small velocity because its mass is large.

Short Answer Questions

Q. State Newton's first law.

A body continues in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless an external unbalanced force acts on it.

Q. What is inertia? What measures it?

Tendency of a body to resist change in its state of rest or motion. It is measured by mass.

Q. Define 1 newton.

The force that produces an acceleration of 1 m/s² in a body of mass 1 kg.

Q. Why does a cricketer draw his hands back while catching a ball?

It increases the time of stopping, so the rate of change of momentum, i.e. force, decreases and the hands are not hurt.

Q. Why do action and reaction not cancel out?

They act on two different bodies, while cancelling needs forces on the same body.

Q. Why does a passenger lean backward when a bus starts suddenly?

Lower part moves with the bus but the upper body stays at rest due to inertia of rest.

Long Answer and Numerical Questions

Q. A force acts on a 5 kg body and produces acceleration 2 m/s². Find the force.

F = ma = 5 × 2 = 10 N

Q. Find the momentum of a 20 g bullet moving at 100 m/s.

m = 0.02 kg, v = 100 m/s.
p = mv = 0.02 × 100 = 2 kg m/s

Q. A 3 kg gun fires a 20 g bullet at 100 m/s. Find the recoil velocity of the gun.

Initial momentum = 0.
0.02×100 + 3×V = 0, so 2 + 3V = 0
V = –0.67 m/s (opposite to the bullet)

Q. A 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s is stopped in 4 s. Find the retarding force.

a = (0–20)/4 = –5 m/s².
F = ma = 1000 × (–5) = –5000 N, i.e. a force of 5000 N opposite to motion.
