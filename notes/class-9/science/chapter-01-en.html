Class 9 Science – Chapter 1: Matter in Our Surroundings

UP Board / NCERT • Notes with Q&A

1. What is Matter?

Anything that has mass and occupies space (volume) is matter. Eg: air, water, book, milk. Not matter: light, heat, sound, love, smell (they are energy/feelings).

Ancient Indian philosophers said matter is made of 5 elements (Panch Tatva): air, earth, fire, sky, water. Modern science classifies matter by its physical properties (states) and chemical nature.

2. Particle Nature of Matter

Matter is made of tiny particles

(a) Particles are very small: Dissolving 2–3 crystals of KMnO₄ in 100 mL water, then diluting it again and again, still gives a coloured solution — so one crystal contains millions of tiny particles. 10 mL of dilute solution can be split into 10⁻⁵ parts or smaller.

(b) Particles have space between them: Sugar/salt dissolves in water without raising water level – particles fit into gaps.

(c) Particles are continuously moving: They have kinetic energy; speed rises with temperature. Proof: Diffusion — smell of incense stick/perfume spreads, ink spreads in water, faster in hot water.

(d) Particles attract each other: Force of attraction (cohesion) is strong in solids, weaker in liquids, weakest in gases. Iron nail is hard to break, chalk breaks easily, water cannot be cut with fingers.

Diffusion: intermixing of particles of two different types of matter on their own. Gas > liquid > solid in speed.

Other states: Plasma – ionised gas of super-energetic charged particles (stars, Sun, tube light, neon sign). Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC) – formed by cooling a gas of very low density to near absolute zero (1920s, Satyendra Nath Bose & Einstein; made in 1995).

4. Change of State

Temperature scale: K = °C + 273. Water freezes at 0°C = 273 K; boils at 100°C = 373 K.

Melting (fusion): solid → liquid at fixed melting point (ice: 273 K). Latent heat of fusion = heat needed to change 1 kg solid to liquid at its melting point without temperature rise. The heat energy overcomes attraction between particles, so thermometer stays constant. Hence 0°C water particles have more energy than 0°C ice.

Vaporisation: liquid → gas at boiling point (373 K for water). Latent heat of vaporisation = heat to change 1 kg liquid to gas at boiling point. Steam at 373 K is hotter in effect than water at 373 K (extra latent heat), so it burns more.

Sublimation: solid → gas directly (camphor, naphthalene, ammonium chloride, dry ice = solid CO₂). Deposition is the reverse.

Effect of pressure: Increasing pressure + lowering temperature liquefies gases. Solid CO₂ (dry ice) at 1 atm turns directly to gas; hence the name.

5. Evaporation

Evaporation = conversion of liquid to vapour below boiling point, at any temperature, only at the surface.

Factors

① Larger surface area ↑ ② Higher temperature ↑ ③ Lower humidity ↑ ④ Higher wind speed ↑.

Cooling caused by evaporation

Fastest (highest energy) particles leave the liquid, taking heat with them, so remaining liquid cools. Examples: sweating cools body; clothes dry in sun & wind; water in an earthen pot (matka) stays cool; acetone on palm feels cold; we wear cotton in summer (absorbs sweat); we sip hot tea from saucer (more surface area).

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Define matter.
✍ Anything that has mass and occupies space.
Q2. What is diffusion?
✍ Spontaneous mixing of particles of one substance into another.
Q3. Convert 25°C to Kelvin.
✍ K = 25 + 273 = 298 K.
Q4. Why are gases compressible?
✍ Large gaps between particles, so they can be pushed closer.
Q5. What is latent heat?
✍ Hidden heat absorbed/released during change of state without change in temperature.
Q6. Why does a desert cooler cool better on dry days?
✍ Evaporation is faster when humidity is low.
Q7. Name a substance that sublimes.
✍ Camphor / naphthalene / ammonium chloride.
Q8. What is plasma?
✍ Ionised gas with highly energetic charged particles, as in stars.
Q9. Why does steam cause more severe burns than boiling water?
✍ Steam carries extra latent heat of vaporisation (2260 kJ/kg).
Q10. Why is ice at 273 K more effective in cooling than water at 273 K?
✍ Ice absorbs latent heat of fusion, so it cools more.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the characteristics of particles of matter with examples.
✍ 1) Very small: KMnO₄ dilution. 2) Spaces between them: sugar in water. 3) Constant motion: diffusion of ink/perfume; speed rises with temperature. 4) Attraction: strong in solid, weak in gas. Each is supported by the activity described above.
Q2. Differentiate solid, liquid and gas on basis of shape, volume, compressibility, density and particle arrangement.
✍ See the table in Section 3 and Fig 1: solids are rigid with tightly packed vibrating particles; liquids flow with loosely packed sliding particles; gases fill any container with widely spaced fast random particles.
Q3. What is evaporation? Explain the factors and why it causes cooling.
✍ Evaporation is surface vaporisation below boiling point. Depends on surface area, temperature, humidity, wind. Cooling occurs because high-energy particles escape, removing heat; examples: sweating, matka.
Q4. Explain the change of states with a heating curve of ice.
✍ Ice at 253 K warms up to 273 K; at 273 K temperature stays constant while ice melts (latent heat of fusion); then water warms to 373 K; stays constant while it boils (latent heat of vaporisation); then steam warms further.
