1. Discovery of the Cell and Cell Theory

Robert Hooke (1665) observed a thin slice of cork under a self-designed microscope and saw honey-comb like compartments, which he named cells (Latin cellula = little room). Leeuwenhoek (1674) first saw free-living cells (pond water). Robert Brown (1831) discovered the nucleus. Purkinje (1839) coined ‘protoplasm’ for the fluid substance of the cell.

Cell theory (Schleiden 1838 for plants and Schwann 1839 for animals): all plants and animals are made of cells; cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Virchow (1855) added: all cells arise from pre-existing cells (omnis cellula e cellula). The electron microscope (1940) later revealed cell structure in detail.

2. The Cell – Types, Shape and Size

Unicellular organisms: one cell does all functions (Amoeba, Paramecium, Chlamydomonas, bacteria). Multicellular: many cells forming tissues, organs (humans, plants). Cells vary in size: smallest is Mycoplasma (0.1 µm); largest is the ostrich egg; longest are nerve cells; human RBC ≈ 7 µm. Shapes vary by function: RBC – round/biconcave; nerve – long and branched; muscle – spindle; Amoeba – changing shape.

3. Plasma Membrane and Transport

Plasma membrane (cell membrane) is the outermost covering of animal cells, made of lipids and proteins (fluid-mosaic structure). It is selectively permeable: it allows some substances to pass but not others.

Movement across membrane

Diffusion: movement of molecules from higher to lower concentration (O₂ and CO₂ exchange). Osmosis: net diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of high water concentration to low water concentration (Fig 1).

SolutionConcentration compared with cellEffect
HypotonicLower solute outsideWater enters, cell swells
IsotonicEqualNo net movement
HypertonicHigher solute outsideWater leaves, cell shrinks

Activities: dried raisins/apricots swell in water and shrink in sugar syrup; peeled egg in salt water shrinks, in distilled water swells; potato osmometer. Unicellular freshwater organisms and plant roots depend on osmosis.

Endocytosis: flexibility of membrane lets Amoeba engulf food (phagocytosis). Lipids are made in membrane itself and some protein-lipid synthesis occurs in ER of cells.

4. Cell Wall

Present in plants, fungi and bacteria outside the plasma membrane. In plants it is made of cellulose, rigid and permeable; gives shape, protection and support, and lets cells withstand large changes in surrounding medium without bursting. In hypotonic medium the cell takes in water and swells but wall prevents bursting (turgidity).

Plasmolysis: when a plant cell is placed in hypertonic solution (eg strong salt/sugar), water leaves, the protoplast shrinks away from the cell wall. Animal cells lack a cell wall.

5. Nucleus

Dark-stained spherical organelle in the centre, double-layered nuclear membrane with nuclear pores for exchange between nucleus and cytoplasm. Contains chromosomes (visible only during cell division) made of DNA and proteins; DNA contains genetic information in genes for inheritance. When not dividing, genetic material appears as thread-like chromatin. The nucleolus makes ribosomes.

Functions: controls all cell activities; has genetic information for inheritance; role in cell division and reproduction. Chromosomes are made of chromatin strands; function in inheritance of characters.

6. Cytoplasm and Cell Organelles

Cytoplasm = fluid content inside the plasma membrane, containing organelles; protoplasm = cytoplasm + nucleus. Organelles are membrane-bound structures with specific functions (Figs 2 and 3).

7. Cell Division and Cell Growth

Mitosis: a parent cell divides into two identical daughter cells with same chromosome number; occurs in growth, repair and replacement of body cells. Meiosis: in reproductive organs, forms gametes with half the chromosome number (4 daughter cells), for sexual reproduction.

Cell → growth in size → DNA duplicates → nucleus divides → cytoplasm divides → 2 cells. Tissues, organs and organ systems form from cells; new cells keep life going.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Who discovered cells and how?
✍ Robert Hooke (1665) saw box-like compartments in a thin slice of cork under a microscope.
Q2. State the cell theory.
✍ All organisms are made of cells; cell is the basic unit of structure and function; all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
Q3. Who discovered the nucleus?
✍ Robert Brown (1831).
Q4. Why is the plasma membrane called selectively permeable?
✍ It allows only some substances to pass through it.
Q5. Define osmosis.
✍ Net movement of water from high water concentration to low water concentration across a selectively permeable membrane.
Q6. What happens to an animal cell in a hypotonic solution?
✍ It swells because water enters.
Q7. What is plasmolysis?
✍ Shrinking of the protoplast away from the cell wall in hypertonic solution.
Q8. Why does a plant cell not burst in hypotonic solution?
✍ The rigid cell wall gives it strength.
Q9. Name the largest and smallest cells.
✍ Largest: ostrich egg; smallest: Mycoplasma.
Q10. Why are mitochondria called powerhouses?
✍ They release energy as ATP during respiration.
Q11. Why are lysosomes called suicide bags?
✍ On damage, their enzymes digest the cell itself.
Q12. What is the function of the Golgi apparatus?
✍ Storage, modification and packaging of materials, and formation of lysosomes.
Q13. What is the nucleolus?
✍ Dense structure in the nucleus making ribosomes.
Q14. What is the difference between chromatin and chromosome?
✍ Chromatin is thread-like and seen when the cell is not dividing; chromosomes are condensed and visible during cell division.
Q15. Name the three kinds of plastids.
✍ Chloroplasts, chromoplasts, leucoplasts.
Q16. Which organelles have their own DNA?
✍ Mitochondria and plastids.
Q17. What are ribosomes?
✍ Sites of protein synthesis.
Q18. State two functions of the vacuole.
✍ Stores substances and gives turgidity to cells.
Q19. What are prokaryotes? Give examples.
✍ Cells without a true nucleus: bacteria, blue-green algae.
Q20. Why is the ER called the cell’s transport network?
✍ It forms channels to carry materials between different regions of cytoplasm and nucleus.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Draw a neat labelled diagram of an animal cell and write functions of any five parts.
✍ See Fig 2 and the table in Section 6: nucleus controls; mitochondria produce energy; ER transports/makes proteins; Golgi packages; lysosomes digest.
Q2. Differentiate between plant cell and animal cell with labelled diagrams.
✍ See Figs 2, 3 and the comparison table in Section 6.
Q3. What is osmosis? Explain with an experiment the effect of hypotonic, isotonic and hypertonic solutions on cells.
✍ Osmosis defined in Section 3. Experiment: peel an egg and place it in water → swells; in salt solution → shrinks; in isotonic → no change. See Fig 1.
Q4. Differentiate between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
✍ See the table in Section 2: nucleus, chromosomes, organelles, size, examples.
Q5. Describe the structure and functions of the nucleus.
✍ Double membrane with pores, nucleoplasm, chromatin, nucleolus. Controls activities and carries hereditary DNA. See Section 5.
Q6. Explain the structure and functions of mitochondria and plastids.
✍ Mitochondria: double membrane with cristae, ATP production. Plastids: chloroplast (photosynthesis), chromoplast (colour), leucoplast (storage). Both have their own DNA and ribosomes.
Q7. Explain the role of the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus and lysosomes.
✍ ER makes proteins/lipids and transports; Golgi modifies and packs; lysosomes digest waste. Together they form a cooperative system.
Q8. What would happen if the plasma membrane ruptured? Why does the cell wall matter in plants?
✍ The cell would lose its contents and control over substances, and die. In plants the cell wall prevents bursting and gives support.
