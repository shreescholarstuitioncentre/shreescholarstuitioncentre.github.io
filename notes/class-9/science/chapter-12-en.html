Class 9 Science – Chapter 12: Improvement in Food Resources (UP Board)

1. Improvement in Crop Yields

India's population is rising, so food production must rise without more land. The Green Revolution increased grain production (wheat, rice) using high-yield varieties and better farming. The White Revolution (Operation Flood) increased milk production. Farming is done as Kharif crops (June–Oct, monsoon: paddy, soybean, maize, cotton) and Rabi crops (Nov–April, winter: wheat, gram, peas, mustard, linseed).

2. Crop Variety Improvement

Done by hybridisation (crossing genetically different plants): intervarietal (different varieties), interspecific (two species of the same genus) and intergeneric (different genera). Another method is genetic modification (introducing a gene for a desired character, giving transgenic crops).

Factors for improvement: higher yield; improved quality (protein, oil, preservability); biotic resistance (diseases, pests) and abiotic resistance (drought, salinity, heat, cold, waterlogging); change in maturity duration (shorter duration allows more crops and reduces cost); wider adaptability; desirable agronomic characters (tallness and branching in fodder crops, dwarfness in cereals).

3. Crop Production Management

(a) Nutrient management: Plants need 16 nutrients: carbon and oxygen from air, hydrogen and oxygen from water, and 13 from soil: macronutrients (needed in large amounts) – nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, sulphur; micronutrients (small amounts) – iron, manganese, boron, zinc, copper, molybdenum, chlorine.

Manure is organic (decomposed animal waste and plant residue): compost, vermi-compost (earthworms), green manure (plants like sun hemp, guar ploughed in). It adds humus, improves soil structure and water retention. Fertilizers are commercially made inorganic salts (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) giving quick, high yield; excess use pollutes water and harms soil, so they are used with care. Organic farming uses little or no chemicals, relying on manure, biofertilizers and biological pest control.

(b) Irrigation: wells (dug and tube wells), canals, river lift systems, tanks. Rain-fed areas use rainwater harvesting, check dams and watershed management.

(c) Cropping patterns: Mixed cropping: two or more crops grown together on the same land (wheat + gram), reduces risk. Inter-cropping: two crops in definite row patterns (soybean + maize), so pests and diseases do not spread. Crop rotation: growing different crops one after another in a planned sequence; a legume in the cycle restores soil nitrogen.

4. Crop Protection Management

Crops are harmed by weeds (unwanted plants that compete for food, water and light, e.g. Xanthium, Parthenium), insect pests (cut roots, stems, leaves, suck cell sap, bore into stems and fruits) and diseases (caused by bacteria, fungi and viruses, spread through air, water, soil).

Control: pesticides (herbicides, insecticides, fungicides) – harmful if overused as they pollute and enter food chains; preventive methods: proper seedbed preparation, timely sowing, intercropping, crop rotation, resistant varieties, summer ploughing; biological control and weeding by hand.

Storage of grains: losses due to biotic factors (insects, rodents, fungi, mites, bacteria) and abiotic factors (moisture, temperature). They cause weight loss, poor quality, discolouration and poor germination. Control: strict cleaning, drying in sun, fumigation with chemicals, proper storage in godowns.

5. Animal Husbandry

Cattle farming: for milk (milch animals: cows, buffaloes) and farm labour (draught animals). Milk output depends on the lactation period (milk-producing period after birth of a calf). Indian breeds (Red Sindhi, Sahiwal) resist disease and exotic breeds (Jersey, Brown Swiss) have long lactation; cross-breeding combines both. Needs clean shelter, roughage (fibre-rich fodder) and concentrates (protein-rich), and care against external parasites (skin), internal parasites (worms, flukes) and infectious diseases (prevented by vaccination).

Poultry farming: for eggs (layers) and meat (broilers). Cross-breeding of Indian (Aseel) and exotic (Leghorn) breeds improves chicks. Needs clean housing, suitable temperature, balanced feed (vitamins A, K for broilers), and protection from diseases and parasites.

6. Fish Production

Marine fisheries: fish caught from seas using fishing nets (purse seines), guided by echo-sounders and satellites (GPS); marine fish include pomfret, mackerel, tuna, sardines; mariculture farms prawns, oysters, mussels and seaweed. Inland fisheries: freshwater ponds, rivers, reservoirs and brackish water (estuaries).

Fig 3: Composite fish culture

Composite fish culture raises 5 or 6 species together: catla (surface feeder), rohu (middle feeder), mrigal and common carp (bottom feeders), grass carp (feeds on weeds). Fish do not breed in ponds, so seed is supplied by hormonal stimulation. No competition for food as each species feeds in a different zone; this increases total yield.

7. Bee Keeping (Apiculture)

Honey bees are kept in apiaries for honey and wax (used in cosmetics and medicines). Varieties: Apis cerana indica (Indian bee), Apis dorsata (rock bee), Apis florea (little bee) and the Italian bee Apis mellifera, which is commonly reared as it has high honey collection, stings less, stays in the hive for long and breeds well. Quality of honey depends on pasturage (flowers available for nectar and pollen). Bees also help in pollination of crops.

Short Answer Questions

Q. What is the Green Revolution?

The large increase in grain production achieved through high-yield varieties, fertilizers and better farming methods.

Q. Differentiate between manure and fertilizer.

Manure: organic, from plant and animal waste, adds humus. Fertilizer: inorganic, factory-made, gives quick nutrients.

Q. What is crop rotation? State one advantage.

Growing different crops one after another in a planned order. A legume in the cycle restores soil nitrogen.

Q. Differentiate between mixed cropping and inter-cropping.

Mixed: seeds mixed and sown together. Inter-cropping: crops sown in definite row patterns.

Q. What are weeds? Name two.

Unwanted plants growing with the crop that compete for nutrients. Xanthium, Parthenium.

Q. Why are fish seeds produced by hormonal stimulation?

Fish do not breed in ponds, so seeds are obtained artificially using hormones.

Q. What are broilers and layers?

Broilers are poultry birds raised for meat; layers are raised for egg production.

Q. What is pasturage in bee keeping?

The flowers available to bees for nectar and pollen, which decide the quantity and quality of honey.

Long Answer Questions

Q. Describe the factors for crop variety improvement.

Higher yield; improved quality; biotic and abiotic resistance; change in maturity duration; wider adaptability; desirable agronomic characters such as tallness and branching in fodder crops and dwarfness in cereals. Varieties are obtained by hybridisation or genetic modification.

Q. Explain nutrient management in crops.

Plants need 16 nutrients. Air gives C and O, water gives H and O, soil gives 13: macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and micronutrients (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl). Nutrients are supplied by manure (compost, vermi-compost, green manure) which enriches humus and soil structure, and by fertilizers which give quick results but cause pollution when overused. Organic farming reduces chemical use.

Q. What are the different cropping patterns? Explain.

Mixed cropping: two or more crops together to reduce risk. Inter-cropping: crops in alternate rows so pests do not spread and nutrients are used differently. Crop rotation: crops grown in a planned sequence on the same land, including legumes to restore nitrogen. Each reduces pests and soil exhaustion.

Q. Explain composite fish culture and its advantages.

Five or six species (catla, rohu, mrigal, common carp, grass carp) are reared together in one pond. Each feeds in a different zone (surface, middle, bottom, weeds), so there is no competition, and total fish yield is greater from the same pond.

Q. Describe the causes of grain storage loss and the methods to prevent it.

Biotic: insects, rodents, fungi, mites, bacteria. Abiotic: moisture and temperature. They reduce weight and quality and spoil germination. Prevention: strict cleaning of the store, drying grains, fumigation with chemicals and proper godown storage.
