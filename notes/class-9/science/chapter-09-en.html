Class 9 Science – Chapter 9: Gravitation and Floatation (UP Board)

1. Gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object. This force is called gravitation. The force by which the Earth attracts bodies towards its centre is gravity. The Moon revolves around the Earth and planets around the Sun because of gravitational force, which provides the centripetal force.

2. Universal Law of Gravitation

Every object attracts every other object with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
F = G m₁m₂ / d²
G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²
Fig 2: Universal law of gravitation

G is the universal gravitational constant, same everywhere in the universe. Force is attractive, acts along the line joining centres, and forms an action–reaction pair (third law). Importance: it explains the motion of the Moon and planets, tides, and why bodies fall to the Earth.

3. Free Fall and Acceleration due to Gravity (g)

When a body falls only under gravity, it is in free fall. Its acceleration is g, independent of its mass (in vacuum a feather and a stone fall together).

From F = GMm/R² and F = mg: g = GM/R²
g = 9.8 m/s² on Earth (M = 6×10²⁴ kg, R = 6.4×10⁶ m)
Fig 1: Free fall under gravity

g is maximum at poles, minimum at equator, and decreases with height and depth. Equations of motion with g: v = u + gt, s = ut + ½gt², v² = u² + 2gs (take g positive downward, negative for upward throw).

4. Mass and Weight

Mass: quantity of matter, constant everywhere, scalar, unit kg.
Weight W = mg: force of gravity on the body, changes with place, vector (downward), unit N.

g on the Moon is about 1/6 of Earth's, so the weight of a body on the Moon is 1/6 of its weight on Earth, while its mass stays the same.

5. Thrust and Pressure

Thrust = force acting perpendicular to a surface (unit N).
Pressure = thrust / area, P = F/A (unit pascal, 1 Pa = 1 N/m²).

Same force on smaller area gives greater pressure: a sharp knife cuts easily, nails have pointed tips, school bags have wide straps, tractors have wide tyres. Liquids and gases (fluids) also exert pressure, which increases with depth.

6. Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle

An upward force exerted by a fluid on a body immersed in it is buoyant force or upthrust. It is why a bucket feels lighter in water and why objects float.

Archimedes' principle: when a body is partly or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.
Upthrust = ρ × V × g
Fig 3: Archimedes' principle (Eureka can)

Applications: ships, submarines, hydrometers (measure density of liquids), lactometers, hot-air balloons.

7. Floatation (Sinking and Floating)

Weight > upthrust: body sinks (density of body > density of liquid).
Weight = upthrust: body floats (density equal or lower).
Weight < upthrust: body rises up to the surface.
Fig 4: Sinking and floating

Density = mass / volume (kg/m³). Relative density = density of substance / density of water (no unit). Iron nail sinks in water but an iron ship floats because its hollow shape displaces a lot of water. A ship sinks deeper in river water than in sea water because sea water is denser.

Short Answer Questions

Q. State the universal law of gravitation.

Every object attracts every other with a force proportional to the product of masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance: F = Gm₁m₂/d².

Q. Difference between gravitation and gravity?

Gravitation is the attraction between any two objects; gravity is the attraction of the Earth on objects.

Q. Difference between mass and weight?

Mass is constant, scalar, in kg; weight = mg varies with place, is a vector, in N.

Q. Define free fall.

Motion of a body falling towards the Earth under gravity alone, with acceleration g.

Q. State Archimedes' principle.

A body immersed in a fluid feels an upward force equal to the weight of fluid displaced.

Q. Why does a sharp knife cut better?

Small edge area gives greater pressure for the same force.

Q. Why does an iron ship float but a nail sinks?

The ship's hollow shape displaces water whose weight equals the ship's weight, so its average density is below water.

Long Answer and Numerical Questions

Q. Find the force between two 1 kg masses 1 m apart.

F = Gm₁m₂/d² = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 1 × 1 / 1² = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N, extremely small.

Q. A stone is dropped from a tower and takes 2 s to reach the ground (g = 9.8). Find the final velocity and tower height.

u = 0, t = 2.
v = gt = 9.8×2 = 19.6 m/s
s = ½gt² = ½×9.8×4 = 19.6 m

Q. A 60 kg person goes to the Moon. Find mass and weight on Earth and Moon (g = 9.8, g_moon = g/6).

Mass = 60 kg everywhere.
Weight on Earth = 60×9.8 = 588 N
Weight on Moon = 588/6 = 98 N

Q. Find the pressure when a force of 500 N acts on an area of 0.5 m².

P = F/A = 500/0.5 = 1000 Pa

Q. A 100 cm³ block is fully immersed in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8). Find the upthrust.

V = 100 cm³ = 10⁻⁴ m³.
Upthrust = ρVg = 1000 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.8 = 0.98 N
