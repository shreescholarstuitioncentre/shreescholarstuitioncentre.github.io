1. Laws of Chemical Combination

(a) Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier)

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.

Activity: Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ (white ppt) + 2NaCl. Weighing before and after mixing in a closed flask shows no change in mass (Fig 1). Landolt verified this experimentally.

(b) Law of Constant Proportions (Proust)

A chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed ratio by mass, whatever its source.

Water: H : O = 1 : 8 by mass (9 g water = 1 g H + 8 g O). Ammonia: N : H = 14 : 3. CO₂: C : O = 3 : 8.

Example: 5 g H₂ burns in 40 g O₂ → 45 g water? No. 1 g H needs 8 g O, so 5 g H needs 40 g O → 45 g water ✓. If 20 g O₂ is given, only 2.5 g H is used.

2. Dalton’s Atomic Theory (1808)

1) All matter is made of very small indivisible particles called atoms.
2) Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
3) Atoms of different elements have different masses and properties.
4) Atoms combine in small whole-number ratios to form compounds.
5) The relative number and kinds of atoms in a compound are constant.
6) Atoms can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

Limitations (later found): atoms are divisible (electron, proton, neutron); isotopes of same element have different masses; isobars of different elements have same mass number.

Dalton’s theory explains both laws: because atoms are not created/destroyed, mass is conserved; because the number of atoms in a compound is fixed, the mass ratio is fixed.

3. Atoms, Symbols and Atomic Mass

Atom – smallest particle of an element taking part in a chemical reaction; size ~ 10⁻¹⁰ m (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m); hydrogen atom radius 10⁻¹⁰ m. Atoms are too small to be seen even by a microscope, but are visible by scanning tunnelling microscope.

Symbols

Dalton used pictorial symbols; Berzelius suggested letters. IUPAC symbols: first letter capital, second small. From English names: H, O, N, C, Al, Cu(Cuprum). From Latin names: Na (Natrium), K (Kalium), Fe (Ferrum), Ag (Argentum), Au (Aurum), Pb (Plumbum), Hg (Hydrargyrum), Sn (Stannum).

Atomic mass

Defined as relative mass compared with carbon-12 atom: 1 atomic mass unit (u) = 1/12 of mass of one C-12 atom. (Earlier: oxygen/hydrogen scale.)

4. Molecules

Molecule = smallest particle of an element or compound that can exist independently and shows all the properties of that substance. Atomicity = number of atoms in a molecule (Fig 2).

5. Ions

Ion = a charged particle (atom/group of atoms with net charge). Cation = positive (loses electrons) – Na⁺, Ca²⁺. Anion = negative (gains electrons) – Cl⁻, O²⁻. Polyatomic ion = group of atoms with charge: NH₄⁺, OH⁻, NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻, PO₄³⁻.

Ionic compound (NaCl, MgCl₂) forms by transfer of electrons – Fig 3. The compound is neutral overall because positive and negative charges balance. Valency = combining capacity of an atom.

6. Writing Chemical Formulae

Rules: 1) Valencies/charges of ions must balance. 2) Write metal first, non-metal after. 3) Polyatomic ions in brackets if more than one. Criss-cross method: write symbols with valency, then swap valencies as subscripts.

7. Molecular Mass, Formula Unit Mass and Mole

Molecular mass = sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule. H₂O = 2×1+16 = 18 u; CO₂ = 12+32 = 44 u; HNO₃ = 1+14+48 = 63 u; NH₃ = 17 u. Formula unit mass is for ionic compounds: NaCl = 23+35.5 = 58.5 u; Al₂O₃ = 54+48 = 102 u; CaCO₃ = 100 u.

Mole concept

1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, ions). This is Avogadro number (Nₐ). Mass of 1 mole in grams = atomic/molecular mass = molar mass.
Moles n = given mass ÷ molar mass = number of particles ÷ 6.022×10²³ = Volume at STP ÷ 22.4 L

Eg 1. 1 mol H₂O = 18 g = 6.022×10²³ molecules. Eg 2. Moles in 22 g CO₂ = 22/44 = 0.5 mol; molecules = 0.5 × 6.022×10²³ = 3.011×10²³. Eg 3. 4 g He = 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ atoms; 12 g C = 1 mole. Eg 4. Mass of 0.2 mol of oxygen atoms = 0.2×16 = 3.2 g. Eg 5. Number of atoms in 1 mol H₂O = 3 × 6.022×10²³ = 1.806×10²⁴.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. State the law of conservation of mass.
✍ Mass is neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.
Q2. State the law of constant proportions.
✍ A pure compound always has the same elements in a fixed mass ratio.
Q3. In water, what is the mass ratio of H and O?
✍ 1 : 8.
Q4. What is 1 atomic mass unit?
✍ 1/12 of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.
Q5. Define atomicity. Give two examples.
✍ Number of atoms in a molecule: O₂ (2), P₄ (4).
Q6. What is an ion?
✍ A charged atom or group of atoms.
Q7. Give symbols of sodium, potassium and iron.
✍ Na, K, Fe.
Q8. What is the formula of aluminium sulphate?
✍ Al₂(SO₄)₃.
Q9. Find the molecular mass of H₂SO₄.
✍ 2+32+64 = 98 u.
Q10. What is Avogadro’s number?
✍ 6.022 × 10²³ per mole.
Q11. How many moles in 9 g water?
✍ 9/18 = 0.5 mol.
Q12. What is the difference between atom and molecule?
✍ Atom is the smallest particle of an element; molecule is a group of two or more atoms chemically bonded.
Q13. Why is a molecule of noble gas monoatomic?
✍ They are chemically inert, so their atoms do not combine with each other.

Long Answer / Numerical Questions

Q1. State and explain Dalton’s atomic theory with limitations.
✍ The six postulates and limitations are given in Section 2. Atoms are indivisible, same-element atoms are identical, they combine in whole-number ratios, and are conserved in reactions. Limitations: subatomic particles and isotopes/isobars exist.
Q2. What is a mole? Explain mole concept with an example.
✍ A mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles. 1 mol of any substance has mass equal to its atomic/molecular mass in grams. Example: 1 mol of H₂O = 18 g = 6.022×10²³ molecules, and 1 mol of any gas at STP occupies 22.4 L.
Q3. Calculate the number of molecules in 4.4 g of CO₂.
✍ Moles = 4.4/44 = 0.1 mol. Molecules = 0.1 × 6.022×10²³ = 6.022 × 10²² molecules.
Q4. Write the formula of: (i) magnesium nitrate (ii) ammonium phosphate (iii) iron(III) oxide. Find their formula unit mass.
✍ (i) Mg(NO₃)₂ = 24+2(14+48) = 148 u. (ii) (NH₄)₃PO₄ = 3×18+95 = 149 u. (iii) Fe₂O₃ = 112+48 = 160 u.
Q5. A compound has H and O in the ratio 1:8. How much H is in 27 g of water, and how much O?
✍ H = 27/9 × 1 = 3 g; O = 27/9 × 8 = 24 g, showing the law of constant proportions.
Q6. Explain how a sodium chloride ion pair forms.
✍ Na (2,8,1) loses 1 electron to become Na⁺ (2,8); Cl (2,8,7) gains it to become Cl⁻ (2,8,8). Opposite charges attract strongly, forming the ionic bond in NaCl (Fig 3).
