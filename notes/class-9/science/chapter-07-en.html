Class 9 Science – Chapter 7: Motion (UP Board)

1. Rest and Motion

A body is at rest if its position does not change with time w.r.t. its surroundings; it is in motion if it does. Rest and motion are relative: a passenger sitting in a moving train is at rest w.r.t. the train but in motion w.r.t. a tree outside.

Types: straight-line (rectilinear), circular, periodic/oscillatory (pendulum), random.

2. Distance and Displacement

Distance = total length of path travelled. Scalar, always positive, SI unit m.
Displacement = shortest straight-line distance from initial to final position, with direction. Vector, can be +, – or zero.

Displacement ≤ distance. They are equal only for straight-line motion without turning back.

Fig 1: Distance vs Displacement (A to B)

Example: An athlete runs one full lap of a 400 m circular track. Distance = 400 m, displacement = 0.

3. Speed and Velocity

Speed = distance / time (scalar)
Velocity = displacement / time (vector)
Average speed = total distance / total time
Unit: m/s (1 km/h = 5/18 m/s)

Uniform motion: equal distances in equal time intervals. Non-uniform: unequal distances in equal intervals. Speed is the magnitude of velocity only when motion is in a straight line.

4. Acceleration

a = (v – u) / t   SI unit m/s²

u = initial velocity, v = final velocity. If velocity increases, a is positive; if it decreases, a is negative (retardation/deceleration). Uniform acceleration = equal velocity change in equal time (free fall, g = 9.8 m/s²).

5. Graphs of Motion

Distance–time graph: straight sloping line = uniform speed (slope = speed); line parallel to time axis = body at rest; curve = non-uniform speed.

Velocity–time graph: line parallel to time axis = uniform velocity; sloping straight line = uniform acceleration (slope = a); area under graph = displacement.

Fig 2: Velocity–time graph, uniform acceleration

6. Equations of Motion (uniform acceleration)

(i) v = u + at
(ii) s = ut + ½at²
(iii) v² = u² + 2as

Derivation from v–t graph (Fig 2): slope = a = (v–u)/t, so v = u + at. Area of trapezium = ½ × (u+v) × t = s. Put v = u+at: s = ½(2u+at)t = ut + ½at². Eliminate t using t=(v–u)/a in s = ½(u+v)t: v² = u² + 2as.

Sign convention: take direction of motion as positive; for retardation put a negative. For free fall a = g.

7. Uniform Circular Motion

Motion along a circle at constant speed. Velocity keeps changing direction, so it is accelerated motion. Velocity at any point is along the tangent. v = 2πr / T (r = radius, T = time period).

Fig 3: Uniform circular motion

Examples: tip of a clock hand, Earth around the Sun, athlete on circular track.

Short Answer Questions

Q. Define acceleration.

Rate of change of velocity with time: a = (v–u)/t. Unit m/s².

Q. Can distance be zero when displacement is non-zero?

No. If no path is covered, displacement is also zero; displacement ≤ distance.

Q. When is average speed equal to average velocity?

When the body moves in a straight line without changing direction.

Q. What does a straight line parallel to the time axis in a distance–time graph show?

The body is at rest; its distance does not change.

Q. Why is circular motion accelerated even at constant speed?

Direction of velocity changes continuously, so velocity changes and acceleration exists.

Q. Give two differences between speed and velocity.

Speed is scalar, velocity is vector; speed is always positive, velocity can be positive, negative or zero.

Long Answer and Numerical Questions

Q. A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 5 s. Find final velocity and distance covered.

Given u = 0, a = 2, t = 5.
v = u + at = 0 + 2×5 = 10 m/s
s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½×2×25 = 25 m

Q. A train moving at 20 m/s is brought to rest in 4 s. Find retardation and distance before stopping.

u = 20, v = 0, t = 4.
a = (0–20)/4 = –5 m/s² (retardation 5 m/s²)
s = (u+v)t/2 = 20×4/2 = 40 m

Q. Derive s = ut + ½at² by graphical method.

Draw v–t graph: line from (0,u) to (t,v). Displacement = area of rectangle (u×t) + triangle (½×t×(v–u)). Since v–u = at, s = ut + ½t(at) = ut + ½at².
