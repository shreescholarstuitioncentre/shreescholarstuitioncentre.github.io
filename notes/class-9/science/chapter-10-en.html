Class 9 Science – Chapter 10: Work, Energy and Power (UP Board)

1. Work

Work is done when a force acts on a body and the body is displaced in the direction of the force.
W = F × s (force × displacement)
SI unit: joule (J). 1 joule = work done when a force of 1 N displaces a body by 1 m in its own direction.
Fig 1: Work done by a force

Conditions for work: (i) a force must act, (ii) the body must be displaced. Zero work: pushing a rigid wall (s = 0); a coolie walking on level ground carrying a load on his head (force and displacement are perpendicular); Moon moving around the Earth. Positive work: force and displacement in the same direction (lifting down, pushing a cart). Negative work: force opposite to displacement (friction, lifting a body upward against gravity by gravity's force).

2. Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work. Its SI unit is also joule (1 kJ = 1000 J). It is a scalar. A body that can do more work has more energy. Forms: mechanical (kinetic and potential), heat, light, sound, electrical, chemical, nuclear. Sun is the ultimate source of energy.

3. Kinetic Energy

Energy possessed by a body due to its motion.
KE = ½ m v²

Derivation: A body of mass m at rest is accelerated by force F to velocity v over displacement s. Using v² = u² + 2as with u = 0: s = v²/2a. Work done = F × s = ma × v²/2a = ½ mv². This work is stored as KE.

KE is proportional to mass and to the square of velocity: doubling speed makes KE four times. Examples: moving car, flowing water, bullet, wind.

4. Potential Energy

Energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration.
PE = m g h (gravitational PE)

Derivation: To lift a body of mass m to height h, force equal to its weight mg is needed. Work done = force × displacement = mg × h = mgh, stored as PE. PE depends only on the height, not on the path taken. Examples: water in a dam, stretched bow or spring, a raised hammer.

5. Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be changed from one form to another. Total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

Free fall check: Body of mass m at height h. At top: PE = mgh, KE = 0, total = mgh. After falling x: v² = 2gx, so KE = ½m(2gx) = mgx, PE = mg(h–x); total = mgh. At ground: PE = 0, KE = mgh. Total is constant throughout.

Fig 2: Free fall – PE changes into KE

Pendulum: at extreme positions PE is maximum and KE zero; at the mean position KE is maximum and PE zero. The sum stays constant (ignoring air resistance).

Fig 3: Energy in a simple pendulum

6. Transformation of Energy

Electric fan: electrical → mechanical. Bulb: electrical → light + heat. Microphone: sound → electrical. Loudspeaker: electrical → sound. Battery: chemical → electrical. Solar cell: light → electrical. Photosynthesis: light → chemical. Hydroelectric plant: PE of water → KE → electrical. Burning candle: chemical → heat + light.

7. Power

Power is the rate of doing work.
P = W / t   SI unit: watt (W). 1 W = 1 J/s.
1 kW = 1000 W, 1 horsepower = 746 W
Fig 4: Power – rate of doing work

Average power = total energy consumed / total time. Machines doing the same work in less time are more powerful.

Commercial unit of energy: kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 1 unit of electricity. 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h).

Short Answer Questions

Q. Define 1 joule of work.

Work done when a force of 1 N displaces a body by 1 m in the direction of the force.

Q. Define energy and give its unit.

Capacity to do work. Unit joule.

Q. Write the formulas of KE and PE.

KE = ½mv², PE = mgh.

Q. State the law of conservation of energy.

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted from one form to another.

Q. Define power. What is its SI unit?

Rate of doing work, P = W/t. Unit watt (1 J/s).

Q. Is work done by a person carrying a bag on level ground? Why?

No. Force (upward) is perpendicular to displacement (horizontal), so work = 0.

Q. What energy change occurs in a dam hydroelectric plant?

PE of water → KE of water → mechanical energy of turbine → electrical energy.

Long Answer and Numerical Questions

Q. A force of 10 N moves a body 5 m in its own direction. Find the work done.

W = F × s = 10 × 5 = 50 J

Q. Find the KE of a 2 kg body moving at 10 m/s.

KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 10² = 100 J

Q. Find the PE of a 5 kg body at 10 m height (g = 9.8).

PE = mgh = 5 × 9.8 × 10 = 490 J

Q. A boy does 1000 J of work in 20 s. Find his power.

P = W/t = 1000/20 = 50 W

Q. A 100 W bulb glows for 5 hours daily for 30 days. Find the energy consumed in kWh.

P = 0.1 kW, t = 5×30 = 150 h.
Energy = 0.1 × 150 = 15 kWh (15 units)

Q. A body falls freely from 10 m. Find its speed just before reaching ground (g = 9.8).

PE at top = KE at bottom: mgh = ½mv²
v = √(2gh) = √(2×9.8×10) = √196 = 14 m/s
