📌 Introduction: If the denominator of a fraction contains a surd (like √2), making it rational without changing the value is called rationalisation. It makes finding values and comparing numbers easier.

1. Surd

ⁿ√a is a surd if a is rational and ⁿ√a is irrational. √2 and ∛5 are surds, but √16 = 4 is not (it is rational).

2. Conjugate and Rationalising Factor

• Conjugate of a + √b is a − √b (and vice versa).
• Conjugate of √a + √b is √a − √b.
• The product of conjugates is always rational: (a+√b)(a−√b) = a² − b.
• Rationalising factor (RF): the number that, on multiplying, gives a rational result. RF of √a is √a; RF of a+√b is a−√b.

Useful Identities

(a+b)(a−b) = a²−b²  |  (√a+√b)(√a−√b) = a−b  |  (a±b)² = a²±2ab+b²

4. Solved Examples

Rationalise: 1/√3
Step 1: The denominator has √3, so multiply numerator and denominator by √3.
Step 2: (1×√3)/(√3×√3) = √3/3.
Answer: √3/3
Rationalise: 1/(√5+√2)
Step 1: Multiply by the conjugate √5 − √2 (top and bottom).
Step 2: Denominator = (√5)² − (√2)² = 5 − 2 = 3.
Answer: (√5 − √2)/3
Simplify: (√3−1)/(√3+1)
Step 1: Multiply by the conjugate (√3−1): (√3−1)²/[(√3)²−1²]
Step 2: Numerator = 3 + 1 − 2√3 = 4 − 2√3; denominator = 3 − 1 = 2
Step 3: (4−2√3)/2 = 2 − √3.
Find: Given √2 = 1.414, find 1/√2.
Step 1: 1/√2 = √2/2 (by rationalising).
Step 2: 1.414/2 = 0.707.
Find a and b: (√3−√2)/(√3+√2) = a + b√6
Step 1: Multiply by (√3−√2): (√3−√2)²/(3−2)
Step 2: = 3 + 2 − 2√6 = 5 − 2√6
Step 3: Comparing, a = 5, b = −2.
Simplify: 1/(3+2√2) + 1/(3−2√2)
Step 1: (3+2√2)(3−2√2) = 9 − 8 = 1
Step 2: 1/(3+2√2) = 3−2√2 and 1/(3−2√2) = 3+2√2
Step 3: Sum = 6.
Hard: If x = 2+√3, find x + 1/x.
Step 1: 1/x = 1/(2+√3) = (2−√3)/(4−3) = 2−√3
Step 2: x + 1/x = (2+√3)+(2−√3) = 4.

5. Q & A (click to see the answer)

Short Answer

Q. What is a surd?

Ans: An irrational root ⁿ√a where a is rational, e.g. √2.

Q. Conjugate of 3−√5?

Ans: 3+√5.

Q. RF of √7?

Ans: √7 (since √7×√7 = 7).

Q. RF of 2+√3?

Ans: 2−√3.

Q. (√5+√2)(√5−√2) = ?

Ans: 5−2 = 3.

Q. Rationalise 1/√5.

Ans: √5/5.

Q. Is √16 a surd?

Ans: No, √16 = 4 is rational.

Q. RF of 3√2?

Ans: √2 (3√2×√2 = 6).

Q. 6/√3 = ?

Ans: 6√3/3 = 2√3.

Q. 1/(√2−1) = ?

Ans: (√2+1)/(2−1) = √2+1.

Q. Is 1/√2 rational?

Ans: No, 1/√2 = √2/2 is irrational.

Q. (2+√3)(2−√3) = ?

Ans: 4−3 = 1.

Multiple Choice (MCQ)

Q. RF of 5−√3:
(a) 5−√3 (b) 5+√3 (c) √3 (d) 5

Ans: (b) 5+√3.

Q. 1/(√3−√2) = ?
(a) √3+√2 (b) √3−√2 (c) 1 (d) 5

Ans: (a) denominator = 3−2 = 1, numerator = √3+√2.

Q. 2/√2 = ?
(a) 1 (b) √2 (c) 2 (d) 2√2

Ans: (b) 2√2/2 = √2.

Q. (√7+√3)(√7−√3) = ?
(a) 4 (b) 10 (c) √4 (d) 2

Ans: (a) 7−3 = 4.

Q. 1/(2+√3) = ?
(a) 2+√3 (b) 2−√3 (c) 1 (d) 7−4√3

Ans: (b) 2−√3.

Long Answer

Q. Rationalise 1/(√7−√6).

Ans: (√7+√6)/(7−6) = √7+√6.

Q. Prove: 1/(√2+1) + 1/(√3+√2) + 1/(2+√3) = 1.

Ans: Rationalise each: (√2−1) + (√3−√2) + (2−√3) = 1 (middle terms cancel). ∎

Q. If x = 3+2√2, find x + 1/x.

Ans: 1/x = 3−2√2 (since 9−8=1). Sum = 6.

Q. Simplify: (√3+1)/(√3−1) + (√3−1)/(√3+1).

Ans: First = (√3+1)²/2 = 2+√3; second = 2−√3; sum = 4.
