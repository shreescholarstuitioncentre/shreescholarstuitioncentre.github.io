Class 9 Maths – Probability

UP Board · Handwritten-style notes

📘 Notes

1. Basic terms

  • Experiment: an activity whose result is uncertain (tossing a coin, throwing a die).
  • Trial: one performance of the experiment. Outcome: a possible result of a trial.
  • Event: a collection of outcomes (e.g. getting an even number on a die).
  • Favourable outcomes: those outcomes that make the event happen.

Coin: outcomes H, T. Die: outcomes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.

2. Empirical (experimental) probability

Probability found by actually performing the experiment and recording results:

P(E) = (number of trials in which E happened) ÷ (total number of trials)

  • The more trials we do, the more reliable the probability.
  • Keep a frequency table and count each outcome carefully.

3. Range of probability

  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1 – probability is never negative and never more than 1.
  • Impossible event: P = 0 (e.g. getting 7 on a die).
  • Sure (certain) event: P = 1 (e.g. getting a number ≤ 6 on a die).
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1, so P(not E) = 1 − P(E).
  • Sum of probabilities of all possible outcomes = 1.

4. Solving probability questions

  1. Read the table; find total trials (sum of all frequencies).
  2. Count the trials in which the event happened.
  3. Write P(E) = favourable ÷ total, reduce the fraction (or write as decimal).
  4. Use “not E” or “at least / at most” by adding the right frequencies.
  5. Check: all probabilities add to 1.

Probability can be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage – never as a negative number or above 1.

✏️ Solved Examples

EasyQ1. A coin is tossed 100 times: head 45 times, tail 55 times. Find P(head) and P(tail).

Given: A coin is tossed 100 times: head 45 times, tail 55 times. Find P(head) and P(tail).

To prove / find: P(H), P(T)

Solution:
Total trials = 100.
P(head) = 45/100 = 0.45.
P(tail) = 55/100 = 0.55.
Check: 0.45 + 0.55 = 1 ✓.

EasyQ2. A die is thrown 60 times and 6 appears 12 times. Find P(6).

Given: A die is thrown 60 times and 6 appears 12 times. Find P(6).

To prove / find: P(6)

Solution:
P(6) = 12/60 = 1/5 (= 0.2).

IntermediateQ3. Three coins are tossed together 200 times: 3 heads – 23 times, 2 heads – 72, 1 head – 77, no head – 28. Find P(at least 2 heads) and P(no head).

Given: Three coins are tossed together 200 times: 3 heads – 23 times, 2 heads – 72, 1 head – 77, no head – 28. Find P(at least 2 heads) and P(no head).

To prove / find: Two probabilities

Solution:
At least 2 heads = (2 heads) + (3 heads) = 72 + 23 = 95.
P(at least 2 heads) = 95/200 = 19/40.
P(no head) = 28/200 = 0.14.

IntermediateQ4. In a survey of 400 students: 120 walk, 180 come by bus, 100 by cycle. Find P(walk) and P(not by bus).

Given: In a survey of 400 students: 120 walk, 180 come by bus, 100 by cycle. Find P(walk) and P(not by bus).

To prove / find: P(walk), P(not bus)

Solution:
Total = 120 + 180 + 100 = 400.
P(walk) = 120/400 = 0.3.
P(bus) = 180/400 = 0.45 ⇒ P(not bus) = 1 − 0.45 = 0.55.

HardQ5. Prove that P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

Given: Prove that P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

To prove / find: P(E) + P(not E) = 1

Solution:
Let the experiment be done n times and E happen m times.
Then “not E” happens (n − m) times.
P(E) = m/n and P(not E) = (n − m)/n.
Adding: m/n + (n − m)/n = n/n = 1.

HardQ6. In 1500 families with 2 children: 475 had 2 girls, 814 had 1 girl, 211 had no girl. Find each probability and verify their sum.

Given: In 1500 families with 2 children: 475 had 2 girls, 814 had 1 girl, 211 had no girl. Find each probability and verify their sum.

To prove / find: P(2 girls), P(1 girl), P(0 girl)

Solution:
Total families = 1500.
P(2 girls) = 475/1500 = 19/60 ≈ 0.317.
P(1 girl) = 814/1500 = 407/750 ≈ 0.543.
P(no girl) = 211/1500 ≈ 0.141.
Check: 475 + 814 + 211 = 1500, so sum = 1500/1500 = 1 ✓.

❓ Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is an experiment, trial and event?

Answer: Experiment: an activity with uncertain result. Trial: one performance of it. Event: a set of outcomes we are interested in.

Q2. Why does probability lie between 0 and 1?

Answer: Favourable trials can never be less than 0 or more than total trials, so m/n lies from 0 to 1.

Q3. Give an example of an impossible and a sure event.

Answer: Impossible: getting 7 on a die (P = 0). Sure: getting a head or tail on tossing a coin (P = 1).

Q4. Can the probability of an event be 1.5 or −0.3?

Answer: No. Probability is always from 0 to 1.

Q5. If P(E) = 0.7, find P(not E).

Answer: 1 − 0.7 = 0.3.

Q6. A coin is tossed 1000 times and head comes 520 times. Find P(head).

Answer: 520/1000 = 0.52. With more trials this approaches 0.5.

Q7. Why is more trials better in empirical probability?

Answer: A larger number of trials gives a more reliable and stable estimate.

Q8. A bag has only red balls. Find P(drawing a red ball).

Answer: It is a sure event, so P = 1.

Q9. Find P(getting a number greater than 6) on a die.

Answer: No such outcome ⇒ impossible event ⇒ P = 0.

✅ MCQs

Q1. Probability of a sure event:
  1. 0
  2. 0.5
  3. 1
  4. 2

Answer: C – 1. Certain to happen.

Q2. Probability of an impossible event:
  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. ½
  4. −1

Answer: A – 0. Never happens.

Q3. Which cannot be a probability?
  1. 0.4
  2. 1
  3. 1.2
  4. 0

Answer: C – 1.2. More than 1 is impossible.

Q4. P(E) = 0.35. P(not E) = ?
  1. 0.35
  2. 0.65
  3. 1.35
  4. 0.5

Answer: B – 0.65. 1 − 0.35.

Q5. Coin tossed 50 times, head 20 times. P(head):
  1. 2/5
  2. 3/5
  3. 1/2
  4. 1/5

Answer: A – 2/5. 20/50.

Q6. Sum of probabilities of all outcomes:
  1. 0
  2. ½
  3. 1
  4. Depends

Answer: C – 1. Always 1.

Q7. Die: P(even number) from 60 throws with 28 even:
  1. 28/60
  2. 32/60
  3. 1/2
  4. 28

Answer: A – 28/60. Favourable ÷ total.

Q8. Value of P(E) always lies:
  1. Below 0
  2. Between 0 and 1
  3. Above 1
  4. Above 2

Answer: B – Between 0 and 1. 0 ≤ P ≤ 1.
