Class 9 Maths – Chapter 13: Circles

UP Board · Handwritten-style notes

📘 Notes

1. Circle, its parts and chords

A circle is the set of all points in a plane at a fixed distance (radius) from a fixed point (centre).

  • Chord: segment joining two points of the circle. Diameter: chord through the centre – the longest chord (= 2 × radius).
  • Arc: part of the circle. Segment: region between chord and arc. Sector: region between two radii and an arc.

Theorems:

  • Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre (and converse).
  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord (and converse: the line joining centre to mid-point of a chord is perpendicular to it).

2. Equal chords and distance from centre

  • Equal chords of a circle are equidistant from the centre.
  • Chords equidistant from the centre are equal.
  • The longer the chord, the closer it is to the centre.

Useful formula: if chord = 2a, radius = r, distance from centre = d, then r² = a² + d² (Pythagoras in right △OMA).

Exactly one circle passes through three non-collinear points.

3. Angle subtended by an arc

  • The angle at the centre is double the angle at any point on the remaining circle.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • Angle in a semicircle = 90°.
  • If a line segment subtends equal angles at two points on the same side, the four points are concyclic.

4. Cyclic quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on a circle is cyclic.

  • Opposite angles are supplementary: ∠A + ∠C = 180°, ∠B + ∠D = 180°.
  • Converse: if opposite angles are supplementary, the quadrilateral is cyclic.
  • Exterior angle = interior opposite angle.

Only a rectangle (or square) is a cyclic parallelogram.

✏️ Solved Examples

EasyQ1. Radius of a circle is 13 cm and chord AB = 24 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.

Given: Radius of a circle is 13 cm and chord AB = 24 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.

To prove / find: Distance OM

Solution:
OM ⊥ AB bisects it: AM = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 cm.
In right △OMA: OM² = OA² − AM² = 13² − 12² = 169 − 144 = 25.
OM = 5 cm.

EasyQ2. Angle subtended by an arc at the centre is 100°. Find the angle at a point on the major arc.

Given: Angle subtended by an arc at the centre is 100°. Find the angle at a point on the major arc.

To prove / find: Angle at circumference

Solution:
Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference.
Required angle = 100° ÷ 2 = 50°.

IntermediateQ3. Prove that equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

Given: Prove that equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

To prove / find: ∠AOB = ∠COD when AB = CD

Solution:
In △AOB and △COD: OA = OC (radii), OB = OD (radii), AB = CD (given).
△AOB ≅ △COD (SSS).
∠AOB = ∠COD (CPCT).

IntermediateQ4. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with ∠A = 80° and ∠B = 70°. Find ∠C and ∠D.

Given: ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with ∠A = 80° and ∠B = 70°. Find ∠C and ∠D.

To prove / find: ∠C, ∠D

Solution:
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.
∠C = 180° − ∠A = 180° − 80° = 100°.
∠D = 180° − ∠B = 180° − 70° = 110°.
Check: 80 + 70 + 100 + 110 = 360° ✓.

HardQ5. Prove that the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

Given: Prove that the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

To prove / find: AM = MB

Solution:
Let O be the centre, OM ⊥ AB. Join OA and OB.
In right △OMA and △OMB: ∠OMA = ∠OMB = 90°, OA = OB (radii – hypotenuse), OM = OM (common).
△OMA ≅ △OMB (RHS) ⇒ AM = MB (CPCT).

HardQ6. Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

Given: Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

To prove / find: ∠A + ∠C = 180°

Solution:
Join OB and OD (O is the centre).
Arc BCD makes reflex angle ∠BOD at centre; so reflex ∠BOD = 2∠A.
Arc BAD makes angle ∠BOD at centre; so ∠BOD = 2∠C.
Adding: ∠BOD + reflex ∠BOD = 2∠C + 2∠A. The left side = 360°.
2(∠A + ∠C) = 360° ⇒ ∠A + ∠C = 180°.

❓ Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is a chord? Which chord is the longest?

Answer: A segment joining two points of a circle. The diameter is the longest chord.

Q2. What is the angle in a semicircle?

Answer: 90°, because the arc is a semicircle and the angle at the centre is 180°.

Q3. State the converse of the theorem on perpendicular from the centre.

Answer: The line drawn from the centre to the mid-point of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

Q4. How many circles pass through three non-collinear points?

Answer: Exactly one.

Q5. A chord of length 6 cm is in a circle of radius 5 cm. Find its distance from the centre.

Answer: Half chord = 3. d² = 5² − 3² = 16 ⇒ d = 4 cm.

Q6. What is a cyclic quadrilateral?

Answer: A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on one circle.

Q7. Can a parallelogram be cyclic?

Answer: Only if it is a rectangle (opposite angles equal and supplementary ⇒ each 90°).

Q8. Angles ∠ABD and ∠ACD are in the same segment. If ∠ABD = 35°, find ∠ACD.

Answer: Angles in the same segment are equal: ∠ACD = 35°.

Q9. In a cyclic quadrilateral, one side is extended. Exterior angle is 75°. Find the interior opposite angle.

Answer: Exterior angle = interior opposite angle ⇒ 75°.

✅ MCQs

Q1. Longest chord of a circle is:
  1. Radius
  2. Diameter
  3. Tangent
  4. Secant

Answer: B – Diameter. It passes through the centre.

Q2. Angle in a semicircle is:
  1. 45°
  2. 60°
  3. 90°
  4. 180°

Answer: C – 90°. Half of 180°.

Q3. Angle at centre is 120°. Angle on the remaining circle:
  1. 240°
  2. 120°
  3. 60°
  4. 30°

Answer: C – 60°. Half of 120°.

Q4. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to:
  1. 90°
  2. 180°
  3. 270°
  4. 360°

Answer: B – 180°. They are supplementary.

Q5. Equal chords are ___ from the centre.
  1. Equidistant
  2. Nearer
  3. Farther
  4. Parallel

Answer: A – Equidistant. Equal chords – equal distances.

Q6. Chord 16 cm, radius 10 cm. Distance from centre:
  1. 4 cm
  2. 6 cm
  3. 8 cm
  4. 2 cm

Answer: B – 6 cm. √(100 − 64) = 6.

Q7. In a cyclic quad, ∠A = 105°. ∠C = ?
  1. 75°
  2. 105°
  3. 85°
  4. 90°

Answer: A – 75°. 180° − 105°.

Q8. Number of circles through 3 non-collinear points:
  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. Infinite

Answer: B – 1. Unique circle.
