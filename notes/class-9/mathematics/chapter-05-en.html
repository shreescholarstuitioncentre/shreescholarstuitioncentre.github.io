📌 Introduction: Polynomials are algebraic expressions in which the powers of the variable are whole numbers (0, 1, 2, …). In this chapter we study types of polynomials, zeros, the Remainder Theorem, the Factor Theorem and factorisation.

1. What is a Polynomial?

E.g. 3x² − 5x + 7 is a polynomial. Here 3, −5, 7 are coefficients; 3x², −5x, 7 are terms; the highest power 2 is the degree.

Not polynomials: 1/x + 2 (negative power), √x + 3 (fractional power).
A constant polynomial has degree 0; the degree of the zero polynomial is not defined.

2. Zero of a Polynomial

If p(a) = 0, then a is a zero of p(x). A linear polynomial has 1 zero, a quadratic at most 2, a cubic at most 3. A zero is where the graph cuts the x-axis.

Animated Diagram 1: p(x) = 2x − 4

The straight line cuts the x-axis at x = 2, so the zero is 2 (2×2−4 = 0).

Animated Diagram 2: p(x) = x² − 5x + 6

The parabola cuts the x-axis at x = 2 and x = 3, so there are two zeros.

3. Remainder Theorem and Factor Theorem

Remainder Theorem: When p(x) is divided by (x − a), the remainder = p(a).
Factor Theorem: (x − a) is a factor of p(x) ⇔ p(a) = 0.

4. Solved Examples

State: degree, coefficient of x² and constant term of 5x³ − 2x² + x − 7.
Step 1: Highest power is 3 ⇒ degree 3.
Step 2: The x² term is −2x² ⇒ coefficient −2.
Constant term = −7.
Find the zero: p(x) = 3x − 6
Step 1: p(x) = 0 ⇒ 3x − 6 = 0
Step 2: 3x = 6
x = 2.
Remainder: x³ − 3x² + 2x + 4 divided by (x − 2).
Step 1: a = 2, find p(2).
Step 2: 8 − 12 + 4 + 4 = 4
Remainder = 4.
Check: Is x + 1 a factor of x³ + x² + x + 1?
Step 1: x + 1 = x − (−1), so a = −1.
Step 2: p(−1) = −1 + 1 − 1 + 1 = 0
Yes, it is a factor.
Factorise: x² − 5x + 6
Step 1: Numbers with product 6 and sum −5: −2, −3.
Step 2: x² − 2x − 3x + 6 = x(x−2) − 3(x−2)
(x−2)(x−3).
Find k: (x−2) is a factor of x² + kx + 6.
Step 1: p(2) = 0
Step 2: 4 + 2k + 6 = 0 ⇒ 2k = −10
k = −5.
Factorise: x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6
Step 1: p(1) = 1 − 6 + 11 − 6 = 0 ⇒ (x−1) is a factor.
Step 2: Dividing gives quotient x² − 5x + 6.
Step 3: x² − 5x + 6 = (x−2)(x−3).
(x−1)(x−2)(x−3).
Hard: If (x+1) and (x−2) are factors of x³ + ax² + bx − 6, find a and b.
Step 1: p(−1) = 0 ⇒ −1 + a − b − 6 = 0 ⇒ a − b = 7 …(i)
Step 2: p(2) = 0 ⇒ 8 + 4a + 2b − 6 = 0 ⇒ 2a + b = −1 …(ii)
Step 3: (i)+(ii): 3a = 6 ⇒ a = 2, then b = −5.
a = 2, b = −5.

5. Q & A (click to see the answer)

Short Answer

Q. What is a polynomial?

Ans: An algebraic expression whose variable powers are whole numbers.

Q. Is 1/x + 2 a polynomial?

Ans: No, x has power −1.

Q. Degree of the constant polynomial 7?

Ans: 0.

Q. Degree of the zero polynomial?

Ans: Not defined.

Q. Zero of x − 5?

Ans: 5.

Q. Maximum zeros of a cubic polynomial?

Ans: 3.

Q. State the Remainder Theorem.

Ans: Dividing p(x) by (x−a) leaves remainder p(a).

Q. State the Factor Theorem.

Ans: (x−a) is a factor iff p(a) = 0.

Q. Is √x + 2 a polynomial?

Ans: No, the power 1/2 is not a whole number.

Q. If p(x) = x² − 3x + 2, find p(2).

Ans: 4 − 6 + 2 = 0.

Q. Coefficient of x in 4x² − 7x + 1?

Ans: −7.

Q. For p(x) = x³ + 2, find p(−1).

Ans: −1 + 2 = 1.

Multiple Choice (MCQ)

Q. Degree of 3x⁴ − x² + 5?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5

Ans: (c) 4.

Q. Zero of 2x + 5?
(a) 5/2 (b) −5/2 (c) 2/5 (d) −2/5

Ans: (b) 2x+5=0 ⇒ x = −5/2.

Q. A factor of x²−4x+3?
(a) x+1 (b) x−1 (c) x+3 (d) x−2

Ans: (b) p(1) = 1−4+3 = 0.

Q. Remainder when x³+1 is divided by x+1?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) −1

Ans: (a) p(−1) = 0.

Q. Which is a polynomial?
(a) x + 1/x (b) √x (c) x²+2x (d) x⁻²

Ans: (c) x² + 2x.

Long Answer

Q. Factorise: 2x² + 7x + 3.

Ans: Product 2×3 = 6, sum 7 ⇒ 1, 6. 2x² + x + 6x + 3 = x(2x+1) + 3(2x+1) = (2x+1)(x+3).

Q. Find the remainder when x³ + 3x² + 3x + 1 is divided by (x+2).

Ans: a = −2. p(−2) = −8 + 12 − 6 + 1 = −1.

Q. Factorise: x³ − 2x² − x + 2.

Ans: p(1) = 0 ⇒ (x−1). Dividing gives x² − x − 2 = (x−2)(x+1). Answer: (x−1)(x−2)(x+1).

Q. Prove the Factor Theorem.

Ans: By division, p(x) = (x−a)q(x) + r. Putting x = a gives p(a) = r. If p(a) = 0 then r = 0 ⇒ (x−a) is a factor. Conversely, if (x−a) is a factor then r = 0 ⇒ p(a) = 0. ∎
