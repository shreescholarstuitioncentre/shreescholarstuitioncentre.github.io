2. Rational Numbers and Decimal Expansion

The decimal expansion of a rational number is either terminating (3/4 = 0.75) or non-terminating recurring (1/3 = 0.333… = 0.3̄). An irrational number has a non-terminating non-recurring expansion, e.g. √2 = 1.41421356…

Converting recurring decimals to p/q

Example: Express 0.6666… in p/q form.
Solution: Let x = 0.666… …(i)
10x = 6.666… …(ii)
(ii) − (i): 9x = 6
⇒ x = 6/9 = 2/3.
Example: Express 0.4777… in p/q form.
Solution: x = 0.4777…
10x = 4.777… …(i)
100x = 47.777… …(ii)
(ii) − (i): 90x = 43 ⇒ x = 43/90.
Example: Express 0.235235235… in p/q form.
Solution: The repeating block has 3 digits, so multiply by 1000.
1000x = 235.235235…; x = 0.235235…
999x = 235 ⇒ x = 235/999.

3. Irrational Numbers

A number that cannot be written as p/q (q ≠ 0, p, q integers) is irrational. Theorem: If a prime p divides a², then p divides a.

Prove: √2 is irrational.
Proof (by contradiction):
Step 1: Assume √2 is rational, so √2 = a/b with a, b co-prime and b ≠ 0.
Step 2: Squaring: 2 = a²/b² ⇒ a² = 2b².
Step 3: So 2 divides a² ⇒ 2 divides a. Let a = 2c.
Step 4: (2c)² = 2b² ⇒ 4c² = 2b² ⇒ b² = 2c².
Step 5: So 2 divides b too.
Step 6: Then a and b share the factor 2, contradicting co-primality.
Hence √2 is irrational. ∎
Example: Prove that 3 + √2 is irrational.
Solution: Suppose 3 + √2 = r (rational). Then √2 = r − 3, which is rational — contradiction since √2 is irrational. Hence 3 + √2 is irrational. ∎

6. Operations on Real Numbers and Rationalisation

• Rational ± irrational = irrational  • Non-zero rational × irrational = irrational
• Sum/product of two irrationals may or may not be irrational (√2 × √2 = 2).
• Identities: √a·√b = √ab; (√a+√b)(√a−√b) = a−b.
Rationalise: 1/(√5 + √2).
Step 1: Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate (√5 − √2).
Step 2: = (√5 − √2) / [(√5)² − (√2)²]
Step 3: = (√5 − √2)/(5 − 2) = (√5 − √2)/3.

7. Laws of Exponents

aᵐ·aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ; (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ; aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ; aᵐbᵐ = (ab)ᵐ; a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a; a⁰ = 1.
Find: 64^(1/3)
64 = 4³ ⇒ 64^(1/3) = (4³)^(1/3) = 4^(3×1/3) = 4.
Simplify: 2^(2/3) · 2^(1/3)
= 2^(2/3 + 1/3) = 2¹ = 2.

8. Q & A (click to see the answer)

Very Short / Short Answer

Q. Define a rational number.

Ans: A number that can be written as p/q with p, q integers and q ≠ 0.

Q. Is 0 a rational number?

Ans: Yes, 0 = 0/1.

Q. Write an irrational number between 1 and 2.

Ans: √2 = 1.414…, which lies between 1 and 2.

Q. Write two rational numbers between 3 and 4.

Ans: 3.2 = 16/5 and 3.5 = 7/2.

Q. Is π rational or irrational?

Ans: Irrational; its expansion is non-terminating non-recurring (22/7 is only an approximation).

Q. What kind of decimal is 1/7?

Ans: Non-terminating recurring: 0.142857 repeating.

Q. What type of number is √9?

Ans: √9 = 3, so rational (and an integer).

Q. Value of (√3 + √2)(√3 − √2)?

Ans: 3 − 2 = 1.

Q. Write 0.3̄ as p/q.

Ans: x = 0.333…; 10x − x = 3 ⇒ x = 1/3.

Q. Why do we rationalise the denominator?

Ans: To make the denominator rational so the value is easier to find.

Q. Conjugate of 2 + √3?

Ans: 2 − √3.

Q. Value of 81^(1/4)?

Ans: 81 = 3⁴ ⇒ 3.

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

Q. Which is irrational?
(a) √4 (b) √7 (c) 0.25 (d) 22/7

Ans: (b) √7, as 7 is not a perfect square.

Q. 0.7̄ = ?
(a) 7/10 (b) 7/9 (c) 7/99 (d) 70/99

Ans: (b) 7/9: 10x−x=7 ⇒ 9x=7.

Q. √2 × √8 = ?
(a) 4 (b) 16 (c) √10 (d) 2

Ans: (a) √16 = 4.

Q. 2^(1/2) × 2^(1/2) = ?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) √2

Ans: (b) 2^(1/2+1/2) = 2.

Q. Is N ⊂ Z true?
(a) Yes (b) No

Ans: (a) Natural numbers are part of integers.

Long Answer

Q. Prove that √3 is irrational.

Ans: Assume √3 = a/b (co-prime). Then a² = 3b² ⇒ 3 | a² ⇒ 3 | a, let a = 3c. Then 9c² = 3b² ⇒ b² = 3c² ⇒ 3 | b. So a, b share factor 3 — contradiction. Hence √3 is irrational.

Q. Rationalise 1/(7+3√2).

Ans: Multiply by conjugate 7 − 3√2: (7−3√2)/(49 − 18) = (7−3√2)/31.

Q. Simplify: (2^(1/3))·(2^(1/4)) ÷ 2^(1/12).

Ans: Exponent: 1/3 + 1/4 − 1/12 = (4+3−1)/12 = 1/2 ⇒ √2.

Q. Express 0.121212… as p/q.

Ans: x = 0.1212…; 100x = 12.1212…; 99x = 12 ⇒ x = 12/99 = 4/33.
