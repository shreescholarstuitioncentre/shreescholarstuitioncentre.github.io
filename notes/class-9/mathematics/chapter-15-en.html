Class 9 Maths – Statistics

UP Board · Handwritten-style notes

📘 Notes

1. Data and its presentation

Statistics deals with collecting, organising, presenting and interpreting data. Each number is an observation.

  • Primary data: collected by yourself. Secondary data: taken from existing sources.
  • Raw (ungrouped) data is unorganised. A frequency distribution table lists each value with its frequency (how many times it occurs); tally marks (||||) help count.
  • Bar graph: equal-width bars with equal gaps; height = frequency.

2. Grouped frequency distribution

  • Large data is grouped into class intervals, e.g. 10–20.
  • Lower limit = 10, upper limit = 20. Class size (width) = upper − lower = 10.
  • Class mark = (lower + upper) ÷ 2 = 15.
  • Range = highest value − lowest value.
  • Exclusive classes (10–20, 20–30): upper limit not included in that class. Inclusive classes (10–19, 20–29) are converted to exclusive by subtracting 0.5 from lower and adding 0.5 to upper limits.

3. Histogram and frequency polygon

  • Histogram: for continuous classes – rectangles with no gaps; class sizes on x-axis, frequencies on y-axis.
  • If class widths are unequal, take height = (frequency ÷ class width) × smallest class width.
  • Frequency polygon: mark class marks at the tops of bars, join them with line segments, and extend to class marks of an imaginary class of zero frequency at each end.

4. Mean, median and mode

  • Mean = (sum of all observations) ÷ (number of observations) = Σx/n. With frequencies: Σfx/Σf.
  • Median: arrange in order. n odd → ((n + 1)/2)th value. n even → average of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th values.
  • Mode: the value occurring most often (highest frequency).

If each observation is increased by k, the mean also increases by k.

✏️ Solved Examples

EasyQ1. Find the mean of 5, 7, 8, 10, 15.

Given: Find the mean of 5, 7, 8, 10, 15.

To prove / find: Mean

Solution:
Sum = 5 + 7 + 8 + 10 + 15 = 45. n = 5.
Mean = 45 ÷ 5 = 9.

EasyQ2. Find the median of 3, 9, 5, 7, 11, 2, 8.

Given: Find the median of 3, 9, 5, 7, 11, 2, 8.

To prove / find: Median

Solution:
Arrange in order: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11. n = 7 (odd).
Median = ((7 + 1)/2)th = 4th value = 7.

IntermediateQ3. Find the mean from: x = 2, 4, 6, 8 with f = 3, 5, 4, 3.

Given: Find the mean from: x = 2, 4, 6, 8 with f = 3, 5, 4, 3.

To prove / find: Mean

Solution:
Σf = 3 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 15.
fx = 6, 20, 24, 24 ⇒ Σfx = 74.
Mean = Σfx ÷ Σf = 74 ÷ 15 = 4.93 (approx).

IntermediateQ4. Find the median of 12, 7, 9, 15, 10, 6.

Given: Find the median of 12, 7, 9, 15, 10, 6.

To prove / find: Median

Solution:
Arrange: 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 15. n = 6 (even).
3rd value = 9, 4th value = 10.
Median = (9 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9.5.

HardQ5. Mean of 5 numbers is 27. If one number is excluded, mean of the remaining is 25. Find the excluded number.

Given: Mean of 5 numbers is 27. If one number is excluded, mean of the remaining is 25. Find the excluded number.

To prove / find: Excluded number

Solution:
Sum of 5 numbers = 5 × 27 = 135.
Sum of remaining 4 = 4 × 25 = 100.
Excluded number = 135 − 100 = 35.

HardQ6. Mean of 25 observations is 36. Mean of the first 13 is 32 and of the last 13 is 40. Find the 13th observation.

Given: Mean of 25 observations is 36. Mean of the first 13 is 32 and of the last 13 is 40. Find the 13th observation.

To prove / find: 13th observation

Solution:
Total = 25 × 36 = 900.
Sum of first 13 = 13 × 32 = 416. Sum of last 13 = 13 × 40 = 520.
The 13th observation is counted twice: 416 + 520 = 936 = 900 + (13th).
13th observation = 936 − 900 = 36.

❓ Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is the difference between primary and secondary data?

Answer: Primary data is collected first-hand by the investigator; secondary data is taken from already published sources.

Q2. What is frequency?

Answer: The number of times an observation occurs in the data.

Q3. Find the class mark of the class 20–30.

Answer: (20 + 30) ÷ 2 = 25.

Q4. Find the range of 3, 9, 15, 2.

Answer: Highest 15 − lowest 2 = 13.

Q5. Find the mode of 2, 4, 4, 5, 7, 4, 9, 5.

Answer: 4 occurs 3 times (most often) ⇒ mode = 4.

Q6. How does a histogram differ from a bar graph?

Answer: Histogram: continuous classes, no gaps, width has meaning. Bar graph: separate categories, equal gaps, width is not meaningful.

Q7. How do you draw a frequency polygon?

Answer: Find class marks, plot (class mark, frequency), join the points by line segments and close the ends on the x-axis at class marks of imaginary classes at both ends.

Q8. The mean of 10 observations is 20. Each is increased by 5. New mean?

Answer: 25 – the mean also increases by 5.

Q9. Find the mean of the first 5 natural numbers.

Answer: (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5) ÷ 5 = 15 ÷ 5 = 3.

✅ MCQs

Q1. Mean of 2, 4, 6, 8:
  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 20

Answer: B – 5. 20 ÷ 4 = 5.

Q2. Median of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9:
  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 7
  4. 25

Answer: B – 5. Middle (3rd) value.

Q3. Mode of 3, 5, 5, 6, 7:
  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 6
  4. 7

Answer: B – 5. 5 occurs twice.

Q4. Class mark of 10–20:
  1. 10
  2. 15
  3. 20
  4. 30

Answer: B – 15. (10 + 20)/2.

Q5. In a histogram the bars:
  1. Have gaps
  2. Have no gaps
  3. Are circles
  4. Are lines

Answer: B – Have no gaps. Continuous classes.

Q6. Range of 4, 9, 2, 12:
  1. 8
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 14

Answer: B – 10. 12 − 2.

Q7. Median of 4 observations lies between:
  1. 1st & 2nd
  2. 2nd & 3rd
  3. 3rd & 4th
  4. None

Answer: B – 2nd & 3rd. Average of (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th.

Q8. Class size of 30–40:
  1. 5
  2. 10
  3. 30
  4. 70

Answer: B – 10. 40 − 30.
