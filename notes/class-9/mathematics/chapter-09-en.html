📌 Introduction: The word Geometry comes from the Greek geo (earth) and metrein (to measure). The Greek mathematician Euclid (about 300 BC, Alexandria) is called the Father of Geometry. His book Elements has 13 chapters.

Undefined terms: In modern geometry, point, line and plane are taken as undefined.

2. Axioms and Postulates

Axioms: universal self-evident truths used throughout mathematics. Postulates: self-evident truths specific to geometry. Theorem: a statement proved using axioms.

Euclid's 7 Axioms

1. Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
2. If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
3. If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
4. Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another.
5. The whole is greater than the part.
6. Things which are double of the same things are equal to one another.
7. Things which are halves of the same things are equal to one another.

Euclid's 5 Postulates

1. A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.
2. A terminated line can be produced indefinitely.
3. A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius.
4. All right angles are equal to one another.
5. If a straight line falling on two straight lines makes the interior angles on the same side together less than two right angles, the two lines, if produced indefinitely, meet on that side.

Playfair's Axiom (equivalent to the fifth postulate): For every line l and a point P not on it, there is exactly one line through P parallel to l.
Axiom 5.1: Given two distinct points, there is a unique line passing through them.

3. Solved Examples / Proofs

Prove: A, B, C are three points on a line with B between A and C. Then AB + BC = AC.
Step 1: Placing segment AB and segment BC together makes them coincide with AC.
Step 2: By Axiom 4 (things which coincide are equal), AB + BC = AC. ∎
Prove: An equilateral triangle can be constructed on any line segment AB.
Step 1: Draw a circle with centre A and radius AB (Postulate 3).
Step 2: Draw another circle with centre B and the same radius; they meet at C.
Step 3: AC = AB and BC = AB (radii of the same circle).
Step 4: By Axiom 1, AC = BC = AB. So ΔABC is equilateral. ∎
Prove: If a = b then a + 3 = b + 3 and a − 2 = b − 2.
Step 1: Adding equals (3) to equals gives equal wholes (Axiom 2) ⇒ a + 3 = b + 3.
Step 2: Subtracting equals (2) from equals gives equal remainders (Axiom 3) ⇒ a − 2 = b − 2. ∎
Prove: Two distinct lines cannot have more than one point in common.
Step 1: Suppose two lines meet at two distinct points P and Q.
Step 2: Then two lines pass through both P and Q.
Step 3: This contradicts Axiom 5.1 (only one line through two points).
So two lines meet in at most one point. ∎
Prove: Points A, B, C, D lie on a line in this order and AC = BD. Then AB = CD.
Step 1: AC = AB + BC and BD = BC + CD.
Step 2: AC = BD ⇒ AB + BC = BC + CD.
Step 3: Subtract BC from both (Axiom 3) ⇒ AB = CD. ∎
Hard: Prove that every line segment has one and only one mid-point.
Step 1: Suppose AB has two mid-points M and N (M ≠ N).
Step 2: AM = ½AB and AN = ½AB.
Step 3: Halves of the same thing are equal (Axiom 7) ⇒ AM = AN.
Step 4: Only one point of AB lies at that distance from A ⇒ M and N coincide. Contradiction. ∎

Short Answer

Q. Who is the Father of Geometry?

Ans: Euclid.

Q. Name of Euclid's book and its chapters?

Ans: Elements, 13 chapters.

Q. Define a point.

Ans: That which has no part.

Q. Difference between an axiom and a postulate?

Ans: Axioms are universal truths for all mathematics; postulates are self-evident truths specific to geometry.

Q. What is a theorem?

Ans: A statement proved using axioms or earlier proved results.

Q. How many postulates and axioms did Euclid give?

Ans: 5 postulates and 7 axioms.

Q. Euclid's definition of a line?

Ans: Breadthless length.

Q. Euclid's definition of a surface?

Ans: That which has length and breadth only.

Q. What are the ends of a line?

Ans: Points.

Q. State Axiom 1.

Ans: Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.

Q. Give an example of 'the whole is greater than the part'.

Ans: The whole length of a rope is more than any piece of it.

Q. Name the three undefined terms of modern geometry.

Ans: Point, line, plane.

Long Answer

Q. Write Euclid's five postulates.

Ans: Write the 5 postulates in Section 2: (1) line through two points, (2) extend a line, (3) draw a circle, (4) all right angles equal, (5) the interior-angles postulate.

Q. Explain the fifth postulate and Playfair's axiom.

Ans: By the fifth postulate, if on one side of a transversal the interior angles sum to less than 180°, the lines meet on that side. Playfair's axiom is equivalent: through a point P not on a line l there is exactly one line parallel to l.

Q. Prove that every line segment has one and only one mid-point.

Ans: See the hard example: AM = ½AB = AN (Axiom 7) ⇒ M = N. ∎

Q. If AC = BD, prove AB = CD (A, B, C, D in order).

Ans: AB + BC = BC + CD; subtracting BC (Axiom 3) gives AB = CD. ∎
