🐻 The Bond of Love

by Kenneth Anderson · Class 9 English (Beehive) · A true story of love between a man and a bear

1. About the Author

Kenneth Anderson (1910–1974) was a hunter-turned-naturalist and writer, born in India. He wrote many books about the jungles of South India. In this lesson he tells how an orphan bear cub grew up in his family. Message: love between humans and animals is real and deep.

4. Summary (Detailed)

Kenneth Anderson's family adopted a very small sloth bear cub called Bruno, whose mother had been killed. The cub was weak and helpless, so the family fed it milk from a bottle and cared for it like a baby. Bruno soon became part of the household and made friends with the family's dogs. He enjoyed playing and wrestling with them, and ate bananas, honey and other sweet foods.

As Bruno grew bigger and stronger, the family realised that a full-grown bear could hurt people unknowingly, even in play. So it was decided to send him to a zoo, where he would be safe and also keep others safe. It was a painful decision for everyone.

After some time, the author visited the zoo. Bruno, although he had not seen him for long, recognised his old master and showed deep affection. This proves that animals never forget love and kindness. The story ends by showing the strong bond of love between human and animal.

6. Themes

  • Love and kindness cross the boundary between human and animal.
  • Gratitude and loyalty; animals remember love.
  • Responsibility: sometimes a loving decision is a painful one.
  • Wild animals belong in safe, natural or protected places.

9. Long Questions & Answers

Q1. Describe Bruno's life with the author's family.

Bruno came as a helpless orphan cub. The family bottle-fed him and gave him a safe home. He became friends with the dogs, played, ate honey and fruits, and was treated like a family member. His naughty, loving nature made everyone love him.

Q2. Why did the family send Bruno to the zoo? Was it right?

As Bruno grew into a large bear, even his playful hugs could injure people. The family loved him, but safety came first, so they sent him to a zoo where experts could take care of him. It was a painful but responsible and right decision, because a wild animal cannot stay in a house forever.

Q3. How does the lesson show the "bond of love"?

The bond is seen in the family's care for Bruno, Bruno's friendship with dogs and the family, and finally in Bruno recognising Anderson after a long gap. It proves that love is understood by animals too and that they never forget kindness.

Q4. Draw a character sketch of Kenneth Anderson.

Anderson is kind, patient and animal-loving. He adopts an orphan cub, looks after it, and takes the painful but wise step of sending it to the zoo. He keeps Bruno in his heart and meets him again with love.
