The Duck and the Kangaroo – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Poem & Poet

Poem: The Duck and the Kangaroo (Class 9, UP Board)
Poet: Edward Lear (1812–1888), English poet and painter, famous for nonsense poetry and limericks (The Owl and the Pussycat).
Type: Nonsense / humorous narrative poem written as a conversation.
Theme: Friendship, adventure, wish for change and freedom.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

The bored Duck leaves his pond, sits at the end of the hopping Kangaroo’s tail, and they travel under the moonlight to faraway lands.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • The Duck’s boredom: The Duck says his life in the nasty pond is dull. He admires how the Kangaroo hops quickly over fields and water and wishes he could do the same.
  • The request: He politely asks the Kangaroo for a ride on his back. He promises to sit quietly and say nothing but ‘Quack’ all day. He tempts the Kangaroo with plans to visit lands such as the Dee and the Jelly Bo Lee.
  • The Kangaroo thinks: The Kangaroo considers the matter carefully before replying, as a wise, careful friend should.
  • The agreement: The Kangaroo agrees and says that, to keep balance, the Duck must sit steady at the very end of his tail.
  • The journey: They hop over land and sea, round the whole world, in joy.
  • Ending: The poet asks who could be happier than the Duck and the Kangaroo.

🎯 Central Idea (About 150 words)

“The Duck and the Kangaroo” by Edward Lear is a funny nonsense poem about two very different animals who become friends. The Duck is bored with his small pond and longs to see the wide world, so he politely asks the hopping Kangaroo to give him a ride on his back. The Kangaroo thinks carefully, then kindly agrees and asks the Duck to sit steady at the end of his tail for balance. Together they hop over land and sea and travel round the whole world, happier than anyone else. The poem has no deep moral; it is written to amuse readers with playful words, imaginary places and lively rhythm. Yet it also shows the wish for adventure and change, the joy of friendship, and how helping and trusting each other can make life exciting. Differences do not matter when two friends share a dream.

🧠 Characters & Literary Points

  • Duck: bored, adventurous, polite, dreamy and persuasive.
  • Kangaroo: cautious, thoughtful, kind and cooperative.
  • Nonsense verse: Invented names (Jelly Bo Lee) and funny situations, with no realistic logic.
  • Personification: Animals talk and behave like humans.
  • Rhyme & rhythm: Musical, bouncy lines that suit the hopping.
  • Repetition: ‘Said the Duck to the Kangaroo’ gives a ballad-like effect.
  • Onomatopoeia: ‘Quack’ imitates the duck’s sound.
  • Alliteration: Repeated sounds like ‘hop… hills’ add fun.
  • Imagery: Moonlit land and sea, hopping across the world.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Gracious – used to show surprise
  • Bore – dull, tiresome thing
  • Nasty – unpleasant
  • Sate / Sit – to sit
  • Balance – steadiness, not falling
  • Nonsense – words or ideas with no real sense, written for fun

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote The Duck and the Kangaroo?

Edward Lear, an English poet famous for nonsense verse.

Q2. Why was the Duck unhappy?

He was bored with life in his small, dull pond.

Q3. What did the Duck want to do?

He wanted to travel the world on the Kangaroo’s back.

Q4. What did the Duck promise?

To sit quietly and say only ‘Quack’.

Q5. Where did the Kangaroo ask the Duck to sit?

At the very end of his tail, to balance.

Q6. What kind of poem is this?

A humorous nonsense poem.

Q7. What is the poem’s main theme?

Friendship, adventure and the wish for change.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe the Duck’s character.

The Duck is bored, curious and adventurous. He is tired of his pond and dreams of seeing the world. He is also polite and persuasive, as he asks the Kangaroo humbly, promises good behaviour and describes the lands they could visit. He is playful and hopeful, and so he wins a friend.

Q2. How does the Kangaroo respond to the Duck’s request?

The Kangaroo does not answer at once. He thinks it over, which shows him to be sensible and cautious. Then he kindly agrees and tells the Duck to sit steady at the end of his tail to keep him balanced. This shows his helpful nature.

Q3. Why is it called a nonsense poem?

Edward Lear wrote it for fun. A duck riding a kangaroo, imaginary places such as the Jelly Bo Lee, and animals who talk are impossible in real life. Its charm lies in humour, rhythm and play with words, not in a serious moral.

Q4. What does the poem tell us about friendship?

It shows that friends can be very different, one webbed and watery, the other hopping and land-loving, yet help each other. Trust and cooperation turn their wish into a joyful journey. True friends share dreams and make each other’s lives happier.

Q5. Write the central idea of the poem in your own words.

The poem tells how a bored Duck and a kind Kangaroo become friends and travel round the world together. It entertains with nonsense and rhythm, while quietly praising adventure, friendship and cooperation.
