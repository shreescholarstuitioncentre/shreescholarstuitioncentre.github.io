Iswaran the Storyteller – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Author & Story

Story: Iswaran the Storyteller (Class 9 supplementary reader, UP Board)
Author: R.K. Narayan (1906–2001), famous Indian English writer (Malgudi Days, The Guide).
Type: Humorous and suspenseful short story with ‘stories within a story’.
Main characters: Mahendra (young engineer) and Iswaran (cook and storyteller).
Theme: Power of imagination, storytelling and belief; reason versus superstition.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • Introduction: Mahendra is a young engineer who is often posted in remote, lonely places. His greatest help is his cook, Iswaran, who goes everywhere with him without complaint.
  • Iswaran’s skills: Even in wild places with no shops, Iswaran magically produces tasty dishes. He is also a wonderful storyteller, influenced by Tamil thrillers and detective stories. He tells tales of adventure, horror and suspense.
  • Entertainment: Mahendra’s home has no TV, so Iswaran’s stories more than make up for it. Mahendra looks forward to them every evening.
  • The elephant story: Iswaran says his native place is famous for timber. A mad tusker once pulled down a tree and blocked the highway and entered his school area. Iswaran, then a junior-class student, watched from the rooftop. He claims he knocked the beast down with a stick, and that a veterinary doctor was called to revive it. He pauses at the exciting parts to keep Mahendra curious.
  • The special dinner: One day Iswaran prepares a special dinner. Mahendra enjoys it and praises his cooking.
  • The ghost story: After dinner, Iswaran says that the place where they live was once a burial ground, and that he still finds skulls and bones. He adds that a female ghost walks there on full-moon nights.
  • Mahendra’s reaction: Mahendra shivers, then scolds Iswaran sharply, calling him crazy, and says there are no ghosts. He leaves the room.
  • The aftermath: Though he laughs at ghosts, Mahendra is deeply affected. He avoids looking out of his window when there is a full moon. One night he wakes up and sees a dark cloudy form and breaks into a cold sweat.
  • Ending: The next day Iswaran greets him with his lunch packet and bag as if nothing happened. This shows the power of Iswaran’s storytelling over even a practical, rational man.

🧠 Theme & Central Idea

The story shows how a gifted storyteller can fascinate and even frighten people. Mahendra, a logical young engineer, mocks ghosts but is secretly affected by Iswaran’s ghost story. The tale highlights the power of imagination and words, the comfort that a loyal servant gives in loneliness, and the way fear and superstition can enter even a rational mind. It also adds humour through Iswaran’s exaggerated adventures.

👥 Character Sketches

  • Iswaran: Loyal, hardworking, resourceful and cheerful; an excellent cook and a gifted storyteller who exaggerates for effect; clever and a good judge of how to hold his listener.
  • Mahendra: Young, practical, logical and kind; loves stories and relies on Iswaran; scolds him for ghost tales but is secretly afraid.
  • Role of the narrator: The story is told by a third person, with humour and irony.

✍️ Literary Points

  • Story within a story: Iswaran’s tales are inside Narayan’s main story.
  • Suspense: Iswaran pauses at the climax and delays the ending.
  • Humour and exaggeration: The elephant story is funny and unbelievable.
  • Irony: The man who laughs at ghosts is afraid of the moon and the window.
  • Contrast: Reason (Mahendra) versus superstition (Iswaran’s ghost story).
  • Simple, lively language: Typical Narayan style.
  • Realism: Everyday characters in a believable setting.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Asset – useful person or thing
  • Tusker – elephant with long tusks
  • Thrall – spell, captivated
  • Culinary – related to cooking
  • Supernatural – beyond natural laws (ghosts)
  • Figment – something imagined

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. In what way is Iswaran an asset to Mahendra?

Iswaran is Mahendra’s loyal cook who accompanies him wherever he is posted, even to remote and desolate places. He prepares delicious meals from limited things and keeps the household running. He also entertains Mahendra with exciting stories, making up for the lack of TV. Thus he gives both comfort and company.

Q2. Describe the story of the mad elephant narrated by Iswaran.

Iswaran said that his native place was famous for timber. A mad tusker once uprooted a tree and blocked the highway, causing panic. It entered the area of his school, where Iswaran, in the junior class, watched from the rooftop. He claims he knocked the beast down with a stick, and a veterinary doctor revived it. He paused at suspenseful moments to excite Mahendra.

Q3. Why was Iswaran a fascinating storyteller?

Iswaran used vivid details, exaggeration and suspense. He paused at the climax and delayed the ending, so the listener became more curious. His stories were influenced by Tamil thrillers and he narrated them with confidence, as if he had really experienced them. These qualities held Mahendra spellbound.

Q4. How did Iswaran’s ghost story affect Mahendra?

Mahendra scolded Iswaran and said ghosts were imaginary, but deep down he got scared. He avoided looking out of the window on full-moon nights and, one night, saw a dark cloudy form and broke into a cold sweat. This shows that a well-told story can frighten even a rational person.

Q5. Write a character sketch of Iswaran.

Iswaran is a loyal, resourceful and cheerful cook who serves Mahendra in remote places. He cooks wonderfully, runs the house well and tells entertaining stories. He is clever, since he knows how to build suspense and keep the listener hooked. He is an imaginative exaggerator, and not at all offended when scolded.

Q6. Write a character sketch of Mahendra.

Mahendra is a young, practical engineer who enjoys Iswaran’s stories and values his help. He believes in logic and rejects ghosts, even scolding Iswaran. Yet, he is also imaginative and gets scared by the ghost story, showing his human side.

Q7. What does the story teach us about the power of storytelling?

The story teaches that stories can entertain, influence and even frighten people. A good storyteller can make us believe unbelievable things. It also shows how fear and superstition may affect even logical people.
