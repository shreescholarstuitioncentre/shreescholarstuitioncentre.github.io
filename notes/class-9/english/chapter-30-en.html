The Accidental Tourist

by Bill Bryson | Class 9 English (Supplementary) | UP Board

1. About the Author and Story

Bill Bryson is an American-British writer famous for humorous travel writing. This story is autobiographical, told in the first person with self-mockery. He is a frequent traveller, yet he is clumsy and "easily confused", so even simple things go wrong. That is why he calls himself an accidental tourist.

4. Summary (Step by Step)

  • Confession: Bryson says, "I am, in short, easily confused."
  • Lavatory: He goes looking for the lavatory in a cinema and ends up in an alley, on the wrong side of a self-locking door.
  • Hotel: He forgets his room number and visits the desk two or three times a day.
  • Air miles: He collects air miles but either cannot show his card or the clerk refuses to give points.
  • Spilt drinks: His arm seems to move by itself and knocks drinks on neighbours.
  • Zip: The zip of his carry-on bag bursts. He hurts his fingers, bleeds, and papers, magazines, passport, money, a film roll and a tobacco tin fly out.
  • Crash position: Leaning down to tie a shoelace, he is pinned helplessly.
  • Pen: He sucks his pen while writing, so his teeth and gums look navy blue.
  • Family: His wife tells the children, "Take the lids off the food for Daddy."

5. Theme and Message

  • Humour: Laughing at ourselves makes embarrassing moments easy.
  • Self-acceptance: We should accept our weaknesses.
  • Travel manners: Travel needs attention; carelessness brings trouble.

8. Short Answer Questions (40-50 words)

Q1. "I am, in short, easily confused." What examples justify this?

He looks for the lavatory in a cinema and ends up in an alley, locked out by a self-locking door. He also forgets his hotel room number and goes to the desk two or three times a day.

Q2. What happens when the zip of his carry-on bag gives way?

His fingers are hurt and bleed. Everything flies out: newspaper cuttings, papers, magazines, passport, English money, a film roll and a tobacco tin whose lid opens. He feels he has made a fool of himself.

Q3. How does he end up in the "crash position"?

On the plane he leans down to tie his shoelace. He gets stuck and is pinned helplessly in a bent posture, like the safety position for a crash. It is a funny, embarrassing situation.

Q4. Why does his wife say, "Take the lids off the food for Daddy"?

She knows his clumsy nature. He often struggles with simple things like opening lids and makes a mess, so she asks the children to help him and avoid trouble.

9. Long Answer Questions (100-120 words)

Q1. What is the significance of the title "The Accidental Tourist"?

The title is apt. Bryson is a frequent traveller, but he is also clumsy and absent-minded, so accidents follow him everywhere. He ends up in an alley when he looks for a lavatory, forgets his room number, spills drinks on others, bursts his bag's zip, and is pinned in the crash position. These are not planned events but happen accidentally. He wants to look elegant but only makes himself ridiculous. So the title perfectly describes him and the humour of the story.

Q2. Describe Bryson's travel blunders.

Bryson shows how simple travel becomes a mess for him. He forgets his hotel room number and visits the desk several times a day. In a cinema he enters the wrong door and is locked in an alley. On planes he spills drinks on neighbours and misses air miles. When his carry-on bag zip bursts, his belongings fly out and his fingers bleed. Bending to tie his lace, he is pinned in the crash position. He tells all this with humour and self-mockery.

Q3. What lesson does the story teach us?

The story teaches us to laugh at ourselves and accept our weaknesses. Bryson does not hide his clumsiness; he narrates it honestly and cheerfully. This makes the embarrassing events enjoyable. It also reminds us to be careful while travelling, because carelessness can create trouble. Real elegance lies in honesty and a sense of humour, not in pretending to be perfect.
