A House Is Not a Home

by Zan Gaudioso | Class 9 English (Supplementary) | UP Board

1. About the Author and Story

Zan Gaudioso wrote this autobiographical story from his own teenage experience. It is told in the first person (the narrator is "I"). It deals with loneliness at a new school, a house fire, and the kindness of people.

4. Story Summary (Step by Step)

  • New school: The narrator starts high school. His old friends are not there; he was a top student in junior high but feels lonely.
  • The smell: One Sunday afternoon, while doing homework, he notices a strange smell.
  • The fire: The fire from the fireplace has spread and engulfed the house.
  • Escape: He and his mother run out. He runs to the neighbours to call the fire department.
  • Mother's risk: She runs back inside to bring the pictures of his dead father, her only memory of him. He is terrified.
  • Loss: The house burns and the cat is missing. A fireman comforts him.
  • School next day: He is embarrassed because everyone knows about the fire, but classmates are kind and offer help.
  • Happy end: The cat is found and returned. The family rebuilds the house and he feels a sense of belonging.

5. Theme and Message

  • House vs Home: A house is walls; a home is love, family and friends.
  • Kindness: Strangers and schoolmates heal his loneliness.
  • Resilience: We can rebuild after loss.
  • Mother-son bond: The fire deepens their love.

8. Short Answer Questions (40-50 words)

Q1. How did the narrator feel about starting high school?

He felt lonely and isolated because his close friends were not with him. In junior high he had been at the top, but in the new school he felt like a stranger and missed his old teachers and friends.

Q2. Why was the narrator embarrassed the next day?

The whole school knew about the fire. He felt people were looking at him and talking about his misfortune. He was shy by nature, so unwanted attention made him uneasy and he wanted to hide.

Q3. How is the bond between the cat and the narrator shown?

The cat was always close to him, following him everywhere. He could not imagine life without her. When she went missing after the fire, his grief doubled, and her return made him very happy.

Q4. How did the schoolmates show concern?

His schoolmates came forward with real concern and kindness. They offered help and support after the fire. Their behaviour changed his idea of people and ended his loneliness.

9. Long Answer Questions (100-120 words)

Q1. Why is the story titled "A House Is Not a Home"?

The title shows the difference between a house and a home. A house is only a building of walls and a roof. A home is a place full of love, care and belonging. The narrator's house was destroyed by fire, but he did not lose his home. His mother's love, the cat's return, and the kindness of schoolmates and neighbours gave him warmth and security. He first felt lonely even in a house, and later felt at home among people. So the title is apt and meaningful.

Q2. Describe the fire and how the mother showed courage.

While doing homework on a Sunday, the narrator smelled smoke. The fire from the fireplace had spread across the house. He and his mother ran out. He rushed to the neighbours to call the fire department. Meanwhile his mother dashed back into the burning house and brought out the pictures of his dead father, the only thing she had to remember him by. The narrator was terrified. Her act shows her love and bravery, and it made their bond stronger.

Q3. How did the experience change the narrator?

At first the narrator was shy, lonely and missed his old friends. The fire made things worse. But the kindness of classmates, teachers and neighbours surprised him. He realised that people care, and that friends stand by us in trouble. His loneliness faded and he made new friends. He learnt that love and relationships make a place home. By the end, he is grateful and emotionally stronger.
