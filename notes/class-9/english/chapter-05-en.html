✎ The Snake and the Mirror ✎

Class 9 • English (Beehive) • by Vaikom Muhammad Basheer • UP Board / NCERT • Detailed Notes + Q&A

About the author: Vaikom Muhammad Basheer was a famous Malayalam writer, known for simple style and gentle humour.
Type of story: A humorous story told in the “story within a story” style – a homeopathic doctor tells his friends about a scary experience of his early days.
Theme: Vanity (self-admiration) and fear; how a serious danger shows a person’s real nature; humour and irony.
Characters: the young homeopathic doctor (narrator), the snake, and the friends who listen.

★ The Story in Steps (Animated)

1. The young doctor. The narrator was a newly qualified homeopathic doctor, just starting his practice. He was unmarried, poor and lived in a small, poorly furnished rented room with a mirror in it.
2. Admiring himself. One night he looked into the mirror, was pleased with his face and smiled. He took an “important” decision: he would shave daily and grow a thin moustache to look more handsome. He also dreamt of marrying a rich woman.
3. The snake arrives. Suddenly he felt something cold: a snake had slid onto his shoulder and coiled around his arm. He froze with terror, sweating and unable to move or shout. He now called himself a “poor, foolish and stupid doctor”.
4. The snake and the mirror. The snake turned its head and saw its reflection in the mirror and seemed fascinated. The doctor, even in fear, humorously imagined the snake deciding about growing a moustache, using eye-shadow or a vermilion spot!
5. The escape. While the snake was busy looking at itself, it slowly unwound from his arm and glided towards the mirror. The doctor seized the chance and ran out of the room.
6. The aftermath. He never went back to that room. When he told the tale, friends laughed and asked funny questions. The story ends on a humorous note, showing his vanity had met fear.

Character Sketch of the Doctor

Young, poor, vain, humorous and imaginative. He admires his face and plans a moustache, but when the snake coils around him he becomes helpless with fear. Still, he keeps a sense of humour and laughs at himself when telling the tale.

Key Words

  • Homeopath – doctor who treats with homeopathic medicines.
  • Vain – too proud of one’s looks.
  • Coiled – wound round in circles.
  • Reflection – image seen in a mirror.
  • Irony – the opposite of what one expects (the snake ignores the doctor, loving itself).
  • Vermilion – bright red powder put on the forehead.
  • Moral: Do not be too proud of looks; danger can humble anyone, and humour helps us face life.

✎ Short Answer Questions

Q1. Who is the author of “The Snake and the Mirror”?

Vaikom Muhammad Basheer, a Malayalam writer.

Q2. Who is the narrator of the story?

A young homeopathic doctor who had just begun his practice.

Q3. What “important” decision did the doctor take before the mirror?

To shave daily and grow a thin moustache to look more handsome.

Q4. How did the doctor feel while looking in the mirror?

Pleased and proud of his appearance; he smiled at himself.

Q5. What happened when the doctor sat in his room?

Q6. How did the doctor react to the snake?

A poor, foolish and stupid doctor.

Q8. What did the snake do on seeing the mirror?

It looked at its reflection, seemed fascinated and uncoiled from his arm.

Q9. What did the doctor humorously imagine about the snake?

He was young, unmarried, poor and lived in a small rented room.

Q12. Why is the story called humorous?

Because the snake’s “vanity” mirrors the doctor’s own, and his funny thoughts come even in danger.

✎ Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the doctor’s encounter with the snake.

The young homeopathic doctor was admiring himself in the mirror and planning to grow a thin moustache when a snake slid onto his shoulder and coiled around his arm. He froze with terror. The snake then noticed its reflection in the mirror and seemed fascinated by it. Forgetting the doctor, it slowly unwound and moved towards the mirror. The doctor seized the chance and ran out, never returning to the room.

Q2. How does the story show the doctor’s vanity and his fear?

At first he smiles at his reflection and makes grand decisions about moustache and marriage, showing vanity. When the snake coils around him he is helpless, sweating, and calls himself a poor, foolish, stupid doctor. His fear removes his pride, showing how danger reveals the truth about us.

Q3. Explain the irony in the story.

The doctor admires his own looks, but the snake, instead of attacking him, becomes absorbed in admiring its own reflection. The snake’s behaviour mirrors the doctor’s vanity. Also, his “earth-shaking” decisions about a moustache become silly beside real danger, and he is saved not by courage but by the snake’s self-love.

Q4. How is humour created in the story?

Basheer creates humour through the doctor’s exaggerated “important decisions”, his funny imagining of the snake using mascara or a vermilion spot, and his light-hearted self-mockery in narration, along with the friends’ comic questions at the end.

Q5. What message does the story give?

We should not be proud of our looks or possessions, as danger can strike suddenly and humble us. We should keep a sense of humour, learn from experience and laugh at our own weaknesses.
