🏔️ Kathmandu

by Vikram Seth · Class 9 English (Beehive) · A travel account

1. About the Author

Vikram Seth (born 1952) is an Indian poet and novelist, famous for A Suitable Boy. The lesson is taken from his travel writing and records his visit to the city of Kathmandu, the capital of Nepal. Theme: religions and cultures live together; sacred places should remain pure and peaceful.

4. Summary (Detailed)

The writer visits Kathmandu, a city where Hindu temples and Buddhist shrines stand side by side. He first goes to the famous Pashupatinath Temple, dedicated to Lord Shiva, on the bank of the Bagmati river. Only Hindus are allowed inside the temple, so he cannot enter. He notices the crowd of devotees, priests, small shops and tourists, and some visitors who treat the sacred place carelessly. He feels that such holy places should be kept neat and pure.

He then visits Boudhanath Stupa, one of the biggest Buddhist stupas in the world. Here he sees the Buddha's eyes, prayer flags and people (many of Tibetan origin) walking around the stupa in prayer. The atmosphere is calm and peaceful, though also commercial, with shops selling souvenirs.

Through these two visits Seth shows how Hinduism and Buddhism coexist in Kathmandu and how faith, tradition and tourism meet in one city. The writer also feels a strong personal connection, as the city resembles India in many ways.

9. Long Questions & Answers

Q1. Describe the Pashupatinath Temple as seen by the writer.

Pashupatinath is a sacred Hindu temple of Lord Shiva on the Bagmati river. Devotees, priests, shopkeepers and tourists fill the area. As a non-Hindu entry is not allowed, the writer cannot go inside. He feels that the sacred place should be kept clean and respected.

Q2. Describe the Boudhanath Stupa.

It is a giant white dome with a gold spire and the Buddha's painted eyes. Prayer flags flutter around. Pilgrims walk around it, and shops sell souvenirs. The place is peaceful and shows deep faith.

Q3. How does the lesson show unity in diversity?

The two big shrines of Hindus and Buddhists lie close in one city. People of different faiths pray and live together in peace, showing harmony among religions.

Q4. What is the message of "Kathmandu"?

We should respect all faiths, protect the purity of holy places and enjoy cultural diversity. Tourism should not spoil the sacred atmosphere.
