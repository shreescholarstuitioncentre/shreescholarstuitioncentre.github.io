✎ The Little Girl ✎

Class 9 • English (Beehive) • by Katherine Mansfield • UP Board / NCERT • Detailed Notes + Q&A

About the author: Katherine Mansfield (1888–1923) was a famous New Zealand short-story writer, known for showing the feelings of children and families. She died at the young age of 34.
Theme: A strict parent may look unkind from outside but may carry deep, hidden love. The story shows a child’s feelings changing from fear to love.
Main characters: Kezia (the little girl), Father, Mother, Grandmother.

★ The Story in Steps (Animated)

1. A frightened child. Little Kezia was afraid of her father. He was a big man with big hands, neck and mouth, spoke in a loud harsh voice and never talked to her lovingly. When she was near him she became nervous and stuttered, which annoyed him more.
2. Father’s routine. Mornings: a quick, casual kiss before leaving for the office. Evenings: tea, newspaper and slippers. Sundays: he lay on the sofa in the drawing room with a handkerchief over his face. Kezia kept away from him.
3. Grandmother’s advice. Kezia’s loving grandmother encouraged her to talk to her parents and suggested she make a pin-cushion as a birthday gift for Father.
4. The mistake. Kezia made the cushion, but she had no stuffing. She found sheets of paper and tore them up to stuff it. She did not know these papers were Father’s important speech for the Port Authority.
5. The punishment. Father was furious and hit her little palms with a ruler. Kezia could not understand why he was so cruel to her when she only wanted to please him.
6. The change. Later Kezia had a nightmare. Father came, picked her up, comforted her and took her to his bed. Feeling his warmth and care, she realised how loving he truly was.

Character Sketch – Kezia

Innocent, sensitive, shy, obedient and loving. She is afraid of Father yet wants to please him. She stutters when nervous. Her heart quickly turns warm when she gets love.

Character Sketch – Father

Big-built, stern, short-tempered, hard-working and unexpressive. He does not know how to show affection to a child, and punishes her harshly. But at the nightmare he proves to be caring and soft-hearted.

Key Words

  • Stutter – to speak with pauses and repeated sounds because of fear.
  • Pin-cushion – small cloth pad to keep pins.
  • Nightmare – a frightening dream.
  • Furious – extremely angry.
  • Moral: Parents’ strictness hides love; parents should also express affection and understand children’s innocence.

✎ Short Answer Questions

Q1. Why was Kezia afraid of her father?

He was big, strict and harsh-voiced, and never spoke to her affectionately, so she felt nervous near him.

Q2. Who were the members of Kezia’s family?

Kezia, her father, her mother and her grandmother.

Q3. What was Father’s routine on Sundays?

He stretched out on the drawing-room sofa with a handkerchief over his face.

Q4. What did Father do before leaving for the office?

He gave Kezia a casual, quick kiss and went away.

Q5. What did Kezia’s grandmother suggest?

She told Kezia to talk to her parents and make a pin-cushion for Father’s birthday.

Q6. What did Kezia use to stuff the pin-cushion?

Sheets of paper which she tore up, not knowing they were Father’s speech.

Q7. Why was Father angry?

Kezia had torn his important speech for the Port Authority.

Q8. How did Father punish Kezia?

He hit her small palms hard with a ruler.

Q9. What changed Kezia’s opinion of her father?

When she had a nightmare, Father comforted her and took her to his bed.

Q10. Who wrote “The Little Girl”?

Katherine Mansfield, a New Zealand writer.

Q11. Why did Kezia stutter before Father?

She was frightened of his serious look and loud voice.

Q12. What does the pin-cushion symbolise?

Kezia’s love and her wish to please her father.

✎ Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe Kezia’s feelings for her father at the beginning of the story.

Kezia feared her father deeply. He was large, loud and strict, and never spoke to her with love. She avoided him, felt relieved when he left for the office, and became tense when he came home. When forced to speak to him she stuttered. She believed he was cruel and did not love her.

Q2. How did Kezia’s attempt to please her father fail?

On grandmother’s advice she made a pin-cushion as a birthday gift. Having no stuffing, she tore sheets of paper and filled it. Those sheets were Father’s important speech for the Port Authority. Furious, he hit her palms with a ruler and Kezia could not understand why he was so cruel, though she had meant only love.

Q2b. How did Kezia’s feelings change by the end?

After the punishment she thought him heartless. But when she woke from a nightmare, Father came, lifted her, comforted her and took her into his bed. She felt warm and safe and understood that beneath his strictness he truly loved her. Fear turned into trust and love.

Q3. Write a character sketch of Kezia’s father.

He is a big, stern, hard-working and short-tempered man. He believes in discipline and does not show affection easily, so Kezia fears him. He punishes her harshly for tearing his speech. Yet at night, when she is scared, he becomes gentle and protective, showing the hidden love of a father.

Q4. What message does the story give?

Mansfield shows that parental love can remain hidden behind strictness. Parents should understand children’s innocence, avoid harsh punishment and express love openly; children should realise that a stern parent can still care deeply.
