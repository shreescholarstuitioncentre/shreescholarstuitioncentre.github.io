The Lost Child – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Author & Story

Story: The Lost Child (Class 9, UP Board)
Author: Mulk Raj Anand (1905–2004), an Indian English novelist and short-story writer (Untouchable, Coolie).
Type: Short story with a child’s point of view.
Setting: A village spring fair (festival of spring) with a temple, shops and a crowd.
Theme: A child’s love for his parents is more valuable than toys and sweets.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

The child lags behind his parents, is tempted by flowers, sweets, toys, balloons and the roundabout, and gets lost near the temple.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • The festival: It is the spring festival. A small boy walks with his parents to the fair through yellow mustard fields.
  • Curiosity: He is easily drawn to things: he runs after butterflies and plays with flowers. He lags behind and has to be called by his parents.
  • The tempting shops: At the sweet shop he asks for sweets, then for a toy at the toy shop, then a balloon and a ride on the roundabout. His parents refuse again and again, saying ‘later’ or scolding him.
  • Getting lost: While looking at the fair’s attractions, he forgets to follow his parents and is soon among the crowd. When he turns, they have gone.
  • Panic: The child runs around crying ‘Mother, Father!’ and asks people. He sees strangers, but nobody takes care of him. His desire for sweets and toys disappears, and fear and loneliness take over.
  • Kindness of the crowd: Some people try to calm and help him, but he only wants his parents.
  • The reunion: He finds his parents at last. He is held in their arms, weeping. They offer him toys and sweets, but he no longer wants them. He wants only their love and safety.

🧠 Theme & Central Idea

The story shows the innocence of childhood and how a child’s attraction to colourful things fades when he loses his parents. Toys, sweets and fun mean nothing compared with a parent’s love and security. It also reminds adults to be patient and kind with children’s curiosity, and warns that crowded places are dangerous for them.

👦 Character Sketch of the Child

  • Innocent and curious: He loves flowers, butterflies and bright things.
  • Demanding: He wants sweets, toys, balloons and rides.
  • Dependent: He cannot live without his parents’ presence.
  • Emotional: He cries and panics when lost.
  • Loving: At the end, he gives up all things to be in his parents’ arms.

✍️ Literary Points

  • Point of view: Told through the child’s eyes and feelings.
  • Simple language: Short, easy sentences matching a child’s mind.
  • Imagery: Colourful sights and sounds of the fair (yellow fields, sweets, toys).
  • Contrast: The happy beginning versus the fearful middle versus the safe ending.
  • Symbolism: The fair stands for the temptations of the world; parents stand for love and security.
  • Repetition: The child’s cries stress his fear.
  • Irony: The child who wanted everything finally wants nothing but his parents.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Fair – gathering with shops and amusements
  • Lag behind – walk slowly and fall behind
  • Roundabout – merry-go-round
  • Allure – attraction
  • Crowd – many people together
  • Reunion – meeting again after separation

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote The Lost Child?

Mulk Raj Anand.

Q2. Where was the child going with his parents?

To the spring fair.

Q3. What attracted the child on the way?

Yellow mustard flowers and butterflies.

Q4. What did he ask for at the fair?

Sweets, toys, a balloon and a ride on the roundabout.

Q5. Did the parents buy the things?

No, they refused or put him off.

Q6. How did the child get lost?

He lagged behind looking at attractions and lost sight of his parents in the crowd.

Q7. What did the child cry?

He cried out for his mother and father.

Q8. How did he feel when lost?

Frightened, lonely and helpless.

Q9. What did he want after being found?

Only his parents’ love and protection.

Q10. What is the moral of the story?

A child’s true need is parental love, not toys or sweets.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe how the child is attracted by the things at the fair.

As soon as the child enters the fair, he is drawn to colour and joy. He runs after butterflies and flowers, wants sweets at the sweet shop, toys at the toy stall, balloons from the balloon seller and a ride on the roundabout. Each thing is a new wonder for him. His parents refuse his demands, but his curiosity keeps pulling him away. This shows his innocence and also leads to him getting lost.

Q2. How did the child feel when he lost his parents?

The child is terrified. He turns round and cannot find them, and runs about crying ‘Mother, Father’. He asks strangers but nobody can help him at once. The sweets, toys and balloons now mean nothing. He feels fear, loneliness and helplessness, which shows how much he depends on his parents.

Q3. Why did the child not accept the toys and sweets after he found his parents?

Earlier he wanted these things badly, but the pain of being lost changed his priorities. He realised that love and security are more precious. Held in his parents’ arms, he needs nothing else. This makes the ending touching and meaningful.

Q4. What does the story tell us about children and parents?

Children are curious and easily distracted, so parents must keep a close eye on them and show patience. For children, a parent’s presence is the greatest source of safety and happiness. Parents’ love is more valuable than any material gift.

Q5. Write a character sketch of the parents.

The parents are loving but busy and strict. They walk quickly to the fair and refuse to buy everything the child wants, sometimes scolding him. They do not realise that he has stayed behind. When they find him, they hold him tightly and offer all the things he asked for, showing their deep love and relief.

Q6. Comment on the title ‘The Lost Child’.

The title is simple and suitable. It focuses on the boy and his experience of being lost, which is the central event. It also suggests emotional loss, since he loses his sense of safety. The story finally shows him ‘found’ in his parents’ love.
