Wind – Class 9 Notes (English)

Poet: Subramania Bharati (1882–1921), Tamil poet, freedom fighter  |  Poem: Wind (translated from Tamil)  |  Form: Free verse, no fixed rhyme  |  Subject: Wind as a force that tests us  |  Book: Beehive (Class 9)

1. About the Poet

Subramania Bharati, called Mahakavi Bharati, was a great Tamil poet, journalist and freedom fighter. His poems awakened patriotism, courage and social reform, and also celebrated nature, women's freedom and fearlessness. He died young, at 38.

2. Part-wise Summary

Part 1 – A plea to the wind

The poet speaks to the wind as if to a naughty child. He asks it to come softly. It should not break the shutters of the windows, scatter papers or throw the books from the shelf. But the wind does exactly that. It also tears pages and brings the rain.

Part 2 – The wind's destruction

The poet calls the wind a pest who loves to break things. It crumbles weak doors, rafters and thatched roofs, blows out weak fires and damages everything frail. It is cruel to the weak.

Part 3 – Weak vs strong

The wind only plays with the weak. It cannot harm the strong; it even befriends them. A strong fire becomes stronger when wind blows on it. So the poet realises it is useless to plead with the wind.

Part 4 – The lesson

The poet says: do not beg the wind to stop. Instead, make strong homes, strong doors and strong hearts. Learn to build well, praise life, and face the wind bravely. Then the wind will be our friend and we will live in harmony with nature.

3. Animated Diagram – The Wind at Work

Explanation: Blue moving lines are the wind. On the left, the weak house suffers: shutters shake and papers fly away. On the right, the strong house and the sturdy tree stand firm, only swaying a little. This is the poem's idea: the wind harms the weak but cannot defeat the strong.

4. Central Idea (≈150 words)

The central idea of "Wind" is that difficulties and hardships test us and make us strong. The poet speaks to the wind as if it were a naughty and powerful person who breaks shutters, scatters papers, tears the pages of books and brings the rain. The wind destroys weak houses, doors and fires, but it spares and even helps the strong ones. Subramania Bharati says that the wind should not be blamed or feared; instead, we must make our homes, our bodies and our minds strong. When we are firm and brave, the wind becomes our friend, and its blows fall powerless. The poem teaches us that life's storms are not meant to defeat us but to teach us strength, courage and determination. Only the weak are afraid of troubles; the strong face them and rise higher. Thus, the poem inspires us to build inner strength and meet challenges with confidence.

5. Theme, Tone, Devices

  • Themes: strength vs weakness, facing hardships, harmony with nature, courage.
  • Tone: begins complaining and playful, ends firm, wise and inspiring.
  • Symbol: wind = troubles/challenges of life; weak house = weak will; strong house = strong character.
  • Personification & Apostrophe: the wind is addressed like a living person.
  • Imagery: broken shutters, flying papers, torn books, rain.
  • Repetition: commands "don't…" to the wind; Alliteration in lines about breaking and crumbling.
  • Style: simple, conversational free verse.

7. Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Who wrote "Wind"?

Subramania Bharati, a Tamil poet.

Q2. What does the poet ask the wind not to do?

Not to break the shutters, scatter papers or throw down books.

Q3. Why does the poet call the wind a pest?

Because it loves breaking and damaging things.

Q4. What does the wind do to weak things?

It breaks, crumbles or blows them out, like weak doors and fires.

Q5. How does the wind treat the strong?

It becomes their friend and even strengthens them.

Q6. What should we do to face the wind?

Make our homes, hearts and minds strong.

Q7. What literary device is used in addressing the wind?

Personification (and apostrophe).

Q8. Is there a rhyme scheme?

No, it is free verse.

8. Long Questions & Answers

L1. What is the message of the poem "Wind"?

The poem teaches that hardships are like the wind: they harm the weak but cannot defeat the strong. Instead of blaming troubles, we should build inner strength, courage and a firm will. When we do that, difficulties become our friends and push us towards success.

L2. How does the poet personify the wind?

He addresses the wind as a naughty person and scolds it. He asks it to come softly, calls it a pest and blames it for breaking things. These human behaviours make the wind alive and help us see it as a challenge that can be faced.

L3. Explain: "The wind befriends the strong."

A strong fire, strong house or strong person is not afraid of the wind. The wind makes a good fire burn brighter and cannot move a sturdy house. So the strong gain from it. This shows that struggle only improves those who are ready.

L4. Why is it useless to plead with the wind?

The wind is a natural force and does not listen. Begging cannot stop it. The real solution is to improve ourselves and our surroundings so the wind cannot harm us.
