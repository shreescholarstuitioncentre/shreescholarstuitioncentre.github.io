No Men Are Foreign – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Poem & Poet

Poem: No Men Are Foreign (Class 9, UP Board)
Poet: James Kirkup (1918–2009), an English poet and travel writer.
Type: Lyric poem with a message of peace.
Theme: Universal brotherhood; war is foolish and harms everyone.

🎬 Animated Concept Diagram (Labeled)

People of different countries stand hand in hand under the same sun, with the same heart beating, on the same earth. On the right is what war leaves: ruins and a grave.

📖 Poem Explained (Part by Part)

  • Part 1 – We are alike: The poet asks us to remember that no man is foreign. Under their different uniforms, all men have the same body, the same breath, and the same bodily needs. They use the same sun, air and earth, and they sleep, eat, love and feel pain like us.
  • Part 2 – Harming others harms us: If we hurt or kill others, we are really hurting ourselves. Their hands, eyes and hearts are like ours. When we attack their land, we spoil the one earth we all share.
  • Part 3 – The common grave: War makes graves, and in the end the earth takes everyone, friend or enemy. So the poet pleads: remember this truth and live as one human family.
  • Message: Hatred divides, understanding unites. Choose peace over war.

🎯 Central Idea (About 150 words)

“No Men Are Foreign” by James Kirkup is a short poem about universal brotherhood and the foolishness of war. The poet asks us to remember that no human being is truly foreign. Whatever their country, colour, language or uniform, all people share the same body, the same heart and blood, the same needs and feelings. They live under the same sun and sky, breathe the same air, and feel hunger, love, joy and pain just as we do. Kirkup warns that when we attack others in war, we actually harm ourselves, because we destroy the common earth and end up digging a grave for all of humanity. Enemies are not strange beings; they are fellow humans. The poem urges us to give up hatred, prejudice and violence, to respect every person, and to live in peace as one human family on one shared earth.

🧠 Moral & Literary Points

  • Moral: All humans are equal; war is a loss for everyone.
  • Tone: Gentle, pleading and serious.
  • Repetition: The words ‘remember’ and ‘same’ are repeated to stress the shared humanity.
  • Imagery: Sun, rain, earth, grave, bodies (simple natural pictures).
  • Uniforms: A symbol of man-made divisions between nations.
  • Language: Simple, direct, like a speaker advising us.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Foreign – belonging to another country; strange
  • Uniform – official dress of soldiers
  • Brotherhood – feeling of unity among all
  • Grave – burial place
  • Prejudice – unfair dislike
  • Hostility – enmity, hatred

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote No Men Are Foreign?

James Kirkup, an English poet.

Q2. What does the poet ask us to remember?

That no man is foreign; all people are alike and belong to one human family.

Q3. What do all men share?

The same sun, air, earth, body, feelings and needs.

Q4. What do uniforms stand for in the poem?

They stand for man-made divisions between nations that make people enemies.

Q5. What happens when we harm others?

We harm ourselves and destroy the common earth.

Q6. What is the main theme?

Universal brotherhood and the foolishness of war.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. What is the message of the poem No Men Are Foreign?

The poem gives a message of universal brotherhood and peace. People of all nations are essentially alike, with the same body, feelings and needs. Differences of uniform, language or country are man-made. War only destroys life and earth, and in the end all of us share one grave. So we should give up hatred and treat everyone as our own.

Q2. How does the poet show that all men are alike?

Kirkup points out that everyone breathes the same air, lives under the same sun and walks the same earth. All feel hunger, love, joy and pain, and all sleep and wake. Their bodies and hearts are like ours. By this the poet proves that no one is truly a stranger.

Q3. Why does the poet say that harming others means harming ourselves?

All humans are one family on one planet. If we attack others, we destroy the common earth and its life, and the loss comes back to us. War leaves ruins and graves for all sides, so the victor also loses.

Q4. How is the poem relevant today?

In a world of conflict, racism and terror, the poem reminds us that humanity is one. It calls for tolerance, understanding and peace among nations, which is still needed today.
