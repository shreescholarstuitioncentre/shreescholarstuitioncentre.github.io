The Snake Trying – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Poem & Poet

Poem: The Snake Trying (Class 9, UP Board)
Poet: W.W.E. Ross (William Wrightson Eustace Ross, 1894–1966), a Canadian poet and geophysicist.
Type: Short modern poem in free verse (16 lines).
Theme: Kindness to animals; the snake is an innocent victim.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

The man chases the snake with a stick. The harmless green snake glides from the sand over the ripples into the reeds.

📖 Poem Explained (Step by Step)

  • The snake in danger: A small green snake is trying to escape from a pursuing stick, which means a person who wants to hit it.
  • Harmless creature: The poet says the snake is harmless, even to small children. It lay on the sand, minding its own business, until it was seen and chased.
  • The escape: It slips into the water, gliding through the ripples with a graceful, wavy motion.
  • Hiding: It disappears among the green reeds where it will be safe.
  • The poet’s wish: He sympathises with the snake and hopes it escapes unhurt. He finds it beautiful rather than frightening.

🎯 Central Idea (About 150 words)

“The Snake Trying” by W.W.E. Ross describes a small green snake that is trying to escape from a person chasing it with a stick. The poet shows the snake as a harmless creature, so gentle that it would not even hurt little children. It had been resting quietly on the sand until someone noticed it and chased it away. With graceful, gliding movements, it slips into the water and disappears among the green reeds. The poet looks at the snake with sympathy and admiration, finding its movements beautiful rather than frightening. He wishes the snake to reach its safe hiding place and live unharmed. The poem reminds us that creatures we fear are often innocent victims of our own fear and cruelty. Its central message is kindness, compassion and respect for all living beings, and it asks us to stop needless violence against animals.

🧠 Literary Points & Themes

  • Title: ‘Trying’ shows the snake’s effort to survive.
  • Free verse: No regular rhyme or metre; the lines are short and flow like the snake.
  • Imagery: Visual pictures of the green snake, the sand, ripples and reeds.
  • Sympathy: The poet takes the side of the snake, not the man.
  • Contrast: The poet’s gentleness versus the man’s violence; feared snake versus innocent victim.
  • Tone: Gentle, sympathetic and persuasive.
  • Theme: Compassion, non-violence, harmony with nature and breaking false fears.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Pursuing – chasing
  • Harmless – not dangerous
  • Ripples – small waves
  • Reeds – tall water plants
  • Glide – to move smoothly
  • Sympathy – feeling of pity and kindness

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote The Snake Trying?

W.W.E. Ross, a Canadian poet.

Q2. What is the snake trying to escape from?

From a person chasing it with a stick.

Q3. Is the snake dangerous?

No. It is harmless, even to small children.

Q4. What colour is the snake?

Small and green.

Q5. Where was the snake lying?

On the sand.

Q6. Where does the snake hide?

Among the green reeds in the water.

Q7. What does the poet wish for the snake?

That it reaches safety and is not hurt.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe the snake as shown in the poem.

The snake is small, green, harmless and graceful. It lay quietly on the sand until someone noticed it. It is not a danger even to children. Its gliding, wave-like movement is called beautiful by the poet. It is a gentle, innocent creature that is only trying to save its life.

Q2. What is the poet’s attitude towards the snake?

The poet is kind and sympathetic. He sees the snake as a victim and admires its beauty. He wishes it a safe escape and does not share the man’s fear and anger. His attitude is one of compassion and respect for all creatures.

Q3. What message does the poem give?

The poem asks us to be kind to all living beings. Many animals, like this snake, are harmless but are killed because of fear and ignorance. Needless violence must be avoided, and we should live peacefully with nature.

Q4. How does the poem end?

The snake escapes by slipping into the water and vanishing among the reeds. The ending is hopeful because the snake reaches safety and the poet’s wish is fulfilled.

Q5. Why is the poem’s title ‘The Snake Trying’?

The title shows the snake’s struggle. It is not attacking anyone but is only trying to escape and save its life. The title draws our sympathy towards the snake.
