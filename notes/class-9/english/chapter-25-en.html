In the Kingdom of Fools – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Story

Story: In the Kingdom of Fools (Class 9 supplementary reader, UP Board)
Type: Folk tale from Karnataka (a Kannada folk tale), satire and fable.
Characters: The Guru, the Disciple, the King, the Minister, the Merchant, the villagers.
Theme: Wisdom versus folly; greed brings danger; clever thinking defeats foolish power.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

1 = The topsy-turvy kingdom (sleep by day), 2 = Everything costs one duddu, 3 = The stake and the fat disciple, 4 = The Guru’s trick and the new king.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • The arrival: A guru and his disciple reach a kingdom ruled by a foolish king and minister. They find two strange things. It is bright day, yet the town is silent as everyone is asleep, because the people sleep by day and work at night. Also, everything in the market, from vegetables to sweets, costs the same: one duddu.
  • The guru’s advice: The guru understands it is a ‘kingdom of fools’ where anything can happen, and says they should leave at once.
  • The disciple’s greed: The disciple loves the cheap food and refuses to leave. The guru goes away and the disciple stays, eating well and becoming fat.
  • The unjust trial: A man complains to the king about a loss caused by a merchant’s wall. The foolish king blames the merchant, who passes the blame to others. Finally, the king orders someone to be put on the stake. But the thin merchant does not fit the stake, so the guards look for someone who fits.
  • The disciple is caught: The soldiers find the fat disciple, who fits the stake perfectly. He is arrested and sentenced to die. He now remembers his guru’s warning.
  • The guru returns: The disciple prays for help, and the guru comes back. He whispers a plan to his disciple.
  • The trick: At the stake, both the guru and the disciple insist on being hanged first. The king is puzzled. The guru says that whoever dies at this time will be reborn as the king, and the other as the minister.
  • The king’s greed: The greedy king and minister do not want anyone else to be king, so they take the stake themselves and are executed.
  • The new rulers: The people, now without a king, request the guru and the disciple to rule. They become king and minister and restore order, including normal day and night.

🧠 Theme & Central Idea

The story is a satire showing how foolish rulers create chaos and unjust laws. It warns that greed (the disciple’s love for cheap food) can bring danger, and that wise advice should be heeded. It also shows that intelligence and clever thinking can defeat stupidity and tyranny. In the end, the foolish king is punished by his own greed, and wisdom wins.

👥 Character Sketches

  • Guru: Wise, farsighted, caring and clever; he warns the disciple and later rescues him by a smart plan.
  • Disciple: Greedy, foolish, careless and unable to resist cheap food; he realises his mistake only when in danger.
  • King: Foolish, greedy and unjust; he rules by whims and orders absurd things like changing day and night.
  • Minister: Equally foolish, follows the king without thinking.
  • Merchant/villagers: Victims of the king’s unjust system.

✍️ Literary Points

  • Satire: Makes fun of foolish rulers and unjust justice.
  • Irony: The king dies on the stake meant for others; the greedy disciple is saved by the one he ignored.
  • Humour: The topsy-turvy kingdom and the argument to die first.
  • Folk tale: Simple language and a clear moral.
  • Contrast: Wisdom (guru) versus folly (king, disciple).

📚 Word Meanings

  • Duddu – a small coin
  • Stake – pointed wooden pole used for execution
  • Disciple – student/follower
  • Guru – teacher
  • Absurd – unreasonable
  • Tyranny – cruel rule

People sleep by day and work at night, and everything costs one duddu.

Q2. What did the guru advise his disciple?

To leave the kingdom at once because it was ruled by fools.

Q3. Why did the disciple stay?

Because of the cheap food.

Q4. What happened to the disciple?

He became fat and fit the stake, so he was arrested.

Q5. Why did the king want someone on the stake?

To punish the guilty, but the merchant did not fit, so any fitting person was taken.

Q6. How did the guru save the disciple?

He tricked the king that whoever died would be reborn as king.

Q7. Who were executed in the end?

The king and the minister.

The guru and the disciple.

Q9. When does the disciple remember the guru’s words?

When he is arrested to be put on the stake.

Q10. What is the moral of the story?

Greed is dangerous, and wisdom defeats folly.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe the Kingdom of Fools.

The Kingdom of Fools is a strange land ruled by a foolish king and minister. People sleep during the day and work at night. Everything is available at the same price, one duddu, whether it is a cheap vegetable or costly sweets. Justice is absurd, since the king punishes anyone he likes without reason. It is a place where no sensible person is safe.

Q2. Why did the disciple decide to stay in the kingdom?

The disciple was tempted by the cheap food. Everything cost one duddu, so he could eat anything he liked. He ignored his guru’s warning and enjoyed rich food, becoming fat. His greed brought him into danger.

Q3. What are the guru’s words of wisdom? When does the disciple remember them?

The guru said it was a kingdom of fools, so one could not predict what might happen, and advised leaving. The disciple remembered them when the king’s men arrested him to put him on the stake, because he fitted it.

Q4. How does the guru manage to save his disciple’s life?

The guru returned and secretly advised his disciple. At the stake, both argued about who would die first. The king asked the reason. The guru said that whoever died at this special moment would be reborn as king and the other as minister. The greedy king and minister took their places on the stake and were executed, so the guru and disciple were freed.

Q5. Why did the king and minister take the place of the guru and disciple?

They were greedy for rebirth as kings. They believed the guru’s story, and did not want anyone else to become king. So they took the stake themselves, which caused their end.

Q6. Write a character sketch of the Guru.

The guru is wise and farsighted. He notices at once that the kingdom is dangerous and warns his disciple. When the disciple is in danger, he returns and saves him with a clever plan. He shows presence of mind and loyalty, and in the end he becomes a just ruler.

Q7. What lesson does the story teach us?

The story teaches that greed can lead us into danger, that we should listen to wise advice, and that intelligence can win over folly. It also tells us that bad rulers eventually destroy themselves.
