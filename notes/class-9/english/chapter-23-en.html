The Adventures of Toto – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Author & Story

Story: The Adventures of Toto (Class 9 supplementary reader, UP Board)
Author: Ruskin Bond (born 1934, Kasauli), a famous Indian author of children’s books and stories.
Taken from: Grandfather’s Private Zoo.
Type: Humorous story, told by the narrator (grandson).
Theme: Wild animals are not meant to be pets; humour in animal mischief.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

1 = Buying Toto, 2 = Kit-bag and railway station, 3 = Kettle incident, 4 = Pullao dish and window escape.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • Buying Toto: Grandfather bought a little monkey from a tonga-driver for five rupees and named him Toto. Toto was ‘pretty’, with silky golden-brown fur, bright eyes and an expressive face.
  • Hidden from Grandmother: Grandfather’s private zoo already had many animals. Fearing Grandmother’s anger, he kept Toto secretly in a closet. Toto, however, soon escaped and tore the narrator’s blazer and clothes.
  • Moved to a cage: After the trouble, Toto was shifted to a cage in the servants’ quarters, where he behaved mischievously and could not be left free.
  • Railway journey: Toto travelled in a black canvas kit-bag. At Saharanpur station, he poked his head out and grinned at the ticket collector, who insisted a monkey must be charged as a dog. Grandfather paid for him.
  • Nana the pet: Toto never made friends with Nana, another of Grandfather’s animals; he was quite rough with her.
  • The bath: Toto liked warm baths in winter and bathed in his own way, like a human.
  • Kettle incident: Toto sat in a kettle of water on the fire. He lifted the lid to feel the air, but it was cold outside, so he sat down again and nearly boiled himself.
  • Lunch trouble: One day Toto ate rice in the dining room. When Grandmother screamed, he threw a plate at her.
  • The final prank: At a meal, Toto grabbed the dish of pullao and escaped through a window.
  • Ending: Toto was too troublesome to keep. Grandfather found the tonga-driver and sold Toto back to him for three rupees.

🧠 Theme & Central Idea

The story shows Toto’s endless mischief and the humour it creates. Though intelligent and lovable, a wild animal like a monkey cannot adjust to life in a house and brings trouble to everyone. Grandfather’s love of animals is shown, but he finally accepts that Toto belongs elsewhere. The story teaches that wild animals should not be kept as pets, and also that humour can be found even in chaos.

🐒 Character Sketches

  • Toto: Pretty, intelligent, restless, mischievous, destructive, imitative (copies humans), fearless and funny; his tail works like a third hand.
  • Grandfather: Animal-lover, kind, tolerant, humorous and a little careless; he enjoys Toto’s tricks but finally acts wisely.
  • Grandmother: Strict, practical and orderly; she dislikes the mess and the pets.
  • Narrator: A young boy who loves animals and describes everything with humour.
  • Tonga-driver: Sells Toto at first, and takes him back at a lower price.

✍️ Literary Points

  • Humour: Toto’s tricks (kettle, rice, pullao) create comedy.
  • First-person narration: The narrator tells the story with warmth.
  • Vivid description: Toto’s looks, tail and expressions are shown clearly.
  • Personification: Toto behaves like a human boy.
  • Simple language: Easy and lively style typical of Ruskin Bond.
  • Understatement: Serious trouble is described lightly.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Tonga – horse-drawn carriage
  • Mischievous – playfully naughty
  • Canvas – strong cloth
  • Turnstile – revolving gate
  • Pullao – spiced rice dish
  • Haunches – hindquarters

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe how Toto came to Grandfather’s private zoo.

Grandfather bought Toto from a tonga-driver for five rupees. Toto was a pretty little monkey with silky golden-brown fur, bright eyes and a tail that acted like a third hand. Grandfather already had many animals, so Toto joined his private zoo. He kept him secret from Grandmother at first.

Q2. Narrate the incident at the railway station.

Toto travelled in a black canvas kit-bag. At Saharanpur, while Grandfather showed his ticket at the turnstile, Toto popped out his head and grinned at the ticket collector. The collector said that a monkey must be charged like a dog, as railway rules had no rate for monkeys. Grandfather had to buy a dog ticket for him.

Q3. How did Toto nearly boil himself alive?

Toto climbed into a kettle of water kept on the fire. As the water grew warm, he found it pleasant. When it grew hotter, he lifted the lid and put out his head, but felt the cold outside, so he sat down again. Soon the water was nearly boiling, and he was saved just in time.

Q4. Why was Toto not a suitable pet?

Toto was clever and funny but wild and destructive. He tore clothes, threw plates, stole food, fought with other pets and disturbed the house. Grandmother was upset by the chaos. Hence Grandfather decided to sell him back to the tonga-driver.

Q5. What does the story teach about keeping wild animals as pets?

Wild animals do not suit the discipline of a home. They have natural instincts and cause damage and danger. Though Toto is lovable, he lives best in freedom or with experts. We should respect animals and not keep them as toys.

Q6. Write a character sketch of Toto.

Toto is a lovable and clever monkey. He is restless, curious and always imitating human actions, like taking a bath. He is also destructive, as he tears clothes and steals food. His humour and energy make the story lively, but his wild nature shows he cannot live as a pet.

Q7. Comment on the title ‘The Adventures of Toto’.

The title suits the story, which is a series of exciting and funny incidents caused by Toto. The word ‘adventures’ shows his bold, unexpected deeds, such as the kettle, the railway station and the pullao escape.
