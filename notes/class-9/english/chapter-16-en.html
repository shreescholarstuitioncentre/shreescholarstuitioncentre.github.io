A Legend of the Northland – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Poem & Poet

Poem: A Legend of the Northland (Class 9, Beehive/UP Board)
Poet: Phoebe Cary (1824–1871), an American poet known for simple, moral verses.
Type: Narrative poem / ballad based on a folk legend.
Theme: Greed and selfishness are punished; kindness is rewarded.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

1 = The saint asks for food, 2 = Curse, 3 = Woodpecker today.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • Setting: The Northland is far north, where winter days are short, nights long and very cold. An old woman lives in a cottage and is famous for her cakes.
  • The visitor: A weary, hungry saint knocks at her door and humbly asks for a bit of food.
  • First cake: She bakes a cake, but thinks it is too big to give a stranger, and keeps it.
  • Second and third cakes: She bakes a smaller one, then an even smaller one. Each time greed says it is still too big. Her heart never grows kind.
  • Punishment: The saint is displeased. She is turned into a woodpecker with a red cap and black dress and flies up the chimney.
  • Result: Even today she pecks the bark of trees for food, remembering her selfishness.

🎯 Central Idea (150 words)

“A Legend of the Northland” by Phoebe Cary is a folk legend told in verse. In the cold Northland, an old woman lives in a cottage and is baking cakes when a tired, hungry holy traveller asks her for food. She promises him a cake, but each time she bakes one, her greed makes her think it is too big to give away. She bakes smaller and smaller cakes, yet refuses every one of them. Angered by her selfishness, the saint punishes her by turning her into a woodpecker. She flies away through the chimney and is condemned to peck at tree bark for her food forever. The poem teaches that greed, selfishness and lack of kindness bring punishment, while generosity and hospitality are the true virtues. It also explains, in a playful way, why the woodpecker has a red cap and a black body, and why it always pecks at trees.

🧠 Moral & Literary Points

  • Moral: Be generous. Greed makes a person lose everything.
  • Character: The old woman is greedy, selfish, miserly and unkind; the saint is patient, holy and just.
  • Imagery: Cold Northland, hot oven, cakes growing smaller (visual and funny).
  • Repetition: Three cakes, each smaller, stress her growing greed.
  • Folk legend: Explains a natural fact (the woodpecker’s looks and habit) through a story.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Legend – a traditional story passed down
  • Northland – cold northern region
  • Saint – holy, virtuous person
  • Greed – selfish desire for more
  • Hospitality – kind welcome to guests
  • Woodpecker – bird that pecks tree bark

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote A Legend of the Northland?

Phoebe Cary, an American poet.

Q2. Where did the old woman live?

In a cottage in the Northland, a cold region where days are short and nights long.

Q3. What did the saint ask for?

He was tired and hungry, and asked for some food (a cake).

Q4. Why did she refuse to give the cakes?

Because of greed, she thought each cake was too big to give away.

Q5. What did she become?

A woodpecker with a red cap and black body.

Q6. How does the woodpecker live now?

It pecks at tree bark to find food.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe the character of the old woman.

The old woman is greedy, selfish and unkind. Though a stranger comes tired and hungry, she is unwilling to share. She bakes three cakes, each smaller than the last, but still thinks each is too big to give. This shows her miserly nature. She has no sense of hospitality or charity, and so she is punished.

Q2. What is the message of the poem?

The poem teaches that selfishness and greed are bad and bring punishment, while kindness and generosity are great virtues. Sharing what we have with the needy is a duty. The old woman could have been blessed for one small act of kindness, but she lost her human form because she refused.

Q3. Was the punishment justified? Give reasons.

Yes. The saint tested her kindness three times, giving her many chances. She failed each time. Her punishment fits her fault: she who would not share food must now search for food in tree bark all her life. It is a lesson for all readers.

Q4. How does the poem explain the woodpecker’s appearance and habits?

As a folk legend, it says the woman became a woodpecker. The red cap and black dress match her appearance, and her pecking of bark is the search for food she must now do forever.
