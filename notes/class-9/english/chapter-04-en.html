✎ A Truly Beautiful Mind ✎

Class 9 • English (Beehive) • Life of Albert Einstein • UP Board / NCERT • Detailed Notes + Q&A

Chapter at a glance: A biographical account of Albert Einstein, one of the greatest scientists ever. It shows his odd childhood, his struggles at school, his great discoveries, his personal life and his love for peace and humanity.
Why “beautiful” mind? Because his mind was not only brilliant but also kind, humble, peace-loving and concerned for all people.

★ Einstein’s Life in Steps (Animated)

1. Birth & strange childhood. Born on 14 March 1879 in Ulm, Germany. His head was unusually large and his mother thought he was a freak. He did not speak until about 2½ years. He played alone, so his playmates found him boring.
2. Violin. At six he began violin because his mother wanted it. He later became a gifted amateur violinist and played all his life.
3. School trouble. In Munich he disliked strict discipline and rote learning. A headmaster told his father Einstein “would never make a success at anything”. He was not a bad pupil – he was good in mathematics. He left Munich school for good; his family moved to Milan (Italy); he chose to study in Switzerland.
4. Mileva & Bern. At the Zurich college he met Mileva Maric, who understood his ideas – an ally. His mother disapproved. In 1902 he got a job at the patent office in Bern. He called his desk drawer “the department of theoretical physics”.
5. Miracle year 1905. He published the special theory of relativity and the equation E = mc² (also photoelectric effect work). He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921. Marriage with Mileva ended in divorce (1919); he married his cousin Elsa.
6. War & peace. As Nazi persecution rose he went to the USA. He wrote a letter to President Franklin Roosevelt warning that Germany might build an atomic bomb. Later the destruction at Hiroshima and Nagasaki deeply shook him; he campaigned for peace. He died in 1955, remembered as a “world citizen”.

Character Sketch of Einstein

Curious, creative, independent, hard-working, humble and humane. He hated blind rote learning, loved mathematics and music, and used his fame to speak for peace and human welfare.

Key Words

  • Freak – a strange, abnormal person.
  • Ally – a supporter who shares one’s views.
  • Patent office – office that registers new inventions.
  • Rote learning – learning by mere memorising.
  • Pacifist – one who opposes war.
  • Moral: Real greatness lies in using intelligence for the good of humanity; marks do not measure genius.

Q1. When and where was Einstein born?

On 14 March 1879 in Ulm, Germany.

Q2. Why did his mother think he was a “freak”?

His head was unusually large and angular when he was born.

Q3. At what age did Einstein start speaking?

He did not speak until about two and a half years of age.

Q4. What opinion did his playmates have of him?

They thought he was boring because he preferred to play alone.

Q5. Why did he learn the violin?

His mother wanted him to; at six he began and later became a gifted amateur violinist.

Q6. Why did Einstein leave school in Munich?

He disliked its strict discipline and rote learning.

Q7. What did the headmaster say about Einstein?

That he would never make a success at anything.

Q8. Who was Mileva Maric?

A fellow student at Zurich who understood Einstein’s ideas and became his ally and first wife.

“The department of theoretical physics.”

For his services to theoretical physics, especially the photoelectric effect, in 1921.

To warn that Germany might build an atomic bomb and urge US action.

He was deeply shaken by the destruction and campaigned for peace.

Q13. When did Einstein die?

In 1955.

✎ Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe Einstein’s childhood and school days.

Einstein was born in Ulm in 1879 with an unusually large head, and his mother feared he was a freak. He started speaking only at about two and a half. He played alone and his playmates called him boring. He began violin at six to please his mother. At the Munich school he disliked discipline and rote learning, and a headmaster said he would never succeed. He left Munich, his family moved to Milan, and he studied in Switzerland. Despite everything he was not a bad pupil; he excelled in mathematics.

Q2. Why is the title “A Truly Beautiful Mind” suitable?

Einstein’s intelligence gave the world relativity and E = mc², and he won the Nobel Prize. But his mind was “beautiful” because he used it for humanity. He warned about the bomb, grieved over Hiroshima and Nagasaki, campaigned for peace, loved music and lived simply. Thus he was both genius and good human being.

Q3. Why is Einstein called a “world citizen”?

Though a German Jew by birth, his ideas, achievements and concern for peace belonged to the whole world. He lived in Germany, Italy, Switzerland and the USA and worked for humanity everywhere, so he is remembered as a citizen of the world.

Q4. Write about Einstein’s letter to Roosevelt and its result.

As Nazi power grew, Einstein feared Germany would make an atomic bomb. He wrote to US President Franklin Roosevelt in 1939 urging research, which helped lead to the arms race and the bombs dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Einstein was deeply shaken by this destruction and spent his later years campaigning for peace and disarmament.

Q5. Describe Einstein’s personal life and his bond with Mileva.

At college he met Mileva Maric, who understood his ideas; he saw in her an ally. His mother disapproved of the match. They married but later the relationship strained and they divorced in 1919. He then married his cousin Elsa. As per the divorce settlement, his Nobel Prize money went to Mileva.
