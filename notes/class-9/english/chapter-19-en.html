On Killing a Tree – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Poem & Poet

Poem: On Killing a Tree (Class 9, UP Board)
Poet: Gieve Patel (1940–2023), an Indian poet, painter, playwright and doctor from Mumbai.
Type: Free verse, descriptive and ironic poem.
Theme: Man’s cruelty to nature, the tree’s will to live, importance of conservation.

🎬 Animated Diagram (Labeled)

1 = A healthy tree, 2 = Hacked stump with new sprouts, 3 = Root pulled out and scorched in the sun.

📖 Poem Explained (Step by Step)

  • Killing is not easy: A simple jab of a knife cannot kill a tree. It has grown slowly for years, feeding on earth and soaking up sunlight, air and water.
  • Hacking and chopping: Cutting the trunk and branches is not enough. The bleeding bark heals, and green twigs sprout near the ground and can grow into a full tree again.
  • Pulling out the root: The root must be pulled out of the earth with force, tied with ropes and tugged out, like a tooth torn from the mouth of the earth.
  • Scorching: The root is left in the sun and air for months until it turns brown, dry and brittle. Only then is the tree truly dead.
  • Message: A tree has a deep will to live, and man destroys it with great effort and cruelty.

🎯 Central Idea (About 150 words)

“On Killing a Tree” by Gieve Patel shows how difficult it is to destroy a tree and, in doing so, criticises human cruelty to nature. The poet explains that a tree is not killed by a simple jab of a knife, because it has grown slowly for years by taking in earth, sunlight, air and water. Even if we hack and chop it, the bleeding bark heals and new green shoots sprout and may grow into a full tree again. To truly kill it, the roots must be pulled out of the earth and left to scorch and dry in the sun and air until they turn brown and brittle. The poem is ironic, because the poet explains this violent process in a calm, matter-of-fact tone. It shows the tree’s strong will to live and the greed and cruelty of man. It also silently urges us to respect, protect and conserve trees and the environment.

🧠 Literary Points

  • Title: Ironic; ‘killing’ is usually for animals, here it is for a tree.
  • Personification: The tree is shown as a living being with life and will; the earth has a ‘mouth’.
  • Metaphor: Pulling a root is compared to snapping out a tooth from the earth’s mouth.
  • Imagery: Visual and tactile pictures (bleeding bark, brown dry roots).
  • Tone: Calm, matter-of-fact and ironic, which makes the cruelty more striking.
  • Free verse: No fixed rhyme or rhythm; natural speech.
  • Symbol: The tree stands for nature’s strength and endurance.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Jab – sharp stab or poke
  • Consume – to take in, use up
  • Hack – to cut roughly
  • Bleeding – losing sap or blood
  • Sprout – to grow new shoots
  • Scorch – to burn the surface with heat
  • Brittle – hard and easily broken
  • Anchoring – holding firmly in place

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote On Killing a Tree?

Gieve Patel, an Indian poet and painter.

Q2. Can a tree be killed by a simple jab of a knife?

No. It has grown slowly for years and is too strong for one stab.

Q3. What helps the tree to grow?

Earth, sunlight, air and water.

Q4. What happens when the trunk is only hacked?

The bark heals and green shoots sprout again.

Q5. What must be done to kill the tree?

Its root must be pulled out and scorched in the sun and air.

Q6. What is the tone of the poem?

Calm, serious and ironic.

Q7. What does the poem teach?

To respect and protect trees and nature.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Why is killing a tree not easy, according to the poet?

The tree grows slowly over many years, taking nourishment from the earth, sun, air and water. So it is deeply rooted and full of life. Hacking its branches or trunk only makes it bleed; the bark heals and new shoots spring up. Real killing needs pulling out the root and drying it in the sun.

Q2. Explain the process of killing the tree as described in the poem.

First the tree is hacked and chopped, but this is not enough because new green twigs sprout. Then the root is pulled out of the earth with ropes. Finally the root is left in the sun and air to scorch until it is brown and dry. Only then is the tree dead.

Q3. How is the poem ironic?

The poet describes the killing in a calm, practical way, like giving instructions. But the real feeling is a protest against cruelty. The effort needed to kill a tree shows its strength and man’s selfishness, so the calm tone makes the cruelty more painful.

Q4. What is the message of the poem?

The poem teaches that trees are living beings with great strength and will to live. Destroying them is cruel and harms the earth. We should protect trees, plant more of them and respect nature.

Q5. Comment on the use of personification and imagery.

The tree is treated as a living being that bleeds, heals and ‘wants’ to remain. The earth is given a mouth from which the root is snapped like a tooth. Such images make the reader feel the pain of the tree and see the cruelty clearly.

✔ (a) a simple jab of a knife

✔ (b) earth, sun, air and water

✔ (b) scorched in the sun

✔ (b) free verse

7. The tone of the poem is…

8. The poem’s message is to…
(a) cut trees   (b) protect trees   (c) fight   (d) travel
