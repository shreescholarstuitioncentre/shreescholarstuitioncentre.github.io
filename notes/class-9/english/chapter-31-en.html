✏️ The Beggar – Anton Chekhov

Class 9 • UP Board • Prose • Notes in English

1. About the Author

Anton Pavlovich Chekhov (1860–1904) was a Russian doctor-turned-writer. He is famous for short stories and plays like The Cherry Orchard. His stories show everyday life, small human weaknesses and deep sympathy.

3. Animated Scene (labelled)

👆 The beggar walks towards the lawyer; Olga swings the axe at the wood pile near the house.

4. Story Explained (in steps)

  1. Meeting: Lushkoff begs for alms with a sad story of a lost teaching job.
  2. Exposure: Skvortsoff recalls that he had lied before as a ‘student’.
  3. Test: Skvortsoff offers wood-chopping work. Lushkoff, ashamed, agrees.
  4. Kindness: Too weak to chop, he is helped by Olga, who also advises him.
  5. Change: He stops drinking and finds honest work.
  6. Reunion: Two years later he is a notary and thanks Olga.

5. Theme & Moral

Reform comes from love, shame and honest labour, not from free charity alone. Never judge a person as hopeless; kindness can change anyone.

6. Word Meanings

Alms – charity money • Intrigue – secret plot • Reform – to improve • Notary – legal official • Compassion – deep pity

7. Short Q & A

Q1. Who is the author of 'The Beggar'?

Anton Chekhov, a famous Russian short-story writer and playwright.

Q2. Who is Lushkoff?

He is a poor, drunken beggar who tells false sad stories to get money. At last he reforms and becomes a respected notary.

Q3. Who is Skvortsoff?

A young, kind lawyer. He wants to cure the beggar's laziness by making him work, not by giving free alms.

Q4. What story did Lushkoff first tell?

He said he was a teacher who lost his job because of intrigues, and now had no work and no food.

Q5. What did Skvortsoff remember?

He had seen the same man before, begging as a ‘student expelled from school’. So the stories were lies.

Q6. Why did Skvortsoff offer wood-chopping?

To test the beggar. Real need would accept honest work; a lazy liar would refuse.

Q7. Who is Olga?

The cook in Skvortsoff's house. She is poor, simple and has a very kind heart.

Q8. Why did Olga chop the wood herself?

Lushkoff was drunk and too weak to swing the axe. She felt pity and finished the work.

Q9. What did Lushkoff say two years later?

He was now a notary. He thanked Skvortsoff, but said it was Olga's words and kindness that changed him.

Q10. What is the moral of the story?

Kindness and honest work can reform a person. Real help makes a man self-reliant, not dependent.

8. Long Q & A

Q1. Describe the character of Lushkoff.

Lushkoff is a young, drunken beggar. At first he is lazy, dishonest and shameless: he invents different sad stories (teacher, student) to win sympathy. He is also weak and sickly because of drink. But he is not wholly bad. When Skvortsoff exposes him he feels shame, and Olga's pity touches his heart. He stops drinking, works hard and becomes a notary. He shows gratitude by visiting Olga. So he is a round character who changes from a liar to an honest man.

Q2. Describe the character of Skvortsoff.

Skvortsoff is an educated and clever lawyer. He is sharp (he remembers the beggar's earlier lie), practical (he gives work, not money) and kind (he wishes to reform the man). He is also a bit harsh and proud: he forces the beggar to chop wood to shame him. Yet he does not mock him cruelly. His method begins the change, though the real cure comes from Olga.

Q3. Why is Olga the real hero of the story?

Olga is a poor cook, yet she has what the lawyer lacks: real compassion. She scolds Lushkoff for drinking, then chops the wood for him and sends him away with kind words. She asks nothing in return. Her love, not the lawyer's lecture, makes Lushkoff see his own wrongdoing. Lushkoff himself says so at the end. Hence Olga is the true hero.

Q4. Write the summary of 'The Beggar'.

Skvortsoff, a lawyer, meets a beggar named Lushkoff who says he is an unemployed teacher. Skvortsoff recalls him as a ‘student’ who begged earlier. Ashamed, Lushkoff admits his bad habits. Skvortsoff offers him wood-chopping. The weak, drunken man cannot work, so the cook Olga chops the wood herself and advises him kindly. Lushkoff leaves, reforms and, two years later, meets Skvortsoff at a theatre as a notary. He credits Olga for his change. Skvortsoff is moved, and the story shows that sympathy transforms people.
