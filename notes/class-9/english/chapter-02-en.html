✎ The Sound of Music ✎

Class 9 • English (Beehive) • UP Board / NCERT • Detailed Notes + Q&A

Chapter at a glance: This chapter has two parts (both are about people who loved music deeply).
Part I – Evelyn Glennie: a deaf Scottish girl who became a world-famous percussionist.
Part II – The Shehnai of Bismillah Khan: how the shehnai became a classical instrument and the life of its greatest maestro.

★ Part I: Evelyn Glennie – A True Inspiration

1. The first sign. Evelyn Glennie was born in Scotland. At about age 8 her mother noticed her hearing was becoming weak. By age 11 she was profoundly deaf.
2. No giving up. Evelyn loved music. Doctors and teachers thought she should leave it, but she refused to let deafness stop her.
3. The magic lesson. Her percussion teacher Ron Forbes played a drum and told her to put her hands on the wall and feel the vibrations. She learnt sound can be felt, not only heard.
4. Feeling with the body. She learnt to feel low sounds in her legs and stomach and high sounds in her face, neck and chest. She plays barefoot to feel the stage vibrate.
5. Success. She joined the Royal Academy of Music (London), became the first full-time solo percussionist in the world and won many international awards.

Key Points & Meanings

  • Percussionist – player of instruments struck for sound (drums, xylophone).
  • Vibration – quick back-and-forth movement that carries sound.
  • Profoundly deaf – almost totally unable to hear.
  • Moral: Determination and a positive attitude can beat any disability.

★ Part II: The Shehnai of Bismillah Khan

Origin of the word “shehnai”: Long ago the pungi, a reed pipe, was banned from royal courts because its sound was shrill. A barber (nai) in a royal household cut a pipe with a natural hollow stem and seven holes; it gave a sweet, mellow sound. It was played in the shah’s (emperor’s) chamber, so it was called “shehnai” (shah + nai).
  • The shehnai was played at weddings and temples, in the nahabat (ensemble at the gate of royal palaces).
  • Bismillah Khan (1916–2006) was born in Dumraon, Bihar, into a family of court musicians; he trained under his uncle Ali Bux at the Vishwanath temple, Varanasi.
  • He made the shehnai a classical concert instrument.
  • On 15 August 1947 he played at the Red Fort, Delhi, at Independence celebrations; the tradition continued on later Independence Days.
  • He loved Varanasi and the river Ganga; he refused to settle abroad, saying he could not take Ganga with him.
  • He received the Bharat Ratna in 2001.

✎ Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe Evelyn Glennie’s struggle and success.

Evelyn’s hearing began failing at eight, and by eleven she was profoundly deaf. People thought a musical career was impossible. But she loved music and her teacher Ron Forbes taught her to feel vibrations through her hands, legs, stomach, face, neck and chest. She trained at the Royal Academy of Music, performed barefoot to feel the stage, and became the world’s first full-time solo percussionist, winning many awards. Her life shows that willpower can defeat disability.

Q2. “Sound can be felt as well as heard.” Explain with reference to Evelyn.

Sound is vibration. Normal ears turn vibrations into signals for the brain. Evelyn’s ears do not work, so she senses the same vibrations through her body. Low notes shake her legs and stomach; high notes are felt in her face, neck and chest. By interpreting these signals she “hears” music and plays perfectly.

Q3. Write the story of the shehnai and Bismillah Khan’s contribution.

The pungi was banned in royal courts for its harsh sound. A barber found a hollow reed with seven holes giving a mellow tone and played it before the shah, so it was called shehnai. It was used at temples and weddings. Bismillah Khan, born in Dumraon in 1916, trained with his uncle in Varanasi and raised the shehnai to a classical concert instrument. He played at the Red Fort on 15 August 1947, loved Ganga and Varanasi, and received the Bharat Ratna in 2001.

Q4. Compare Evelyn Glennie and Bismillah Khan.

Similarities: both devoted their lives to music, worked hard, became world-famous and inspire others. Differences: Evelyn overcame deafness and plays percussion; Bismillah Khan played the wind instrument shehnai and made it classical; Evelyn is Scottish, Khan was Indian and rooted in Varanasi.
