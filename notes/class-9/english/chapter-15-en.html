The Lake Isle of Innisfree – Class 9 Notes (English)

Poet: W. B. Yeats (1865–1939), Irish poet, Nobel Prize 1923  |  Written: 1888, published 1890  |  Form: Lyric, 3 stanzas × 4 lines = 12 lines  |  Rhyme: ABAB  |  Book: Beehive (Class 9)

1. About the Poet

William Butler Yeats was an Irish poet, dramatist and a leader of the Irish Literary Revival. He was born in Dublin and spent childhood holidays in County Sligo, whose lakes and hills filled his poems. He won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1923. He wrote this poem while living in noisy London.

2. About Innisfree

Innisfree is a tiny island in Lough Gill, County Sligo, Ireland. The poet remembered it from childhood. The poem was also inspired by Thoreau's idea of simple living in nature, as in Walden.

3. Stanza-wise Summary

Stanza 1 – The decision

The poet announces that he will get up and go to Innisfree at once. There he will build a small cabin from clay and wattles (woven sticks), plant nine rows of beans, keep a hive for honey bees and live alone in a glade loud with the humming of bees.

Stanza 2 – The peace

He will have peace there. It comes slowly, dropping from the veils (mist) of morning to the place where the cricket sings. Midnight will glimmer, noon will glow purple with heather, and evening will be filled with the wings of linnets (small birds).

Stanza 3 – The inner call

He repeats that he will go, because day and night he hears the lake water lapping gently on the shore. Even while standing on a road or on grey city pavements, he hears it in the deep core of his heart.

4. Animated Diagram – Innisfree Island

Explanation: The island in the lake has the poet's dream cabin, nine bean rows and a beehive with bees flying around. Waves move gently, the sun glows and a bird (linnet) flies across the sky. This shows the poet's imagined simple, peaceful, self-reliant life far from the city.

5. Central Idea (≈150 words)

The central idea of "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" is man's deep longing for peace, simplicity and closeness to nature. Living in the noisy, grey city of London, the poet dreams of escaping to the quiet island of Innisfree. There he plans to build a small cabin of clay and wattles, grow nine rows of beans and keep a hive for honey bees, living alone in the peaceful glade. He imagines peace slowly descending from the morning mist to the singing cricket, the glimmering midnight, the purple glow of noon and the evening full of linnets' wings. The sound of lake water lapping on the shore calls him constantly and echoes in his heart even on grey pavements. The poem shows that true happiness lies in a simple natural life, away from noise and materialism, and that the memory of nature can live within us wherever we are.

6. Theme, Tone, Devices

  • Themes: love of nature, escape from city life, peace and simplicity, nostalgia, self-reliance.
  • Tone: longing, dreamy, peaceful and nostalgic.
  • Symbol: Innisfree = peace and inner happiness; city = noise and stress; bees and beans = simple self-sufficient life.
  • Imagery: visual (purple glow, glimmer, linnet's wings) and auditory (bee-loud glade, lapping water, cricket).
  • Personification/Metaphor: peace "comes dropping slow" like drops from the morning veil.
  • Repetition: "I will arise and go"; Alliteration: "bee-loud glade", "low sounds"; Onomatopoeia: "lapping".
  • Rhyme scheme: ABAB in each stanza.
  • Contrast: grey city pavements vs green peaceful island.

8. Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Who wrote the poem?

W. B. Yeats, an Irish poet.

Q2. Where is Innisfree?

It is a small island in Lough Gill, County Sligo, Ireland.

Q3. What will the poet build there?

A small cabin made of clay and wattles.

Q4. How many rows of beans will he plant?

Nine rows.

For honey, and to enjoy the humming sound of bees in the glade.

9. Long Questions & Answers

L1. Why does the poet want to go to Innisfree?

The poet lives in noisy, grey London and longs for peace. Innisfree reminds him of the calm lake, birds and nature of his childhood. He wants a simple life of building a cabin, growing beans and keeping bees, away from city stress.

L2. Describe the peace the poet expects at Innisfree.

Peace will come slowly from the morning mist to where the cricket sings. At midnight the sky glimmers, at noon the heather glows purple and in the evening the air is full of linnets. Every part of the day will be beautiful and calm.

L3. What is the meaning of the last stanza?

The poet says he keeps hearing the lapping of lake water night and day. Even on grey city roads, he hears it in his heart. It means nature is not only outside but lives inside him, and this inner call makes him decide to leave.

L4. What message does the poem give?

The poem tells us that true happiness lies in simplicity and nature, not in noise and wealth. We should stay connected with nature and find inner peace. Even in the busiest place, memories of nature can calm us.
