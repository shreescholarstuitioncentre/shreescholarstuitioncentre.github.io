📦 Packing

by Jerome K. Jerome (from Three Men in a Boat) · Class 9 English

1. About the Author

Jerome Klapka Jerome (1859–1927) was an English humorist. Three Men in a Boat (1889) is his most famous book. His style: light humour, exaggeration and self‑mockery.

4. Summary (Detailed)

The three friends plan a boating trip up the river Thames. On Friday evening they sit to plan what to take. The narrator proudly claims he is wonderful at packing, so he asks George and Harris to relax while he does it all. They happily agree and lie down, smoking, and watch him work.

The narrator packs the bags first, then the hamper. But he keeps finding things missing and unpacks everything again. The toothbrush is his big problem: he is sure he packed it, searches all bags, and finally finds it in a boot. By then the packing is a mess and has to be redone.

When Harris offers to pack the hamper, he does it carelessly: he puts heavy things on soft things, sits on the pies, steps into the butter and breaks things. George, in the middle of it, hurts his finger. The dog Montmorency also "helps": he walks into everything, puts his leg in the jam and takes the teaspoon. Everyone loses temper and the work gets done very late at night.

Humour comes from the narrator's boasting, the friends' laziness and the dog's naughtiness. Moral: Pride and laziness make easy work difficult; real work needs cooperation.

5. Themes & Style

  • Humour and irony (the "expert" packs worst).
  • Human weakness: pride, laziness, carelessness.
  • Teamwork vs. leaving work to others.
  • Simple, conversational, first‑person style with exaggeration.

7. Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Who are the characters in the lesson?

The narrator (J.), George, Harris and the dog Montmorency.

Q2. What did the narrator claim about himself?

He claimed he was a very good packer and had a special talent for it. He insisted on doing all the packing himself.

Q3. What did George and Harris do while the narrator packed?

They lay back, smoked and watched him, offering no help.

Q4. What was the narrator's problem with the toothbrush?

He could not remember where he had put it, so he unpacked everything to find it, and wasted a lot of time.

Q5. How did Harris spoil the packing?

He packed carelessly, sat on the pies and stepped into the butter.

Q6. How did Montmorency "help"?

He got in the way, put his leg in the jam and took the teaspoon, treating packing as a game.

Q7. Which book is the lesson taken from?

Three Men in a Boat by Jerome K. Jerome.

8. Long Questions & Answers

Q1. Describe the character of the narrator (J.).

The narrator is proud and bossy. He thinks he is the best packer in the world and orders the others to sit back. But his confidence is false: he forgets where things are, packs and unpacks again and again, and finally loses his temper. He is also humorous because he can laugh at his own mistakes. Through him the author shows how boasting makes a person look foolish.

Q2. How does the author create humour in "Packing"?

Humour comes from (i) the contrast between the narrator's claims and his actions, (ii) exaggeration of small incidents like the lost toothbrush, (iii) the silly behaviour of Harris and George, and (iv) the dog's naughty tricks. The simple, chatty language makes the reader feel the author is speaking to him directly.

Q3. Write a note on Montmorency's role.

Montmorency is the funniest "helper". He sits on things being packed, walks over the clothes, puts his leg into the jam and steals the teaspoon. He thinks all work is play. His behaviour adds to the confusion and shows that a helper without sense is worse than no helper.

Q4. What lesson do we learn from the chapter?

We learn not to boast and not to leave work to others. Work is done better when everyone cooperates carefully. Planning and keeping things in order saves time.
