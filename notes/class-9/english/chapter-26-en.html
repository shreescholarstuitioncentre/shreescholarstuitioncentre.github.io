The Happy Prince – Class 9 English Notes

📌 About the Author & Story

Story: The Happy Prince (Class 9 supplementary reader, UP Board)
Author: Oscar Wilde (1854–1900), Irish poet and playwright (The Picture of Dorian Gray, The Importance of Being Earnest).
Collection: The Happy Prince and Other Tales (1888).
Type: Literary fairy tale / moral allegory.
Theme: Compassion, sacrifice, kindness versus selfishness, and true values.

🎬 Animated Story Diagram (Labeled)

Left: the statue of the Happy Prince and the swallow. Middle: the three gifts to the poor (ruby and two sapphires). Right: winter, the dead swallow, and the angel carrying the two most precious things to God.

📖 Story Explained (Step by Step)

  • The statue: The Happy Prince stands on a tall column above the city. He is covered with thin gold leaf, has two bright sapphires for eyes and a large red ruby on his sword-hilt. People admire him.
  • The swallow: A little swallow is late for his journey to Egypt, as his friends left six weeks earlier. He had stayed behind for the Reed, but she was fickle, so he finally leaves. He rests at the Prince’s feet for the night.
  • The tears: Drops of water fall on him. The Prince is weeping. When alive, he lived in the palace of Sans-Souci, where sorrow was not allowed, so he never knew of misery. Now, from his high place, he sees all the ugliness and misery of the city.
  • The seamstress: A poor seamstress works on a gown for a court ball, while her feverish little son begs for oranges. The Prince asks the swallow to pluck out the ruby and take it to her. The swallow cools the boy’s forehead with his wings.
  • The playwright: A young writer in a cold attic is too weak with hunger to finish his play. The Prince gives one sapphire eye.
  • The match-girl: A little girl has dropped her matches in the gutter and fears her father’s beating. The Prince gives the second sapphire, becoming blind.
  • The swallow stays: Moved by love, the swallow decides to stay with the blind Prince and acts as his eyes. He tells him about Egypt, but the Prince asks him to tell the sufferings of men and women. The swallow flies over the city and reports about the rich enjoying themselves, and beggars at the gates.
  • The gold leaf: The Prince asks the swallow to pick off his gold leaf, piece by piece, and give it to the poor. He becomes dull and grey, but the children’s faces turn rosy.
  • The death: Winter comes, and snow and frost fall. The swallow grows weak, kisses the Prince on the lips and dies at his feet. At that moment the lead heart cracks into two.
  • The Mayor’s decision: The next morning, the Mayor and Councillors see the shabby statue and say it is hardly better than a beggar. They pull it down, and melt it, but the broken lead heart will not melt, and is thrown onto the dust heap beside the dead swallow.
  • The reward: God asks an angel to bring the two most precious things in the city. The angel brings the lead heart and the dead bird. God says the little bird will sing forever in His garden of Paradise, and the Happy Prince will praise Him in His city of gold.

🧠 Theme & Central Idea

The story praises compassion and selfless sacrifice. The statue, once a symbol of wealth, becomes truly great only when it gives away its jewels and gold to help the poor, and the swallow gives up its life out of love. The tale also criticises a society that values outward beauty and money, as the Mayor and Councillors do, and ignores real goodness. It teaches that true happiness comes from helping others, and that God values loving hearts above wealth and appearance.

👥 Character Sketches

  • The Happy Prince: Compassionate, selfless, sensitive and generous; he gives away everything and finds real happiness in helping others.
  • The Swallow: Loyal, kind, loving and sacrificing; he first thinks of himself, but later chooses love and duty over his own life.
  • The Seamstress, Playwright, Match-girl: Poor and suffering people who represent society’s misery.
  • The Mayor and Councillors: Vain, selfish and materialistic; they care only for outward show.
  • The Art Professor (the university’s professor of art): Says the statue is ‘no longer beautiful and so no longer useful’, which shows narrow thinking.
  • The Angel and God: Judges of true values, who reward goodness.

✍️ Literary Points

  • Allegory and symbols: The swallow stands for love and devotion; the Prince for charity; the lead heart for true goodness; gold and jewels for worldly wealth.
  • Irony: He is called ‘Happy’, yet he weeps; the dull statue is worth more than the gold one.
  • Contrast: Palace luxury and the city’s misery; outer beauty and inner goodness.
  • Satire: Wilde mocks the vanity of town officials and the art critic.
  • Pathos: Sad scenes of the poor and the swallow’s death.
  • Personification: A statue and a bird think, speak and feel.
  • Fairy-tale style: Simple, poetic language with a moral.

📚 Word Meanings

  • Gilded – covered with gold
  • Sapphire – blue precious stone
  • Ruby – red precious stone
  • Seamstress – woman who sews
  • Garret – attic room
  • Fickle – changing often
  • Pathos – feeling of pity

✍️ Short Q&A

Q1. Who wrote The Happy Prince?

Oscar Wilde.

Q2. Where did the Happy Prince stand?

On a tall column above the city.

Q3. How was the statue decorated?

With gold leaf, sapphire eyes and a ruby on his sword-hilt.

Q4. Why was the swallow late in leaving for Egypt?

He had stayed behind because of his love for the Reed.

Q5. Why was the Prince weeping?

He saw the misery of the poor from his high place.

Q6. What did the seamstress’s son want?

Oranges, since he had a fever.

Q7. To whom did the Prince give the first sapphire?

To the young playwright.

Q8. Why did the Prince give his second sapphire?

To help the little match-girl.

Q9. Why did the swallow stay with the Prince?

Out of love and pity for the blind Prince.

Q10. What did the swallow do with the gold leaf?

He took it off piece by piece and gave it to the poor.

Q11. What happened to the lead heart?

It broke into two when the swallow died, and did not melt in the furnace.

Q12. What were the two most precious things for God?

The lead heart and the dead bird.

📝 Long Q&A (Detailed)

Q1. Describe the Happy Prince’s statue.

The statue stood on a tall column above the city. It was gilded all over with thin gold leaf, had two bright sapphires for eyes and a large red ruby on the hilt of his sword. People admired its beauty, and a Town Councillor wished to be as artistic.

Q2. Why was the Prince called ‘happy’ in life, and why did he weep as a statue?

In life, the Prince lived in the palace of Sans-Souci, where sorrow was not allowed to enter, so he knew only pleasure. As a statue he stood high and saw all the ugliness and misery of the city. His heart, though of lead, was moved, and he wept.

Q3. How did the Prince help the poor? Narrate the three episodes.

He first sent his ruby to a seamstress whose son was ill with fever. Next, he gave a sapphire eye to a young playwright who was starving in an attic. Finally, he gave the other sapphire to a little match-girl whose matches had fallen in the gutter. Later, he gave his gold leaf to the poor.

Q4. Why did the swallow decide to stay with the Prince?

The swallow was moved by the Prince’s kindness. Once the Prince became blind, the swallow felt pity and love and decided to be his eyes. He stayed in the cold and finally died at his feet, showing loyalty and sacrifice.

Q5. Describe the death of the swallow and the Prince’s heart.

As winter came, the swallow grew weaker. He kissed the Prince on the lips and fell dead at his feet. At that moment the leaden heart broke in two, as if with grief. The Mayor and Councillors later pulled down the statue, but the heart could not be melted.

Q6. Why did the Mayor and the Councillors pull down the statue?

The statue had lost its gold, ruby and sapphires, and looked shabby. The Mayor said it was little better than a beggar. They cared only about outward beauty, and also wanted a statue of themselves, so they melted it.

Q7. What are the two most precious things in the city? Why?

They are the leaden heart and the dead swallow. The heart stands for pure compassion and the bird for love and sacrifice. God values kindness and sacrifice more than gold and jewels, so He takes them to Paradise.

Q8. What message does The Happy Prince give?

The story teaches us to be kind, sacrifice for others and not judge people by their appearance. Real happiness lies in helping the needy. Society should value goodness more than wealth.
