Class 9 Drawing & Painting – Ch.8: Still Life (Sthir Vastu Chitran)

UP Board • Notes in handwritten style

1. What is Still Life?

A still life is a drawing or painting of non-moving objects such as fruits, vases, jugs, books, cups and cloth, arranged by the artist and drawn from direct observation.

It is the best exercise for learning shape, proportion, light and shade, and colour, because the objects do not move and the light can be controlled.

2. Arranging a Still Life (Composition)

1. Choose 3–5 objects of different shapes, heights and colours.
2. Make a triangular group: tall object at the back, smaller ones in front.
3. Let some objects slightly overlap for depth.
4. Place a plain cloth as background and a drapery on the table.
5. Light the group from one side only so shadows are clear.
6. Sit at a comfortable distance; keep your eye level fixed.

4. Light and Shade

PartMeaning
HighlightBrightest spot facing light
Light toneLit area around highlight
Core shadowDarkest band on the object
Reflected lightFaint light bounced back at the edge in shadow
Cast shadowShadow thrown on the table, opposite to the light

5. Steps of Still-Life Drawing

1. Observe the group and decide the paper layout.
2. Lightly mark the total height and width on paper.
3. Draw basic shapes of each object.
4. Check proportions by measuring with a pencil.
5. Make the final outline.
6. Shade or colour: light, mid-tone, shadow, cast shadow.
7. Add details (texture, stalks, patterns) and clean up.

6. Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the basic shape of each object.
Objects: apple, glass, book.
Step 1: Apple is round → sphere.
Step 2: Glass has circular top and straight sides → cylinder.
Step 3: Book has flat faces → cuboid.
Example 2: Find the size of the jug on paper.
Given: Holding the pencil at arm's length, apple width = 1 unit and jug height = 3 units. You draw the apple 4 cm wide.
Step 1: Ratio jug : apple = 3 : 1.
Step 2: Jug height = 3 × 4 cm = 12 cm.
Answer: draw the jug 12 cm tall.
Example 3: Where will the cast shadow fall?
Light comes from the right.
Step 1: Shadow is always opposite the light.
Step 2: So it falls on the left of the object.
Step 3: The side facing the right is lighter; the left side is darker.
