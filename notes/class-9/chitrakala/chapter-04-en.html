1. What is Colour?

Colour is the sensation produced in our eye by light. White light splits into seven colours (VIBGYOR) in a rainbow. Without light there is no colour.

2. Classification of Colours

GroupColours
PrimaryRed, Yellow, Blue – cannot be made by mixing.
SecondaryOrange (R+Y), Green (Y+B), Violet (B+R).
TertiaryPrimary + neighbouring secondary: red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet, red-violet.
NeutralBlack, white and grey – no hue.

3. Complementary Colours

Colours opposite each other on the wheel are complementary: Red–Green, Yellow–Violet, Blue–Orange. Side by side they look very bright; mixed together they make a dull brownish-grey.

4. Warm and Cool Colours

Warm: red, orange, yellow – sun and fire; they seem to come forward. Cool: blue, green, violet – water and sky; they seem to go back. Use warm for the foreground and cool for distance to create depth.

5. Properties of Colour

Hue – the name of colour. Value – its lightness or darkness. Intensity – its purity or brightness.
Tint = colour + white (lighter). Shade = colour + black (darker). Tone = colour + grey.

6. Colour Schemes (Rang Sanyojan)

SchemeMeaning / Example
MonochromaticOne hue in many tints and shades (light to dark blue).
AnalogousNeighbouring colours (yellow, yellow-green, green); calm look.
ComplementaryOpposite colours (blue + orange); strong contrast.
TriadicThree colours equally spaced (red, yellow, blue).

7. Meaning of Colours

Red – energy, danger, love. Yellow – joy, brightness. Blue – peace, vastness. Green – nature, freshness. White – purity, peace. Black – seriousness, mourning. Saffron – sacrifice and courage.

8. Colour Media and Tips

Poster colour, water colour, oil colour, pastels, crayons. Keep brush and water clean, mix colours on the palette, start with light colours, and let each coat dry before the next.
