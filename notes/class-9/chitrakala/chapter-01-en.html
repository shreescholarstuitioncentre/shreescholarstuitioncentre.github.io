Class 9 Drawing & Painting – Ch.1: Introduction to Painting (Chitrakala)

UP Board • Notes in handwritten style

1. Meaning of Chitrakala

Chitrakala (painting) is the art of expressing our feelings, thoughts and observations on a surface (paper, cloth, wall) using lines, shapes and colours. It is called the mother of all fine arts because every visual art begins with a drawing.

Definition: Painting is the creative expression of an artist's imagination through line, form and colour on a flat surface.

2. Importance of Painting

✦ Develops creativity and imagination.
✦ Improves observation and concentration.
✦ Preserves our history and culture (cave art, Ajanta, miniatures).
✦ Gives relaxation and a career (designer, illustrator, art teacher).

3. Elements of Art

ElementExplanation
Line (Rekha)Path of a moving point; base of every drawing. Straight, curved, zig-zag.
Form / Shape (Roop)Outline of an object: circle, square, triangle, or natural shapes.
Colour (Varna)Gives life and mood. Hue, value, intensity.
Tone (Tan)Lightness or darkness of a colour; creates depth.
Texture (Pot)Surface quality: rough, smooth, soft.
Space (Antaral)Area around and between objects.

4. Principles of Composition

Balance (equal weight), Rhythm (repetition giving movement), Proportion (right size relation), Unity (all parts form one whole), Emphasis (main centre of attention), Harmony (pleasing combination).

5. Six Limbs of Indian Painting (Shadanga)

1. Roopabheda – knowledge of forms
2. Pramana – proportion and measurement
3. Bhava – emotion
4. Lavanya Yojana – grace and beauty
5. Sadrishya – likeness
6. Varnika Bhanga – use of colours and brush

6. Materials Used

Drawing paper, pencils (HB, 2B…), eraser, sharpener, colour box, poster/water colours, brushes (flat, round), palette, drawing board.

7. Colours

Primary: Red, Yellow, Blue (cannot be made by mixing).
Secondary: Orange = Red+Yellow, Green = Yellow+Blue, Violet = Blue+Red.
Tertiary: Primary + neighbouring secondary, e.g. Yellow-green.

8. Diagram: Colour Wheel (animated)

Big circles = primary colours, small circles = secondary colours. Each secondary sits between the two primaries that make it.
