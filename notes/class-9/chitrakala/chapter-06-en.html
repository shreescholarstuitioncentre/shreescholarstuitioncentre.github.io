1. Introduction

Indian painting has a history of thousands of years. It began on cave walls, then moved to temple and cave murals, palm-leaf manuscripts, court miniatures and finally modern canvases. Each period shows the life, religion and taste of its people.

2. Prehistoric Painting – Bhimbetka

Bhimbetka rock shelters are near Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh (UNESCO World Heritage Site). Early humans painted on cave walls with colours made from minerals: red (geru), white and green. Subjects: hunting, animals like bison and deer, dance, and daily life. Figures are simple, stick-like and full of movement.

3. Ajanta Paintings

Ajanta is near Aurangabad, Maharashtra. It has 29 rock-cut Buddhist caves made between about the 2nd century BCE and 6th century CE. They were rediscovered in 1819 by a British officer.

Subjects: life of Buddha and Jataka stories. Famous: Padmapani Bodhisattva (Cave 1), Vajrapani, Mother and Child, Dying Princess.
Technique: colours (tempera) painted on dry lime-plaster walls (fresco-secco).
Features: graceful flowing lines, expressive eyes, rich red, ochre, green and blue, natural beauty of flowers and animals.

4. Pala and Jain Schools

Pala school (Bengal-Bihar, 8th–12th c.) painted Buddhist texts on palm leaves. Jain school (Gujarat-Rajasthan, 11th–15th c.) illustrated texts like the Kalpasutra; faces are angular, eyes large and bulging, with gold and ultramarine used.

5. Mughal School

Emperor Humayun brought Persian artists (Mir Sayyid Ali, Abdus Samad). Under Akbar a studio (Tasvirkhana) was made and works like Hamzanama and Akbarnama were illustrated. Jahangir loved nature, birds and portraits; Ustad Mansur and Abul Hasan were famous painters. Features: fine lines, realism, portraits, durbar and hunting scenes, rich colours.

6. Rajasthani School

Centres: Mewar, Bundi, Kishangarh, Jaipur, Marwar. Subjects: Ramayana, Krishna-Radha, Ragamala, Barahmasa. Bright colours, flat treatment, and bold designs. Bani Thani of Kishangarh (painter Nihal Chand) is the most famous.

7. Pahari School

Hill states of Himachal and Jammu: Basohli (bold red-yellow colours, large eyes), Guler (natural, delicate), Kangra (soft colours, graceful lines; Radha-Krishna and Gita Govinda themes).

8. Modern Period

Company school: Indian artists for the British, painting plants, birds and daily life. Raja Ravi Varma: oil paintings of gods and Shakuntala. Bengal School: Abanindranath Tagore (Bharat Mata), Nandalal Bose revived Indian traditions.
