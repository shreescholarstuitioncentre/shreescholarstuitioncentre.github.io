Class 9 Drawing & Painting – Ch.5: Primary and Secondary Colours

UP Board • Notes in handwritten style

1. Primary Colours (Pratham Rang)

Red, Yellow and Blue are the primary colours of pigment. They are pure: no mixing of other colours can make them, and every other colour is made from them.

Red – warm, bold; seen in rose, blood, fire.
Yellow – bright, cheerful; sunflower, sun.
Blue – cool, calm; sky, sea.

Note: in light (TV, mobile) the primaries are Red, Green and Blue (RGB). In painting we use pigments, so our primaries are R, Y, B.

2. Secondary Colours (Dwitiya Rang)

Made by mixing equal parts of two primaries.
MixtureResultSeen in
Red + YellowOrangeorange, marigold
Yellow + BlueGreenleaves, grass
Blue + RedVioletbrinjal, lavender
Red + Yellow + BlueBrown / dull greysoil, wood

3. Rules for Mixing Colours

1. Use a clean palette and brush.
2. Take the lighter colour first and add the darker one drop by drop.
3. Mix equal parts for a pure secondary; more of one primary shifts it (more yellow → yellowish orange).
4. Add white for a lighter tint, a little black for a shade.
5. Test the colour on spare paper before using it.

4. Worked Examples (step by step)

Example 1: To make green.
Step 1: Green is secondary = yellow + blue.
Step 2: Put one brush of yellow on the palette.
Step 3: Add an equal amount of blue.
Step 4: Mix. Answer: medium green.
Example 2: To make light orange.
Step 1: Orange = red + yellow.
Step 2: Mix equal red and yellow.
Step 3: Add white to make it lighter. Answer: light orange (tint).
Example 3: To make yellowish-green.
Step 1: Green = yellow + blue.
Step 2: Take more yellow than blue (e.g., 2 parts : 1 part).
Step 3: Mix well. Answer: yellow-green.

5. Do's and Don'ts

Don't mix all three primaries in large amounts (muddy brown). Wash the brush between colours. Close tubes after use.

6. Diagram: Mixing of Colours (animated)

Three primary circles move together. Where two overlap they make a secondary colour; where all three overlap the colour turns brown.
