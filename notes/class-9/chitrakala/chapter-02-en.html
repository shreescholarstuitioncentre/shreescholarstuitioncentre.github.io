1. What are Elements of Art?

The basic building blocks used to create any picture are called elements of art. Like letters make words, these elements make a painting. The six elements are: Line, Form, Colour, Tone, Texture and Space.

2. Line (Rekha)

A line is the path traced by a moving point. It is the first and most important element; it has length and direction but almost no width.
TypeFeeling / Use
HorizontalRest, calm, peace (sea horizon, sleeping figure)
VerticalHeight, strength, stability (trees, pillars)
SlantingMovement, action, instability (running figure)
CurvedSoftness, grace, flow (waves, hills)
Zig-zagExcitement, danger, lightning

Thick lines show strength or nearness; thin lines show delicacy or distance.

3. Form (Roop)

Form is the shape or outline of an object. Geometric forms (circle, square, triangle) are regular; organic forms (leaf, cloud, animal) are free and natural. A flat form has length and breadth (2-D); a solid form also has depth (3-D), shown with shading.

4. Colour (Varna)

Colour gives life and mood. It has three properties: Hue (name of colour), Value (lightness/darkness) and Intensity (brightness or purity). Warm colours (red, orange, yellow) feel near and energetic; cool colours (blue, green, violet) feel far and calm.

5. Tone (Tan)

Tone is the lightness or darkness of a surface. Light falls on one side (highlight) and the other is in shadow, so a flat circle with shading looks like a ball. Pencil tone scale: light → medium → dark → darkest.

6. Texture (Pot)

Texture is the surface quality: rough, smooth, soft, hard. Tactile texture can be felt (sand paper); visual texture is only drawn to look real (fur, bark) using dots, lines and hatching.

7. Space (Antaral)

Space is the area in, around and between objects. The object is positive space; the empty background is negative space. Overlapping, size change and lighter far-away colours create the feeling of depth.
