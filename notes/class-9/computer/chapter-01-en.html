1. What is a Computer?

A computer is an electronic device that works under a set of instructions (program). It takes input, processes it, stores it and gives output – the IPO cycle.

Data → Process → Information
Marks of 3 subjects (data) → add and divide (process) → average = 78% (information).

7. Worked Examples

Example 1 (Easy): Convert bytes. Find bits in 3 bytes.
Step 1: 1 byte = 8 bits. Step 2: 3 × 8 = 24 bits.
Example 2 (Medium): Find KB in 4096 bytes.
Step 1: 1 KB = 1024 bytes. Step 2: 4096 ÷ 1024 = 4 KB.
Example 3 (Hard): A file is 2 MB. How many bytes?
1 MB = 1024 KB; 1 KB = 1024 B. So 2 × 1024 × 1024 = 2,097,152 bytes.

8. Short Questions

What is a computer?

An electronic machine that accepts data (input), processes it by following instructions, stores it, and gives results (output) at high speed and accuracy.

Full form of COMPUTER?

Commonly Operated Machine Particularly Used for Trade, Education and Research.

What is data? What is information?

Data = raw facts and figures (e.g., 45, 'Ravi'). Information = processed, meaningful data (e.g., 'Ravi scored 45 marks').

Name four characteristics of a computer.

Speed, accuracy, diligence (no tiredness), storage capacity, versatility.

Who is the 'Father of Computer'?

Charles Babbage (Analytical Engine, 1837).

Who was the first programmer?

Ada Lovelace, who wrote a program for Babbage's Analytical Engine.

What is the CPU? Name its parts.

Central Processing Unit – the brain of the computer. Parts: Control Unit (CU), Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU), Memory/Registers.

What is ALU?

Performs arithmetic (+ − × ÷) and logical (<, >, =, AND, OR, NOT) operations.

Difference between hardware and software?

Hardware = physical parts you can touch (mouse, RAM). Software = programs/instructions (MS Word, Windows).

Which device was used in the 1st generation?

Vacuum tubes (e.g., ENIAC, UNIVAC).

9. Long Questions

Explain the generations of computers.

1st (1940–56): vacuum tubes; large, hot, costly; machine language. 2nd (1956–63): transistors; smaller, faster; assembly & high-level languages (FORTRAN, COBOL). 3rd (1964–71): Integrated Circuits (IC); keyboard & monitor; operating systems. 4th (1971–present): microprocessor (VLSI); personal computers, GUI, networks. 5th (present & future): Artificial Intelligence, ULSI, voice recognition, quantum ideas.

Describe the block diagram of a computer.

Input unit sends data to the CPU. The Control Unit directs all operations, the ALU calculates, the Memory Unit holds data and instructions temporarily. Results go to the Output unit; permanent data goes to secondary storage (HDD, SSD).

Write the history of computing devices in order.

Abacus (c. 3000 BC) → Napier's Bones (1617) → Pascaline by Blaise Pascal (1642) → Leibniz's calculator (1671) → Jacquard loom cards (1801) → Babbage's Difference & Analytical Engine (1822/1837) → Hollerith tabulator (1890) → Mark-I (1944) → ENIAC (1946).
