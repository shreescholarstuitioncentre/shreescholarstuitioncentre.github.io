1. What is the Internet?

The Internet is a worldwide network of networks. Millions of computers, phones and servers are connected and talk to each other using the TCP/IP protocol. Nobody owns the whole Internet; many ISPs, companies and governments run its parts.

4. World Wide Web (WWW)

The WWW is a service on the Internet: a huge collection of linked web pages written in HTML, joined by hyperlinks and kept on web servers. A website is a group of related pages; its first page is the home page. A browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge) shows them. Pages use HTTP/HTTPS.

9. Internet Safety and Netiquette

Keep passwords secret and strong, never share OTP/PIN, avoid unknown links (phishing), check for https, be polite online and respect copyright. Report cyber bullying or fraud (India cyber crime helpline 1930).

10. Solved Examples (step by step)

Easy 1: Break the URL https://www.upmsp.edu.in/results/class9.html into parts.

Protocol: https; subdomain: www; domain name: upmsp.edu.in (name upmsp, category .edu, country .in); path: /results/class9.html (folder results, page class9.html).

Easy 2: Find the errors in the e-mail address rahul sharma@gmail

(1) Space is not allowed in the username → rahul.sharma or rahul_sharma. (2) The domain is incomplete; it must end with .com. Correct: rahul.sharma@gmail.com.

Intermediate 1: A 4 MB song is downloaded at 2 Mbps. Find the time.

Size = 4 × 8 = 32 Mb. Time = 32 ÷ 2 = 16 seconds.

Intermediate 2: An e-mail allows 25 MB attachments. How many 4.5 MB photos can be attached? How much space is left?

25 ÷ 4.5 = 5.55 → only whole photos: 5 photos. Used = 5 × 4.5 = 22.5 MB. Left = 25 − 22.5 = 2.5 MB (not enough for a 6th photo).

Intermediate 3: Mobile plan gives 1.5 GB/day. Video uses 250 MB per hour. How many hours of video per day?

1.5 GB = 1.5 × 1024 = 1536 MB. Hours = 1536 ÷ 250 = 6.14 → 6 full hours (6 × 250 = 1500 MB, 36 MB left).

Hard 1: A 2 MB web page loads on a 2 Mbps and a 20 Mbps connection. Find both times and the ratio.

Size = 2 × 8 = 16 Mb.
At 2 Mbps: 16 ÷ 2 = 8 s. At 20 Mbps: 16 ÷ 20 = 0.8 s.
Ratio = 8 ÷ 0.8 = 10 = 20 ÷ 2, i.e. time is inversely proportional to speed ✓.

Hard 2: A mail has 3 To, 2 Cc and 4 Bcc recipients with a 2 MB attachment. Find total recipients, addresses visible to a To receiver and to a Bcc receiver, and total data delivered.

Total = 3 + 2 + 4 = 9.
To receiver sees To + Cc = 3 + 2 = 5 addresses (Bcc hidden).
A Bcc receiver also sees To + Cc = 5 addresses and only their own Bcc, not the other 3 Bcc.
Delivered data = 9 × 2 = 18 MB, but the sender uploads the 2 MB attachment only once.

Hard 3: Prove that the number of possible 3-character domain names using only lowercase letters is 17,576, and that using letters and digits it is 46,656.

By the multiplication principle, each of the 3 positions is chosen independently.
Letters only: 26 choices each → 26 × 26 × 26 = 26³ = 17,576.
Letters + digits: 26 + 10 = 36 choices each → 36³ = 36 × 36 × 36 = 1,296 × 36 = 46,656. Hence proved.

Hard 4: A 4,000-byte message is split into packets of 500 bytes data, each with a 20-byte header. Find packets, total bytes sent and header overhead %. If 1 packet is lost, how many packets are sent in all?

Packets = 4000 ÷ 500 = 8.
Size of each = 500 + 20 = 520 bytes; total = 8 × 520 = 4,160 bytes.
Header bytes = 8 × 20 = 160. Overhead = 160 ÷ 4160 × 100 = 3.85%.
One lost packet is re-sent: 8 + 1 = 9 packets; the receiver reorders them to rebuild the message.

11. Short Q & A (tap for answer)

Q1. What is the Internet?

A worldwide network of interconnected computer networks that use TCP/IP to share information.

Q2. Which network was the origin of the Internet?

ARPANET (1969), funded by the US Department of Defense.

Q3. What is WWW?

World Wide Web: a collection of linked web pages on the Internet, invented by Tim Berners-Lee.

Q4. Internet vs WWW?

Internet is the network (hardware and connections); WWW is a service running on it (web pages). E-mail also uses the Internet but not the WWW.

Q5. What is an ISP? Examples.

Internet Service Provider: company giving Internet access, e.g. BSNL, Jio, Airtel.

Q6. What is a web page and website?

A web page is one document on the web; a website is a collection of related pages.

Q7. What is a home page?

The first or main page of a website.

Q8. What is a web browser? Examples.

Software used to view web pages: Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari.

Q9. What is a web server?

A computer that stores websites and sends pages when a browser asks.

Q10. What is a URL?

Uniform Resource Locator: the address of a web page, e.g. https://www.upmsp.edu.in.

Q11. What is HTTP and HTTPS?

Protocols for web pages; HTTPS is the secure, encrypted version (padlock).

Q12. What is a domain name?

The readable name of a website, e.g. google.com, converted to an IP address by DNS.

Q13. What is a hyperlink?

Text or image that opens another page when clicked.

Q14. What is HTML?

HyperText Markup Language used to create web pages.

Q15. What is a search engine? Examples.

A website that finds information for a keyword: Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo.

Q16. What is e-mail?

Electronic mail: sending messages and files over the Internet.

Q17. Write the format of an e-mail address.

username@domain, e.g. rahul@gmail.com; @ separates user and mail server.

Q18. Cc vs Bcc?

Cc receivers are visible to all; Bcc receivers are hidden from others.

Q19. What is an attachment?

A file sent along with an e-mail.

Q20. What is downloading and uploading?

Download: copy data from the Internet to your device. Upload: send data from your device to the Internet.

Q21. What is e-commerce?

Buying and selling goods and services online.

Q22. What is cyber crime?

Illegal activity using computers or the Internet: hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyber bullying.

Q23. What is phishing?

Fake e-mails or sites that trick you into giving passwords or bank details.

Q24. What is netiquette?

Good manners on the Internet: be polite, do not spam or bully, respect privacy and copyright.

Q25. What are intranet and extranet?

Intranet: private network of one organisation using Internet technology. Extranet: intranet partly opened to outsiders such as partners.

12. Long Q & A

Q1. Describe the history of the Internet.

ARPANET started in 1969 to link research computers. E-mail appeared in 1971. In 1983 TCP/IP became the standard, creating the Internet. Tim Berners-Lee invented the WWW (1989–91), and Mosaic (1993) made browsing popular. VSNL began public Internet in India on 15 August 1995. Later broadband, Wi-Fi and 4G/5G made access fast and common (see animated timeline).

Q2. Explain the services of the Internet.

E-mail, WWW, chat and video calls, e-commerce, e-banking, e-learning, e-governance, cloud storage, social media and entertainment (see table). They save time, money and distance.

Q3. Explain the working of e-mail with diagram.

The sender writes a mail in a client; SMTP of the sender's server forwards it across the Internet to the receiver's mail server, which keeps it in the mailbox until the receiver reads it using POP3 or IMAP (see animated flow).

Q4. Write advantages and disadvantages of the Internet.

Advantages: instant information, fast communication, online education and shopping, entertainment, jobs. Disadvantages: cyber crime, viruses, misinformation, addiction, privacy loss, harmful content, eye and sleep problems.

Q5. Explain how a search engine works.

It crawls pages with bots, indexes their content in a database, matches your keyword to the index, ranks pages by relevance and shows results (see animated steps).

Q6. How can we use the Internet safely?

Use strong, different passwords; enable two-step verification; install antivirus; do not click unknown links; do not share OTP/PIN; check for https; share little personal information; report cyber bullying.
