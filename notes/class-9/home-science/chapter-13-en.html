Chapter 13 : Composition, Classification and Functions of Food

Class 9 · Home Science · UP Board (Notes)

1. Food and nutrients

Food is any substance which, when eaten, gives the body energy, growth, repair and protection from disease. Useful components of food are nutrients.

Nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals and water. (Fibre/roughage is not a nutrient but is necessary.)

2. Composition of food

Every food is a mixture of these nutrients: wheat is rich in carbohydrate, pulses in protein, ghee in fat, orange in vitamin C. Milk has almost all nutrients, so it is called a nearly complete food.

3. Classification

(a) By function

  • Energy-giving: carbohydrates, fats – cereals, sugar, jaggery, potato, ghee, oil.
  • Body-building: proteins – pulses, milk, egg, meat, fish, soyabean.
  • Protective/regulatory: vitamins, minerals, water – fruits, green vegetables, milk.

(b) By source

Plant (cereals, pulses, fruits, vegetables, oils), animal (milk, egg, meat, fish), mineral (salt, water).

(c) Five food groups

1. Cereals and pulses 2. Milk and meat products 3. Fruits 4. Vegetables 5. Fats and sugars.

4. Carbohydrates

Made of C, H, O; 1 g gives 4 kcal.

  • Types: sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose), starch, cellulose (fibre).
  • Functions: energy, protein-sparing, fibre aids digestion and prevents constipation.
  • Sources: wheat, rice, potato, sugar, honey, banana.
  • Excess: obesity, diabetes; deficiency: weakness.

5. Proteins

Made of C, H, O, N (sometimes S); 1 g = 4 kcal. Built from amino acids.

  • First-class (complete): all essential amino acids – milk, egg, meat, soya.
  • Second-class (incomplete): pulses, cereals; mixing pulse + cereal supplements each other.
  • Functions: growth, repair, making enzymes, hormones, antibodies, energy.
  • Deficiency: kwashiorkor (protein), marasmus (protein + energy).

6. Fats

Made of fatty acids and glycerol; 1 g = 9 kcal, the highest energy.

  • Saturated: ghee, butter (solid); unsaturated: mustard, soyabean oil (liquid).
  • Functions: energy, insulation, protect organs, help absorb fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K).
  • Excess: obesity, heart disease.

9. Water and fibre

Water: about 60–70% of body; 2–3 litres daily. Functions: solvent, carries nutrients, controls temperature (sweat), removes wastes.

Fibre (roughage): fruits, vegetables, peels, whole grains; not a nutrient but increases peristalsis and prevents constipation.

10. Balanced diet

A diet with all nutrients in proper amounts according to age, sex, work and condition. It includes cereals, pulses, milk, green vegetables, fruits and a little fat and sugar.

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

1. Energy-giving nutrients —
(a) protein, vitamins (b) carbohydrates, fats (c) minerals, water (d) fibre

Answer: (b) carbohydrates and fats

2. Highest energy per gram —
(a) carbohydrate (b) protein (c) fat (d) vitamin

Answer: (c) fat (9 kcal)

3. Body-building nutrient —
(a) protein (b) fat (c) water (d) starch

Answer: (a) protein

4. Night blindness is due to lack of —
(a) vitamin A (b) B (c) C (d) D

Answer: (a) vitamin A

5. Scurvy is due to lack of —
(a) A (b) C (c) D (d) K

Answer: (b) vitamin C

6. Rickets is due to lack of —
(a) vitamin D (b) B₁ (c) C (d) K

Answer: (a) vitamin D

7. Goitre is due to lack of —
(a) iron (b) iodine (c) calcium (d) sodium

Answer: (b) iodine

8. Needed to make haemoglobin —
(a) iron (b) iodine (c) calcium (d) zinc

Answer: (a) iron

9. Beri-beri is caused by lack of —
(a) B₁ (b) B₂ (c) C (d) A

Answer: (a) vitamin B₁

10. Fat-soluble vitamins are —
(a) A, D, E, K (b) B, C (c) B, K (d) C, D

Answer: (a) A, D, E, K

11. Source of first-class protein —
(a) milk (b) rice (c) potato (d) sugar

Answer: (a) milk

12. Main function of fibre —
(a) energy (b) prevent constipation (c) growth (d) make blood

Answer: (b) prevent constipation

Short Answer Questions

1. What is food?

Anything eaten that gives energy, growth, repair and protection.

2. How many nutrients are there? Name them.

Six: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water.

3. Classify food by function.

Energy-giving, body-building and protective foods.

4. Two functions of carbohydrates.

Give energy; fibre helps digestion.

5. Difference between first- and second-class proteins.

First-class has all essential amino acids (milk, egg); second-class lacks some (pulses, cereals).

6. Source of vitamin C and its deficiency disease.

Amla, lemon, orange; deficiency causes scurvy.

7. What does iodine deficiency cause?

Less thyroxine is made; goitre results.

8. What is a balanced diet?

A diet giving all nutrients in proper amounts.

Long Answer Questions

1. Classify food by function with examples.

(1) Energy-giving: cereals, sugar, ghee, oil (carbohydrates, fats). (2) Body-building: pulses, milk, egg, meat (proteins). (3) Protective: fruits, green vegetables, milk (vitamins, minerals, water). (Figure 1)

2. Write sources, functions and deficiency effects of proteins.

Sources: milk, egg, pulses, meat, soya. Functions: growth, repair, enzymes, hormones, antibodies, energy. Deficiency: kwashiorkor and marasmus.

3. Give sources, functions and deficiency diseases of vitamins.

A – carrot, night blindness; D – sunlight, rickets; K – greens, bleeding; B₁ – whole grains, beri-beri; C – amla, scurvy. (Detailed table in notes)

4. Explain the importance of minerals.

Calcium: bones, teeth; iron: haemoglobin; iodine: thyroxine; sodium-potassium: water balance; phosphorus: bones and energy.
