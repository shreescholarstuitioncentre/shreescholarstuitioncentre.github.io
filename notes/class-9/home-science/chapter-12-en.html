Chapter 12 : Digestion of Food and Related Physiology

Class 9 · Home Science · UP Board (Notes)

1. What is digestion?

Conversion of complex, insoluble food into simple, soluble, absorbable form with the help of enzymes is digestion.

Steps: ingestion → digestion → absorption → assimilation → egestion

2. Alimentary canal

Mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine → rectum → anus. Total length about 9 metres.

Accessory glands: salivary glands, liver (with gall bladder), pancreas.

3. Digestion in the mouth

  • Teeth chew food. Adult: 32 teeth (incisors 8, canines 4, premolars 8, molars 12); milk teeth 20.
  • Tongue mixes food, gives taste, helps swallowing.
  • Saliva: enzyme ptyalin (salivary amylase) changes starch to maltose (sugar). Mucus lubricates food into a bolus.

4. Oesophagus – peristalsis

The bolus moves to the stomach by wave-like contraction and relaxation of the wall, called peristalsis. No digestion occurs here.

5. Digestion in the stomach

J-shaped bag; food stays 3–4 hours and is churned into chyme. Gastric glands secrete gastric juice:

  • HCl: kills bacteria, gives acid medium.
  • Pepsin: protein → peptones.
  • Rennin: (in infants) curdles milk protein.
  • Mucus: protects stomach wall from acid.

6. Small intestine: digestion and absorption

About 6–7 m long. In the duodenum bile (from liver) and pancreatic juice are added.

  • Bile: alkaline; neutralises acid chyme, emulsifies fats; has no enzymes.
  • Pancreatic juice: trypsin (proteins), amylase (starch), lipase (fats).
  • Intestinal juice: maltase, sucrase, lactase, erepsin, lipase – final digestion.

End products

Carbohydrates → glucose; proteins → amino acids; fats → fatty acids + glycerol.

Absorption

Finger-like villi in the ileum increase surface area. Glucose and amino acids enter blood capillaries; fatty acids and glycerol enter the lacteal (lymph vessel).

7. Large intestine

About 1.5 m long. Absorbs water and minerals from undigested food; the rest becomes faeces, stored in the rectum and expelled through the anus. The appendix is a vestigial organ.

9. Liver and pancreas

  • Liver: largest gland; makes bile, stores glycogen, detoxifies poisons, forms urea.
  • Pancreas: gives digestive juice and also insulin, which controls blood sugar.

10. Assimilation

Cells use the absorbed nutrients: glucose for energy, amino acids for new proteins/growth, fat for storage. Extra glucose is stored as glycogen in liver and muscles.

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

1. Digestion of starch begins in —
(a) stomach (b) mouth (c) small intestine (d) large intestine

Answer: (b) mouth, by ptyalin in saliva

2. Enzyme in saliva —
(a) pepsin (b) ptyalin (c) trypsin (d) lipase

Answer: (b) ptyalin

3. Protein digestion begins in —
(a) mouth (b) stomach (c) large intestine (d) oesophagus

Answer: (b) stomach, by pepsin

4. Bile is made in the —
(a) pancreas (b) liver (c) stomach (d) spleen

Answer: (b) liver

5. Function of bile —
(a) protein digestion (b) emulsifies fat (c) starch digestion (d) water absorption

Answer: (b) emulsifies fat

6. Absorption mainly occurs in —
(a) stomach (b) small intestine (c) mouth (d) oesophagus

Answer: (b) small intestine through villi

7. Largest gland —
(a) pancreas (b) liver (c) salivary (d) thyroid

Answer: (b) liver

8. Water is mainly absorbed in —
(a) large intestine (b) stomach (c) mouth (d) liver

Answer: (a) large intestine

9. Total teeth in an adult —
(a) 20 (b) 28 (c) 32 (d) 36

Answer: (c) 32

10. End product of fat digestion —
(a) glucose (b) amino acids (c) fatty acids and glycerol (d) maltose

Answer: (c)

11. Which is NOT a function of HCl in stomach?
(a) kills bacteria (b) acid medium (c) digests starch (d) activates pepsin

Answer: (c) digests starch

12. Movement that pushes food forward —
(a) peristalsis (b) diffusion (c) osmosis (d) respiration

Answer: (a) peristalsis

Short Answer Questions

1. What is digestion?

Conversion of complex food into simple soluble form by enzymes.

2. Write the steps of digestion.

Ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, egestion.

3. What is peristalsis?

Wave-like movement of the alimentary canal wall that pushes food forward.

4. What is the importance of villi?

They increase surface area and speed up absorption of digested food.

5. Write two functions of bile.

Makes acidic chyme alkaline; emulsifies fats.

6. Which juices act in the stomach?

Gastric juice: HCl, pepsin, rennin and mucus.

7. State the function of the large intestine.

Absorbs water and salts, forms and stores faeces.

8. How to avoid constipation?

Fibre-rich diet, more water, exercise, regular toilet time.

Long Answer Questions

1. Describe the human alimentary canal.

Mouth, buccal cavity, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum and anus. Accessory glands: salivary glands, liver and pancreas. (Figure 1)

2. Explain digestion from mouth to large intestine.

Mouth: starch → maltose by ptyalin. Stomach: protein → peptones by pepsin. Small intestine: bile, pancreatic and intestinal juices complete digestion (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol); villi absorb. Large intestine: water absorbed, faeces formed.

3. How does absorption occur in the small intestine?

Villi in the ileum have blood capillaries and a lacteal. Glucose and amino acids enter blood; fatty acids and glycerol enter lymph. (Figure 2)

4. Write the functions of the liver.

Makes bile, stores glycogen, detoxifies poisons, forms urea, metabolises proteins and fats.
