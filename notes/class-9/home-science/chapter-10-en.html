Chapter 10 : Textile Fibres – Types and Uses in Daily Life

Class 9 · Home Science · UP Board (Notes)

1. Fibre, yarn and fabric

A fibre is a thin hair-like strand used to make cloth. Fibres are spun into yarn, and yarn is woven or knitted into fabric.

Fibre → spinning → yarn → weaving → fabric

2. Classification of fibres

(a) Natural fibres: from plants, animals or minerals. (b) Man-made fibres: made by humans through chemical processes.

  • Plant: cotton, jute, flax (linen), coir.
  • Animal: wool, silk.
  • Mineral: asbestos.
  • Artificial (regenerated): rayon, acetate – from wood pulp (cellulose).
  • Synthetic: nylon, polyester (terylene), acrylic – from petrochemicals.

3. Cotton

Obtained from the boll (fruit) of the cotton plant (Gossypium). Chemically it is cellulose.

  • Properties: absorbs moisture, good conductor of heat, stronger when wet, wrinkles and shrinks.
  • Uses: summer clothes, bedsheets, towels, handkerchiefs, bandages.
  • Major producers in India: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Punjab etc.

4. Jute and linen

Jute: from the plant stem, the "golden fibre". Coarse and rough. Uses: gunny bags, mats, ropes, carpets; mainly grown in West Bengal.

Linen: from the stem of flax. Strong, lustrous, very absorbent; costly. Uses: shirts, suits, tablecloths.

5. Wool

From the hair of sheep; chemically protein (keratin). Goat (pashmina, cashmere), angora rabbit, yak, camel and alpaca also give wool.

  • Properties: poor conductor of heat (warm), elastic, absorbs moisture, shrinks and felts when wet.
  • Uses: sweaters, shawls, blankets, coats, carpets.

6. Silk

From the cocoon of the silkworm (Bombyx mori). A protein (fibroin) coated with sericin gum. Rearing silkworms is sericulture.

  • Properties: lustrous, soft, strong, poor conductor of heat.
  • Types: mulberry, tasar, eri, muga.
  • Uses: sarees, kurtas, scarves, curtains.

7. Man-made fibres

Blended fibres

Two fibres mixed to improve properties: terrycot (polyester + cotton), polywool (polyester + wool).

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ)

1. Cotton is obtained from the —
(a) stem (b) boll (fruit) (c) root (d) leaf

Answer: (b) boll (fruit)

2. Wool comes from the hair of —
(a) cow (b) sheep (c) dog (d) horse

Answer: (b) sheep (also goat, yak etc.)

3. Silk is obtained from —
(a) cocoon (b) seed (c) stem (d) leaf

Answer: (a) the cocoon of silkworm

4. The first fully synthetic fibre —
(a) rayon (b) nylon (c) wool (d) jute

Answer: (b) nylon

5. Jute is called —
(a) golden fibre (b) white gold (c) black fibre (d) silver

Answer: (a) golden fibre

6. Sericulture is —
(a) bee keeping (b) silkworm rearing (c) fish farming (d) sheep rearing

Answer: (b) silkworm rearing

7. Which smells like burning hair?
(a) cotton (b) wool (c) nylon (d) jute

Answer: (b) wool (silk too)

8. Artificial silk is —
(a) nylon (b) rayon (c) acrylic (d) linen

Answer: (b) rayon

9. Used in parachutes and ropes —
(a) cotton (b) nylon (c) wool (d) linen

Answer: (b) nylon

10. Terrycot is made of —
(a) polyester + cotton (b) wool + silk (c) jute + cotton (d) nylon + wool

Answer: (a) polyester + cotton

11. Best fabric for summer —
(a) woollen (b) cotton (c) nylon (d) acrylic

Answer: (b) cotton, as it absorbs sweat.

12. Chemically, wool is —
(a) cellulose (b) protein (keratin) (c) fat (d) starch

Answer: (b) protein (keratin)

Short Answer Questions

1. What is a fibre?

A thin hair-like strand from which yarn and fabric are made.

2. Write the main types of fibres.

Natural (plant, animal, mineral) and man-made (artificial, synthetic).

3. Write two properties and two uses of cotton.

Properties: absorbs moisture, good conductor of heat. Uses: cotton clothes, bedsheets, towels.

4. Why is wool warm?

Wool is a poor conductor of heat and traps air between fibres, so body heat does not escape.

5. What are blended fibres?

Fibres made by mixing two or more fibres, e.g. terrycot.

6. Why is rayon called artificial silk?

It is lustrous like silk but made from wood pulp.

7. How do you distinguish cotton from nylon by burning?

Cotton burns with a paper smell and leaves ash; nylon melts into a hard bead.

8. What is sericin?

The natural gum coating silk fibre, removed by boiling.

Long Answer Questions

1. Classify fibres with examples.

Fibres are of two types. Natural: plant (cotton, jute, linen), animal (wool, silk), mineral (asbestos). Man-made: artificial (rayon, acetate) and synthetic (nylon, polyester, acrylic). (Figure 1)

2. Write source, properties and uses of cotton.

Source: boll of the cotton plant. Properties: absorbs moisture, good conductor of heat, stronger when wet, wrinkles and shrinks. Uses: summer wear, bedsheets, towels, bandages, handkerchiefs.

3. Compare wool and silk.

Both are animal protein fibres. Wool: sheep hair; thick, fluffy, insulating; winter clothes. Silk: cocoon; lustrous, soft, strong; sarees, kurtas. Both smell like burning hair.

4. Write merits and demerits of synthetic fibres.

Merits: strong, durable, wrinkle-resistant, quick drying, cheap. Demerits: absorb little moisture, uncomfortable in summer, melt and stick on burning, non-biodegradable (pollution).
