Chapter 8 : Air – Importance of Pure Air and Ventilation

Class 9 · Home Science · UP Board (Notes)

1. What is air?

The layer of gases surrounding the earth is called air. It is a colourless, odourless and tasteless mixture.

Composition of air

GasAmount
Nitrogen78%
Oxygen21%
Carbon dioxide0.03–0.04%
Argon and othersabout 0.9%
Water vapour, dustvariable
Exhaled air has about 16% oxygen and about 4% CO₂.

2. Importance of air

  • Respiration: oxygen oxidises food to release energy.
  • Helps burning (combustion).
  • Plants take CO₂ for photosynthesis.
  • Carries sound; makes wind, rain and weather.
  • Keeps temperature balanced and shields earth from harmful rays.

3. Impure air: causes and harm

Impurities

  • Dust, smoke, ash (vehicles, factories, chulha).
  • Harmful gases: CO, SO₂, excess CO₂.
  • Germs (bacteria, viruses) and bad smell.

Harm

Headache, tiredness, nausea, cough and cold, asthma, bronchitis, pneumonia, tuberculosis (TB) and eye irritation.

4. Features of pure air

  • Enough oxygen, little CO₂.
  • Free from dust, smoke and smell.
  • Cool, fresh and slightly moist.

5. Ventilation

Removing impure air from a room and letting fresh air in is called ventilation.

Principle: warm air is light and rises; cool, heavy air takes its place.

Natural ventilation

Windows, doors, ventilators, courtyards, chimneys, skylights.

  • Diffusion: gases spread on their own.
  • Perflation: wind blowing straight through.
  • Convection: air moves due to temperature difference.

Artificial ventilation

Exhaust fan, ceiling fan, cooler, air conditioner, kitchen chimney.

6. Conditions of good ventilation

  • Windows face each other so air flows across.
  • Ventilators near the ceiling.
  • Window and door area about 1/5 of floor area.
  • No overcrowding; enough space per person.
  • Chimney or exhaust fan in kitchen.
  • Keep ventilators open in winter; never burn an angithi in a closed room (CO danger).

Short Answer Questions

1. What is air?

The layer of gases surrounding the earth is air.

2. What is ventilation?

Removing impure air from a room and bringing in fresh air is ventilation.

3. Why is ventilation necessary?

It maintains oxygen and removes CO₂, moisture, smell and germs, keeping us healthy.

4. Name the means of natural ventilation.

Windows, doors, ventilators, skylights, courtyards and chimneys.

5. Why are ventilators placed high?

Warm, light, impure air rises, so it escapes through a high opening.

6. Name two diseases caused by impure air.

Asthma and tuberculosis (TB).

7. Name the main components of air.

Nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, CO₂ 0.03%, argon and water vapour.

8. What is artificial ventilation?

Changing room air with devices such as fans, exhaust fans, coolers or AC.

Long Answer Questions

1. Describe the importance of pure air in detail.

Pure air is the basis of life. (1) It supplies oxygen for respiration, which oxidises food to give energy. (2) It supports burning. (3) Plants use CO₂ for food and release O₂. (4) Sound, wind, rain and weather depend on air. (5) Pure air prevents disease and keeps us active and cheerful.

2. Define ventilation and explain natural ventilation.

Removing polluted air and bringing fresh air in is ventilation. Methods: (1) Diffusion – gases spread from high to low concentration. (2) Perflation – wind flows across through opposite windows. (3) Convection – warm air rises out of the ventilator while cool air enters by the window.

3. Write the conditions of good ventilation.

Windows facing each other, ventilators near the ceiling, window and door area about 1/5 of floor area, no overcrowding, a chimney in the kitchen, no angithi in closed rooms, and a clean, dry room.

4. Explain causes, harms and prevention of air pollution.

Causes: smoke from vehicles, factories and chulhas, dust, burning waste. Harm: breathing, eye and lung diseases, headache. Prevention: planting trees, clean fuel, good ventilation, not burning waste, checking vehicles.
