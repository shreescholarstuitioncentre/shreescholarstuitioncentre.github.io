Chapter 4: Economy – Basic Needs of the Family

1. Meaning of Need

A desire for a good or service that a person is ready to pay for, and whose non-fulfilment causes hardship, is a need.

Basic needs: food, clothing, shelter. Today health and education are also counted.

2. Characteristics of Needs

  • Unlimited – one is met, another arises
  • Recurring (hunger returns daily)
  • Change with income, age, taste, place and time
  • Resources are limited, so choice is necessary

3. Types of Needs (diagram)

  • Necessities: essential for life – food, clothing, shelter, medicine.
  • Comforts: make life pleasant – fan, fridge, bicycle.
  • Luxuries: unnecessary but liked – costly car, jewellery.

As income grows, comforts may become necessities (e.g. a mobile phone).

4. Family Income

  • Money income: cash received as salary, wages, rent.
  • Real income: goods and services obtained with that money.
  • Psychic income: satisfaction and happiness – living in one's own house, eating vegetables from one's own garden.

5. Family Budget

An advance estimate of income and expenditure for a fixed period. Benefits: stops wasteful spending, creates savings, keeps money for emergencies.
Steps: estimate income → list needs and rank them → divide expenses → set aside savings → review from time to time.

6. Importance of Savings

For future needs such as illness, marriage, children's studies and old age. Ways: bank account, post office, insurance, recurring deposit (RD).
