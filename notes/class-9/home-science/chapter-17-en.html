1. Introduction: Home Remedies (Deshaj Aushadhi)

Deshaj aushadhi (देशज औषधि) = indigenous / native medicines. These are common things found in our kitchen, garden or surroundings (herbs, spices, fruits, honey, salt, etc.) that are used to treat small illnesses at home and give first aid before the doctor comes.

Why are they useful?

  • Easily available in every home (ginger, turmeric, tulsi, ajwain).
  • Cheap – no big expense.
  • Few side effects if used in correct quantity.
  • Immediate help in emergency, when doctor or medicine shop is far.
  • Part of our traditional Indian knowledge (Ayurveda).

Limits (Important)

  • Home remedies are for minor / common problems only.
  • If the illness is serious, continues for 2–3 days, or the patient is a baby/old person – call the doctor immediately.
  • Use correct quantity; excess can harm. Do not give honey to babies below 1 year.
  • Remedies are first aid, not a replacement for doctor’s treatment.

4. ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) – Home Preparation

In diarrhoea, body loses water and salts (dehydration). ORS replaces them.

Recipe

  1. Boil and cool 1 litre clean water (or 1 glass for a small quantity with a pinch of salt and a spoon of sugar in proportion).
  2. Add 6 level teaspoons sugar and ½ teaspoon salt.
  3. Stir well till dissolved.
  4. Give in small sips repeatedly; use within 24 hours.

Dehydration signs: dry mouth, sunken eyes, little urine, weakness, in babies – sunken soft spot (fontanelle).

5. Poison: Meaning, Types and Ways of Entry

Poison (Vish) = a substance which, when it enters the body in small quantity, harms health or causes death.

Types of poison

TypeExamplesEffect
Corrosive (acid/alkali)Sulphuric acid, HCl, caustic soda, ammonia, phenylBurns mouth, throat, food pipe, stomach
IrritantArsenic, mercury, copper salts, stale/spoiled foodPain, vomiting, diarrhoea
NarcoticOpium, sleeping pills, alcoholSleepiness, slow breathing, coma
NeuroticDhatura, Nux-vomicaEffect on brain/nerves, convulsions
Gas / InsecticideCarbon monoxide, DDT, rat poison, pesticidesBreathing trouble, unconsciousness
Animal / BitesSnake, scorpion, bee, wasp, dog (rabies)Pain, swelling, paralysis, fever

Ways poison enters the body

  • By mouth (swallowing medicines, chemicals, spoiled food)
  • By inhalation (gas, fumes)
  • Through skin (pesticides)
  • By injection / bite / sting

Signs of poisoning

  • Burn marks around lips and mouth, smell of chemical
  • Vomiting, stomach pain, diarrhoea
  • Unusual sleepiness or unconsciousness, convulsions
  • Difficulty in breathing, blue lips
  • Empty bottle / tablet strip near patient

6. General Principles of First Aid in Poisoning

  1. Stay calm and call the doctor / ambulance (108) quickly.
  2. Find out the poison – what, how much, when. Keep the bottle/packet/sample of vomit to show doctor.
  3. Remove patient from the source (fresh air for gas; remove clothes and wash skin with water for pesticide).
  4. Give antidote as per poison (see table).
  5. Induce vomiting only if patient is conscious and poison is NOT acid, alkali or kerosene/petrol. Method: 1 glass warm water + 2 spoons salt; or mustard powder in warm water; or touch back of throat with finger.
  6. If unconscious: lay on one side (recovery position), keep airway clear, give nothing by mouth, give artificial respiration if breathing stops.
  7. Keep the patient warm and awake; shift to hospital at once.
Do NOT induce vomiting in: corrosive acid/alkali (burns again food pipe), kerosene/petrol (goes into lungs), unconscious or convulsing patient.

7. Antidotes (Pratikarak) for Common Poisons

PoisonAntidote / First aid
Acid (H₂SO₄, HCl, nitric)No vomiting. Give milk of magnesia, lime water, chalk in water, egg white, milk. Plenty of water.
Alkali (caustic soda, ammonia)No vomiting. Give diluted vinegar or lemon juice, then milk / egg white.
Kerosene / PetrolNo vomiting. Keep patient calm, shift to hospital.
Sleeping pills / Opium / AlcoholInduce vomiting if conscious; strong black tea or coffee; keep awake, walk, splash cold water on face.
Arsenic, Mercury, Copper, Lead (metal salts)Induce vomiting; give milk, raw egg white (forms harmless compound).
Food poisoningLet vomiting happen; ORS, lemon water, light food; doctor if severe.
Insecticide / PesticideRemove clothes, wash skin and eyes, induce vomiting if swallowed and conscious; hospital.
Carbon monoxide / SmokeTake to fresh air, loosen clothes, artificial respiration; hospital.
DhaturaInduce vomiting, strong tea/coffee, hospital.
IodineGive starch (rice water, flour paste) or milk.

Universal antidote (Sarvamanya Pratikarak)

Old textbooks mention a mixture of 2 parts burnt toast powder (charcoal) + 1 part strong tea + 1 part milk of magnesia given in a glass of warm water. Charcoal adsorbs poison, tea (tannin) neutralises alkaloids, magnesia neutralises acid. It is only first aid – hospital is a must.

8. Bites and Stings

CaseFirst aid
Snake biteKeep patient calm and still (fear speeds poison). Keep bitten limb lower than heart and immobilise it. Remove rings/tight things. (Old textbooks: tie a loose band 5–8 cm above bite.) Do not cut, burn or suck. Rush to hospital for anti-snake-venom.
Dog / Monkey biteWash wound with soap and running water for 10–15 min, apply antiseptic, do not bandage tightly. Take anti-rabies vaccine immediately.
Scorpion stingTie band above the sting, cold compress, keep calm, go to doctor.
Bee sting (acidic venom)Remove the sting with tweezers/nail edge; apply paste of baking soda or lime water (alkali); cold compress.
Wasp / Hornet sting (alkaline venom)Apply vinegar or lemon juice (mild acid); cold compress.
Insect in earPut few drops of lukewarm oil; do not use pins; doctor if it does not come out.

If swelling spreads, breathing is difficult or patient faints (allergy) – hospital at once.

9. Prevention of Poisoning

  • Keep medicines, insecticides, phenyl, acids in locked / high shelf, away from children.
  • Store chemicals in original bottles with labels; never in cold-drink or water bottles.
  • Do not eat stale, uncovered or fungus-infected food; wash fruit and vegetables.
  • Keep kitchen ventilated; check gas leaks; do not sleep in a closed room with burning coal/angithi.
  • Do not take medicine without doctor’s advice; check expiry date.
  • Keep a first-aid box and emergency numbers (108, doctor) at home.

10. Home First-Aid Box

HardExample 28. Why is milk given in poison?

Given: Explain why milk/egg white is an antidote for metal poisons.

To find: Reason

Solution:

Step 1: Proteins in milk/egg bind metal ions forming insoluble harmless compounds.
Step 2: This reduces absorption in intestines.
Step 3: They also coat stomach lining; patient is then taken to hospital.

Step 3: Keep first-aid box and 108/doctor number on wall.
Step 4: Teach family basic first aid.

Step 2: Do not burst blisters or apply ghee/toothpaste.
Step 3: Cover with clean cloth; large or face/hand burns → doctor.

Long Answer Questions (12)

Q1. What are home remedies? Explain their importance and limitations.
Meaning: Native medicines available at home, used for first aid and minor illness.
Importance: cheap, available, safe if correct dose, quick help, part of Indian tradition.
Limitations: not for serious disease, may delay proper treatment, wrong dose harms, not suitable for infants without advice. Hence consult doctor when illness persists.
Q2. Describe any eight kitchen herbs/spices and their medicinal uses.
Tulsi (cold-cough), Ginger (indigestion, cough), Haldi (antiseptic), Ajwain (stomach ache), Hing (colic), Clove (toothache), Pepper (sore throat), Mint (vomiting, indigestion), Neem (skin), Aloe vera (burns). Explain each with the method of use as given in the table of Section 2.
Q3. Explain the home treatment of cold, cough and fever.
Cold: steam, tulsi-ginger kadha, haldi milk. Cough: honey + ginger juice, pepper + honey, salt gargle. Fever: tulsi kadha, light diet, rest, sponging with normal water, plenty of fluids; consult doctor if fever lasts above 2 days or very high.
Q4. Describe diarrhoea, its dangers and home treatment (ORS).
Frequent watery stools; loss of water and salts causes dehydration, weakness, shock. Treatment: ORS (1 litre water + 6 tsp sugar + ½ tsp salt), curd, banana, rice water, boiled water, clean hygiene; seek doctor for babies, blood in stool or no improvement.
Q5. What is poison? Describe its types and ways of entry.
Poison = harmful substance in small dose. Types: corrosive, irritant, narcotic, neurotic, gases/insecticides, animal poisons. Entry: mouth, inhalation, skin, bite/injection. Give one example for each type.
Q6. Explain general rules (principles) of first aid in poisoning.
Stay calm; call doctor/108; identify poison and save sample; remove source; give antidote; induce vomiting only when allowed; position unconscious patient on side; keep warm and awake; shift to hospital fast.
Q7. Write antidotes for acid, alkali, sleeping pills, metals and kerosene poisoning with reasons.
Acid → milk of magnesia/lime water (base neutralises acid). Alkali → diluted vinegar/lemon (acid neutralises). Sleeping pills → vomiting, strong coffee, keep awake. Metals → milk/egg white (form harmless compounds). Kerosene → no vomiting, hospital.
Q8. What is universal antidote? Write its composition and working.
2 parts burnt toast powder, 1 part strong tea, 1 part milk of magnesia. Charcoal adsorbs, tannin of tea precipitates alkaloids, magnesia neutralises acids. It is only an emergency measure.
Q9. Describe first aid for snake, dog, scorpion and bee/wasp bites.
Use Section 8 table: calm patient, immobilise, wash, anti-rabies vaccine, cold compress, alkali for bee, acid for wasp; reach hospital.
Q10. Explain how poisoning can be prevented at home.
Store chemicals safely and labelled, medicines locked, check expiry, covered/fresh food, ventilation, no mixing of cleaners, educate children, keep emergency numbers.
Q11. Write a note on the home first-aid box.
Importance (quick help), contents (bandage, cotton, antiseptic, ORS, scissors, tweezers, thermometer, burn gel, torch, list of numbers), keep in a known, dry, accessible place; replace expired items regularly.
Q12. Describe the first aid of burns, cuts and sprains.
Burn: cold water 10 min, aloe/honey, cover. Cut: wash, press with cloth, antiseptic/turmeric, bandage. Sprain: rest, cold compress, bandage, raise limb, turmeric-lime paste.
