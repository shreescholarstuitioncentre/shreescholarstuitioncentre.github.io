Class 9 Home Science — Ch. 21: The Patient's Bed

UP Board • Handwritten-style notes

1. Introduction

  • The patient spends most of the day in bed, so the bed must be comfortable, clean and safe.
  • A well-made bed gives rest, prevents bed-sores, keeps the patient warm and makes nursing easy.

2. Qualities of a Patient's Bed

  • Size about 6 ft x 3 ft, height 2 to 2.5 ft so the nurse need not bend much.
  • Made of iron or wood, light, firm, easy to move and clean; head end can be raised if needed.
  • Mattress is firm, even and clean (cotton or foam, not too soft) with no lumps.
  • Placed head to wall, away from draught and strong light, with space on both sides.

3. Articles Needed for Bed Making

  • Mattress, mackintosh (rubber/waterproof sheet), bottom sheet, draw sheet.
  • Top sheet, blanket, counterpane (bed spread), pillows with covers.
  • Extra: towel, bed-pan, air ring/water pillow, back-rest, bed-cradle.

4. Purpose of Each Layer

  • Mackintosh: protects mattress from urine, sweat and discharge.
  • Bottom sheet: clean soft surface, tucked tightly under mattress.
  • Draw sheet: small sheet over mackintosh in middle; changed easily without disturbing the patient.
  • Top sheet and blanket: warmth and cover; counterpane: neat look and protection from dust.

5. Types of Bed Making

  • Unoccupied bed: patient not on bed; the whole bed is remade with fresh linen.
  • Occupied bed: patient is on the bed; linen is changed by rolling the patient to one side, then the other.
  • Post-operative / anaesthetic bed: kept ready with warm covers and a mackintosh for a patient returning from operation.

6. Steps of Making an Unoccupied Bed

  • Wash hands, collect all articles in order of use on a chair.
  • Remove old linen, brush mattress, turn it over if needed.
  • Spread bottom sheet with fold at the centre; tuck in at head, then foot with a mitred (hospital) corner; pull tight; tuck sides.
  • Spread mackintosh and draw sheet in the middle and tuck under.
  • Spread top sheet, blanket and counterpane; fold top edge neatly; tuck at foot with mitred corners.
  • Put pillow with clean cover, open end away from the door; the bed must be wrinkle-free.

7. Steps of an Occupied Bed

  • Explain to patient, screen the bed, loosen the linen.
  • Roll the patient gently to one side; roll the old sheet toward the centre.
  • Spread clean half-sheet and tuck; roll patient to the other side, remove old sheet, pull clean one.
  • Cover the patient and change top sheet under the blanket, keeping the patient warm.

8. Positions of the Patient

  • Supine (on back), prone (on stomach), lateral/side-lying.
  • Fowler's (half sitting) for heart or breathing problems; Sims' (semi-prone) for examination.
  • Change position every 2 hours to avoid bed-sores.

9. Care and Prevention of Bed-sores

  • Keep skin and sheets dry and wrinkle-free; massage pressure points (back, hips, heels).
  • Use air ring, soft pads; give a balanced diet.
  • Soiled linen should be soaked in disinfectant before washing; dry in sunlight.

Questions & Answers (tap to open)

Q1. Why should a patient's bed be 2 to 2.5 ft high?

So the nurse can work without bending and the patient can get in and out easily.

Q2. What is the use of a mackintosh?

It keeps the mattress from becoming wet or dirty from urine, sweat and discharges.

Q3. What is a draw sheet?

A small sheet laid over the mackintosh in the middle of the bed, easily changed without disturbing the patient.

Q4. What is a mitred corner?

A neat folded corner of the bed sheet at the bed's foot, which keeps sheets tight and tidy.

Q5. Differentiate between occupied and unoccupied bed.

Unoccupied: patient is out of bed and all linen is changed. Occupied: patient lies on bed and linen is changed by rolling the patient side to side.

Q6. Why should bed sheets be wrinkle-free?

Wrinkles press on skin and cause bed-sores.

Q7. What are bed-sores? How are they prevented?

Wounds on skin from pressure on bony parts. Prevent by turning patient every 2 hours, keeping skin dry, smooth sheets, air ring and massage.

Q8. What is Fowler's position?

Half-sitting position with the head end of the bed raised, used in breathing and heart troubles.

Q9. Describe the method of making an unoccupied bed.

Collect articles, remove old linen, spread the bottom sheet with mitred corners, then mackintosh and draw sheet, top sheet, blanket and counterpane, and finally the pillow with a clean cover.

Q10. Why is the open end of the pillow cover placed away from the door?

It looks tidy and prevents dust entering.

Q11. How is the soiled linen handled?

Soaked in disinfectant, washed with soap and hot water, dried in the sun; kept separately for infectious patients.
