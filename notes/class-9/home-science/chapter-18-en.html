1. Bandage: Meaning, Objects and Qualities

Bandage (Patti) = a strip of cloth used to cover, hold, support or press an injured part of the body. It is the most useful item of first aid.

Objects (Uddeshya) of bandaging

  • To hold dressing / pad in position on a wound.
  • To stop bleeding by pressing (pressure bandage).
  • To support an injured limb, joint or sprain and give rest.
  • To immobilise a fracture with splints.
  • To prevent germs, dust and dirt from entering the wound.
  • To reduce swelling (by even pressure).
  • To hold a sling, splint or ice-pack; to give comfort to patient.

Qualities of a good bandage

  • Clean, soft, light and strong; made of cotton, muslin or linen.
  • Porous – allows air to pass.
  • Washable and can be sterilised; no rough edges or knots.
  • Elastic (crepe) for sprains; not too thick.

Main kinds

  • Triangular bandage (Tikoni patti)
  • Roller bandage (Lambi / gol patti)
  • (Also: tailed bandage, adhesive plaster, tubular bandage)

2. Triangular Bandage (Tikoni Patti)

A triangular bandage is a piece of cotton / muslin / linen cloth cut in the shape of a right-angled triangle.

How it is made

  1. Take a square cloth about 1 metre (90–100 cm) on each side.
  2. Cut it diagonally (corner to opposite corner).
  3. We get two triangular bandages.

Size of one bandage: base ≈ 125–135 cm, two sides ≈ 90 cm each. (A small one for children may be about 75 cm square.)

Parts

PartMeaning
Base (Aadhar)The longest side (opposite to the right angle).
Apex / Point (Shirsh)The corner opposite the base (right-angle corner).
Two ends (Siray)The two other corners at the ends of the base; used for tying.
Two sidesThe two shorter edges joining apex and ends.

Best if a safety pin is kept with it to fix the apex.

3. Folded Forms: Broad and Narrow Bandage

The triangular bandage can be folded to make a strip bandage.

4. Uses of Triangular Bandage

  • As arm sling (best known use) to support the injured arm / collar bone / shoulder.
  • As head bandage to hold dressing on scalp.
  • To cover hand, foot, knee, elbow, chest, shoulder wounds.
  • To cover large burns (clean, dry, not tight).
  • As broad / narrow bandage to tie splints and bind legs together in fracture.
  • As pad ring / tourniquet in emergency (not too long).
  • To carry a patient (by tying two legs, improvised stretcher).

Application on different parts

PartMethod (short)

ShoulderApex up on shoulder, base round middle of arm and tie; ends brought and tied to apex sling.

5. Arm Sling (Most Important)

A sling supports the forearm so that weight does not pull the injured shoulder, arm or collar bone.

Arm sling – steps

  1. Make the patient sit comfortably; support the injured arm with the elbow bent to a right angle (90°) and hand slightly higher than elbow.
  2. Slip the open triangular bandage between body and forearm; the apex towards the elbow, the base along the side of body.
  3. Take the upper end over the shoulder of the uninjured side and round the back of the neck.
  4. Bring the lower end up over the forearm to the front of the same shoulder.
  5. Tie both ends with a reef knot in the hollow above the collar bone on the injured side (never on the neck bone).
  6. Fold / tuck the apex neatly around the elbow and pin with safety pin.
  7. Fingers must be seen – check circulation (nail colour, warmth). Hand should be a bit higher than elbow.
St John sling: for a fracture of collar bone / ribs / forearm in which the hand is placed on the opposite shoulder. Narrow arm sling: a narrow bandage hung from neck to support only wrist and hand.

6. Reef Knot (Square Knot)

The knot used in bandaging is the reef knot.

  1. Take the right end over left and twist.
  2. Then take left end over right and twist again.
  3. Pull both ends to make it flat and firm.
  • It is flat, comfortable, secure and easy to untie.
  • Granny knot (right over left twice) slips and is wrong.
  • Tie the knot on the side, not on the wound, bony part or back where the patient will lie. Tuck loose ends.

7. Roller Bandage (Lambi / Gol Patti)

A roller bandage is a long, narrow strip of cloth (gauze, cotton, muslin or crepe) wound into a roll.

Parts

PartMeaning
HeadThe rolled part of bandage.
Tail (free end)The loose, unrolled end – applied first.
Width and lengthChosen as per the body part.

Kinds by material

  • Gauze / muslin roller – light, porous, for dressings.
  • Cotton / calico roller – strong, for firm support.
  • Crepe (elastic) bandage – for sprains, varicose veins, gives even pressure.
  • Flannel – for warmth.

Width of roller bandage (approx.)

WidthUsed for

5 cmHand, wrist, head, foot, forearm of child
7.5 cmArm, forearm, ankle, lower leg

Usual length is about 3 to 5 metres (varies by book/brand).

8. Methods (Turns) of Applying Roller Bandage

TurnWhere usedHow
CircularStart and end of every bandage; forehead, wrist, finger baseEach turn covers the previous exactly
Simple spiralParts of equal thickness: finger, upper arm, thigh, chestEach turn goes upward in a slant and covers ⅔ (or ½) of previous turn
Reverse spiralParts that taper: forearm, lower legOn every turn, turn (twist) the bandage over itself using thumb so it fits; turns lie flat
Figure of eightJoints: elbow, knee, ankle, wristTurns go alternately above and below joint, crossing in the middle and make “8”
Spica (ear of wheat)Shoulder, thumb, groinLike figure of eight but overlapping like ears of wheat
RecurrentHead, finger tip, stumpBandage goes back and forth over the end of the part, then fixed with circular turns

9. Rules for Applying Roller Bandage

  1. Wash hands; clean the wound and put sterile dressing first.
  2. Make patient comfortable, injured part in position of comfort and well supported.
  3. Stand in front of the patient (on the injured side), hold roll with head upward.
  4. Apply from inside to outside of the limb and from below (far part) upward – towards the heart – to help blood return.
  5. Start with two circular turns (anchor) and finish with two circular turns.
  6. Unroll gradually; keep equal tension, each turn overlaps ⅔ of the previous.
  7. Do not make bandage too tight or too loose. Keep fingers/toes uncovered to check circulation.
  8. End by pinning, tying or taping on the side, away from wound and bone; reef knot if tied.
  9. Pad bony areas and between fingers/toes; place padding in joints.
  10. Roller of correct width must be used for the part (see table).

10. Checking Circulation and Removing Bandage

Signs bandage is too tight

  • Fingers/toes become blue or very pale, cold
  • Numbness, tingling, pain
  • Swelling below bandage
  • No pulse below the bandage; nail does not become pink in 2 seconds after pressing

Remedy: loosen at once and re-apply.

Removing

  • Unroll by passing roll from one hand to the other.
  • If stuck, wet with water/lukewarm water and remove gently.
  • If urgent, cut away from wound with scissors; do not pull.

12. Care of Bandages and Home Kit

  • Wash with soap, boil/iron and keep dry; store in clean packet/box.
  • Keep at least 2 triangular bandages and rollers of 5, 7.5 and 10 cm in the first-aid box.
  • Check for damage, stains and moisture; replace old crepe (loses elasticity).
  • Keep safety pins, scissors, cotton and gauze with bandages.

To hold dressing, stop bleeding, support injured part, immobilise fracture, keep wound clean, reduce swelling.
Q2. Write qualities of a good bandage.
Clean, soft, light, strong, porous, washable, sterilisable, no rough knots.
Q3. Describe a triangular bandage.
Triangular piece of cotton/muslin cut diagonally from a 1 m square cloth; base about 130 cm, sides 90 cm; it has base, apex, two ends, sides.
Q4. How will you make broad and narrow bandages?
Fold apex to base to get a flat triangle band, then again to get broad (≈10 cm); fold once more for narrow (≈5 cm).
Q5. Write four uses of triangular bandage.
Arm sling; head; hand and foot; chest, knee, elbow; to cover burns; to tie splints.
Q6. Write the method of applying arm sling.
Elbow bent 90°; place bandage under forearm with apex at elbow; carry upper end over opposite shoulder behind neck; bring lower end up; tie with reef knot above collar bone; pin apex; check fingers.
Q7. How is head bandaged with triangular bandage?
Base above eyebrows, apex hanging behind; take ends behind below the apex, cross, bring to forehead, tie reef knot; tuck and pin the apex.
Q8. How is hand bandaged?
Place hand on bandage fingers towards apex; fold apex over back of hand; cross ends round wrist and tie; tuck apex.
Q9. Describe reef knot.
Right end over left, then left over right and pull; flat, secure, easy to untie; tie on side away from wound.
Q10. Why should knot not be tied over wound or bone?
It causes pain, pressure on bones and delays healing; patient lying on it gets hurt.
Q11. What is a roller bandage? Write its parts.
Long narrow strip of gauze/cotton/crepe wound into a roll. Parts: head (roll) and tail (free end).
Q12. Write the width of roller bandage for different parts.
Finger 2.5 cm; hand/wrist/head/foot 5 cm; arm/ankle 7.5 cm; leg/thigh 10 cm; trunk 15 cm.
Q13. Write the types of roller bandage by material.
Gauze/muslin, cotton/calico, crepe (elastic) and flannel.
Q14. Explain simple spiral turn.
Bandage goes upward in a slant, each turn covering ⅔ of the previous; used on equal-thickness parts like finger, upper arm and thigh.
Q15. Explain reverse spiral turn.
Bandage is twisted over itself on each turn with the thumb so it fits a tapering part; used on forearm and leg.
Q16. Explain figure of eight turn.
Turns go alternately above and below joint crossing in the middle; used on elbow, knee, ankle.
Q17. Write the rules for applying roller bandage (any five).
Clean wound; comfort patient; start two circular turns; bottom to top; inside to outside; equal tension, ⅔ overlap; fingers visible; end with two circular turns and secure on side.
Q18. How do you know bandage is too tight?
Blue/pale, cold fingers, numbness, tingling, pain, swelling below, delayed nail refill.
Q19. How will you remove a bandage stuck to wound?
Wet it with lukewarm water, loosen gently; cut away from wound if needed.
Q20. Distinguish triangular and roller bandage (any three points).
Shape – triangle / roll; parts – base apex ends / head tail; use – sling, head / limbs, joints; making – easy / needs preparation.
Q21. How should bandages be kept?
Washed, ironed/boiled, dry, clean and in a closed first-aid box.
Q22. Write use of crepe bandage.
Gives uniform pressure and support in sprain, strains, varicose veins and swollen joints.
Q23. What is St John sling?
Sling for collar bone / forearm injuries where the hand rests on opposite shoulder.
Q24. What is a pressure bandage?
A bandage over a thick pad on a bleeding wound applied firmly (not too tight) to stop bleeding.
Q25. Why is hand kept slightly higher than elbow in sling?
To reduce swelling by allowing fluid to drain towards the heart.

Q1. What is a bandage? Write its objects and qualities.
Meaning: strip of cloth used to hold dressing, support, press, immobilise. Objects: hold dressing, stop bleeding, support, prevent infection, immobilise fracture, reduce swelling. Qualities: clean, soft, strong, porous, washable, elastic when needed.

Follow the seven steps of Section 5: elbow bent, hand higher, apex at elbow, ends round neck, reef knot above collar bone, pin apex, check fingers.

Head and tail; gauze, cotton, crepe, flannel; widths 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 15 cm with their parts; length 3–5 m.

Circular, simple spiral, reverse spiral, figure of eight, spica, recurrent – with places of use as in table of Section 8. Draw Fig. 6.

Clean wound; comfortable position; stand in front; below to above; inside to outside; two circular turns at start and end; ⅔ overlap; equal tension; leave tips uncovered; pad bony parts; secure away from wound.
Q9. What is reef knot? Why is it used? Explain how to tie.
Square knot; flat, secure, easily untied, comfortable. Right over left, left over right; do not tie like granny knot; place on side.

Q11. How will you give first aid for a sprained ankle using bandage?
Rest, remove shoe, cold compress, apply crepe/roller bandage from toes upward in figure of eight around ankle, ⅔ overlap, raise leg, check toes for circulation, rest and see doctor.

Wash, boil/iron, dry; keep in clean closed box; 2 triangular + rollers of different widths; check expiry/elasticity; replace used items; keep away from children.
