Class 9 Home Science — Ch. 20: Patient's Room and its Arrangement

UP Board • Handwritten-style notes

1. Introduction

  • Patient's room = the room set apart for a sick person's treatment, rest and nursing.
  • A good room helps quick recovery, protects other family members from infection and makes nursing easy.

2. Location

  • Preferably ground or first floor, so the patient can be shifted easily.
  • Away from noise, kitchen smoke, road traffic and crowd; near bathroom and toilet.
  • Should get plenty of sunlight (east/south side best) and fresh air.

3. Size

  • Large enough for bed, furniture and free movement of nurse: about 12 ft x 12 ft (or 10 ft x 12 ft).
  • Each patient needs about 100 sq. ft floor area and about 1000 cubic ft air space.

4. Light and Ventilation

  • Windows and doors on opposite walls give cross-ventilation; ventilators near the roof let out warm, impure air.
  • Sunlight kills germs, so windows should be large; use light curtains to control glare.
  • Artificial light should be soft; a bedside lamp and a night lamp are useful. Direct light must not fall on patient's eyes.

5. Walls, Floor and Roof

  • Walls: smooth, plain, light-coloured (cream, light green, light blue), washable paint, no cracks where germs/insects hide.
  • Floor: smooth, hard, non-slippery, easy to wash (marble, tiles, mosaic).
  • Roof: high, with no cobwebs or leakage.

6. Furniture

  • Keep minimum furniture: bed, bedside table, chair/stool, almirah, medicine tray, dustbin.
  • Furniture should be light, simple and easy to clean; no heavy curtains, carpets or showpieces which collect dust.

7. The Bed

  • About 6 ft x 3 ft, height 2 to 2.5 ft; iron or wooden, with firm mattress.
  • Placed with head to wall, in a position where nurse can reach from both sides, away from direct draught and strong light.
  • Bed sheet should be clean, tight and without wrinkles to avoid bed-sores.

8. Other Points

  • Temperature: 20°C to 25°C, comfortable, no extreme heat or cold.
  • Quietness: no loud talk, radio or visitors; footwear of nurse should be noiseless.
  • Cleanliness: daily damp dusting and mopping with disinfectant; never sweep dry (dust spreads germs).
  • Disinfection: wash things in hot water, phenyl, Dettol; sunlight for bedding; burn waste dressings.
  • Isolation: for infectious diseases (TB, chickenpox, typhoid) keep patient in separate room; use separate utensils and towels.
  • Pleasant: a few flowers, soft colours and cheerful behaviour lift the patient's mood.

Questions & Answers (tap to open)

Q1. What is a patient's room?

A room specially arranged for rest, treatment and nursing of a sick person.

Q2. Why should the patient's room be on the ground floor?

Easy shifting of patient, less climbing, quick access to doctor and ambulance in emergency.

Q3. Why should the room have cross-ventilation?

Fresh air enters from one side and impure air leaves from the other, so air stays fresh and germs do not collect.

Q4. Why is sunlight important?

It kills germs, keeps room dry and cheerful, and gives the patient vitamin D.

Q5. Why should the walls be light-coloured and smooth?

Light colours reflect light and look calm; smooth walls are easily washed and do not keep dust or germs.

Q6. Why should furniture be minimum?

Less furniture means more space, easy movement and cleaning, and less dust.

Q7. Describe the ideal bed for a patient.

6 ft x 3 ft, 2 to 2.5 ft high, of iron or wood, firm mattress, tight wrinkle-free sheet, reachable from both sides, away from draught.

Q8. Why is dry sweeping avoided?

It raises dust carrying germs into air. Damp dusting and mopping with disinfectant is used.

Q9. What is isolation?

Keeping an infectious patient separate from others with own utensils and towels to stop the spread of disease.

Q10. Write a long note on the arrangement of a patient's room.

Location: quiet, ground floor, sunny. Size: about 12x12 ft. Light and air: cross-ventilation, curtains, soft lamps. Walls and floor: smooth, light, washable. Furniture: minimum and simple. Bed: correctly placed with firm mattress. Temperature 20-25°C, quiet, clean and pleasant environment. Daily cleaning and disinfection.

Q11. Why must the nurse wear soft footwear?

To avoid noise that disturbs the patient's sleep and rest.
