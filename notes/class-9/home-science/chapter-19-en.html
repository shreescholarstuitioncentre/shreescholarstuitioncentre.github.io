1. Introduction

DefinitionHome nursing = caring for a sick person at home, following the doctor's instructions, so that the patient recovers quickly.
Home nurse = the person (often the mother, sister, wife or daughter) who looks after the patient at home.

A sick person needs proper care along with medicines. Good nursing speeds recovery, reduces suffering and stops the disease from spreading.

Three parts of nursing: (1) physical comfort of the patient, (2) medicine and diet as advised by the doctor, (3) mental peace and sympathy.

RememberHome nursing is a helper of the doctor, not a substitute. In a serious illness call the doctor or go to hospital at once.

2. Need and Importance of Home Nursing

  • Not every family can afford hospital costs; common illnesses recover well at home.
  • After discharge, the convalescence stage needs care at home.
  • Elderly people, pregnant women, small children and long-term patients (diabetes, asthma, BP) need daily care at home.
  • Among family members the patient's morale stays high, so recovery is faster.
  • Lower risk of hospital infections; diet can be prepared as the patient likes.
  • In emergencies, first care before the doctor arrives can save life.
  • Saves time, money and travel; strengthens love and service spirit in the family.

3. The Home Nurse

(a) Qualities of an ideal home nurse

  1. Physical: healthy, strong, active; good eyesight and hearing; gentle hands; soft footsteps; clean with short trimmed nails and simple washed clothes.
  2. Mental: patience, tolerance, sympathy, calm nature, good memory, sharp observation, good judgement, a cheerful face.
  3. Moral: honesty, trustworthiness, keeping the patient's secrets, spirit of service, unselfishness, punctuality.
  4. Professional: knowledge and practice of nursing (temperature, pulse, bandage, medicine), disease prevention, first aid, diet knowledge.
  5. Discipline: follows every instruction of the doctor; never changes medicine on her own.

(b) Duties of a home nurse

  • Keep the room clean, airy and quiet; make the bed properly.
  • Give medicine on time and in the right dose as the doctor says.
  • Prepare and serve the prescribed diet; ensure enough fluids.
  • Record temperature, pulse, respiration, stool-urine and sleep on a chart.
  • Keep the patient clean: mouth, hair, nails, clothes, sponge bath.
  • Prevent infection: wash hands, separate/boil utensils and clothes, dispose of excreta properly.
  • Cheer up the patient, control visitors, never give bad news.
  • Call the doctor at once for danger signs (unconsciousness, very high fever, breathlessness).
  • Take care of her own health: rest, proper food, hand-washing.

4. The Sick-room

Features of a good sick-room

PointWhat and why
LocationA quiet part of the house, away from noise, kitchen smell and smoke; toilet nearby.

LightPlenty of sunlight (kills germs); no strong light in the eyes; dim lamp at night.
TemperatureNeither too hot nor too cold; fan/heater as the season needs.

⚠ Why a damp cloth for cleaning?Dry dusting raises dust into the air; it can carry germs into the patient's breathing or wounds.

5. The Patient's Bed and Bed-making

An ideal bed

  • Iron or wooden, of medium height (so the nurse need not bend), about 3 ft wide and 6 ft long, approachable from both sides.
  • Mattress level, neither too soft nor too hard; washable/sun-dried.
  • Cotton, white sheets without creases; 2-3 pillows (also for back support).
  • Bed slightly away from the wall and from direct draught of the window.

Layers of the bed (bottom to top)

  1. Mattress
  2. Mackintosh (rubber/plastic sheet) - protects the mattress from getting wet or soiled.
  3. Bottom sheet - tuck corners tightly (mitred corners).
  4. Draw sheet - small sheet under the patient from shoulder to knee; easy to change when soiled.
  5. Top sheet and blanket/quilt
  6. Pillow (open end away from door)

Types of bed-making

TypeWhen / how
1. Closed bedBefore the patient arrives; top sheet and blanket cover the whole bed to keep it dust-free.
2. Open bedPatient about to arrive; top sheet and blanket folded back at the foot end so the patient can lie down at once.
3. Occupied bedPatient is lying on the bed; sheet is changed half at a time by rolling the patient on to a side.

Changing the sheet with the patient in bed - steps

  1. Keep everything ready (clean sheet, draw sheet, pillow covers, towel, laundry basket); tell the patient.
  2. Cover the patient with a blanket; turn the patient on one side with a pillow for support.
  3. Roll the soiled bottom sheet to the middle near the back; spread the clean sheet half way and roll its other half close by.
  4. Turn the patient over the roll to the clean side; remove the dirty sheet; pull the clean sheet tight.
  5. Change mackintosh and draw sheet the same way; make the patient lie straight; spread the clean top sheet and slide out the dirty one.
  6. Change pillow covers; leave the patient comfortable. Put soiled linen in a closed basket (never shake in air).
CarefulNo creases, crumbs, buttons or thick seams under the patient - they cause bed sores. Never shake sheets in the air.

6. Patient's Positions and Bed Sores

PositionUse
Lying on the backGeneral rest, examination; thin pillow under head.
Lateral (on the side)Relieves back pressure; protects airway in vomiting/unconsciousness.
Semi-sitting (Fowler's)Breathlessness, asthma, heart disease; 2-3 pillows behind the back.
Prone (on the stomach)Back massage/dressing as advised by the doctor.
Bed soreA wound or red patch formed on the skin over bony areas (lower back, heels, elbows, shoulders, back of head) due to prolonged pressure that stops blood circulation.

Causes: continuous pressure, moisture, creased/dirty bedding, poor nutrition, unclean skin.

Prevention:

  • Turn the patient every 2 hours.
  • Wash pressure areas with soap and water, dry, apply spirit/powder and massage gently.
  • Use air ring/soft padding; keep the sheet dry, smooth, crease-free.
  • Nourishing diet (protein, vitamins) and enough water.
  • If a sore forms, show the doctor; avoid pressure on it.

7. The Patient's Diet

Therapeutic dietAn easily digestible, nutritious and appetising diet planned according to the disease, age, digestion and the doctor's advice.

Types of diet

TypeExamplesWhen
LiquidWater, coconut water, fruit juice, dal water, buttermilk, ORS, milk, soupHigh fever, diarrhoea-vomiting, early after surgery
Semi-solid / softPorridge (dalia), khichdi, suji kheer, mashed potato-banana, curd-rice, custardAs fever falls, weak digestion
Light solidBoiled vegetables, dal-rice, soft roti, paneerConvalescence
Normal / fullComplete balanced family mealOn full recovery

Principles of feeding the sick

  1. As per doctor's advice and the disease.
  2. Easily digestible; low in ghee, spice and fried items.
  3. Small amounts, often (every 2-3 hours); never force.
  4. Fresh, clean, served at the right temperature.
  5. Clean utensils and tray, napkin, attractive serving to raise appetite.
  6. Respect likes and dislikes; avoid monotony.
  7. At regular times; do not wake a sleeping patient.
  8. Plenty of boiled/filtered water.

Diet in some diseases

DiseaseAllowedAvoid
FeverLiquids, glucose, lemon waterFried, heavy food
Diarrhoea / choleraORS, rice water, curd-riceExcess milk, raw fruit-vegetables
JaundiceSugarcane juice, glucose, boiled vegetablesGhee, oil, fried
DiabetesHigh-fibre food, bitter gourd, greensSugar, sweets
High blood pressureLow salt, fruits, vegetablesPickles, salty snacks
TyphoidLiquid/soft diet, glucoseHigh-fibre, hard food

How to feed a patient

  • Wash your hands; wipe the patient's face and hands; sit the patient up with support (to avoid choking).
  • Napkin on the chest; feed slowly with a small spoon and give time to swallow.
  • Give liquids to a lying patient with a feeding cup (sipper).
  • Rinse the mouth afterwards; wash/boil the utensils; never leave leftover food in the room.

8. Observing the Patient: Temperature, Pulse, Respiration

The main vital signs: temperature, pulse, respiration (+ blood pressure).

(A) Body temperature

Normal temperature98.4°F or 37°C (mouth). Armpit about 1°F lower (97.4°F); rectum about 1°F higher (99.4°F).

Formulas: °C = (°F - 32) × 5/9   |   °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

StateTemperature (approx.)
Normal98.4°F
Low fever99 - 100.4°F
Moderate fever101 - 102°F
High fever103°F or more
Hyperpyrexiaabove 105-106°F - emergency

Structure of the clinical thermometer

A glass tube with a bulb at one end holding mercury; a constriction in the tube stops mercury from falling back when removed from the body; scale 94°F-108°F (or 35°C-42°C); an arrow/red mark at normal.

How to take the temperature

  1. Clean the thermometer with cold soap-water or spirit-cotton (not hot water - it may break).
  2. Hold firmly and shake until mercury goes below 95°F.
  3. Make the patient sit/lie comfortably; no hot/cold drink or exercise for 15 minutes before.
  4. In the mouth: under the tongue, lips closed, 2-3 minutes (never bite).
  5. In the armpit: dry armpit, bulb in the centre, arm pressed to the body, 5 minutes; use for children, unconscious or convulsing patients.
  6. Read the end of the mercury at eye level; record with time on the chart.
  7. Clean, shake and put back in its case.
PrecautionsNever boil it; keep away from heat/sun; if broken, do not touch mercury with bare hands or let it enter the mouth (poisonous). Disinfect with spirit for each patient.

(B) Pulse

The wave felt in arteries with each heartbeat. Place three fingers (index, middle, ring) on the radial artery at the wrist, thumb side - not the thumb, which has its own pulse.

AgeNormal pulse / min
Newborn120-140
Child (1-10 yrs)80-100
Adult72 (60-80)
Old age60-70
  • In fever, pulse rises about 10 beats per 1°F; slightly faster in women than men.
  • Take it at rest (not after fear or exertion); count a full 1 minute, and note rhythm and strength too.

(C) Respiration

One breath = one inhale + one exhale. Count the rise and fall of the chest/abdomen, without telling the patient (else the rate changes) - for example while seeming to hold the wrist.

AgeRespiration / min
Newborn40-45
Child20-30
Adult16-20 (avg 18)
RatioPulse : respiration = 4 : 1 (72 : 18). In pneumonia respiration rises more and the ratio is disturbed.

(D) Blood pressure

Measured with a sphygmomanometer (BP machine). Normal 120/80 mm Hg (upper: systolic, lower: diastolic). Patient calm and seated, arm at heart level.

Reading the chart: a fever that climbs step by step and then falls (like typhoid) - keep the chart safe to show the doctor.

9. Giving Medicine

Precautions

  1. Read the prescription/label: name, dose, time, before/after food, duration.
  2. Check the expiry date; never give medicine with changed colour/smell, or an old prescription without asking.
  3. Wash hands; measure with a measuring glass/spoon (1 teaspoon = 5 ml, 1 tablespoon = 15 ml).
  4. Shake liquid medicines; pour with the label side held in the palm.
  5. Give with water in a sitting position; stay until swallowed.
  6. Give on time; do not double the dose if one is missed - ask the doctor.
  7. Keep medicines out of children's reach, cool and dry, labelled; poisons separately.
  8. If rash, vomiting or allergy appears, stop and tell the doctor.
  9. Note medicines on the chart; do not play doctor.
TermMeaning

External medicine: ointment/lotion on clean skin; eye-ear-nose drops: tilt the head and drop without touching the dropper to the skin.

10. Hot Applications, Cold Applications and Steam

(A) Hot application

Uses: pain, stiffness, stomach ache, cold, joint pain, warming the body.

Using a hot-water bag/bottle:

  1. Check the bag for leaks.
  2. Fill half with bearable hot (not boiling) water.
  3. Press out the air, screw the stopper tight, turn upside down to test for leaks.
  4. Wrap in a cloth cover/towel, never directly on skin; check the skin often for redness/burns.
  5. Change water when it cools; take extra care with unconscious, insensitive patients, infants and diabetics.

Dry heat: hot sand/salt bag, hot cloth. Moist heat (fomentation): dip flannel in hot water, wring, place on skin with a dry cloth over it, 10-15 minutes.

(B) Cold application

  • Ice bag: half-filled with small ice pieces, air pressed out, wrapped in cloth - for head, injury swelling, nosebleed, vomiting.
  • Cold compress: wet cloth on the forehead, change every 5 minutes - for fever, headache.
  • Cold sponging: when temperature is above 102-103°F wipe the body with lukewarm/normal water; stop near 100°F.

(C) Steam inhalation

For cold, blocked nose, cough, sore throat. Boiling water with ajwain/eucalyptus/Vicks in a vessel; patient bends over it (vessel on a steady table) with a towel over the head; 5-10 minutes; protect eyes, prevent spills; supervise children.

11. Patient's Hygiene and Sponge Bath

  • Mouth care: brush/rinse daily; for unconscious or very weak patients clean tongue and lips with cotton dipped in lemon-glycerine or lightly salted water.
  • Hair: comb daily, keep tied and clean; prevent lice.
  • Nails, eyes, ears: trimmed clean nails; wash eyes with clean water.
  • Clothes: loose, cotton, clean; change at once if wet with sweat or excreta.

Sponge bath (bed bath)

For a patient who cannot bathe (fever, weakness, surgery). Needs: lukewarm water, soap, small towel, sponge/wash-cloth, clean clothes, sheet.

  1. Room warm and draught-free; screen/close doors.
  2. Cover the patient with a sheet; expose only one part at a time.
  3. Order: face → neck-ears → arms → chest → abdomen → back (on side) → legs.
  4. Wash each part with soap, dry well and cover; gentle back massage and powder.
  5. Dress in clean clothes, change bed linen; clean and store the articles.

12. Preventing Infection

Infectious diseaseA disease caused by germs (cholera, typhoid, smallpox, measles, TB etc.) that spreads from one patient to others.

Nursing an infectious patient

  • Isolation: separate airy room; children and elders keep away.
  • Separate utensils, towels, clothes; boil or disinfect and sun-dry.
  • Nurse wears apron/mask; washes hands with soap after every task; keeps nails cut.
  • Sputum, vomit, stool and urine treated with lime/bleaching powder/phenyl and buried deep or flushed; burn dressings.
  • Control flies and mosquitoes; cover food; boil drinking water.
  • After recovery: fumigate/whitewash the room, sun the bedding; mattress and pillows disinfected.
MethodExamples
PhysicalBoiling (about 20 minutes), sunlight, steam, burning
ChemicalPhenyl, Dettol, carbolic acid, bleaching powder, lime, spirit, formalin

Terms: Infection = entry of germs in the body; Disinfection = destroying germs on non-living things; Antiseptic = a substance on living tissue that stops germs (Dettol, spirit); Sterilisation = complete destruction of all germs.

Disposal of excreta

Use a bedpan (stool) and urinal (urine) for bed-ridden patients; after use cover, empty into the toilet, clean with disinfectant and sun-dry. Note colour and quantity on the chart.

13. Records and Emergencies

Patient's chart: date-time, temperature, pulse, respiration, medicines, food-fluids, stool-urine, sleep, special symptoms. It shows the doctor the progress and reduces mistakes.

🚨 Call the doctor immediately forunconsciousness or fits; fever above 103-104°F; difficulty in breathing/blue colour; heavy bleeding; severe abdominal pain or continuous vomiting/diarrhoea; chest pain; poisoning; very slow or very fast pulse.

Entertainment and visitors

  • Books, magazines, radio/music, light talk, meeting children; positive conversation.
  • Few visitors for a short time; none in infectious diseases; no noise.

14. The Home Nurse's Kit

Clinical thermometer, cotton, bandage, gauze, adhesive tape, Dettol/spirit, ointment, scissors, forceps, safety pins, ORS packets, hot-water bottle, ice bag, measuring glass/spoon, dropper, torch, bedpan-urinal, towels, apron-mask, doctor's phone number, medicines prescribed by the doctor.

16. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Note: this chapter is not calculation-heavy, so the examples below are written as Given / To find / Solution for nursing calculations and case studies.

Easy levelExample 1: °F to °CGiven: Patient's temperature 98.6°FTo find: Temperature in °CSolution:
  1. Formula: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
  2. 98.6 − 32 = 66.6
  3. 66.6 × 5 = 333
  4. 333 ÷ 9 = 37.0
Answer: 37°C (normal)
Easy levelExample 2: Converting a high feverGiven: Temperature 104°FTo find: Value in °CSolution:
  1. 104 − 32 = 72
  2. 72 × 5 = 360
  3. 360 ÷ 9 = 40
Answer: 40°C (high fever)
Easy levelExample 3: °C to °FGiven: Temperature 37°CTo find: Value in °FSolution:
  1. Formula: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
  2. 37 × 9 = 333
  3. 333 ÷ 5 = 66.6
  4. 66.6 + 32 = 98.6
Answer: 98.6°F
Easy levelExample 4: Pulse rateGiven: 18 beats in 15 secondsTo find: Pulse per minuteSolution:
  1. 1 minute = 60 s = 4 × 15 s
  2. Rate = 18 × 4 = 72
Answer: 72 beats/min (normal)
Easy levelExample 5: Respiration rateGiven: 9 breaths in 30 secondsTo find: Respiration per minuteSolution:
  1. 1 minute = 2 × 30 s
  2. 9 × 2 = 18
Answer: 18/min (normal)
Easy levelExample 6: Total medicineGiven: 2 teaspoons, 3 times a day, for 5 days; 1 teaspoon = 5 mlTo find: Total medicineSolution:
  1. One dose = 2 × 5 = 10 ml
  2. One day = 10 × 3 = 30 ml
  3. 5 days = 30 × 5 = 150 ml
Answer: 150 ml
Medium levelExample 7: Level of feverGiven: Temperature 101.6°F, normal 98.4°FTo find: (a) value in °C, (b) rise over normalSolution:
  1. (a) 101.6 − 32 = 69.6
  2. 69.6 × 5 = 348
  3. 348 ÷ 9 = 38.67 ≈ 38.7°C
  4. (b) 101.6 − 98.4 = 3.2°F
Answer: 38.7°C; 3.2°F above normal (moderate fever)
Medium levelExample 8: Pulse in feverGiven: Temperature 102.4°F, normal 98.4°F, normal pulse 72; pulse rises about 10 per 1°FTo find: Expected pulseSolution:
  1. Rise in temperature = 102.4 − 98.4 = 4°F
  2. Rise in pulse = 4 × 10 = 40
  3. Pulse = 72 + 40 = 112
Answer: about 112 beats/min
Medium levelExample 9: Pulse-respiration ratioGiven: Adult pulse 80/min; normal ratio 4:1To find: Expected respirationSolution:
  1. Pulse : respiration = 4 : 1
  2. Respiration = 80 ÷ 4 = 20
Answer: 20/min (within 16-20)
Medium levelExample 10: Medicine scheduleGiven: Tablet every 8 hours, first dose at 6 amTo find: Dose timesSolution:
  1. 6 am + 8 h = 2 pm
  2. 2 pm + 8 h = 10 pm
  3. 10 pm + 8 h = 6 am next day
Answer: 6 am, 2 pm, 10 pm
Medium levelExample 11: Number of turnsGiven: Patient laid down at 8 pm, turned every 2 hours until 6 amTo find: Number of turnsSolution:
  1. 8 pm to 6 am = 10 hours
  2. 10 ÷ 2 = 5
  3. Times: 10 pm, 12 am, 2 am, 4 am, 6 am
Answer: 5 turns
Medium levelExample 12: Quantity of ORSGiven: After each loose stool give 200 ml ORS (doctor's advice); 5 stools; 1 ORS packet = 1 litre solutionTo find: Total ORS and packetsSolution:
  1. Total = 5 × 200 = 1000 ml
  2. 1000 ml = 1 litre
  3. packets = 1
Answer: 1 litre; 1 packet
Medium levelExample 13: Mouth vs armpit temperatureGiven: Mouth temperature 99.8°F; armpit reading?To find: Armpit temperatureSolution:
  1. Armpit is about 1°F lower than mouth
  2. 99.8 − 1 = 98.8
Answer: about 98.8°F
Medium levelExample 14: Water for the hot bagGiven: Bag capacity 2 litres, fill half; how many 250 ml glasses?To find: Number of glassesSolution:
  1. Half = 2 ÷ 2 = 1 litre = 1000 ml
  2. 1000 ÷ 250 = 4
Answer: 4 glasses
Hard levelExample 15: 7-day mean temperature (Fig 8)Given: Temperatures: 99.2, 100.4, 102, 103, 101.2, 99.6, 98.4 (°F)To find: Mean temperature, in °C, and type of feverSolution:
  1. 99.2+100.4 = 199.6
  2. 199.6+102 = 301.6
  3. 301.6+103 = 404.6
  4. 404.6+101.2 = 505.8
  5. 505.8+99.6 = 605.4
  6. 605.4+98.4 = 703.8
  7. Mean = 703.8 ÷ 7 = 100.54°F
  8. °C = (100.54 − 32) × 5/9 = 68.54 × 5/9 = 38.08
  9. fever peaked on Day 4 (103°F) and then fell
Answer: Mean ≈ 100.5°F ≈ 38.1°C; peak 103°F on Day 4; normal by Day 7
Hard levelExample 16: How long will the syrup last?Given: Bottle 100 ml; dose 2.5 ml (½ teaspoon), 3 times a dayTo find: Number of daysSolution:
  1. One day = 2.5 × 3 = 7.5 ml
  2. Days = 100 ÷ 7.5 = 13.33
  3. 13 days use 13 × 7.5 = 97.5 ml
  4. Left = 100 − 97.5 = 2.5 ml = one more dose
Answer: 13 full days (+1 extra dose)
Hard levelExample 17: Home-made ORSGiven: 1 litre water with 6 level teaspoons sugar and ½ teaspoon salt; 1 teaspoon sugar = 5 gTo find: Ingredients for 500 ml, and grams of sugar per litreSolution:
  1. 500 ml = half of 1 litre
  2. Sugar = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 teaspoons
  3. Salt = ½ ÷ 2 = ¼ teaspoon
  4. Sugar per litre = 6 × 5 = 30 g
Answer: 3 tsp sugar + ¼ tsp salt; 30 g sugar per litre
Hard levelExample 18: Case study: 104°F feverGiven: Temperature 104°F; doctor can reach in 1 hourTo find: Nurse's stepsSolution:
  1. °C = (104 − 32) × 5/9 = 40°C (high)
  2. Airy room, remove heavy covers
  3. Cold compress on forehead, sponge with lukewarm water
  4. Check temperature every 10-15 minutes; stop sponging near 100°F
  5. Give water/ORS, keep a chart
  6. Phone the doctor at once and report
Answer: Cold sponging + fluids + chart + call doctor immediately
Hard levelExample 19: Pulse-respiration analysisGiven: Adult pulse 96, respiration 24To find: Ratio and conclusionSolution:
  1. Ratio = 96 : 24 = 4 : 1 (normal)
  2. But pulse (60-80) and respiration (16-20) are both above normal
  3. Possible causes: fever, infection, anxiety, exertion
Answer: Ratio normal but both rates high - take temperature and inform the doctor
Hard levelExample 20: Case study: child with diarrhoeaGiven: 5-year-old child, loose motions, weakness, dry mouthTo find: Nursing planSolution:
  1. Risk of dehydration: ORS by spoonfuls after each stool
  2. Easy food: curd-rice, khichdi, banana, rice water
  3. Boiled/filtered water, wash hands before feeding
  4. Dispose stool hygienically; boil child's clothes and utensils separately
  5. Chart: number of stools, urine, temperature
  6. If no improvement, blood in stool or drowsiness - doctor at once
Answer: ORS + light diet + hygiene + chart + doctor if needed

Ans:Crease-free tight sheets; mackintosh and draw sheet correctly placed; never shake sheets in air; clean and dry linen; enough pillows; right bed height.
How can bed sores be prevented?
Ans:Turn every 2 hours, clean and massage pressure areas, keep bed dry and smooth, air ring, nourishing diet and water, show the doctor if a sore forms.
State the principles of a patient's diet.
Ans:As the doctor advises; easily digestible and nutritious; small amounts often; fresh, clean and attractive; respect likes; regular time; plenty of safe water.
What precautions are to be taken while using a thermometer?
Ans:Clean with cold water/spirit, shake below 95°F, under tongue 2-3 minutes, armpit for children/unconscious, never use hot water, do not bite, do not touch broken mercury.
How do you take the pulse?
Ans:Seat the patient at rest, place three fingers on the radial artery at the wrist, count for 1 minute with a watch, note rhythm and strength and record.
Write the precautions for giving medicine.
Ans:Read the label, check expiry, measure the right dose, give on time, shake liquids, wash hands, keep medicines away from children, never change medicine without the doctor.
Explain how to use a hot-water bag.
Ans:Check for leaks, fill half with bearable hot water, press out air, tighten the stopper, put on a cover, apply and watch the skin, change water when cool.
Explain the use of an ice bag and cold compress.
Ans:Fill an ice bag half with small ice pieces, press out air, close, wrap in cloth and place on head/injury; for a compress, soak cloth in cold water and place on the forehead, changing often.
Describe steam inhalation.
Ans:Hot water with ajwain/eucalyptus in a vessel on a steady table, patient bends over with a towel on the head and inhales for 5-10 minutes; guard against burns and spills.
Describe a sponge bath.
Ans:Use lukewarm water; cover the patient with a sheet; wash, dry and cover one part at a time (face, neck, arms, chest, abdomen, back, legs); back massage; change clothes.
How will you nurse an infectious patient?
Ans:Separate room, separate utensils and clothes, wash hands, boil clothes, disinfect excreta, control flies and mosquitoes, no visitors, disinfect the room after recovery.
List ways to entertain a patient.
Ans:Books and magazines, radio/music, light conversation, meeting family and children, looking outside; they divert the mind from illness.
What rules should be followed for visitors?
Ans:Few people, short time; none in infectious illness; no noise or argument; no bad news; at proper hours.
Distinguish between a home nurse and a hospital nurse.
Ans:Home nurse: family member, limited resources, emotional bond; hospital nurse: trained paid professional, modern equipment, many patients. (See table in Section 2.)
How is excreta disposed of?
Ans:Use bedpan/urinal, cover and empty in the toilet, clean with disinfectant and sun-dry; bury deep with lime in infectious disease.
What is a patient's chart and what is written in it?
Ans:A daily record: date-time, temperature, pulse, respiration, medicine, diet, stool-urine, sleep and special symptoms.
When should the doctor be called at once?
Ans:Unconsciousness, fits, very high fever, breathing trouble, heavy bleeding, severe pain, continuous vomiting/diarrhoea, poisoning.

Part C: Long answer questions (12)

Describe in detail the duties of a home nurse.
Ans:(1) Keep room and bed clean and airy. (2) Give medicine on time in the right dose. (3) Prepare and serve prescribed food. (4) Observe and chart temperature, pulse, respiration. (5) Personal hygiene, sponge bath, change clothes. (6) Turn the patient to prevent bed sores. (7) Prevent infection: wash hands, separate utensils, safe disposal of excreta. (8) Cheer the patient, control visitors. (9) Call the doctor in emergencies and give first care. (10) Look after her own health.
Describe an ideal sick-room (with figure).
Ans:In a quiet part of the house with windows and ventilators for air and sunlight, toilet nearby. Light walls, smooth washable floor. Furniture: bed, table, chair, cupboard, covered dustbin. Water, glass, bell, torch, bedpan at hand. Clean with a damp cloth; no noise or crowd; pleasant decoration. (Draw Fig 1.)
Describe the types and method of bed-making.
Ans:Three types: (1) closed bed - before the patient comes; (2) open bed - top sheet folded back; (3) occupied bed - patient lying; sheet changed by rolling the patient to each side. Layers: mattress, mackintosh, bottom sheet, draw sheet, top sheet, blanket, pillow.
Describe the patient's diet.
Ans:Definition; types (liquid, soft, light solid, full); principles (digestible, small and frequent, fresh, liked); disease-wise diet (fever - liquids, diarrhoea - ORS, jaundice - sugarcane juice, diabetes - sugar-free); feeding method (seated, napkin, feeding cup); precautions.
Describe temperature, pulse and respiration.
Ans:Temperature: 98.4°F, thermometer, method (mouth/armpit), precautions. Pulse: 72, radial artery, three fingers, 1 minute. Respiration: 18, by chest movement, patient unaware. Ratio 4:1; chart; all three rise in fever.
Explain the method and precautions for giving medicine.
Ans:Read the label, use measuring vessels (1 tsp = 5 ml), shake, check expiry, follow timing, wash hands, keep out of children's reach, stop on allergy, note on chart; correct use of drops and ointments.
Write the kinds of hot/cold applications and explain the hot-water bag.
Ans:Kinds: dry heat (sand/salt bag, bottle), moist heat (fomentation), steam, cold (ice bag, compress, sponging). Hot bottle: check, half water, press out air, cover, watch skin. Uses: pain, stiffness, cold.
Describe care of an infectious patient and methods of disinfection.
Ans:Isolation, separate utensils-clothes, apron-mask and hand-washing, boiling clothes, chemical disposal of sputum-excreta, control flies and mosquitoes. Disinfection: boiling, sun, steam, burning; phenyl, Dettol, lime, bleaching powder, formalin.
Write causes, signs and prevention of bed sores.
Ans:Causes: continuous pressure, moisture, creased bed, poor nutrition. Signs: red skin, then blisters and wounds. Prevention: turn every 2 hours, wash-massage, air ring, dry smooth bed, nourishing diet, consult the doctor.
Explain the need and method of sponge bath.
Ans:Needed when a patient cannot bathe (fever, weakness, surgery); it cleans, removes sweat, improves circulation and refreshes. Materials and steps: cover with sheet, wash-dry-cover each part, back massage, clean clothes.
Write an essay on the importance of home nursing.
Ans:Illness is part of life; hospitals are not accessible to all; home care is cheaper, convenient and emotional; helps convalescence, elders, children and long illnesses; a trained nurse makes recovery faster and controls infection; hence every homemaker should know nursing.
What should a home nurse's kit contain?
Ans:Thermometer, cotton-bandage-gauze, Dettol/spirit, ointment, scissors-forceps, safety pins, ORS, hot bottle, ice bag, measuring glass, dropper, torch, bedpan, towels, mask-apron, doctor's number.
