Class 9 Social Science – Physical Features of India

Contemporary India–I (Geography) · UP Board · Notes with diagrams, Q&A and MCQs

1. Introduction

India has a great variety of relief: mountains, plains, plateaus, deserts, coasts and islands. These were shaped by endogenic (inner earth) and exogenic (weathering, erosion, deposition) forces. According to the theory of Plate Tectonics, the Indian plate moved north and collided with the Eurasian plate. The sediments between them folded up into the Himalayas.

Remember: India is divided into 6 physical divisions – 1 Himalayas, 2 Northern Plains, 3 Peninsular Plateau, 4 Indian Desert, 5 Coastal Plains, 6 Islands.

2. The Himalayan Mountains

  • Young fold mountains, about 2,400 km long, arc-shaped, from the Indus to the Brahmaputra.
  • Width: 400 km in Kashmir, 150 km in Arunachal Pradesh. Eastern part is higher than the western.

Three parallel ranges (north → south)

  • Himadri (Greater Himalaya): highest, average height 6,000 m, always snow-covered; has glaciers (Gangotri, Yamunotri). Peaks: Kanchenjunga (8,598 m, highest in India), Nanda Devi, Kamet.
  • Himachal (Lesser Himalaya): 3,700–4,500 m; ranges Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mussoorie; hill stations Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling; Kashmir and Kullu valleys.
  • Shiwalik (Outer Himalaya): 900–1,100 m; made of loose sediments; broad longitudinal valleys called Duns (Dehradun, Patli Dun).

Purvanchal (Eastern hills)

Beyond the Dihang gorge: Patkai, Naga, Manipur and Mizo hills – mostly dense forest.

3. The Northern Plains

  • Formed by alluvium deposited by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra. About 7 lakh sq km, 2,400 km long, 240–320 km wide. Very fertile and densely populated.
  • Parts: Punjab plain (Indus), Ganga plain (Ghaggar to Teesta), Brahmaputra plain (Assam).

Four zones by relief

  • Bhabar: 8–16 km wide strip of pebbles at the foot of the Shiwalik; streams disappear here.
  • Terai: south of Bhabar; streams re-emerge, marshy, forests and wildlife.
  • Bhangar: older alluvium, above flood level, with kankar nodules.
  • Khadar: newer alluvium, renewed by floods every year, most fertile.

4. The Peninsular Plateau

  • Oldest landmass (part of Gondwanaland); hard crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks; broad, shallow valleys, rounded hills.
  • Central Highlands: north of Narmada – Malwa plateau, Aravali, Vindhya range, Chotanagpur plateau (Jharkhand, rich in minerals).
  • Deccan Plateau: triangular, south of Narmada. Deccan Trap is black volcanic rock giving black soil.
  • Western Ghats (Sahyadri): continuous, higher, orographic rain; Anai Mudi (2,695 m) is the highest peak of peninsular India. Eastern Ghats: discontinuous, lower, cut by rivers; Mahendragiri (1,501 m).

5. The Indian Desert

West of the Aravali; sand dunes, rainfall below 150 mm a year; barchans (crescent dunes) are common; Luni is the only large river; the region is called the Thar.

6. The Coastal Plains

  • Western coast: narrow; Konkan (Mumbai–Goa), Kannad, Malabar (Kerala; lagoons/backwaters).
  • Eastern coast: wide; Northern Circar and Coromandel; deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri; Chilika Lake (Odisha).

7. The Islands

  • Lakshadweep: coral origin, about 36 islands, Kavaratti is the capital.
  • Andaman & Nicobar: in the Bay of Bengal, mostly volcanic; Barren Island (active volcano); Indira Point is the southernmost point.

8. Short Q&A (tap to see answer)

Q1. Which plate collided with the Indian plate?

The Eurasian plate. The sediments between folded to form the Himalayas.

Q2. What are Duns?

Longitudinal valleys between the Himachal and Shiwalik ranges, e.g. Dehradun.

Q3. What is Bhabar?

A narrow pebbly belt at the foot of the Shiwalik where streams sink and disappear.

Q4. Difference between Bhangar and Khadar?

Bhangar is old alluvium with kankar, above flood level. Khadar is new, fertile alluvium deposited by yearly floods.

Q5. Name the highest peak of India.

Kanchenjunga (8,598 m).

Q6. Which Ghats are discontinuous?

The Eastern Ghats.

Q7. What are barchans?

Crescent-shaped sand dunes of the Thar desert.

Q8. Name the capital of Lakshadweep.

Kavaratti.

9. Long Q&A

L1. Describe the three ranges of the Himalayas.

Himadri is the northernmost, highest (avg. 6,000 m), snow-covered, with glaciers and peaks like Kanchenjunga. Himachal lies south of it, 3,700–4,500 m, with Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar and Mussoorie ranges and hill stations. Shiwalik is the outermost, 900–1,100 m, of loose sediments, with Duns in between. Together they run parallel for about 2,400 km.

L2. Describe the Northern Plains and their divisions.

They are alluvial plains made by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra, about 7 lakh sq km, very fertile and densely populated. By region: Punjab, Ganga and Brahmaputra plains. By relief: Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar and Khadar, running from the Himalayan foothills southward.

L3. Compare the Western and Eastern Ghats.

Western Ghats are continuous, higher (Anai Mudi 2,695 m), and cross only through passes; they cause heavy rainfall on the windward side. Eastern Ghats are discontinuous, lower (Mahendragiri 1,501 m) and cut by rivers like Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

L4. How are the two island groups of India different?

Lakshadweep is in the Arabian Sea and made of coral; Andaman & Nicobar is in the Bay of Bengal and largely volcanic, with a larger area and higher hills.

10. MCQs (tap to see answer)

1. The highest peak in India is: (a) Everest (b) K2 (c) Kanchenjunga (d) Nanda Devi

(c) Kanchenjunga.

2. Shiwalik range is also called: (a) Inner (b) Outer (c) Greater (d) Trans Himalaya

(b) Outer Himalaya.

3. The most fertile plain soil is: (a) Bhabar (b) Terai (c) Bhangar (d) Khadar

(d) Khadar.

4. Deccan Trap gives rise to: (a) red soil (b) black soil (c) alluvial (d) laterite

(b) black soil.

5. Chilika Lake is in: (a) Kerala (b) Odisha (c) Gujarat (d) Tamil Nadu

(b) Odisha.

6. Southernmost point of India: (a) Kanyakumari (b) Indira Point (c) Rameswaram (d) Minicoy

(b) Indira Point.
