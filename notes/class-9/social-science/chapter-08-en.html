Class 9 Social Science – Drainage

Contemporary India–I (Geography) · UP Board · Notes with diagrams, Q&A and MCQs

1. Introduction

Drainage means the river system of an area. A river with its tributaries is a drainage system. The area drained by one river system is its drainage basin. An upland (like a mountain) separating two basins is a water divide.

Remember: Indian rivers are of two groups – Himalayan (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) and Peninsular (Narmada, Tapti, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi). Western Ghats is the main water divide of the peninsula.

2. Himalayan vs Peninsular Rivers

  • Himalayan: perennial (fed by rain and snowmelt), long courses, deep gorges, V-shaped valleys, meanders, oxbow lakes; large deltas; floods are common.
  • Peninsular: seasonal (rain-fed), shorter and shallower courses, older, graded valleys. Narmada and Tapti flow west through rift valleys, forming estuaries; others make deltas on the east.

3. The Indus System

  • Rises near Mansarovar lake in Tibet; about 2,900 km long (1,114 km in India); flows through Ladakh, Jammu & Kashmir, then Pakistan to the Arabian Sea near Karachi.
  • Tributaries: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj.

4. The Ganga System

  • Rises as Bhagirathi from the Gangotri glacier; meets Alaknanda at Devprayag to form the Ganga; enters the plains at Haridwar.
  • Left tributaries: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi. Right: Yamuna (from Yamunotri; meets at Prayagraj), Chambal, Betwa, Son.
  • Length 2,525 km (UP 1,450 km, Bihar 445 km, West Bengal 520 km). At Farakka it splits: Bhagirathi-Hooghly in India; main stream to Bangladesh as Padma, which meets the Brahmaputra and the Meghna.
  • The Ganga–Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans) is the world’s largest delta. Ambala lies on the water divide between the Indus and Ganga systems.

5. The Brahmaputra System

  • Rises from the Chemayungdung glacier in Tibet, where it is the Tsangpo; about 2,900 km long. Enters India in Arunachal Pradesh as the Dihang/Siang, becomes the Brahmaputra in Assam.
  • Carries huge silt, so the bed rises and floods are frequent. Majuli is the world’s largest river island.

6. Peninsular Rivers

  • Narmada: from Amarkantak, 1,312 km, flows west; Dhuandhar falls, Marble Rocks (Jabalpur).
  • Tapti: Satpura (Betul), 724 km, flows west.
  • Godavari: from Nasik hills, 1,465 km, the longest peninsular river, called Dakshin Ganga.
  • Krishna: from Mahabaleshwar, 1,401 km. Kaveri: from Brahmagiri, Shivasamudram falls. Mahanadi: Chhattisgarh, flows to Odisha.

7. Drainage Patterns

Dendritic – like branches of a tree (Ganga plain). Trellis – tributaries join at right angles to parallel main rivers. Rectangular – rivers turn at right angles along rock joints. Radial – rivers flow outward from a central high point (Amarkantak).

8. Lakes

  • Freshwater: Wular (largest freshwater lake in India, Jhelum), Dal, Nainital, Bhimtal, Loktak.
  • Salt water: Sambhar (Rajasthan), Chilika (lagoon, Odisha), Pulicat.
  • Types: lagoons, glacial, oxbow, man-made (Hussain Sagar).

9. Role of Rivers and Pollution

Rivers give water for irrigation, drinking, navigation, hydropower, fisheries and tourism; they also support cities and civilisation. Rising population, industry and sewage pollute rivers. Efforts: Ganga Action Plan (1985), National River Conservation Plan (1995), Namami Gange (2014).

10. Short Q&A (tap to see answer)

Q1. What is a drainage basin?

The area drained by a river and its tributaries.

Q2. What is a water divide?

An upland, such as a mountain, separating two drainage basins.

At Devprayag, forming the Ganga.

Godavari.

Majuli in the Brahmaputra, Assam.

Wular lake.

Narmada and Tapti.

Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Satluj.

11. Long Q&A

L1. Describe the Ganga river system.

The Ganga rises as the Bhagirathi from Gangotri, joins the Alaknanda at Devprayag and enters the plains at Haridwar. Its tributaries include the Yamuna, Chambal, Betwa and Son from the right and Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak and Kosi from the left. It is 2,525 km long. At Farakka it splits, the main stream going to Bangladesh as Padma, and it forms the Sundarbans delta.

L2. Differentiate Himalayan and Peninsular rivers.

Himalayan rivers are perennial, longer, have deep gorges, meanders and big deltas, and cause floods. Peninsular rivers are seasonal, shorter and shallower with fixed courses; Narmada and Tapti make estuaries.

L3. Describe the Brahmaputra.

It rises in Tibet as the Tsangpo, enters Arunachal as the Dihang, flows through Assam carrying heavy silt which raises its bed, causing floods and braided channels; Majuli is the large river island.

L4. Why are rivers important to us? How are they polluted?

Rivers provide irrigation, drinking water, power, transport and fish. Sewage, industrial waste and religious offerings pollute them, so plans like Namami Gange were begun.
