Class 9 Social Science – What Is Democracy? Why Democracy?

Democratic Politics–I (Civics) · UP Board · Notes with diagrams, Q&A and MCQs

1. What is Democracy?

The word comes from Greek demos (people) + kratia (rule). Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people. Abraham Lincoln: “government of the people, by the people, for the people”. Every government can claim to be democratic, so we need exact features.

Remember: A democracy has 4 features – elected rulers take the final decisions; free and fair elections with a chance to change the government; one person one vote one value; and rule of law with respect for rights.

2. Examples to Understand

  • Chile (1973): The elected president Salvador Allende was removed in a military coup led by General Pinochet. He ruled as a dictator and opponents were jailed or killed. In a 1988 referendum people voted against him; democracy returned in 1990. Lesson: military rule is not democracy.
  • Poland (1980s): Rulers called themselves elected, but real power lay with General Jaruzelski and one party; the workers’ union Solidarity challenged them. Lesson: elected rulers must hold real power.
  • Saudi Arabia: hereditary monarchy; people do not choose their ruler. Mexico: elections were held every six years but the PRI always won through pressure and malpractice until 2000. Lesson: elections must be free and fair.
  • Zimbabwe: elections were held, but the ruling party (ZANU-PF) used the state, press and courts against opponents, and rights were limited. Lesson: rule of law and rights matter.
  • China: elections to the National People’s Congress allow only members of the Communist Party and its allies to contest. Estonia: language rules make it hard for the Russian minority to get citizenship. Fiji: the election system gave less weight to the vote of Indian-origin citizens. Lesson: one person, one vote, one value.

3. Features of Democracy

Fig 2: Four pillars of democracy (animated)
  • 1. Final decision by elected rulers: the army, a monarch or a foreign power cannot take the final decisions.
  • 2. Free and fair elections: a real choice and fair chance to change the rulers, regularly; all can contest and vote without fear.
  • 3. One person, one vote, one value: universal adult franchise – each adult has one vote of equal value, irrespective of caste, religion, gender or wealth.
  • 4. Rule of law and respect for rights: the government works within a constitution, citizens enjoy rights and freedom of speech, assembly and association; courts are free.

4. Types of Democracy

Direct democracy – people take decisions themselves (small groups, ancient Greek city-states). Representative democracy – people elect representatives who decide for them; used in India since a country is too big for everyone to meet.

5. Arguments Against Democracy

  • Leaders keep changing, causing instability.
  • It is about political competition and power play, not moral leadership.
  • Too many people must be consulted, so delays.
  • Electoral competition leads to corruption.
  • Ordinary people may not know what is good, so bad decisions.

6. Arguments For Democracy

  • Improves quality of decision-making through consultation and discussion.
  • Provides a method to deal with differences and conflicts peacefully.
  • Enhances the dignity of citizens: all are equal.
  • Allows the people to correct their own mistakes, by voting out a government.

Democracy may be slow, but accountability, equality and freedom make it better than other forms such as monarchy or dictatorship.

7. Broader Meanings

Democracy is not only a government but also a way of life: in families, schools, groups and organisations decisions can be taken by discussion, respecting everyone’s opinion and rights. It is an ideal of equality; as a value it is open to improvement.

8. Short Q&A (tap to see answer)

Q1. Define democracy.

A form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people.

Q2. What does the word democracy mean?

Greek demos (people) + kratia (rule): rule by the people.

Q3. What is universal adult franchise?

The right of every adult citizen to vote, with each vote of equal value.

Q4. Who led the 1973 coup in Chile?

General Augusto Pinochet.

Q5. Name the two kinds of democracy.

Direct and representative democracy.

Q6. Which is a feature of democracy: army taking decisions?

No. Final decisions must be taken by elected rulers.

Q7. Give one argument against democracy.

It leads to delays, because many people must be consulted.

Q8. Why can democracy correct mistakes?

Because people can vote out a government that fails.

9. Long Q&A

L1. Explain the main features of democracy.

Final decisions are taken by elected rulers, not by army or monarch. Elections are free and fair, giving a real choice and chance to change the government. Every adult has one vote of equal value. The government is limited by a constitution and rule of law, with rights such as freedom of speech and association protected by independent courts.

L2. How does the case of Chile show the importance of elected rulers?

In 1973 the elected government was overthrown by General Pinochet, who ended freedoms and ruled as dictator until a 1988 vote started the return of democracy in 1990. It shows that power in a democracy must rest with elected leaders.

L3. Give arguments for and against democracy.

Against: instability, power play, delay, corruption and uninformed voters. For: better decisions, peaceful handling of conflict, dignity and equality of citizens, and the ability to correct mistakes. On balance, accountability and equality make democracy the preferred system.

L4. What is the difference between direct and representative democracy?

In direct democracy people decide matters themselves, possible in small communities. In representative democracy citizens elect representatives who take decisions; it is practical for large countries like India.

L5. Why is democracy called an ideal and a way of life?

It aims at equality, dignity and participation not just in government but in family, school and workplace, and can always be improved.

10. MCQs (tap to see answer)

1. Democracy comes from: (a) Latin (b) Greek (c) French (d) Arabic

(b) Greek.

2. In Chile, the dictator was: (a) Allende (b) Pinochet (c) Jaruzelski (d) Mugabe

(b) Pinochet.

3. ‘One person, one vote, one value’ is: (a) rule of law (b) universal adult franchise (c) direct democracy (d) dictatorship

(b) universal adult franchise.

4. Which is an argument for democracy? (a) delays (b) corruption (c) dignity of citizens (d) instability

(c) enhances dignity.

5. The Polish workers’ union: (a) Solidarity (b) PRI (c) ZANU (d) NPC

(a) Solidarity.

6. India has: (a) direct (b) representative (c) no democracy (d) monarchy

(b) representative democracy.

7. In Mexico, which party won every election till 2000? (a) PRI (b) ZANU-PF (c) NPC (d) Solidarity

(a) PRI.

8. A basic feature of democracy is: (a) hereditary ruler (b) free and fair elections (c) army rule (d) one-party rule

(b) free and fair elections.
