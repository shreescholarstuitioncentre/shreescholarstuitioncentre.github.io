Class 9 Social Science – Climate

Contemporary India–I (Geography) · UP Board · Notes with diagrams, Q&A and MCQs

1. Introduction

Weather is the state of the atmosphere (temperature, pressure, wind, humidity, rain) at a place for a short time. Climate is the average weather over a long period (about 30+ years). India has a monsoon type climate (word from Arabic mausim, meaning season), found in South and Southeast Asia.

Remember: Six controls of India’s climate – latitude, altitude, pressure and winds, distance from the sea, ocean currents, relief.

2. Factors Affecting Climate

  • Latitude: the Tropic of Cancer passes through the middle (Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram). South is tropical (hot), north is subtropical/temperate.
  • Altitude: temperature falls about 6.5°C per 1,000 m; so mountains are cooler than plains (Agra vs Darjeeling).
  • Pressure and winds: depend on latitude and altitude; the Himalayas block cold Central Asian winds.
  • Distance from sea: coasts have moderate (maritime) climate, e.g. Mumbai; interior has extreme (continental) climate, e.g. Delhi.
  • Ocean currents and relief: windward slopes get rain, leeward slopes are dry (rain shadow – Mumbai heavy, Pune less).

3. Mechanism of the Monsoon

  • In summer, land heats faster than sea, so a low pressure forms over north-west India and the ITCZ shifts north. Moist south-east trade winds cross the equator, turn right (Coriolis) and become south-west monsoon.
  • High heating of the Tibetan plateau and the shift of the jet stream also help. El Niño (warm Pacific current) can weaken the monsoon.

4. The Four Seasons

Fig 2: Seasons of India (animated)

Cold weather (Dec–Feb)

Sun is south of the equator; north is cold (Delhi 10–15°C) with high pressure and dry NE winds. Western disturbances bring winter rain to the north-west, good for rabi crops like wheat. Tamil Nadu coast gets rain from the retreating NE monsoon.

Hot weather (Mar–May)

Temperature rises; loo (hot dry winds) blow over the north-west plains. Local storms: mango showers (Kerala, Karnataka), Kaal Baisakhi/Norwesters (Bengal), Bardoli Chheerha (Assam).

Advancing monsoon (Jun–Sep)

Onset at Kerala about 1 June; the sudden rain is called the burst. Covers India by mid-July. Windward Western Ghats and Meghalaya get very heavy rain (Mawsynram – among the wettest places). Breaks in monsoon give dry spells.

Retreating monsoon (Oct–Nov)

Monsoon withdraws; clear skies, hot and humid “October heat”. Cyclones form in the Bay of Bengal and hit the eastern coast.

5. Distribution of Rainfall

  • Heavy (over 200 cm): Western coast, Western Ghats, North-east, Meghalaya.
  • Moderate (100–200 cm): southern Gujarat, eastern TN, NE peninsula, Ganga plain.
  • Low (60–100 cm): UP, Delhi, Haryana, Deccan interior.
  • Very low (below 60 cm): west Rajasthan, Gujarat parts, Ladakh.
  • Rainfall is highly variable and uneven; floods and droughts occur.

6. Monsoon as a Unifying Bond

The monsoon controls the farming year and unites people of the subcontinent; Indian agriculture is called a “gamble in the monsoon”. Rivers, water supply, hydropower and festivals depend on it.

7. Short Q&A (tap to see answer)

Q1. Define weather.

Atmospheric condition of a place at a given time.

Q2. Define climate.

Average weather conditions over a long period.

Q3. What are loo?

Hot dry winds blowing over the north-west plains in summer.

Q4. What are western disturbances?

Cyclonic disturbances from the Mediterranean that bring winter rain to north-west India.

Q5. What is monsoon burst?

Sudden arrival of heavy rain with thunder and lightning at the onset of the monsoon.

Q6. Which place has the highest rainfall?

Mawsynram in Meghalaya.

Q7. What are mango showers?

Pre-monsoon showers in Kerala and Karnataka helping mango ripening.

Q8. Which line divides India into two thermal zones?

The Tropic of Cancer.

8. Long Q&A

L1. Explain the factors that affect India’s climate.

Latitude (Tropic of Cancer divides tropical and subtropical zones), altitude (6.5°C fall per 1,000 m), pressure and wind systems, distance from the sea (maritime vs continental), ocean currents and relief (rain shadow). The Himalayas block cold northern winds and trap monsoon rain.

L2. Explain the mechanism of the monsoon.

Differential heating of land and sea creates low pressure over north-west India in summer; the ITCZ moves north; trade winds cross the equator and turn right to become the south-west monsoon, splitting into Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches. Tibetan plateau heating, jet stream shifts and El Niño affect it.

L3. Describe the season of advancing monsoon.

It lasts June to September. Rain begins in Kerala around 1 June with a burst, reaches Delhi by end June and covers the country by mid-July. Western Ghats and the North-east get heavy rain, while interiors get less; breaks cause dry spells.

L4. Why is the monsoon called a unifying bond?

It gives a common seasonal rhythm to agriculture and life across the subcontinent, though regions differ in temperature and rain.
