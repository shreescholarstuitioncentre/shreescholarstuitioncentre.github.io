Class 9 Social Science – Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

Contemporary India–I (Geography) · UP Board · Notes with diagrams, Q&A and MCQs

1. Introduction

India is one of the 12 mega bio-diversity countries. It has about 47,000 plant species and roughly 90,000 animal species. Flora means plants of a region; fauna means its animals. Natural vegetation is a plant community that grew naturally without human help.

Remember: Five vegetation types – 1 Tropical Evergreen, 2 Tropical Deciduous, 3 Thorn forests and scrubs, 4 Montane, 5 Mangrove (tidal).

2. Factors Affecting Vegetation

  • Relief – land: fertile land supports agriculture; rough, uneven land supports forests and grassland. Soil: sandy soil of desert has cacti and thorny bushes; wet marshy delta soil has mangroves; hill slopes have conical trees.
  • Climate – temperature: altitude and latitude change vegetation. Photoperiod (sunlight): more sunlight in summer, trees grow taller. Precipitation: more rain means dense forests.

3. Types of Forests

(a) Tropical Evergreen Forests

Rainfall above 200 cm, short dry season. Trees never shed leaves together, so it is always green; dense, tall (60 m+). Found in Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar, upper Assam, Tamil Nadu coast. Trees: ebony, mahogany, rosewood, rubber, cinchona. Animals: elephant, monkey, lemur, deer, one-horned rhino (Assam, West Bengal).

(b) Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon) Forests

Most widespread forests; trees shed leaves in dry summer to save water. Moist deciduous (rain 100–200 cm): teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood, khair – eastern Maharashtra, Odisha, Jharkhand. Dry deciduous (70–100 cm): teak, sal, peepal, neem – Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, plateau.

(c) Thorn Forests and Scrubs

Rainfall below 70 cm; north-west India (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh, Deccan). Trees scattered, long roots, thick leaves; acacia, babool, khejri, cacti. Animals: rats, mice, rabbits, camel, fox, wild ass, horse.

(d) Montane Forests

Temperature falls with height, so vegetation changes in layers. Wet temperate (1,000–2,000 m): oak, chestnut. Coniferous (1,500–3,000 m): pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, cedar. Alpine (above 3,600 m): silver fir, juniper, birch. Mosses and lichens form tundra near snow line. Animals: Kashmir stag, spotted deer, wild sheep, yak, snow leopard, Tibetan antelope, red panda.

(e) Mangrove (Tidal) Forests

In river deltas and coasts flooded by tides; roots rise out of water (pneumatophores). Sundari tree of Ganga-Brahmaputra delta; also Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri deltas. Animals: Royal Bengal tiger, turtles, crocodiles, gharials, snakes.

4. Wildlife

India has about 2,000 species of birds, thousands of fish species, mammals, reptiles, amphibians. Elephant (Assam, Karnataka, Kerala), one-horned rhino (Assam), camel (Thar), wild ass (Rann of Kachchh), Asiatic lion (Gir forest, Gujarat), tiger (Madhya Pradesh, Sundarbans, Himalayan foothills), snow leopard (Ladakh), gharial (rivers). Rising population, hunting, deforestation and pollution reduce wildlife.

5. Conservation

  • Wildlife Protection Act 1972; Project Tiger (1973); Project Elephant (1992).
  • National parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves (e.g. Nilgiri, Sundarbans, Nanda Devi, Gulf of Mannar). Examples: Corbett (Uttarakhand), Kaziranga (Assam), Gir (Gujarat), Keoladeo/Bharatpur (birds).
  • Public awareness, afforestation and ban on poaching help.

7. Long Q&A

L1. What factors affect natural vegetation?

Relief (land and soil) decides whether the area has forests, grassland or cultivation; sandy, marshy or hilly soils each support different plants. Climate matters through temperature (altitude and latitude), sunlight duration and rainfall; more rain gives denser forests.

L2. Describe tropical deciduous forests.

These are monsoon forests found where rainfall is 70–200 cm. Trees shed leaves in dry summer. They are of two types: moist deciduous (teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood) and dry deciduous (teak, sal, peepal, neem). They cover most of the peninsula and plains and give valuable timber.

L3. Describe montane vegetation.

Because temperature falls with altitude, Himalayan vegetation changes in layers: deciduous below 1,000 m, wet temperate oak and chestnut, then conifers like pine and deodar, alpine meadows above 3,600 m and tundra with mosses and lichens near the snow line.

L4. Why do we need to conserve wildlife and how?

Forests and animals keep ecological balance, give timber, medicine and tourism. Conservation uses laws like the Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Project Tiger, national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves, and awareness against poaching and deforestation.
