✎ Food Security in India — Class 9 Economics (UP Board)

1. What is Food Security?

Food security means availability, accessibility and affordability of food to all people at all times. Food is available when enough is produced/stored; accessible when every person can get it; affordable when a person has enough money to buy nutritious food.

Food security is ensured only when all three dimensions are satisfied.

2. Why Food Security?

Calamities like drought, flood, earthquake, crop failure reduce production and push prices up, causing hunger. Prolonged shortage may cause famine (e.g. Bengal famine, 1943, about 30 lakh deaths).

3. Who is Food Insecure?

  • Landless people, traditional artisans, petty self-employed, destitute (beggars).
  • Migrant workers hit by natural disasters; SC, ST and some OBC groups.
  • Women (esp. pregnant and nursing) and children under 5.
  • Poorest states: UP (east), Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, MP.

4. Hunger

  • Chronic: consistently inadequate diet; due to low income.
  • Seasonal: related to farming cycle; e.g. rural areas between sowing and harvest.

5. Green Revolution

India achieved self-sufficiency in food grains after the Green Revolution (late 1960s) using HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation — mainly in wheat and rice in Punjab, Haryana, western UP. Yet regional gaps and poor access remain.

6. Buffer Stock

Stock of wheat and rice procured by Food Corporation of India (FCI) (set up 1965) from surplus states at pre-announced Minimum Support Price (MSP). Kept in godowns to meet shortage and distribute at issue price, which is lower than market price.

7. Public Distribution System (PDS)

Foodgrains distributed through ration shops (fair price shops). Items: wheat, rice, sugar, kerosene. Ration card types: Antyodaya (AAY), BPL, APL. Problems: poor quality grain, dealers' malpractice, leakage, shops often shut.

8. Government Schemes

  • AAY (2000): 35 kg subsidised grain to poorest of the poor families.
  • Annapurna: free 10 kg grain to indigent senior citizens.
  • Mid-Day Meal: cooked meal in schools; improves attendance.
  • ICDS: nutrition and health for children and mothers.
  • NFSA 2013: legal right to subsidised grain: 75% rural, 50% urban population; 5 kg per person monthly.

9. Role of Cooperatives and NGOs

Cooperatives sell goods at fair prices: Amul (Gujarat milk revolution), Mother Dairy (Delhi). NGOs: Academy of Development Science (grain banks in Maharashtra).

Questions & Answers

Q1. What is food security? (Short)Availability, accessibility and affordability of food to everyone at all times.
Q2. What is buffer stock? (Short)Stock of wheat and rice bought by FCI at MSP and stored in godowns for use in shortage and to supply PDS.
Q3. What is MSP? (Short)Price announced by the government before sowing season to encourage farmers to grow crops.
Q4. Differentiate chronic and seasonal hunger. (Short)Chronic: long-term inadequate diet due to low income. Seasonal: during certain periods of the farming cycle, e.g. lean season.
Q5. Who are the food insecure people in India? (Long)Landless labourers, artisans, petty self-employed, beggars, migrants, SC/ST/OBC groups; women and children are most affected. Regionally, backward states of eastern and central India have most food-insecure people.
Q6. Explain the working of PDS. (Long)FCI procures grain at MSP, stores it as buffer stock, allots to states; ration shops sell at issue price against ration cards (AAY, BPL, APL). It protects poor from price rise and shortage, though dealers’ malpractices, leakage and poor quality weaken it.
Q7. Describe any three food schemes. (Long)AAY: 35 kg grain to poorest families. Mid-Day Meal: cooked meals in schools improve nutrition and attendance. ICDS: nutrition, immunisation and health check-up for children and mothers. NFSA also gives legal right to grain.
Q8. Role of cooperatives in food security? (Long)They set up shops selling quality items at lower prices. Amul’s milk cooperative made India the largest milk producer; Mother Dairy provides milk and vegetables at controlled rates.
