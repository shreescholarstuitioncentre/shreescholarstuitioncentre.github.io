Chapter 1: The French Revolution

Class 9 | Social Science (History) | UP Board

1. French Society in the 18th Century

In 1774, Louis XVI (age 20) of the Bourbon family became king and married Marie Antoinette of Austria. France's treasury was empty because of long wars (e.g. helping the American colonies against Britain), and the lavish Versailles court. Debts rose; lenders demanded 10% interest. The state raised taxes.

  • Population: 23 million (1715) → 28 million (1789), so demand for grain and bread rose faster than supply.
  • Bread prices shot up, wages stayed low: the poor faced a subsistence crisis (struggle for basic survival).
  • Droughts and hail in bad years caused famine, so bread riots broke out.

The Three Estates

  • First Estate - Clergy: collected tithe (1/10 of farm produce) from peasants; exempt from state taxes.
  • Second Estate - Nobility: owned land, collected feudal dues, exempt from taxes.
  • Third Estate: big businessmen, merchants, court officials, lawyers; peasants, artisans, landless labourers, servants. Only they paid taxes: taille (direct tax), tithe to church, and indirect taxes on salt and tobacco.

Third Estate: businessmen, merchants, lawyers (rich) + peasants, artisans, servants (poor).

2. The Struggle to Survive

The Third Estate paid everything while enjoying no privilege. Prices rose but wages did not. This gave rise to a new middle class that was educated, wealthy and wanted equal opportunity based on merit, not birth.

3. Role of Philosophers

  • John Locke - Two Treatises of Government: rejected the divine, absolute right of kings.
  • Jean-Jacques Rousseau - The Social Contract: government based on a contract between people and their rulers.
  • Montesquieu - The Spirit of the Laws: division of power among legislature, executive and judiciary.

These ideas were discussed in salons and coffee-houses and spread through books and newspapers, even read aloud to the illiterate.

4. The Outbreak of Revolution

Louis XVI called the Estates General on 5 May 1789 to pass new taxes. The First and Second Estates had 300 members each, the Third Estate 600. Voting was by estate (one vote each), but the Third Estate demanded one vote per member. The king refused, so they walked out.

  • 20 June 1789: Third Estate gathered at an indoor tennis court in Versailles, declared itself the National Assembly and swore (Tennis Court Oath) to draft a constitution.
  • Bread shortage; 14 July 1789: crowds stormed the Bastille (a prison, symbol of royal despotism) - now France's national day.
  • Peasants attacked chateaux and burnt feudal records. On 4 August 1789 the Assembly abolished feudal obligations and tithes.

5. France Becomes a Constitutional Monarchy

  • 1791 Constitution limited the king's powers; separated powers among legislature, executive, judiciary.
  • Only men over 25 who paid taxes equal to 3 days' wages were active citizens (could vote); the rest were passive citizens.
  • Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen: rights to life, freedom of speech, equality before law. Natural and inalienable rights.
  • Symbols: the tricolour flag, the song Marseillaise, the slogan Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.

6. France Becomes a Republic

The king secretly tried to flee (Varennes, June 1791) and was caught. Wars with Prussia and Austria (1792) made things worse; people suspected the king. 10 Aug 1792 crowds stormed the Tuileries Palace. The Convention was elected by all men aged 21+, abolished monarchy and declared France a Republic (21 Sept 1792). Louis XVI was executed publicly by the guillotine on 21 Jan 1793; Marie Antoinette followed. Sans-culottes (without knee-breeches), working men, were radical supporters.

7. Reign of Terror (1793-94)

Robespierre followed a policy of severe control: all "enemies of the republic" (nobles, clergy, even party members) were arrested and guillotined. Price and wage ceilings, rationing, and a uniform "equality bread" were enforced. Churches were shut. In July 1794 Robespierre was himself convicted and guillotined.

8. A Directory Rules France

The new constitution gave power to wealthy middle classes; a Directory of 5 members ruled, but quarrelled. This political instability paved the way for Napoleon Bonaparte (1799).

9. Women in the Revolution

  • Women worked, did domestic chores and had little education, equal wages denied. They formed clubs like Society of Revolutionary Republican Women.
  • Demanded vote, election to Assembly. Olympe de Gouges wrote the Declaration of the Rights of Woman and Citizen (executed 1793).
  • Women got the right to vote in France only in 1946.

10. Abolition of Slavery

The Jacobin Convention freed all slaves in French colonies in 1794. Napoleon reintroduced it; it was finally abolished in 1848.

11. Impact on the World (India too)

Ideas of liberty and equality inspired Tipu Sultan and Raja Rammohan Roy; Tipu planted a "Tree of Liberty" at Srirangapatna.

Very Short Q&A (1 mark)

Q1. Who was the king in 1789?
Louis XVI of the Bourbon dynasty.
Q2. What was the Bastille?
A Paris fortress-prison, symbol of royal tyranny.
Q3. What is tithe?
A tax of one-tenth of produce paid to the Church.
Q4. What is taille?
Direct tax paid to the state.
Q5. Who wrote The Social Contract?
Jean-Jacques Rousseau.
Q6. Who were the sans-culottes?
Radical working people who did not wear fashionable knee-breeches.
Q7. Who wrote the Declaration of the Rights of Woman?
Olympe de Gouges.

Short Q&A (3 marks)

Q1. State three causes of the French Revolution.
(i) Empty treasury, debts from wars and royal extravagance. (ii) Unequal society; the Third Estate paid all the taxes while clergy and nobles were exempt. (iii) Food shortage and rising bread prices, and ideas of Enlightenment philosophers.
Q2. What was the Tennis Court Oath?
On 20 June 1789 members of the Third Estate, locked out of the Estates General hall, met at an indoor tennis court, declared themselves the National Assembly and vowed not to disperse until a constitution was framed.
Q3. Differentiate active and passive citizens.
Active citizens were men over 25 paying taxes equal to 3 days' wages and could vote. Passive citizens were the poor men and all women, without voting rights.
Q4. Why is 14 July important?
On 14 July 1789 the Bastille was stormed by the Parisian crowd searching for arms. It marked the start of the revolution and is France's national day.
Q5. How did Montesquieu influence the revolution?
He proposed that power be divided among legislature, executive and judiciary so no one could become a despot; this was adopted in the 1791 constitution.

Long Q&A (5 marks)

Q1. Describe the condition of French society before 1789.
Society was divided into three Estates. The clergy and nobility enjoyed feudal privileges and paid no state taxes. The Third Estate (about 90%) comprised the middle class and peasants and paid taille, tithe and indirect taxes. Population growth raised demand for bread, while wages lagged, causing a subsistence crisis. The middle class, though educated and wealthy, had no political power. These inequalities caused discontent.
Q2. Explain the events from 5 May to 14 July 1789.
Estates General met on 5 May to pass taxes. A dispute over voting (by estate vs by head) caused the Third Estate to walk out and form the National Assembly on 20 June (Tennis Court Oath). Food riots grew; the king moved troops towards Paris. On 14 July people stormed the Bastille. Peasants attacked the manors, and on 4 August feudal privileges were abolished.
Q3. Describe the role of women in the Revolution.
Women of the Third Estate worked as seamstresses, laundresses, vendors and servants, and had low wages and little education. They formed political clubs and newspapers, joined marches (e.g. the Versailles march on bread), demanded equal rights, the vote and equal wages. Olympe de Gouges criticised the Declaration for ignoring women. Despite persecution during the Terror, their struggle continued and finally won them the vote in 1946.
Q4. Why did the Reign of Terror occur and what were its results?
War and internal rebellion threatened the republic, so Robespierre's Committee of Public Safety punished all enemies. Thousands were guillotined, property was seized, prices were controlled, and religion was curbed. Many resented the violence; Robespierre was executed in July 1794 and the Directory followed.
