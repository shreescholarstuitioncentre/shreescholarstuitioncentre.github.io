Chapter 2: Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution

Class 9 | Social Science (History) | UP Board

1. The Age of Social Change

The French Revolution showed that society could be changed. Before it, society was fixed by birth. After it, people debated how society should be organised. Three main groups of thought emerged:

Liberals, Radicals and Conservatives

  • Liberals: wanted a nation tolerant of all religions; opposed birth-based privileges; wanted an elected parliamentary government and protection of individual rights. But they were not democrats: they did not support universal adult franchise; vote only for property-owning men; women had no vote.
  • Radicals: wanted a government based on the majority of the population; supported women's suffrage; opposed privileges of big landowners and rich factory owners. They accepted private property but disliked its concentration in a few hands.
  • Conservatives: opposed radicals and liberals; respected the past and tradition; after the French Revolution accepted that change was needed but wanted it to be slow.

2. Industrial Society and Social Change

Industrialisation brought new cities and factories, but also problems: very long working hours, low wages, unemployment, child labour, shortage of housing, dirty water and poor sanitation. Liberals and radicals looked for solutions; many industrialists were also reformers. Some demanded vote for all, others wanted change in the economic system.

3. The Coming of Socialism in Europe

Socialists were against private property; they saw it as the root of social ills, as individuals owned the property that gave employment but cared only about their own gain. They wanted property to be controlled by the whole society.

  • Robert Owen (English manufacturer): built a cooperative community called New Harmony in Indiana (USA).
  • Louis Blanc (France): wanted the government to encourage cooperatives to replace capitalist enterprises; cooperatives owned by those who produced goods.
  • Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels: capitalists made profits by exploiting workers' labour; workers must overthrow capitalism and build a communist society with collective ownership. (Communist Manifesto, 1848.)

Support for Socialism

By the 1870s, socialist ideas spread across Europe. A body called the Second International was formed to coordinate. Workers formed associations (in England and Germany), fought for better living and working conditions, shorter hours and voting rights. Parties like the Labour Party (Britain) and Socialist Party (France) were formed by 1905. But socialists did not win in Europe before 1914.

4. The Russian Revolution

In 1914, Russia was ruled by Tsar Nicholas II. The empire included Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, parts of Poland, Ukraine, Belarus, etc. Religion: Russian Orthodox Christianity.

Economy and Society

  • About 85% were farmers (in France and Germany 40-50%).
  • Industry was concentrated in St Petersburg and Moscow; foreign investment and state-run factories. Workers worked 12-15 hours; housing was poor.
  • Peasants wanted nobles' land to be given to them and did not respect nobility; unlike in Western Europe they often pooled land (mir commune).

Socialism in Russia

All political parties were illegal in Russia before 1914. Russian Social Democratic Workers Party was founded in 1898 (Marx's ideas); the Socialist Revolutionary Party in 1900 (peasant-centred). Social Democrats split in 1903: Bolsheviks (majority, led by Lenin, wanted a disciplined party) and Mensheviks (minority, wanted a mass party).

The 1905 Revolution

Russia lost the war against Japan (1904-05). On Bloody Sunday (22 Jan 1905) a peaceful procession led by Father Gapon to the Winter Palace was fired on; over 100 killed, 300 wounded. Strikes, peasant revolts and mutinies spread. The Tsar allowed an elected consultative parliament, the Duma, but dissolved it repeatedly.

First World War and Russia

In 1914 Russia joined war against Germany and Austria. Defeats at the front, about 7 million casualties by 1917, retreating armies destroyed crops and buildings creating 3 million refugees; industries closed; railway lines broke; food shortages in the cities.

5. The February Revolution (1917)

  • 22 Feb: a factory was locked out; workers of 50 factories joined the strike.
  • 23 Feb: women workers led strikes (International Women's Day); crowds in Nevskii Prospekt; the Tsar called the cavalry but soldiers refused to fire.
  • Soldiers and workers formed a Soviet (council) in Petrograd.
  • 2 March 1917: the Tsar abdicated; a Provisional Government of Duma leaders took power.

6. After February

The Provisional Government continued the war, which angered the people. In April 1917, Lenin returned from exile and put forward the April Theses: end the war, transfer land to peasants, nationalise banks. Slogan: "Peace, Land and Bread". Bolsheviks gained support in soviets.

7. The October Revolution (1917)

On 24 October 1917 Kerensky left to bring troops. Lenin ordered the Bolsheviks to seize power. The Military Revolutionary Committee led by Leon Trotsky took over government offices; the ship Aurora shelled the Winter Palace. By night the city was in Bolshevik hands; the All Russian Congress of Soviets approved the seizure. (Date 7 Nov in the modern calendar.)

8. What Changed After October

  • Most industries and banks were nationalised (government-owned).
  • Land was declared social property; peasants allowed to seize nobles' land.
  • Large houses were partitioned according to family size.
  • Old titles of aristocracy banned; new uniforms were designed. Bolsheviks renamed Communist Party (1918).
  • Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) took Russia out of the war.

9. The Civil War (1918-1920)

Russian army divided: Reds (Bolsheviks), Whites (pro-Tsarists, nobles) and Greens (Socialist Revolutionaries). Foreign troops (French, American, British, Japanese) supported the Whites because they feared socialism. The Bolsheviks won, but Russia was destroyed; many non-Russian nationalities joined to form the USSR (December 1922).

10. Making of a Socialist Society

  • Centralised planning and Five Year Plans (from 1927): government fixed production targets, prices; industrial growth rose; schools and colleges, nurseries and public health developed.
  • Collectivisation (1929-30s): Stalin forced peasants to farm on collective farms (kolkhoz); many slaughtered cattle; opposition was crushed; the result was a severe famine (1930-33), killing millions. Rich peasants (kulaks) were persecuted.

11. Global Influence

Socialist ideas inspired many Asian leaders; Communist Parties formed in many countries. In India, Jawaharlal Nehru and Rabindranath Tagore (Letters from Russia) were influenced by the experiment. The Soviet model was criticised for suppressing freedom; the USSR broke up in 1991.

Very Short Q&A (1 mark)

Q1. Who was the Tsar of Russia in 1914?
Nicholas II.
Q2. Who led the Bolsheviks?
Vladimir Lenin.
Q3. What was Duma?
Russia's elected consultative parliament.
Q4. What was a Soviet?
A council of workers and soldiers.
Q5. What were kulaks?
Well-off peasants of Russia.

The cruiser Aurora.
Q7. Who founded New Harmony?
Robert Owen.

Short Q&A (3 marks)

Q1. Differentiate liberals and radicals.
Liberals wanted representative government and individual rights but gave vote only to property-owning men. Radicals wanted a government based on the majority, supported votes for women, and opposed concentration of wealth in a few hands.
Q2. State the main ideas of Karl Marx.
Capitalists profit by exploiting workers; workers should overthrow capitalism and set up a socialist, then communist society with collective ownership of property.
Q3. What were Lenin's April Theses?
Lenin's three demands on return in April 1917: end the war, transfer land to peasants, nationalise banks.
Q4. Why did Bloody Sunday happen?
Workers led by Father Gapon marched peacefully with demands (lower hours, higher wages); the Tsar's guards fired, killing over 100 and starting the 1905 Revolution.
Q5. Who were the Reds, Whites and Greens?
Reds: Bolsheviks; Whites: pro-Tsarists; Greens: Socialist Revolutionaries; they fought the civil war of 1918-20.

Long Q&A (5 marks)

Q1. Describe the causes of the February Revolution of 1917.
Russia's WWI defeats, huge casualties, destroyed crops, millions of refugees, food shortages and railway breakdown caused anger. Autocracy, no real parliament and hard industrial conditions added to it. On 22-23 February workers, led by women, struck in Petrograd, soldiers refused to fire, a Soviet formed and the Tsar abdicated on 2 March.
Q2. Explain the events of the October Revolution.
The Provisional Government continued the war and could not solve food and land problems. Lenin returned and promised peace, land and bread. On 24 October Kerensky left the city; the Military Revolutionary Committee under Trotsky seized key offices, the Aurora fired at the Winter Palace, and the Bolsheviks captured power; the Congress of Soviets approved it.
Q3. Explain the changes made by the Bolsheviks after the revolution.
They nationalised banks and industries, declared land social property, made peace by the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, renamed themselves the Communist Party, partitioned big houses, banned aristocratic titles and began centralised planning. Their rule saw a civil war with Whites and Greens and later the Five Year Plans.
Q4. Describe collectivisation and its results.
After a grain shortage in 1927-28, Stalin forced farmers into collective farms in 1929. Peasants who resisted were punished; many killed their cattle. The result was low production and a terrible famine in 1930-33 that killed millions, though state-owned farms did expand.
