✎ People as Resource — Class 9 Economics (UP Board)

1. Introduction

People as Resource means seeing a country's population as a productive asset. When people are educated and healthy they become human capital and add to national income. Example: Sakal (uneducated, earns little) vs Vilas (educated, runs a business and employs others).

Population becomes an asset when it is invested in (education, health); otherwise it is a liability.

2. Human Resource vs Physical Resource

Physical resourcesHuman resource
Land, water, minerals, machinesSkilled, healthy, educated people who use them
Passive; cannot work by themselvesActive; they create and use other resources

3. Human Capital Formation

  • Education: raises skills, income, awareness. Spending on schooling gives higher returns later.
  • Health: a healthy person works efficiently. Includes sanitation, nutrition, safe water, hospitals.
  • On-the-job training: workers learn skills while working (e.g. apprenticeship).
  • Migration: people move for better jobs (rural → urban).
  • Information: knowledge of jobs, wages, markets.

4. Economic Activities

  • Primary: agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining.
  • Secondary: manufacturing, construction.
  • Tertiary: trade, transport, banking, education, health, IT.

Market activities = work for pay/profit (including government service). Non-market = for self-consumption (e.g. housewife's work).

5. Quality of Population

Depends on literacy rate, life expectancy, skill, health. Better quality → more productivity → higher growth.

6. Unemployment

Willing to work at the current wage but cannot find work.

  • Seasonal: work only in some seasons (farming).
  • Disguised: more people than needed on a farm; removing some does not cut output (marginal productivity zero).
  • Educated: educated youth without jobs.

Effects: wastage of resources, poverty, rising dependence, falling living standard.

Key terms: Human capital • Literacy • Life expectancy • Disguised unemployment • Migration

Questions & Answers

Q1. What is meant by People as Resource? (Short)Treating population as a productive asset; educated, healthy and skilled people contribute to GDP.
Q2. Differentiate market and non-market activities. (Short)Market: for pay/profit (a teacher, a farmer selling crops). Non-market: for self-use, no pay (a housewife cooking at home).
Q3. Explain disguised unemployment with an example. (Short)When more workers than needed work on a task. E.g. 5 members farm a plot needing only 3; two extra add nothing to output.
Q4. How does education help in human capital formation? (Long)Education raises skills and productivity, increases income, spreads awareness of health and rights, promotes new technology, reduces poverty and gender gaps, and makes the person a better citizen. Hence govt spends on schools, scholarships, literacy campaigns.
Q5. Why is health important? (Long)A healthy worker is more efficient, absent less, and lives longer. Good nutrition, sanitation, safe water and hospitals improve the workforce; a sick person is a liability for family and nation.
Q6. Explain the three sectors of economic activity. (Long)Primary uses natural resources (agriculture, fishing); Secondary converts raw materials to goods (factories); Tertiary provides services (banking, transport, teaching). Each supports the others.
Q7. Describe types of unemployment in India. (Long)Seasonal, disguised and educated unemployment; explain each with example (see notes). Causes: population growth, low industrial growth, less skill.
