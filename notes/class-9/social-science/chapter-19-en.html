✎ Poverty as a Challenge — Class 9 Economics (UP Board)

1. Introduction

Poverty is when a person cannot meet basic needs — food, clothing, shelter, education and health. Seen in beggars, street dwellers, landless labourers, children working in hotels. It is India's biggest challenge.

2. Different Faces of Poverty

  • Lack of food, clothes, house, school, clean water, jobs.
  • Social exclusion: poor people kept out of facilities used by better-off people (e.g. lower caste).
  • Vulnerability: greater chance of becoming/remaining poor in bad times (flood, job loss).
  • Inequality: unequal share of income and assets.

3. Poverty Line

An imaginary line to identify the poor. Based on minimum calorie need: 2400 per person/day (rural), 2100 (urban) plus clothing, shoes, education, health. Rural needs more calories as work is physical. Money value is updated with prices. Tendulkar (2011-12): about ₹816 rural, ₹1000 urban per person/month.

Poverty line is revised every 5 years by NSSO survey (Planning Commission earlier).

4. Vulnerable Groups

Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, agricultural labour and casual labour households are most poor. Women, elderly and girl child suffer more inside families.

5. Inter-State Disparity

Highest poverty: Odisha, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, UP. Lowest: Kerala, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Gujarat (land reform, agriculture growth, human resource development).

6. Global Poverty

World Bank: below $1.90/day = extreme poverty. China and South-East Asia reduced poverty sharply; Sub-Saharan Africa remains worst. UN Sustainable Development Goal: end poverty by 2030.

7. Causes of Poverty

  • British colonial policy (ruined handicrafts, low growth).
  • Rapid population growth; low economic growth.
  • Unequal land distribution; failure of land reforms.
  • Illiteracy, unemployment, low skills.
  • Heavy spending on marriages, festivals; borrowing → debt.
  • Social and cultural factors, vicious circle of poverty.

8. Anti-Poverty Measures

Two-pronged: economic growth + targeted schemes.

9. Challenges Ahead

Corruption and poor implementation, regional gaps, hunger and health. Need: education, health, jobs, women's empowerment.

Key terms: Poverty line • Vulnerability • Social exclusion • MGNREGA • Calorie norm

Diagram: Vicious Circle of Poverty

Low income → low saving & investment → low productivity → low income again. Breaking it needs education, jobs and credit.

Questions & Answers

Q1. What is poverty? (Short)Inability to meet basic needs like food, clothing, shelter, education and health.
Q2. What is the poverty line? (Short)An imaginary line separating poor from non-poor, based on minimum calorie intake plus other basic needs: 2400 cal (rural), 2100 cal (urban).
Q3. Why is the rural calorie norm higher? (Short)Rural people do more physical labour so need more energy.
Q4. What is social exclusion? (Short)Poor people are denied facilities and equal opportunities enjoyed by better-off groups, e.g. lower castes in poor neighbourhoods.
Q5. Explain the main causes of poverty in India. (Long)Colonial rule destroyed handicrafts; population growth kept per-capita income low; uneven land distribution; illiteracy and unemployment; low growth; social customs (expensive weddings) lead to debt; and the vicious circle of poverty traps families.
Q6. Explain MGNREGA. (Long)Act of 2005 guarantees 100 days of wage employment per year to rural households whose adult members volunteer for unskilled work. One-third jobs for women. Builds assets like ponds, roads; reduces migration.
Q7. Which groups are most vulnerable to poverty? (Long)SC, ST, agricultural labourers, casual workers; within families women, elderly and girls get less food and care.
Q8. Compare poverty in India and the world. (Long)India’s poverty ratio fell from about 45% (1993-94) to about 22% (2011-12) but numbers remain high. Globally China and SE Asia fell sharply, Sub-Saharan Africa stayed high.
