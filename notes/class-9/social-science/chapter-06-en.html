India: Size and Location

Class 9 | Social Science (Geography) | UP Board

1. Location of India

India lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere and in the Eastern Hemisphere. The mainland extends between latitudes 8°4'N and 37°6'N and longitudes 68°7'E and 97°25'E.

  • The Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N) passes almost halfway through India, dividing it into two nearly equal parts: the northern part is in the sub-tropical (temperate) zone and the southern part lies in the tropical zone.
  • It passes through 8 states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram.
  • The southernmost point of the Indian Union is Indira Point (6°45'N) in Great Nicobar; it was submerged in the 2004 tsunami. Kanyakumari (8°4'N) is the southern tip of the mainland.
  • Latitudinal and longitudinal extent are both about 30°.

2. Size of India

  • Area: about 3.28 million sq km, which is 2.4% of the world's total geographical area.
  • India is the seventh largest country in the world after Russia, Canada, the USA, China, Brazil and Australia.
  • North-south extent: 3,214 km; east-west extent: 2,933 km (the north-south extent is greater although both extents in degrees are 30°, because the distance between longitudes decreases towards the poles).
  • Land frontier: about 15,200 km. Total coastline (mainland + islands): 7,516.6 km (mainland 6,100 km).
  • Political units: 28 states and 8 Union Territories.

3. Neighbours of India

India has a land boundary with seven countries. Pakistan and Afghanistan are on the north-west; China, Nepal and Bhutan on the north; Myanmar and Bangladesh on the east. Two island countries are our neighbours: Sri Lanka (separated by the Palk Strait and the Gulf of Mannar) and Maldives (south-west of Lakshadweep).

Islands: Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea, off the Kerala coast) and Andaman and Nicobar (Bay of Bengal).

4. Indian Standard Time (IST)

  • There is a time difference of about 2 hours between Gujarat (west) and Arunachal Pradesh (east); the sun rises in Arunachal two hours earlier. The earth rotates 360° in 24 hours: 15° longitude = 1 hour and 1° = 4 minutes.
  • To avoid confusion, a common standard meridian is chosen: 82°30'E, which passes through Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh). The local time at this meridian is the Indian Standard Time, 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT (Greenwich Mean Time).

5. India's Place in the World

  • The Indian Ocean gives India a central position between East and West Asia, Africa, Europe and South-East Asia. The Deccan Peninsula extends into the Indian Ocean, giving close contact with West Asia, Africa and Europe from the west coast and South-East and East Asia from the east coast.
  • No other country has a long coastline in the Indian Ocean; this is why the ocean is named after India.
  • The opening of the Suez Canal (1869) brought India about 7,000 km closer to Europe.
  • Contact with the world: India has been linked by land routes (Khyber, Bolan passes) and sea routes since ancient times. Indian spices, textiles and the decimal system reached the world, while India received ideas, architecture and goods. Examples: stories of the Panchatantra, Indian numerals, Ayurveda, and the Taj Mahal's Indo-Islamic architecture.

6. India and Its Neighbours

India has had cultural ties with neighbours for centuries. India's size, location and unity in diversity make it a leading country of South Asia. Cordial relations with neighbours foster trade, tourism and peace.

Short Q&A (3 marks)

Q1. Why is there a need for a standard time in India?
India's east-west extent causes about two hours' time difference between Gujarat and Arunachal Pradesh. To avoid confusion, the local time of the 82°30'E meridian (Mirzapur) is used as the Indian Standard Time throughout the country.
Q2. Name the states through which the Tropic of Cancer passes.
Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram.
Q3. Why is the Indian Ocean named after India?
India has the longest coastline projecting into the ocean, and its central position links Asia, Africa and Europe, giving it a dominant role.
Q4. How did the Suez Canal help India?
It shortened the sea route between India and Europe by about 7,000 km, boosting trade and cutting travel time.
Q5. Name India's neighbours and islands.
Land neighbours: Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, Bangladesh. Island neighbours: Sri Lanka and Maldives. Island groups: Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar.

Long Q&A (5 marks)

Q1. Describe the location of India in the world.
India lies in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres between 8°4'N-37°6'N and 68°7'E-97°25'E. The Tropic of Cancer runs through the middle. It is in southern Asia with the Himalayas in the north and the Indian Ocean in the south. Because of its central position between East and West, the Deccan peninsula reaches into the ocean connecting it to many continents.
Q2. Explain the importance of India's location.
Its position on the Indian Ocean gives access to West Asia, Africa and Europe via the west coast and to South-East and East Asia via the east. The Suez Canal shortened the route to Europe by 7,000 km. Ancient land routes through passes and sea routes carried goods and ideas. Thus India influenced and was influenced by the world.
Q3. Explain Indian Standard Time and the need for it.
The sun rises two hours earlier in Arunachal than in Gujarat. If every place used local time, there would be confusion in railways and offices. Hence the local time of the meridian 82°30'E (Mirzapur) was chosen as the Indian Standard Time, 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT.
Q4. Give the size and extent of India and its political divisions.
Area 3.28 million sq km (2.4% of world), seventh largest. North-south 3,214 km, east-west 2,933 km, land frontier 15,200 km, coastline 7,516.6 km. It has 28 states and 8 Union Territories.
