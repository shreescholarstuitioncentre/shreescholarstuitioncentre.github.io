✎ Money: Meaning, Functions, Importance & Evils

Class 9 · Commerce · Chapter 17 · UP Board

1. Meaning & Definitions

Money is anything that is generally accepted by people in payment for goods and services and in settlement of debts.

Prof. Walker: “Money is what money does.” – i.e., whatever performs the functions of money is money.
Crowther: Money is anything generally acceptable as a means of exchange and at the same time acts as a measure and store of value.

Examples: coins, currency notes, bank deposits (cheque), UPI balances.

2. Functions of Money (Diagram)

Explanation:
1. Medium of exchange: money is accepted in all buying and selling, removing the need for double coincidence of wants.
2. Measure of value: every good is priced in rupees, so values can be compared (a pen ₹10, a book ₹100 → book = 10 pens).
3. Store of value: savings can be kept safely and used later.
4. Standard of deferred payment: loans and salaries are fixed in money and paid later.
5. Transfer of value: money moves wealth from city to city, person to person (DD, UPI).
6. Basis of credit: banks create loans against money deposits.
7. Liquidity: property can be converted into cash quickly.
8. Measure of national income: a country’s total output is added up in money terms.

3. Importance of Money

• Consumption: consumers can buy varied goods and maximise satisfaction.
• Production: producers hire land, labour, capital and pay in money.
• Exchange: trade at home and abroad becomes easy.
• Distribution: rent, wages, interest and profit are paid in money.
• Capital formation: savings turn into investment.
• Banking & credit: money makes banks and loans possible.
• Government: taxes are collected and expenditure is made in money.
• Economic growth: money increases specialisation and trade, so the economy grows.

4. Evils (Demerits) of Money

EvilExplanation
InflationExcess money → rising prices → poor suffer.
DeflationLess money → falling prices → fewer jobs, losses.
Unequal distributionHoarding makes rich richer, poor poorer.
Black money & corruptionTax evasion, bribery, smuggling.
MaterialismLove of money reduces morality and values.
Exploitation & class conflictCapitalists exploit workers; tension between classes.
Speculation & crisesReckless trading causes booms and busts.

Conclusion: Money is a good servant but a bad master. The evils come from misuse, so proper control by the RBI and the government is needed.

5. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: A pen cost ₹10 last year and now costs ₹12. Find the inflation rate.

To find: % rise in price.
Step 1: Rise = 12 − 10 = ₹2.
Step 2: % = (2 / 10) × 100.
Step 3: = 20%.

Intermediate: If the price level doubles, what happens to the value of money?

Formula: value of money ∝ 1 / price level.
Step 1: Let old price level = P; value = 1/P.
Step 2: New level = 2P; value = 1/(2P).
Step 3: Ratio = (1/2P) ÷ (1/P) = 1/2. So the value halves.

Hard: Price index rises from 100 to 125. Prove that the purchasing power of ₹1 falls to 80 paise (a 20% fall).

To prove: new purchasing power = 100/125 of the old.
Step 1: Purchasing power of ₹1 = 100 / 125 = 0.8 of its old value.
Step 2: 0.8 × 100 paise = 80 paise.
Step 3: Fall = 20 paise out of 100 = 20%. Hence proved.

6. Question & Answer Bank

A. MCQs

1. “Money is what money does” was said by—
(a) Crowther (b) Walker (c) Marshall (d) Keynes

Ans: (b) Walker.

2. Which is a primary function of money?
(a) Basis of credit (b) Medium of exchange (c) Liquidity (d) Store of value

Ans: (b).

3. Rising prices due to excess money are called—
(a) Deflation (b) Inflation (c) Barter (d) Stagflation

Ans: (b).

4. Money as a measure of value helps in—
(a) Comparing values (b) Printing notes (c) Making coins (d) Banking only

Ans: (a).

5. Which is an evil of money?
(a) Capital formation (b) Black money (c) Exchange (d) Liquidity

Ans: (b).

6. Quick conversion of property into cash shows—
(a) Liquidity (b) Inflation (c) Barter (d) Hoarding

Ans: (a).

B. Short Answers

7. Define money.

Anything generally accepted in payment for goods, services and debts.

8. Name the primary functions.

Medium of exchange and measure of value.

9. What is deflation?

A fall in the general price level due to a shortage of money; it reduces production and jobs.

10. How does money help in banking?

Deposits of money let banks give loans, so money is the basis of credit.

11. Why is money called a store of value?

People keep savings in money and spend them later; it holds purchasing power.

12. What is black money?

Income earned illegally or hidden from tax authorities.
