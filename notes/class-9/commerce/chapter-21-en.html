1. Introduction

The word Economics comes from the Greek Oikonomia: oikos = household and nomos = management. So it first meant “management of the household”. Today it studies how people, firms and governments use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants. Adam Smith is called the Father of Economics (book: Wealth of Nations, 1776).

Basic idea: Human wants are unlimited but resources (money, time, land, labour) are scarce. Because of scarcity we must choose, and every choice has an opportunity cost (the next best thing given up).

3. Definitions Explained

EconomistIdea (in simple words)Criticism
Adam Smith (Wealth definition)Economics is the science that studies the nature and causes of national wealth.Too much stress on wealth; man was ignored.
Alfred Marshall (Welfare definition)Economics studies man’s ordinary business of life, how he earns and uses income, mainly for material welfare.Limited to material welfare; wrongly separates “welfare” and “non-welfare” activities.
Lionel Robbins (Scarcity definition)Economics studies human behaviour as a relation between unlimited wants and scarce means that have alternative uses.Ignores welfare and growth; too neutral about ends.
Paul Samuelson (Growth definition)Economics studies how people and society choose, with or without money, to use scarce resources to produce goods over time and distribute them for consumption.Complex and wide for beginners.

4. Nature, Scope & Basic Problems

Nature: Economics is both a science (systematic study) and an art (practical use to solve problems).
Branches: Microeconomics studies individuals, firms and markets; Macroeconomics studies the whole economy (income, inflation, employment).
Approaches: Positive – what is (facts); Normative – what ought to be (opinions).
Central problems of every economy: (1) What to produce? (2) How to produce? (3) For whom to produce?

5. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: A family wants goods worth ₹50,000 but has an income of ₹30,000. Find the unmet wants in %.

To find: shortage %.
Step 1: Shortage = 50,000 − 30,000 = ₹20,000.
Step 2: % = (20,000 / 50,000) × 100.
Step 3: = 40% (scarcity forces choice).

Intermediate: A farmer’s land can produce 100 quintals of wheat or 60 quintals of sugarcane. Find the opportunity cost of 1 quintal of wheat.

Step 1: 100 wheat = 60 sugarcane given up.
Step 2: 1 wheat = 60 / 100 sugarcane.
Step 3: = 0.6 quintal of sugarcane.

Hard: Wheat output rises from 100 to 160 quintals while sugarcane falls from 80 to 50. Prove that the opportunity cost of 1 extra quintal of wheat is 0.5 quintal of sugarcane.

To prove: opportunity cost = loss of sugarcane ÷ gain of wheat.
Step 1: Gain of wheat = 160 − 100 = 60.
Step 2: Loss of sugarcane = 80 − 50 = 30.
Step 3: Cost per quintal = 30 / 60 = 0.5. Hence proved.

B. Short Answers

7. What is economics?

The study of how scarce resources are used to satisfy unlimited human wants.

8. Who wrote “Wealth of Nations”?

Adam Smith (1776).

9. What is scarcity?

Limited availability of resources compared with wants.

10. Differentiate micro and macroeconomics.

Micro studies individual units such as a consumer or firm; macro studies the whole economy such as national income and inflation.

11. What is positive economics?

A study of facts, “what is”, without value judgements.

12. Name the three central problems.

What to produce, how to produce, for whom to produce.
