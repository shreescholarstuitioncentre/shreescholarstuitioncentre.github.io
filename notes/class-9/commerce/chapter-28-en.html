Class 9 Commerce – Project Work

Key Points Explained

1. Meaning of Project Work: Studying a chosen topic on your own, collecting data, analysing it and presenting it in an orderly way is called project work.
2. Objectives: To make bookish knowledge practical, build self-study habits, research skills and the ability to analyse and present.
3. Step 1: Select the Topic: Choose a simple topic of your interest linked to the syllabus with data available, e.g. "A study of my village market".
4. Step 2: Plan: Fix objectives, a timetable and an outline of work.
5. Step 3: Collect Data: Primary sources: survey, questionnaire, interview, observation. Secondary sources: books, newspapers, internet, government reports.
6. Step 4: Analyse: Tabulate data, find averages and percentages, draw charts and graphs.
7. Step 5: Conclude and Present: Write results, give suggestions and prepare a neat file.
8. Structure of the File: Beginning: cover page, certificate, acknowledgement, index. Main body: introduction, objectives, method, data and analysis. End: conclusion, suggestions, bibliography.
9. Precautions: Do your own work, keep data correct, write neatly, label charts and never copy.
10. Suggested Topics: Importance of saving, study of a local market, consumer awareness, my family budget.

Solved Example

Question: In a survey of 20 students, 5 get ₹200, 8 get ₹300 and 7 get ₹500 monthly pocket money. Find the average pocket money and the percentage of each group.
Step 1: Total = (5×200)+(8×300)+(7×500) = 1,000+2,400+3,500 = ₹6,900.
Step 2: Average = 6,900 ÷ 20 = ₹345.
Step 3: ₹200 group = 5/20×100 = 25%.
Step 4: ₹300 group = 8/20×100 = 40%.
Step 5: ₹500 group = 7/20×100 = 35%.
Check: 25+40+35 = 100% ✔. Answer: Average ₹345.

Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is project work?Self-study of a chosen topic with data collection, analysis and presentation.
Q2. State the objectives of project work.Practical knowledge, research skill, self-study habit and presentation ability.
Q3. Differentiate primary and secondary sources.Primary: collected by yourself (survey, interview); secondary: already available (books, newspapers).
Q4. What should a project file contain?Cover page, certificate, acknowledgement, index, introduction, analysis, conclusion, suggestions and bibliography.
Q5. What to consider when choosing a topic?Interest, link to syllabus, simplicity and availability of data.
Q6. What is a questionnaire?A list of questions prepared to collect data.
Q7. What is written in the conclusion?Main findings of the study and suggestions based on them.
Q8. Why is a bibliography important?It shows where information came from and makes the work reliable.
