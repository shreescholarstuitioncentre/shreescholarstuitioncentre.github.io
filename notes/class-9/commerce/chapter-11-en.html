Copying (Class 9 Commerce)

1. Meaning

Copying (Duplicating) means making one or more copies of a letter, document or record. In an office, copies are needed to keep a record of letters sent, to send the same message to many persons, and as evidence.

2. Need and Importance

  • Keeps record of outgoing letters.
  • Serves as legal evidence.
  • Saves time when many persons need the same information (circulars, notices).
  • Helps in reference and follow-up.
  • Avoids retyping or rewriting.

4. Carbon Copying in Detail

Carbon paper has ink on one side. Place: original sheet on top, carbon paper (ink side down), copy sheet below. Typing/writing pressure transfers ink to the copy sheet. Use thin paper (onion-skin) for copy.
Merits: cheap, simultaneous copy.
Demerits: only a few copies; copies are less clear; dirty hands.

5. Cyclostyling in Detail

A stencil (wax-coated sheet) is typed without ribbon, so letters cut tiny holes. It is fixed on a cyclostyle machine; ink on the roller passes through holes and prints on paper. Merits: low cost per copy for large numbers. Demerits: stencil is costly, copies are not as neat as xerox.

6. Photocopying in Detail

The page is placed on the glass; light and static charge (xerography) make toner stick to paper. Merits: fast, exact copy, copies from books and photos, any size. Demerits: costly per page for large numbers; machine needs power and maintenance.

7. Choosing a Method

Choose by number of copies, cost, quality, speed and secrecy. Few copies → carbon; large numbers → cyclostyle/printing; exact copy of existing paper → photocopy.

9. Solved Example (step by step)

Q. A circular is needed in 40 copies. Cyclostyling costs: stencil ₹20, ink ₹10, paper ₹0.50 per copy. Photocopying costs ₹1 per copy. (a) Which is cheaper for 40 copies? (b) At how many copies are both equal?
  1. Step 1 (a): Cyclostyle cost = 20 + 10 + (40 × 0.50) = 20 + 10 + 20 = ₹50.
  2. Step 2: Photocopy cost = 40 × 1 = ₹40.
  3. Step 3: ₹40 < ₹50, so photocopying is cheaper for 40 copies.
  4. Step 4 (b): Let copies = n. Cyclostyle = 30 + 0.5n; Photocopy = n.
  5. Step 5: Equal when 30 + 0.5n = n, so 0.5n = 30, n = 60.
  6. Check: At 60: cyclostyle = 30 + 30 = ₹60; photocopy = ₹60 ✔. Above 60 copies, cyclostyling is cheaper.

10. Short & Long Questions

Q1. What is copying?

Making one or more copies of a document.

Q2. Why is copying needed?

For records, evidence, many recipients and reference.

Q3. Name any four methods of copying.

Carbon, cyclostyling, photocopying, printing.

Q4. What is a stencil?

A wax-coated sheet typed to cut holes for ink to pass through.

Q5. Which method is best for 500 copies?

Cyclostyling or printing.

Q6. Which paper is used for carbon copy?

Thin onion-skin paper.

Q7. Explain carbon copying. (Long)

Carbon paper is placed between original and copy sheets, ink side down; pressure of writing transfers ink and gives copy. Cheap but gives only a few copies.

Q8. Compare cyclostyling and photocopying. (Long)

Cyclostyling needs stencil and suits many copies at low cost per copy; photocopying gives exact copies quickly but costs more per copy for large numbers.
