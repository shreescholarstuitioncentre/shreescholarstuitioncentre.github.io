Books of Original Entry – Cash Book (Class 9 Commerce)

1. Meaning

Cash Book is the book in which all cash receipts and cash payments are recorded date-wise. It works as both a Journal (first entry) and a Ledger (cash account), so no separate posting to the cash account is needed. Books of Original Entry (Subsidiary Books) are books where transactions are first recorded: Cash Book, Purchases Book, Sales Book, Returns Books, Bills Books, Journal Proper.

2. Features

  • Debit side = Receipts; Credit side = Payments.
  • Always shows a debit balance (cash cannot be negative).
  • Is a book of original entry and a ledger account.
  • Prepared with To on debit and By on credit.

4. Contra Entry

When both aspects of a transaction are in the cash book itself (cash ↔ bank), it is a Contra Entry, marked (C) in L.F. Examples: cash deposited into bank (Dr Bank, Cr Cash); cash withdrawn from bank (Dr Cash, Cr Bank).

5. Preparation Steps

  1. Write the opening cash/bank balance on the debit side (To Balance b/d).
  2. Record receipts on debit side, payments on credit side, date-wise.
  3. Total both sides separately.
  4. Put the difference on the smaller side as Balance c/d and bring it down on the next day.

7. Merits

Saves time and labour; cash position known any time; reduces chances of fraud; no separate cash ledger.

9. Solved Example (step by step)

Q. Prepare a double-column cash book: Apr 1 Started business with cash ₹50,000. Apr 2 Deposited into bank ₹30,000. Apr 3 Purchased goods by cheque ₹10,000. Apr 5 Sold goods for cash ₹15,000. Apr 7 Paid rent in cash ₹2,000.
  1. Step 1 (Apr 1): Cash Dr: To Capital 50,000.
  2. Step 2 (Apr 2): Contra: Bank Dr (To Cash, C) 30,000; Cash Cr (By Bank, C) 30,000.
  3. Step 3 (Apr 3): Bank Cr: By Purchases 10,000.
  4. Step 4 (Apr 5): Cash Dr: To Sales 15,000.
  5. Step 5 (Apr 7): Cash Cr: By Rent 2,000.
  6. Cash totals: Dr 50,000 + 15,000 = 65,000; Cr 30,000 + 2,000 = 32,000; Balance c/d = 33,000 (credit side), totals 65,000 both.
  7. Bank totals: Dr 30,000; Cr 10,000; Balance c/d = 20,000, totals 30,000 both.
  8. Result: Cash in hand ₹33,000, Bank balance ₹20,000 ✔

10. Short & Long Questions

Q1. Define cash book.

Book recording all cash receipts and payments date-wise.

Q2. Why is the cash book called both journal and ledger?

It records first entry like a journal and works as the cash account like a ledger.

Q3. What is contra entry?

Entry where both cash and bank accounts are affected, marked (C).

Q4. Name types of cash book.

Single, double, three column and petty cash book.

Q5. What is petty cash book?

Book for recording small daily expenses.

Q6. Which side shows receipts?

The debit side.

Q7. Explain features of cash book. (Long)

Receipts are on debit, payments on credit; balance is always debit; it is a journal and ledger together; it is balanced regularly.

Q8. Explain steps of preparing a cash book. (Long)

Write opening balances, record receipts and payments date-wise, total both sides, show balance c/d on the smaller side and bring it down as balance b/d.
