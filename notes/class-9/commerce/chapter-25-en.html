Class 9 Commerce – Terminology Relating to Economics

Key Terms Explained

1. Economics: Study of satisfying unlimited wants with limited resources. Adam Smith is called the "Father of Economics".
2. Want: Desire for a thing backed by the ability and willingness to pay. Types: necessaries (food), comforts (fan), luxuries (car).
3. Goods: Physical, tangible items that satisfy wants, e.g. books, clothes.
4. Services: Intangible activities that satisfy wants, e.g. doctor, teacher, banking.
5. Utility: Power of a good to satisfy a want. Types: form, place, time and service utility.
6. Value: Power of one good to be exchanged for another (1 shirt = 2 books).
7. Price: Value of a good expressed in money terms.
8. Wealth: Anything having utility, scarcity and transferability together.
9. Income: Money received for service, production or property: wages, rent, interest, profit.
10. Demand: Quantity a consumer is ready to buy at a given price and time (desire + ability + willingness).
11. Supply: Quantity of a good a seller offers for sale at a given price.
12. Market: A place or arrangement where buyers and sellers exchange goods.

Solved Example

Question: At ₹10 demand is 100 units. When price falls to ₹8, demand rises to 150 units. Find the change in demand and name the law shown.
Step 1: Change in demand = 150 − 100 = 50 units (increase).
Step 2: Change in price = 10 − 8 = ₹2 (fall).
Step 3: Price fell, demand rose → inverse relation.
Answer: Demand rose by 50 units; this is the Law of Demand.

Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is economics?The study of how unlimited wants are satisfied with limited resources.
Q2. Differentiate goods and services.Goods are tangible (a book); services are intangible (a teacher teaching).
Q3. Name the types of utility.Form (wood→chair), place (village→city), time (storage) and service utility (doctor).
Q4. Difference between value and price?Value is exchange power in terms of goods; price is that value in money.
Q5. State three features of wealth.Utility, scarcity and transferability.
Q6. Explain the law of demand.Other things equal, demand falls as price rises and rises as price falls. The demand curve slopes downward from left to right.
Q7. What are the three conditions of demand?Desire for the good, ability to buy and willingness to spend.
Q8. State features of a market.Presence of buyers and sellers, a commodity, one price tendency and competition.
