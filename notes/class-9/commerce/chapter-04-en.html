Book-Keeping: Meaning, Objectives, Merits & Methods (Class 9 Commerce)

1. Meaning

Book-keeping is the art of recording day-to-day financial transactions in a systematic and chronological manner in the books of accounts. It is the base of accounting. A person who does it is a book-keeper. Only money-measurable transactions (cash/credit) are recorded.

2. Objectives

  • Record all business transactions permanently.
  • Know profit or loss of the business.
  • Know financial position (assets and liabilities).
  • Know amounts due from debtors and payable to creditors.
  • Prevent errors and fraud.
  • Meet legal needs (income tax, GST).

3. Merits (Advantages)

  • Systematic records – nothing is forgotten.
  • Profit/loss knowledge through final accounts.
  • Tax help – proof for tax authorities.
  • Legal evidence – accepted in court.
  • Comparison of different years.
  • Control – detects mistakes and fraud.
  • Credit – banks give loans on seeing accounts.

4. Methods of Book-Keeping

(a) Single Entry: incomplete; only one aspect of a transaction (mostly cash, debtors, creditors) is recorded. Used by small shops.
(b) Double Entry: every transaction has two aspects – Debit and Credit, so both are recorded. Complete, scientific, accepted by law.

6. Solved Example (step by step)

Q. Pass journal entries: (i) Started business with cash ₹50,000. (ii) Bought goods for cash ₹20,000. (iii) Sold goods on credit to Ram ₹15,000.
  1. (i) Cash comes in (asset ↑ → Debit), Capital is owed to owner (↑ → Credit).
    Cash A/c Dr ₹50,000 / To Capital A/c ₹50,000
  2. (ii) Purchases come in (expense → Debit), Cash goes out (asset ↓ → Credit).
    Purchases A/c Dr ₹20,000 / To Cash A/c ₹20,000
  3. (iii) Ram is a debtor (asset ↑ → Debit), Sales is income (Credit).
    Ram A/c Dr ₹15,000 / To Sales A/c ₹15,000
  4. Check: total debit = total credit = ₹85,000 ✔

7. Short & Long Questions

Q1. Define book-keeping.

Recording of financial transactions in a systematic manner in books of accounts.

Q2. Who is a book-keeper?

A person who maintains and records the books of accounts.

Q3. State any three objectives.

Record transactions, find profit/loss, know financial position.

Q4. What is single entry?

An incomplete system recording only one aspect of some transactions.

Q5. What is double entry?

Each transaction is recorded twice – once as debit, once as credit.

Q6. Write the basic rule of double entry.

For every debit there is an equal credit.

Q7. Why is book-keeping essential? (Long)

It gives a permanent record, shows profit/loss and financial position, helps tax assessment, supplies legal evidence, and prevents fraud. Without it, a business cannot be managed or compared year to year.
