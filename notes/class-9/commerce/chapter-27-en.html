Class 9 Commerce – Classification & Characteristics of Human Wants

Key Terms Explained

1. Meaning of Want: A desire for a good, backed by the means and willingness to make an effort to get it.
2. Necessaries: Wants without which life is difficult. Three kinds: (i) of existence – food, clothing, shelter; (ii) of efficiency – education, health; (iii) of convention – marriage, festivals.
3. Comforts: Wants that make life convenient but are not essential, e.g. fan, cooler, bicycle, furniture.
4. Luxuries: Wants giving show and extra pleasure at high cost, e.g. car, AC, diamond jewellery.
5. Wants are Unlimited: As one want is satisfied, another arises, so wants never end.
6. A Particular Want can be Satisfied: Repeated consumption of a good satisfies that want (hunger ends after a meal).
7. Wants Recur: Wants such as hunger and thirst return again and again.
8. Substitutes and Complements: Tea–coffee (substitutes); car–petrol (complements).
9. Wants become Habits: Repeated consumption turns a want into a habit, e.g. drinking tea.
10. Relative Intensity: Wants differ in urgency; the more urgent want is satisfied first.
11. Factors Affecting Wants: Income, education, climate, fashion, advertising and social conditions.

Solved Example

Question: Classify as necessary, comfort or luxury: bread, cooler, diamond ring, school books, car.
Step 1: Bread – essential for life → necessary (of existence).
Step 2: School books – raise efficiency → necessary (of efficiency).
Step 3: Cooler – gives convenience, not essential → comfort.
Step 4: Diamond ring – only for show, very costly → luxury.
Step 5: Car – generally costly and for pleasure → luxury.
Answer: Necessaries 2, comfort 1, luxuries 2.

Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is a want?A desire for a good backed by ability to buy and willingness to make an effort.
Q2. Classify human wants.Necessaries (of existence, efficiency, convention), comforts and luxuries.
Q3. Why are wants unlimited?When one want is met a new one arises, so they never end.
Q4. Differentiate comforts and luxuries.Comforts give convenience at moderate cost (fan); luxuries give show at high cost (car).
Q5. Explain substitute and complementary wants.Substitutes: tea–coffee. Complements: pen–ink, car–petrol.
Q6. State the characteristics of wants.Unlimited, satisfiable, recurring, substitutes, complements, habit-forming, differing intensity and influenced by income etc.
Q7. Name factors affecting wants.Income, education, climate, advertising, fashion and social status.
Q8. How does a good’s category change over time?A mobile phone was once a luxury; now it is a necessity for many.
