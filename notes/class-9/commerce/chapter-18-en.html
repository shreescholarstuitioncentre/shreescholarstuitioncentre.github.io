✎ Qualities of a Good Money Material

Class 9 · Commerce · Chapter 19 · UP Board

1. Why Qualities Matter

Not every material can be money. Money must be trusted, handled daily and kept for long, so the material chosen should have certain qualities. Metals like gold and silver were chosen in the past mainly for these reasons.

3. Each Quality Explained

QualityMeaningExample / Reason
General acceptabilityEveryone accepts it in payment.Rupee notes are accepted in every shop; a foreign coin may not be.
PortabilityLarge value in small weight and size.Gold is easier to carry than iron, a ₹500 note than 500 coins.
DurabilityDoes not wear out or perish soon.Metal coins last long; wheat or fruit spoil.
DivisibilityCan be divided into small units without loss of value.₹100 = 2 × ₹50 = 10 × ₹10.
Homogeneity (uniformity)All units of the same denomination are identical.Any two ₹10 coins are equal; two cows are not.
Stability of valueValue does not change often.Unstable money harms debtors and creditors.
CognizabilityEasy to recognise and test.Marks, colours and security threads on notes.
Difficult to counterfeitCannot be copied easily.Watermark, micro-lettering.
MalleabilityCan be shaped into coins.Metals can be stamped.
Limited supplyNot available in unlimited quantity.If anyone could produce it, it would lose value.
Extra quality for paper money – Elasticity: supply can be increased or reduced as the economy needs, but it must be controlled by the RBI to avoid inflation.

4. Why Gold & Silver Were Used

They are scarce, durable, divisible, portable, uniform, easily recognised and of fairly stable value. That is why they were preferred over cattle or grain.

5. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: A coin lasts 20 years and a note lasts 2 years. How many times more durable is the coin?

To find: ratio of lives.
Step 1: Coin = 20 years, note = 2 years.
Step 2: Ratio = 20 / 2.
Step 3: = 10 times.

Intermediate: Express ₹500 using ₹100 notes only. How many notes? If each note weighs 1 g, find the weight to carry ₹1,00,000.

Step 1: ₹500 = 5 notes of ₹100.
Step 2: ₹1,00,000 / 100 = 1,000 notes.
Step 3: Weight = 1,000 × 1 g = 1,000 g = 1 kg.

Hard: Assume a ₹1 coin weighs 5 g and a ₹500 note weighs 1 g. Prove that carrying ₹1,00,000 in ₹1 coins is 2,500 times heavier than in ₹500 notes.

To prove: weight(coins) ÷ weight(notes) = 2,500.
Step 1: Coins needed = 1,00,000; weight = 1,00,000 × 5 g = 5,00,000 g = 500 kg.
Step 2: Notes needed = 1,00,000 / 500 = 200; weight = 200 × 1 g = 200 g.
Step 3: Ratio = 5,00,000 / 200 = 2,500. Hence proved (this shows the importance of portability).

6. Question & Answer Bank

A. MCQs

1. Money should be accepted by all. This quality is—
(a) Durability (b) General acceptability (c) Portability (d) Elasticity

Ans: (b).

2. Wheat is a poor money material because it is not—
(a) Durable (b) Edible (c) Cheap (d) Common

Ans: (a). It spoils.

3. Dividing money into small units refers to—
(a) Homogeneity (b) Divisibility (c) Stability (d) Portability

Ans: (b).

4. All units of the same denomination being equal is—
(a) Uniformity (b) Scarcity (c) Elasticity (d) Acceptability

Ans: (a).

5. Which is NOT a quality of good money?
(a) Durability (b) Perishability (c) Portability (d) Divisibility

Ans: (b).

6. Watermarks on notes help in—
(a) Preventing counterfeiting (b) Weight (c) Divisibility (d) Elasticity

Ans: (a).

B. Short Answers

7. What is portability?

The ability to carry large value in small size and weight.

8. Why must money have stable value?

If value keeps changing, lenders and borrowers, workers and employers suffer losses and trust falls.

9. What is homogeneity?

All units of a denomination are exactly alike, so no one prefers one over another.

10. Why should the supply of money be limited?

Unlimited supply reduces its value and causes inflation.

11. What is cognizability?

The ease of recognising genuine money quickly.

12. Is cattle good money material? Give one reason.

No. It is not divisible or portable, and it can die.
