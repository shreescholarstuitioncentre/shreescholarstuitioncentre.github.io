Class 9 · Commerce · Chapter 20 · UP Board

1. Meaning of Note Issue

Note issue means printing and putting paper currency into circulation. Because notes have almost no metal value, the issuing authority (in India, the RBI) must follow some rules so that people keep their trust and the money supply stays under control. Those rules are called the principles and the ways of applying them are called methods.

4. Methods of Note Issue

1. Fixed fiduciary system: a fixed amount of notes is issued against government securities (no metal). Notes above it need 100% gold/silver. Used in England (1844) and by India earlier.
2. Proportional reserve system: reserve = fixed % of notes issued (e.g., 40%). More elastic, but needs reserve to grow with notes.
3. Minimum reserve system: a fixed minimum reserve is held; notes can be issued above it freely. India follows this since 1957: ₹200 crore reserve (₹115 crore gold + ₹85 crore foreign securities).
4. Maximum limit system: the government fixes the highest total of notes (e.g., in France).
5. 100% reserve system: every note backed by full reserve; very safe but unrealistic.

6. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: Fixed fiduciary limit is ₹400 crore. Notes issued are ₹1,000 crore. Find the reserve needed.

To find: reserve.
Step 1: Notes above the limit = 1,000 − 400 = 600.
Step 2: These need 100% reserve.
Step 3: Reserve = ₹600 crore.

Intermediate: Under a 25% proportional system the reserve is ₹150 crore. Find the maximum notes.

Step 1: Reserve = 25% of notes, so notes = reserve ÷ 0.25.
Step 2: = 150 ÷ 0.25.
Step 3: = ₹600 crore.

Hard: Under the minimum reserve system (₹200 crore), prove that the reserve ratio falls as notes rise: compare notes of ₹1,000 crore and ₹2,000 crore.

To prove: ratio at 2,000 < ratio at 1,000.
Step 1: Ratio = reserve ÷ notes × 100.
Step 2: At 1,000 crore: 200 / 1,000 × 100 = 20%.
Step 3: At 2,000 crore: 200 / 2,000 × 100 = 10%.
Step 4: 10% < 20%, so the ratio falls while the reserve stays fixed. Hence proved (this shows the elasticity of the system).

A. MCQs

1. Which principle is rigid but very safe?
(a) Banking (b) Currency (c) Both (d) None

Ans: (b) Currency principle.

2. India follows the—
(a) Fixed fiduciary (b) Minimum reserve (c) Maximum limit (d) 100% reserve system

Ans: (b).

3. Minimum reserve of India is—
(a) ₹100 crore (b) ₹200 crore (c) ₹300 crore (d) ₹400 crore

Ans: (b) ₹200 crore.

4. Which principle gives elastic currency?
(a) Currency (b) Banking (c) 100% reserve (d) Fiduciary

Ans: (b).

5. Notes above the fiduciary limit need—
(a) No reserve (b) 100% metal reserve (c) 50% reserve (d) Tax

Ans: (b).

6. Who issues notes in India?
(a) SBI (b) RBI (c) SEBI (d) NABARD

Ans: (b).

B. Short Answers

7. What is note issue?

Putting paper currency into circulation by the central bank.

8. State the currency principle.

Notes beyond a fixed limit must be fully backed by gold/silver.

9. State the banking principle.

Notes can be issued as trade needs, provided the bank can convert them on demand.

10. What is the fixed fiduciary system?

A fixed value of notes is issued against securities, and any more needs 100% metal reserve.

11. What is the proportional reserve system?

A fixed percentage of the notes issued is kept as reserve.

12. Why is a minimum reserve system elastic?

The reserve is fixed, so notes can rise with the economy’s needs.
