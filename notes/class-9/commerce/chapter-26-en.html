Class 9 Commerce – Consumption: Meaning, Kinds & Importance

Key Terms Explained

1. Meaning of Consumption: Using goods and services to satisfy human wants is called consumption, e.g. eating food, wearing clothes, getting medical treatment.
2. Consumer: A person who uses goods or services to satisfy his wants.
3. Direct Consumption: Use of a good directly satisfies a want, e.g. eating bread, reading a book.
4. Indirect Consumption: Use of a good in further production, e.g. raw material or machines in a factory.
5. Productive Consumption: Consumption that raises working efficiency, e.g. a labourer’s nutritious food, a student’s education.
6. Unproductive Consumption: Consumption that does not raise or even lowers efficiency, e.g. intoxicants, gambling, wasteful spending.
7. Single-use Consumption: The good is used up at once, e.g. milk, food, coal.
8. Durable Consumption: The good serves repeatedly for a long time, e.g. fridge, house, bicycle.
9. Importance of Consumption: Consumption is the final aim of production. Higher demand raises output, jobs, income and living standard. It also affects saving, investment and growth.
10. Saving: Part of income not spent on consumption. Saving = Income − Consumption.

Solved Example

Question: Ramu’s monthly income is ₹20,000 and he spends ₹15,000 on consumption. Find his saving and the percentage of income spent on consumption.
Given: Income = ₹20,000, Consumption = ₹15,000.
Step 1: Saving = Income − Consumption = 20,000 − 15,000 = ₹5,000.
Step 2: % consumed = (15,000 ÷ 20,000) × 100 = 75%.
Step 3: % saved = 100 − 75 = 25%.
Answer: Saving is ₹5,000; 75% of income is spent on consumption.

Short & Long Q&A

Q1. What is consumption?Use of goods and services to satisfy wants is called consumption.
Q2. Differentiate direct and indirect consumption.In direct consumption the good satisfies a want itself (bread); in indirect it helps production (machine).
Q3. Explain productive and unproductive consumption.Consumption raising efficiency and output is productive (nutritious food); otherwise it is unproductive (intoxicants).
Q4. Give two examples each of single-use and durable goods.Single-use: milk, food. Durable: fridge, bicycle.
Q5. Write the importance of consumption.It is the aim of production, raises demand, output and employment, improves living standard and supports economic growth.
Q6. How is saving calculated?Saving = Income − Consumption.
Q7. Relate consumption and production.Production is meant for consumption, and consumption creates demand that motivates production.
Q8. How does consumption affect living standard?Better consumption improves health, education and comforts, raising the standard of living.
