1. Barter System (Vastu Vinimay)

Before money, people exchanged goods for goods. E.g., a farmer gave 10 kg wheat to get 1 pair of shoes. This is called barter.

Difficulties of barter:
1. Double coincidence of wants – both persons must need each other’s goods.
2. No common measure of value – how many mangoes = 1 shirt?
3. Indivisibility – a cow cannot be split to buy a small item.
4. No store of value – goods like fruit, milk spoil.
5. No deferred payment – hard to settle loans in goods.
6. Difficult to transport & store goods.

3. Meaning & Functions of Money

Money is anything generally accepted by everyone as a medium of exchange, a measure of value and a store of value.

TypeFunctionExplanation
PrimaryMedium of exchangeBuy/sell without needing double coincidence.
Measure of valueAll goods are priced in ₹, so comparing is easy.
SecondaryStore of valueSavings can be kept for the future.
Standard of deferred paymentLoans are taken and repaid later in money.
Transfer of valueWealth moves from one place or person to another.

4. Qualities of Good Money

General acceptability · Durability · Portability · Divisibility · Uniformity · Stable value · Difficult to forge · Limited supply.

5. Types & Growth of Modern Money

Legal tender: must be accepted by law (coins, notes). Credit money: cheque, draft, debit/credit card. Digital money: UPI, net banking, wallets.
In India, notes are issued by the RBI; coins and ₹1 notes by the Government of India (Ministry of Finance).
Growth: banks accepted deposits → cheques replaced cash → plastic cards → mobile/UPI payments. Money has grown from physical to digital.

6. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: In barter, 1 cow = 20 sacks of wheat. If wheat is ₹1,500 per sack, find the cow’s price.

To find: price of cow.
Step 1: Cow = 20 sacks.
Step 2: Price = 20 × 1,500.
Step 3: = ₹30,000. (Money made the common measure easy.)

Intermediate: There are 5 goods. How many exchange rates are needed in barter, and how many prices with money?

Formula: barter rates = n(n−1)/2; with money = n (one price per good).
Step 1: n = 5.
Step 2: 5 × 4 / 2 = 10 rates.
Step 3: With money only 5 prices.

Hard: Prove that for 100 goods money saves 4,850 price lists.

To prove: difference = n(n−1)/2 − n for n = 100.
Step 1: n(n−1)/2 = 100 × 99 / 2 = 4,950.
Step 2: With money = 100.
Step 3: 4,950 − 100 = 4,850. Hence proved.

A. MCQs

1. Exchange of goods for goods is called—

Ans: (b). Barter is direct goods-for-goods exchange.

(a) SBI (b) Finance Ministry (c) RBI (d) NABARD

Ans: (c) RBI (₹1 note is issued by the Government).

3. Which is a primary function of money?

Ans: (b).

4. Double coincidence of wants relates to—
(a) Money (b) Barter (c) Credit (d) Banking

Ans: (b).

5. Which is NOT a quality of good money?
(a) Durability (b) Portability (c) Perishability (d) Divisibility

Ans: (c). Money must not perish.

6. UPI is a form of—
(a) Coin (b) Digital money (c) Barter (d) Commodity money

Ans: (b).

B. Very Short / Short Answers

7. Define money.

Anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange, measure and store of value.

8. What is legal tender?

Money that the law compels everyone to accept in payment, e.g., notes and coins.

9. Name two functions of money.

Medium of exchange; measure of value.

10. Why is divisibility needed?

So that small and large payments can be made exactly (₹1, ₹5, ₹100…).

11. Why did metal replace commodities as money?

Metals are durable, divisible, portable and scarce, while grain and cattle perish or are bulky.

12. What is credit money?

Cheques, drafts and cards, which transfer deposits rather than cash.
