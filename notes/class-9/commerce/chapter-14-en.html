Barter System (Class 9 Commerce)

1. Meaning

Barter System is the system of direct exchange of goods for goods (or services for services) without using money. Example: a farmer gives 10 kg wheat to a weaver and takes cloth. It was used before money was invented, and is still seen in some villages and tribal areas.

2. Features

  • Goods are exchanged directly.
  • No money or common measure of value.
  • Needs mutual agreement on the exchange ratio.
  • Suitable for small, simple societies.

3. How it Works

Person A has wheat and needs cloth. Person B has cloth and needs wheat. They agree on a rate (e.g., 1 sack wheat = 5 metre cloth) and exchange. This needs a double coincidence of wants.

4. Merits

  • Simple; no need of money.
  • No inflation or currency problems.
  • Builds mutual cooperation.
  • Suitable for early societies.

5. Demerits (Difficulties)

DifficultyExplanation
Double coincidence of wantsBoth persons must want each other's goods at the same time
No common measure of valueHard to fix rate between many goods
Indivisibility of goodsA cow cannot be cut to buy small items
No store of valueGoods like fruits and grain spoil
No deferred paymentsLoans difficult to give or repay in goods
Difficulty of transportHeavy goods hard to carry

6. Barter vs Money Exchange

BasisBarterMoney

7. Why Barter Ended

With growth of trade, difficulties of barter increased. Society invented money (first commodity money like cattle, shells, metals; then coins; then paper and digital money) to remove these problems.

9. Solved Example (step by step)

Q. (a) If 1 cow = 8 sacks of wheat and 1 sack of wheat = 4 kg sugar, find sugar for 1 cow. (b) In a barter market of 10 goods, how many exchange rates are needed? How many prices are needed if money is used?
  1. Step 1 (a): 1 cow = 8 sacks wheat.
  2. Step 2: 1 sack = 4 kg sugar, so 8 sacks = 8 × 4 = 32 kg sugar.
  3. Step 3: So 1 cow = 32 kg sugar.
  4. Step 4 (b): With n goods, rates needed = n(n − 1) / 2.
  5. Step 5: For n = 10: 10 × 9 / 2 = 45 exchange rates.
  6. Step 6: With money, each good has one price, so only 10 prices ✔
  7. Conclusion: Money simplifies valuation (45 → 10).

10. Short & Long Questions

Q1. What is barter system?

Direct exchange of goods for goods without money.

Q2. What is double coincidence of wants?

Both persons must want what the other has at the same time.

Q3. State two merits of barter.

Simple and free from inflation.

Q4. State two demerits.

Double coincidence of wants and no common measure of value.

Q5. Why can goods not be stored as wealth in barter?

Goods may spoil or lose value.

Q6. Why was money invented?

To remove the difficulties of barter.

Q7. Explain difficulties of barter. (Long)

Double coincidence of wants, no measure of value, indivisibility, no store of value, no deferred payments and transport difficulty; see section 5.

Q8. Compare barter and money exchange. (Long)

See section 6: money needs no double coincidence, gives a common measure, easy storage and loans.
