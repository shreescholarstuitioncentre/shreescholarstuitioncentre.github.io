Indian System of Book-Keeping (Class 9 Commerce)

1. Meaning

The Indian System of Book-Keeping (Deshi Nama / Bahi-Khata) is the traditional method used for centuries by Indian traders (Marwaris, Gujaratis, Banias). It follows the double entry principle in a local style, written in Mahajani / Hindi script in a bound book called Bahi. Here Nam / Naame = Debit and Jama = Credit.

2. Features

  • Based on double entry; written in local scripts.
  • Accounts are opened at Deepawali with Chopda Pujan ("Shri Ganeshaya Namah" on the first page).
  • Uses red cloth-bound books called Bahi (Bahi-Khata).
  • Uses Hundi (bill of exchange) for credit.
  • Simple, cheap and easily understood by traders.

6. Merits & Demerits

Merits: simple; cheap; local language; suited to small traders; honest tradition of hundi.
Demerits: not standardised; hard for outsiders and tax officers; limited modern accounting facilities.

8. Solved Example (step by step)

Q. Write the Indian-style entries (Nam–Jama): (i) Ram started business with ₹50,000 cash. (ii) Bought goods for cash ₹20,000.
  1. (i) Cash comes in → Nam: Rokad Khata ₹50,000; owner gives capital → Jama: Capital Khata ₹50,000.
  2. (ii) Goods (Kharid) come in → Nam: Kharid Khata ₹20,000; cash goes out → Jama: Rokad Khata ₹20,000.
  3. Check: Total Nam ₹70,000 = Total Jama ₹70,000 ✔

9. Short & Long Questions

Q1. What is the Indian system of book-keeping?

The traditional double-entry method used by Indian traders in local script in Bahi books.

Q2. What is Bahi-Khata?

The bound book in which Indian traders keep accounts.

Q3. What is Nam and Jama?

Nam means debit and Jama means credit.

Q4. What is a Hundi?

An Indian bill of exchange used for credit.

Q5. When is the year opened?

On Deepawali with Chopda Pujan.

Q6. What is Tarij?

Trial balance.

Q7. Explain features of the Indian system. (Long)

Double-entry based, local script, Bahi books, year opens on Deepawali, hundi used, simple and cheap.

Q8. Differentiate Indian and Western systems. (Long)

See section 5: language, books, start of year, terms and users differ.
