✎ Present Currency System in India

Class 9 · Commerce · Chapter 21 · UP Board

1. Meaning & Nature

The currency system is the set of rules by which a country issues, controls and circulates its money. India today has a managed paper currency standard: money is not linked to gold or silver for payment, and the RBI manages its supply under the minimum reserve system (₹200 crore: ₹115 crore gold + ₹85 crore foreign securities).

Main features:
1. Unit of money is the rupee (₹); 1 rupee = 100 paise (decimal system since 1957).
2. Notes are issued by the RBI; the ₹1 note and all coins by the Government of India (Ministry of Finance).
3. Notes are unlimited legal tender; coins are limited legal tender.
4. Paper currency is inconvertible (fiat); it is not exchanged for gold.
5. Supply is managed by the RBI according to the economy’s needs (elastic).
6. Bank deposits (cheques, UPI) are also part of the money supply.

Recent changes: In 2016 old ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes were demonetised and new ₹500 and ₹2,000 notes were introduced. The ₹2,000 note was later withdrawn from fresh circulation (2023). Digital payments (UPI, cards) have grown fast.

5. Solved Examples (Step by Step)

Easy: Convert ₹5.75 into paise.

To find: paise.
Step 1: 1 rupee = 100 paise.
Step 2: 5.75 × 100.
Step 3: = 575 paise.

Intermediate: Currency in circulation is ₹2,000 crore, of which coins are ₹100 crore. Find the percentage of coins and of notes.

Step 1: Coins = (100 / 2,000) × 100 = 5%.
Step 2: Notes = 2,000 − 100 = ₹1,900 crore.
Step 3: Notes % = 100 − 5 = 95%.

Hard: Currency in circulation is ₹1,000 crore; banks hold ₹150 crore as cash; demand deposits are ₹3,000 crore. Prove that money supply (public’s currency + deposits) = ₹3,850 crore.

To prove: M = currency with public + deposits.
Step 1: Currency with public = 1,000 − 150 = 850.
Step 2: Add deposits: 850 + 3,000.
Step 3: = ₹3,850 crore. Hence proved.

B. Short Answers

7. What is a currency system?

The set of rules by which a country issues, controls and circulates money.

8. Who issues coins in India?

The Government of India.

9. What is legal tender?

Money that must be accepted in settlement of debts by law.

10. What is the decimal coinage system?

The system in which 1 rupee = 100 paise, in use since 1957.

11. What is demonetisation?

Withdrawing a currency denomination’s legal tender status (done for ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes in 2016).

12. Name two parts of money supply.

Currency (notes and coins) and bank deposits.
