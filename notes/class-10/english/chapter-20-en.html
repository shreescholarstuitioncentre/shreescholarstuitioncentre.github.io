🐶 A Triumph of Surgery

Class 10 · English (Footprints Without Feet) · Supplementary Chapter · By James Herriot

1. About the Author

James Herriot is the pen name of Alf Wight (1916–1995), a British veterinary surgeon (vet) and writer. He was born in Sunderland, England, grew up in Glasgow, Scotland, and spent most of his working life as a country vet in Yorkshire. He wrote warm and humorous books about his experiences with animals and their owners, such as All Creatures Great and Small and If Only They Could Talk.

  • Style: gentle humour, simple language, and fond observation of people and animals.
  • In this story, he writes as the narrator, a young vet who works with his senior, Siegfried Farnon.
  • The story is set in the Yorkshire countryside, England, in the 1930s.

4. Detailed Summary and Explanation

Part 1 — Mrs Pumphrey and Tricki-Woo

Mrs Pumphrey is a rich, kind-hearted widow who lives in a big house with servants. She is a client of the veterinary practice where Herriot works. Her great love is her Pekingese dog, Tricki-Woo. She treats him like a child, calls herself his "mother", and calls Herriot his "uncle". She is so fond of him that she gives him far too much food, rich meals, cakes, chocolates and cream, and hardly allows him any exercise.

Part 2 — Tricki Falls "Ill"

One day Mrs Pumphrey calls the surgery in great worry. Tricki-Woo is not himself: he has no energy, he is breathless and uninterested in food and walks, and she fears he is seriously ill. Herriot visits and finds Tricki lying on a pile of cushions, very fat and lazy. He looks him over carefully and realises that nothing is really wrong with the dog except that he is terribly overfed and has no exercise.

Part 3 — Herriot's Diagnosis

Herriot knows Mrs Pumphrey adores Tricki, so he speaks to her tactfully but firmly. He tells her that the dog needs a strict, simple diet and plenty of exercise. Mrs Pumphrey is shocked and anxious, and says Tricki hardly eats anything at all. Herriot knows that she will not be able to refuse the dog's every wish at home, so he suggests that Tricki stay at the surgery for a while to be treated. Mrs Pumphrey is upset at being separated from her darling but, trusting the vet, she agrees.

Part 4 — Tricki at the Surgery

At the surgery, Tricki is placed with other dogs. In the first days he is gloomy and refuses to join in, but gradually, with plain food in small amounts and daily play with the other dogs, he begins to take interest in life. Meanwhile, Mrs Pumphrey anxiously phones to ask about him, and, in her love and gratitude, she keeps sending gifts and hampers. The vets are amused by this. Tricki grows slim and lively; he even chases and plays freely.

Part 5 — Mrs Pumphrey Collects Tricki

When Mrs Pumphrey arrives to take Tricki home, she can hardly believe her eyes. Her dog is fit, bright and full of energy, and he rushes to her happily. She is overjoyed and thinks that Herriot has performed a miraculous operation. She thanks him again and again for the "wonderful cure", although Herriot has not done any surgery; he has only put the dog on a proper diet and let him exercise. Herriot does not make a fuss and quietly accepts her thanks.

💡 Key insight: Tricki was not ill with a disease. His trouble was caused by too much love in the wrong form: overfeeding. The cure was simple, a healthy diet and exercise.

7. Character Sketches

Mrs Pumphrey

  • Rich and generous: A wealthy widow who gives lavish gifts to those who help her pet.
  • Kind-hearted and affectionate: She loves Tricki-Woo like a child and is deeply worried about him.
  • Foolishly indulgent: Her love takes the wrong form: she overfeeds and pampers him and so harms him.
  • Gullible and easily excited: She believes the dog was saved by a miracle operation.
  • Grateful: She thanks Herriot warmly and sends gifts out of sincere gratitude.

James Herriot

  • Skilled and honest vet: He quickly sees that Tricki's only problem is overfeeding.
  • Tactful: He manages a very emotional and wealthy client without offending her.
  • Practical and firm: He knows the dog must be separated from Mrs Pumphrey's pampering to recover.
  • Humorous and modest: He narrates the story with gentle fun and accepts credit quietly.

Tricki-Woo

  • A Pekingese dog, spoilt by too much food and attention.
  • At first lazy and sleepy; later, playful and healthy when he gets exercise and the company of other dogs.
  • He shows that animals, like people, suffer from too much indulgence.

8. Central Idea (Board Answer)

Central Idea / Theme: A Triumph of Surgery is a humorous story about Mrs Pumphrey, who ruins her Pekingese dog Tricki-Woo's health by overfeeding and pampering him. When the dog becomes fat and listless, she thinks he is seriously ill. The young vet, James Herriot, sees that nothing is wrong except overindulgence, and he cures Tricki simply by keeping him at the surgery on a plain diet with exercise and company. When Mrs Pumphrey sees the transformation, she believes a marvellous operation has taken place and showers Herriot with thanks and gifts. The story shows that too much love in the form of overindulgence can be harmful, and that real care needs discipline, and it also highlights Herriot's tact and sense of humour.

Key Points to Remember

  • The dog's problem was overeating and lack of exercise, not a disease.
  • The cure was diet, exercise and company; no actual operation was done.
  • The title is ironic: the "surgery" is the vet's clinic, not an operation.
  • The story's humour comes from Mrs Pumphrey's foolish love and Herriot's quiet wit.

12. Important Questions & Answers

A. Short Answer Questions (30–40 words)

Q1. Who is Tricki-Woo?

Tricki-Woo is a Pekingese dog belonging to Mrs Pumphrey, a rich widow. She loves him like a child and spoils him with too much food, so he becomes fat, lazy and unwell.

Q2. What was really wrong with Tricki-Woo?

Tricki had no real disease. He was suffering because he was overfed and given no exercise. This made him fat, sleepy and breathless, and Mrs Pumphrey wrongly feared that he was seriously ill.

Q3. How did Mrs Pumphrey pamper Tricki?

She gave him rich meals, cakes, chocolates and cream, called herself his mother and treated him like a child. She allowed him little exercise and never refused him anything.

Q4. Why did Herriot keep Tricki at the surgery?

Herriot knew that Mrs Pumphrey would keep overfeeding the dog at home. At the surgery Tricki could be put on a plain diet, get exercise and play with other dogs, away from her pampering.

Q5. Why did Mrs Pumphrey send gifts to the surgery?

Mrs Pumphrey was rich, generous and very grateful that Herriot was looking after Tricki. She sent hampers and gifts to show her thanks and her love for the dog.

Q6. Why is the title "A Triumph of Surgery" ironic?

No operation was done. Tricki was cured by diet and exercise at the vet's clinic ("surgery"). Mrs Pumphrey believed it was a miracle operation, so the title is a pun and irony.

B. Long Answer Questions (100–120 words)

Q1. Explain the central idea of "A Triumph of Surgery".

The story is about Mrs Pumphrey, a rich widow, who ruins the health of her Pekingese dog Tricki-Woo by overfeeding and pampering him. When the dog becomes fat and listless, she thinks he is seriously ill and calls the vet, James Herriot. Herriot sees that the trouble is only overindulgence and cures Tricki by keeping him at the surgery on a plain diet with exercise and company. Mrs Pumphrey is amazed at the result and thinks a miracle operation has been done. The story shows that excessive love can harm, and that real care requires discipline, and it also shows Herriot's tact and humour.

Q2. Write a character sketch of Mrs Pumphrey.

Mrs Pumphrey is a rich, kind-hearted widow who loves her Pekingese, Tricki-Woo, like a son. She is generous, grateful and sincere, sending gifts to those who help her pet. But her love is foolish: she overfeeds and pampers Tricki until he is fat and unwell. She is also gullible and excitable, quickly panicking about a non-existent disease and believing that a miracle operation cured him. Through her, the author gently makes fun of those who spoil their pets and shows that love without sense can do harm.

Q3. How does Herriot handle Mrs Pumphrey and Tricki? What qualities does he show?

Herriot examines Tricki carefully and finds that he is only overfed. He tells Mrs Pumphrey politely but firmly that the dog needs a strict diet and exercise. Knowing she would spoil Tricki at home, he suggests the dog stay at the surgery. He treats Tricki with plain food and games with other dogs, and does not argue when Mrs Pumphrey calls it surgery. Thus he shows professional skill, tact, firmness, patience and a good sense of humour. He cures the dog without hurting the feelings of his wealthy client.

Q4. What is the lesson of the story?

The story teaches that too much love, in the form of indulgence, can harm those we love, whether pets or people. Real love includes discipline, a healthy routine and some strictness. Mrs Pumphrey's pampering almost destroyed Tricki's health, while a simple diet and exercise restored it. The story also shows that we should seek advice from experts and accept their guidance even when it is hard. It teaches the value of tact, kindness and humour in handling difficult situations.
