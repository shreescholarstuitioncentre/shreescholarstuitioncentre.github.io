🧜‍♀️ Amanda!

Class 10 · English (First Flight) · Poem – Chapter 10 · By Robin Klein

1. About the Poet

Robin Klein (born 1936) is an Australian author who writes mainly for children and young adults. Her stories and poems are known for their humour, her sharp understanding of young people's feelings, and her lively, easy-to-read style. Her books are popular for showing everyday family life with warmth and fun.

  • Type of writing: light, humorous verse with a serious message underneath.
  • In this poem she looks at the world through a child's eyes, and shows how a child feels when adults keep giving orders.

4. Detailed Explanation (Part by Part)

The poem is built on two voices. One is the mother, who keeps giving Amanda orders. The other is Amanda, whose thoughts drift away into dreams of freedom. The mother's orders come one after another, and Amanda's daydreams interrupt them like short escapes.

Part 1 — The Mother's Constant Orders

The poem opens with the mother scolding Amanda about small habits: she must not bite her nails, she must not slouch or hunch her shoulders, she must sit up straight, and she must take care of her shoes and clothes. The mother also reminds her about her homework and about not eating sweets that spoil her health or looks. These are the kinds of things many parents say, and they may even be well meant. But there are so many of them, and they come so often, that Amanda feels she is never allowed to simply be. The repeated use of her name shows that she is being addressed again and again.

Part 2 — Amanda's First Daydream: The Mermaid

While her mother talks, Amanda's mind slips away. She imagines she is a mermaid, resting on a rock in a calm, green sea, with nothing to do and nobody to tell her how to sit or behave. The sea is peaceful and still; the only creature there is she herself. The mermaid is a perfect image of freedom, ease and peace, the very opposite of her tidy, rule-filled life at home.

Part 3 — The Scolding Goes On

Her mother's voice returns and the orders continue: tidy your room, behave properly, and look after your things. The poet makes the mother's speech feel endless and tiring. We can see that Amanda is not really angry; she is bored, restless and wants space. She does not argue back openly, but retreats inside herself.

Part 4 — Amanda's Second Daydream: The Orphan

Amanda then imagines that she is an orphan, a child with no parents. In her daydream, this means she would be alone and free, with nobody to give her orders. Of course, this is an exaggeration, and a child's way of expressing how much she longs for freedom, not a real wish to lose her family. The poet shows it with gentle humour but also with sadness: a child who feels so controlled that being alone seems better.

Part 5 — The Ending

The poem ends by returning to the real world, where the mother is still talking and Amanda is still being told what to do. Nothing has changed outside; only Amanda's mind has found a little room to breathe. The ending leaves the reader thoughtful: Are we giving children enough freedom?

💡 Key insight: The poem is not against parents. It reminds us that too much control and nagging can make a child feel trapped, and that children need space to dream and be themselves.

6. Central Idea (Board Answer)

Central Idea / Theme: In Amanda!, Robin Klein shows a young girl who is constantly told by her mother what to do and what not to do: how to sit, what to eat, how to look after her habits and her belongings. Although the mother may be well meaning, the endless instructions make Amanda feel restless and trapped. To escape, she daydreams: she imagines herself as a mermaid resting calmly in the sea and as an orphan who is free of anyone's commands. These images stand for her longing for freedom, peace and independence. The poem gently criticises over-controlling parents and conveys the message that children need freedom, understanding and space to be themselves.

Key Points to Remember

  • The poem shows the conflict between a child's wish for freedom and a parent's constant control.
  • Amanda does not argue; she escapes into imagination.
  • The mermaid and the orphan are symbols of freedom and independence.
  • The tone is humorous but the message is serious: children need freedom and understanding.

10. Important Questions & Answers

A. Short Answer Questions (30–40 words)

Q1. Why does Amanda's mother keep scolding her?

The mother wants Amanda to have good habits, be tidy, healthy and disciplined. She corrects her posture, nail-biting, homework and eating. But she does it so often that Amanda feels constantly controlled and annoyed.

Q2. How does Amanda react to her mother's scolding?

Amanda does not argue. Instead she escapes into daydreams. She imagines being a mermaid on a rock in a calm sea and an orphan with no one to command her, which gives her peace and freedom in her mind.

Q3. Why does Amanda imagine being a mermaid?

A mermaid lives in a calm, peaceful sea with nothing to do and nobody to give orders. This image represents the freedom, ease and peace that Amanda lacks at home, so she dreams of it.

Q4. Why does Amanda want to be an orphan?

Amanda feels so controlled that she imagines being an orphan, who would be alone and free from orders. It is an exaggeration that shows how strongly she longs for freedom, not a real wish to lose her family.

Q5. What is the tone of the poem?

The tone is light and humorous, but also sympathetic and slightly sad. The poet smiles at the mother's endless orders while feeling for Amanda's longing for freedom.

Q6. What message does the poem give?

The poem tells parents not to nag and over-control children, because children need freedom, understanding and space to dream and be themselves. Guidance is needed, but not constant pressure.

B. Long Answer Questions (100–120 words)

Q1. Explain the central idea of the poem "Amanda!".

In "Amanda!", Robin Klein presents a girl whose mother constantly instructs her about posture, habits, homework, food and belongings. Though the mother may mean well, the endless orders make Amanda restless and trapped. To escape, she daydreams of being a mermaid resting calmly in the sea and an orphan who has no one to command her. These images stand for her longing for freedom, peace and independence. The poem gently criticises over-controlling parents, and conveys that children need freedom, understanding and space to be themselves. Amanda's escape into imagination shows how a child copes when she cannot express her feelings openly.

Q2. How does the poet contrast the mother's world with Amanda's world?

The poet shows the mother's world as noisy, busy and full of rules: orders about nails, posture, homework, chocolate and a tidy room come one after another. Amanda's world, in contrast, is quiet and free: a mermaid resting on a rock in a calm sea, or an orphan who needs to answer to nobody. The two voices alternate throughout the poem, so we feel the gap between them. The repetition of Amanda's name imitates nagging, while the peaceful images of the sea create calm. This contrast brings out the child's longing for freedom.

Q3. Do you think Amanda's mother is right in the way she treats Amanda? Give reasons.

The mother has good intentions: she wants Amanda to be healthy, tidy and well-behaved, and a little guidance is necessary. But her way is wrong because she nags continuously about small things and gives Amanda no space or freedom. Constant scolding makes a child feel unloved and controlled, and pushes her to escape into daydreams, even to the point of wishing to be an orphan. A better way would be to guide her kindly, explain things, trust her, and allow her time to relax and imagine. Parents should balance care with freedom, as the poem suggests.
