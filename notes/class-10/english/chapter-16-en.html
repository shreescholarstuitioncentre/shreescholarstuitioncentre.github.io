2. Central Idea (as expected in the CBSE Board Exam)

'The Tale of Custard the Dragon' is a funny story poem about courage and cowardice. Belinda and her pets Ink, Blink and Mustard boast that they are very brave, while Custard the dragon says he is a coward and cries for a safe cage. When a pirate attacks the house, all the 'brave' ones run away, and it is Custard who fights the pirate and defeats him. After that, Custard goes back to being his timid self.

The poet wants to tell us that real courage is not about boasting; it is shown in times of danger. A person who seems timid may have hidden strength, and those who talk big may not act when needed. So we should not judge people by their appearance or their words.

  • True courage shows in action, not in words: the boasters run away; Custard fights.
  • Appearances deceive: the 'coward' is the real hero of the poem.
  • Everyone has hidden strength: even a timid person can be brave in a crisis.
  • Humour and irony: the poet makes a serious idea light and enjoyable.

3. Summary in Simple Words

Belinda lives in a little white house with her pets: a little black kitten called Ink, a little gray mouse called Blink, a little yellow dog called Mustard, a little red wagon and a 'realio, trulio' little pet dragon named Custard. Custard has big sharp teeth, a mouth like a fireplace, a nose like a chimney, spikes on his back and scales underneath, but he is a coward and cries for a nice safe cage.

Belinda and the other pets are bold. Belinda says she is as brave as a barrel full of bears, and Ink and Blink chase lions down the stairs. Mustard is as brave as a tiger in a rage. All of them make fun of Custard and call him a coward every day.

One night a pirate climbs in through the window. He is armed with pistols and a cutlass. All the brave pets cry out and run away and hide. Belinda is terrified. But Custard, the so-called coward, suddenly jumps up, snorts fire and fights the pirate. He swallows him whole, and the pirate is gone.

The pets and Belinda are overjoyed and praise Custard as a hero. But once the danger is over, Custard goes back to his old self: he still cries for a safe cage, while the others go on boasting of their bravery. The poem ends with a smile, reminding us who the real hero was.

    6. Part-wise Explanation

    Part 1 — Belinda and her pets

    The first stanzas introduce Belinda, who lives in a little white house with her pets and Custard, the dragon. The repeated word 'little' gives a cosy, toy-like feel.

    Key point: a cheerful, fairy-tale setting.

    Part 2 — The boasts

    Belinda and the other pets boast about their courage with funny comparisons, while Custard says he wants a safe cage. His appearance is fierce, but his nature is timid.

    Key point: contrast between boast and behaviour; humour through exaggeration.

    Part 3 — Teasing Custard

    The pets tease Custard every day and call him a coward. He does not argue, but cries for his cage.

    Key point: judging by words and looks, not deeds.

    Part 4 — The pirate

    One night a pirate climbs in through the window, armed with pistols and a cutlass. The 'brave' ones are terrified and run away.

    Key point: when real danger comes, the boasters fail.

    Part 5 — Custard's brave fight

    Custard leaps up, snorts and fights the pirate, finally swallowing him. The 'coward' becomes the hero.

    Key point: irony; true courage shows in a crisis.

    Part 6 — The ending

    Everyone cheers Custard, but he goes back to crying for a cage, and the others return to their boasting. The poem ends on a humorous note.

    Key point: Custard's modesty and the others' pride remain unchanged.

    7. Character Sketches

    Custard the dragon

    Fierce to look at, with sharp teeth and a fiery mouth, but timid by nature: he cries for a safe cage and is teased by the others. Yet when the pirate attacks, he fights bravely and saves everyone. He is modest and shows that true courage lies within.

    Belinda

    A girl who boasts of being as brave as a barrel full of bears. Yet she is terrified when the pirate comes. She represents loud claims of courage that do not hold up in danger.

    Ink, Blink and Mustard

    Pets who boast of chasing lions and of tiger-like bravery. They run away at the first sign of danger, showing that their courage was only talk.

    The pirate

    An armed and threatening intruder with pistols and a cutlass, who is no match for Custard.

    8. Themes and Message

    True courage

    Real bravery is proved in action, not in words.

    Appearances deceive

    A timid-looking creature can be the real hero, and boasters may be cowards.

    Hidden strength

    Everyone has hidden courage which can come out in a crisis.

    Humility

    Custard remains modest after his victory, while the others boast.

    Humour and irony

    The poem uses fun and irony to teach a serious lesson.

    Moral

    Do not judge anyone by looks or words; judge by what they do when it matters.

    12. Long Answer Questions (5–6 marks)

    Q1. What is the central idea of 'The Tale of Custard the Dragon'?
    The poem is a humorous story about courage. Belinda and her pets boast about their bravery while Custard the dragon cries for a safe cage and is teased as a coward. When a pirate attacks, the boasters run away, and Custard fights and swallows the pirate. After that he goes back to being timid. The poem teaches that true courage is proved in action and that appearances can deceive. It also shows that everyone has hidden strength.
    Q2. How is Custard different from the other pets?
    The other pets and Belinda boast of being as brave as a tiger or a barrel of bears, but Custard admits he is a coward and wants a safe cage. Yet when the pirate comes, the boasters run away, while Custard fights and defeats him. Afterwards, he is modest and goes back to his timid ways, whereas the others continue to boast. So he is a real hero because his courage appears when it is needed.
    Q3. How does the poet create humour in the poem?
    The poet uses funny comparisons (brave as a barrel full of bears), exaggeration (chasing lions down the stairs), made-up words like 'realio, trulio', and rhyming couplets with a bouncing rhythm. He also uses irony, as the 'coward' turns out to be the hero. The silly characters and the comic ending make the poem funny while still giving a lesson about courage.
