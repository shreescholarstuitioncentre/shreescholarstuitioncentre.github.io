2. Central Idea (as expected in the CBSE Board Exam)

'Fog' is a short poem in which the poet compares fog to a cat. Just as a cat moves silently on soft paws, sits quietly looking around, and then goes away, the fog comes in softly, rests for a while over the harbour and the city, and then moves on. Through this one comparison, the poet gives us a vivid picture of the fog's silent, gentle and short-lived visit.

The poem shows how a poet can see the ordinary things of nature in a fresh and imaginative way. It also tells us that fog, like a cat, is quiet, mysterious and temporary: it appears without warning, covers everything for a short time, and disappears just as quietly.

  • Fog is compared to a cat: both come silently, sit still and move on.
  • Fog arrives, rests and leaves: its visit is gentle and short-lived.
  • The poet looks at nature imaginatively: an ordinary natural event becomes a lively picture.
  • Quiet beauty: fog covers harbour and city silently, giving a calm and mysterious mood.

3. Summary in Simple Words

The poet imagines the fog as a cat. The fog comes in on soft, silent feet, just as a cat steps quietly without a sound. It does not rush or make any noise.

Once it arrives, the fog settles over the harbour and the city, as if it is sitting on its haunches (the back part of its body) and looking over everything below. For a while the whole place lies under its quiet gaze, while the boats and buildings are hidden or softened by the mist.

Then, as quietly as it came, the fog moves on, leaving the harbour and the city behind. The poem is very short, just like the fog's visit, and it ends as suddenly as the fog disappears.

In this way the poem uses a single, simple comparison to show how fog behaves: it comes silently, stays for a short while, and leaves without a trace.

    6. Line-wise Explanation

    Lines 1–2: The fog arrives

    The poet says the fog comes on 'little cat feet'. The soft, silent step of a cat is used to describe how gently and quietly the fog comes in.

    Key point: the metaphor begins; the fog is compared to a cat. Fog's arrival is silent and gentle.

    Lines 3–4: Fog looks over the harbour and city

    The fog sits looking over the harbour and the city. Like a cat watching from a spot, it seems to gaze at everything below. The harbour (a sea port for ships) and the city (human world) are both covered.

    Key point: personification; the fog is given the action of 'looking'.

    Lines 5–6: Fog sits and moves on

    The fog sits on silent haunches, motionless like a cat, and then it moves on. The visit is brief; it leaves quickly and quietly.

    Key point: the fog is temporary; the ending is as sudden as it is quiet.

    7. Symbols and Images

    The fog

    Silent, soft, mysterious and temporary. It covers everything for a short time, and then goes.

    The cat

    The image that the poet uses for the fog: quiet, graceful, watchful and independent.

    The harbour

    The place where the sea meets the land and ships rest; it is covered by the fog.

    The city

    The busy human world, which is also silenced and softened by the fog.

    Silent haunches

    The still, resting pose of a cat; it suggests the fog's calm pause over the place.

    8. Themes and Message

    Imagination and observation

    The poet sees fog in a fresh, imaginative way and turns it into a living picture.

    Gentleness and silence of nature

    Nature works quietly; the fog comes and goes without a sound.

    Temporary nature of things

    The fog's visit is short, just like many moments in life.

    Harmony between nature and human world

    The fog covers both the harbour and the city, uniting them under one blanket.

    Moral

    Look closely at nature, and you will find beauty and imagination even in an ordinary event like fog.

    12. Long Answer Questions (5–6 marks)

    Q1. What is the central idea of the poem 'Fog'?
    The poem compares fog to a cat. Just as a cat comes on soft feet, sits quietly looking around and then goes away, the fog comes silently, sits over the harbour and the city for a while and then moves on. The poem shows the gentle, silent and short-lived visit of the fog, and it shows how a poet can look at nature in an imaginative way.
    Q2. Explain the extended metaphor in 'Fog'.
    In the poem, the fog is compared to a cat from beginning to end. The fog comes on silent feet, like a cat. It sits on its haunches and looks over the harbour and the city, like a cat sitting and watching. Then it moves on, as a cat walks away. The comparison turns an ordinary natural event into a lively picture and helps us feel the silent, gentle nature of the fog.
    Q3. How does the poet use personification and imagery in 'Fog'?
    The poet gives the fog the actions of a living creature: it comes, sits, looks and moves on. This personification makes the fog seem alive. The images of soft cat-like feet and silent haunches help us picture and feel the quiet, gentle movement of the fog. Together they create a calm and mysterious mood.
