Madam Rides the Bus

2. Summary in Simple Words

Valli is an eight-year-old Tamil girl who lives in a small village. Her great pastime is to sit on the verandah of her house and watch the world go by, and her favourite sight is the bus that passes through the village on its way to the nearby town. Her strongest wish is to take a ride on that bus to the town and back, which she has never done.

Valli finds out the facts she needs. The town is about six miles away, and the bus conductor charges thirty paise for a one-way ride, so she needs sixty paise for a return trip. To collect it, she saves every coin she gets. She does not spend on toys or peppermints and even misses a village fair, so that she can keep her money safe.

One afternoon, while her mother is asleep, Valli quietly slips out of the house with her coins. She waits at the bus stop, and when the bus arrives, she boards it. She finds the bus new and clean, with a green body and white stripes, and she sits by the window and behaves very seriously, like a grown-up. The conductor, amused by her manner, calls her 'Madam'.

As the bus moves along the narrow road, Valli enjoys the fields, the palm trees and the fresh breeze. She laughs when the bus has to slow down for things on the road, and she sees a young calf running beside the bus. In the town, the conductor offers to buy her a cold drink, but she politely refuses because she must save her money for the return fare. She wants to return at once, so she stays in the bus.

On the return journey the mood changes. She sees the same young cow lying dead on the roadside. Valli is deeply shocked and saddened, and the joy goes out of her day. When she reaches her village, she goes home quietly and keeps her adventure a secret from her mother. The story ends with Valli more thoughtful, as the trip has taught her something about life and death.

    5. Part-wise Explanation

    Part 1 — Valli's curiosity

    Valli sits on the verandah and watches the bus. She is a lively, curious child who wants to see the world beyond her village.

    Key point: curiosity is the beginning of her adventure.

    Part 2 — Planning and saving

    She collects information about the fare and distance and saves sixty paise by giving up small pleasures. She even skips the fair.

    Key point: determination, patience and planning.

    Part 3 — The secret journey

    She waits until her mother sleeps, then goes to the bus stop alone and boards the bus.

    Key point: independence and daring, but also a small secret.

    Part 4 — Valli the 'Madam'

    On the bus she sits seriously, like an adult. The conductor, amused, calls her 'Madam'. This gives the story its title.

    Key point: the playful title is gently ironic.

    Part 5 — Joy of the ride

    She enjoys the fields, the trees and a calf running beside the bus. She laughs freely.

    Key point: pure childish joy.

    Part 6 — In the town

    The conductor offers her a cold drink, but she refuses since she needs the money to return. She chooses to go back at once.

    Key point: Valli is sensible and careful with money.

    Part 7 — The dead cow

    On the return journey she sees the dead cow lying by the road. She is deeply upset and her happiness is gone.

    Key point: first encounter with death and sorrow; innocence turns into experience.

    Part 8 — Return home

    She gets down at her village and goes home quietly, keeping her adventure secret. She is more mature now.

    Key point: growing up through experience.

    6. Character Sketches

    Valli (Valliammai)

    An eight-year-old girl who is curious, observant, independent and determined. She plans carefully and saves money patiently. She is serious and sensible, sitting like a grown-up and refusing the drink to save her fare. She is also sensitive, and the sight of the dead cow shows her tender heart. She grows in understanding by the end of the story.

    The conductor

    Friendly, kind and humorous. He treats Valli with respect and amusement, calling her 'Madam'. He even offers her a cold drink, showing his good nature.

    Valli's mother

    She sleeps in the afternoon and never learns about the trip. She represents the protective but unaware adult world.

    7. Themes and Message

    Curiosity and adventure

    Valli's wish to explore the outside world drives the story.

    Independence

    She plans and completes her journey on her own, which shows her desire for freedom.

    Innocence to experience

    The happy ride ends with the sad sight of the dead cow, so Valli moves from innocence to understanding.

    Planning and determination

    Careful saving and planning help Valli achieve her goal.

    Reality of life

    Joy and sorrow both belong to life; growing up means facing both.

    Moral

    Be curious and brave, but be ready to meet life's harsh realities with a mature heart.

    11. Long Answer Questions

    Q1. Describe how Valli planned and saved for her bus ride.
    Valli watched the bus daily and wished to ride it. She found out that the town was about six miles away and that the fare was thirty paise each way, so she needed sixty paise. She saved every coin she got. She did not buy toys or peppermints and even skipped the fair. When her mother was asleep one afternoon, she quietly slipped out and boarded the bus. This shows her determination and planning.
    Q2. Why is the story titled 'Madam Rides the Bus'? Is the title suitable?
    The conductor, amused by Valli's serious, grown-up manner, calls her 'Madam'. The title is gently ironic, since the 'Madam' is only an eight-year-old girl. It is suitable because it highlights her independence and pride, and shows that the bus ride is more than a simple trip: it is a journey of growing up.
    Q3. How does the dead cow change Valli's experience?
    On the way to the town Valli was delighted to see a young calf running beside the bus. But on the way back she saw the same cow lying dead on the roadside. The sight shocked her and filled her with sorrow, and her joy was gone. For the first time she faced the reality of death. She returned home quiet and thoughtful, and the ride became a lesson in life.
