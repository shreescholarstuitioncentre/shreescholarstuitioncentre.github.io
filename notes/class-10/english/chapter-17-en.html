2. Summary in Simple Words

Gautama Buddha, born as Prince Siddhartha, gave up palace life when he saw human suffering. After attaining enlightenment he taught people the way to free themselves from sorrow. The lesson tells of one of his teachings given in the region of Benares, where Kisa Gotami, a young mother, comes to him for help.

Kisa Gotami's only son has died when he was just able to run about. She is overcome by grief and cannot accept his death. She carries the dead child on her hip and goes from house to house asking for medicine to bring him back. People think she has lost her mind. At last a kind man understands her pain and tells her that only the Buddha can give her the medicine she seeks.

Kisa Gotami goes to the Buddha and begs him for medicine for her son. The Buddha tells her he can help, but she must first bring him a handful of mustard seed. The seed must come from a house where no one has ever lost a child, husband, parent or friend. Hopeful, she goes from door to door in the village.

At every house people are willing to give mustard seed, but each family has lost someone. Some have lost a parent, others a child or a friend. She finds that the dead are more than the living. She understands that she is not the only person who has suffered. Her selfish grief becomes sympathy for everyone. She lays her child to rest, and as she watches the town lights flicker and go out one by one, she understands that human life too is like a lamp that burns for a while and then is gone.

Kisa Gotami returns to the Buddha. He then teaches her that human life is brief and troubled, that everyone who is born must die, and that no one can avoid death. Weeping and grieving do not bring peace; they only add to pain and harm the body, and they cannot save the dead. A wise person must draw out the arrow of lamentation and grief, and one who does so becomes calm and attains peace of mind, free from sorrow.

    5. Part-wise Explanation

    Part 1 — Kisa Gotami's grief

    Her only son has died, and she cannot accept the loss. She carries the body from door to door asking for medicine, and people think she is mad.

    Key point: extreme grief makes a person refuse reality.

    Part 2 — Going to the Buddha

    A kind man understands her pain and sends her to the Buddha. The Buddha says he can help if she brings mustard seed from a home that has never known death.

    Key point: the Buddha uses a gentle, practical way to teach, rather than a lecture.

    Part 3 — The search

    She goes from house to house. Everyone wants to help but every home has lost someone. She sees that the dead outnumber the living.

    Key point: grief is shared by all; suffering is universal.

    Part 4 — Realisation

    She realises that she is not alone in her sorrow. Her selfish grief becomes understanding of all human life. She lays her son to rest.

    Key point: experience teaches better than words.

    Part 5 — The sermon

    The Buddha says life is brief and troubled, death is certain, and no one can escape it. Weeping does not help the dead and harms the living.

    Key point: acceptance of death is the way to peace.

    Part 6 — Drawing out the arrow

    He compares grief to an arrow in the heart that must be drawn out. Whoever does so becomes composed and gets peace of mind.

    Key point: freedom from sorrow comes from letting go of grief.

    6. Character Sketches

    Kisa Gotami

    A young mother who loses her only son and is overcome by grief. At first she cannot accept death and goes from door to door for medicine. When she finds that every house has known death, she realises the truth and becomes calm. She is loving, hopeful, sincere and finally wise.

    Gautama Buddha

    A wise, calm and compassionate teacher. He does not argue with her. Instead, he gives her a task that makes her discover the truth herself. He explains that death is certain and grief must be set aside, and he guides people towards peace.

    The kind man

    A man who understands Kisa Gotami's pain and sends her to the Buddha instead of laughing at her.

    The villagers

    Ordinary people who sympathise with her and share their own losses, helping her realise the truth.

    7. Themes and Message

    Universality of death

    Death comes to everyone; no home is free from it.

    Acceptance of loss

    Grief should be accepted and let go; weeping does not bring back the dead.

    Compassion and wisdom

    The Buddha teaches with kindness and lets Kisa find the answer for herself.

    Shared suffering

    Realising that others also suffer helps us bear our own sorrow.

    Peace of mind

    One who draws out the arrow of grief obtains peace and freedom from sorrow.

    Moral

    Accept the truth of life and death calmly; compassion for others heals our own pain.

    10. Short Answer Questions

    Q1. Who was Kisa Gotami?
    A young mother whose only son died and who was overcome with grief.
    Q2. Why did people think Kisa Gotami was mad?
    Because she carried her dead child from door to door asking for medicine.
    Q3. Who advised her to see the Buddha?
    A kind man who understood her pain.
    Q4. What did the Buddha ask her to bring?
    A handful of mustard seed from a house where no one had lost a child, husband, parent or friend.
    Q5. Why could she not get the mustard seed?
    Because every house had lost a loved one.
    Q6. What did Kisa Gotami realise?
    That death is universal and that she was not alone in her sorrow.
    Q7. What did the Buddha say about weeping?
    That it brings no peace, harms the body and cannot save the dead.
    Q8. What is the 'arrow' the Buddha speaks of?
    The arrow of lamentation, complaint and grief that must be drawn out.
    Q9. What happens to one who draws out the arrow?
    He becomes calm, gets peace of mind and becomes free from sorrow.
    Q10. What is the main message of the lesson?
    Death is certain for all; we must accept it and give up grief to find peace.

    11. Long Answer Questions

    Q1. Narrate the story of Kisa Gotami.
    Kisa Gotami's only son died when he was able to run about. Unable to accept this, she carried him from door to door asking for medicine, and people thought she was mad. A kind man sent her to the Buddha. The Buddha asked her for a handful of mustard seed from a house where no one had lost a child, husband, parent or friend. She went from house to house but every family had lost someone. She realised that death comes to all, laid her son to rest and returned to the Buddha, who taught her the way to peace.
    Q2. What message does the Buddha give in his sermon?
    The Buddha says life is brief and full of trouble, and that all who are born must die. Like ripe fruits, mortals are always in danger of death. Weeping and grieving do not bring peace; they cause more pain and harm the body, and they cannot save the dead. He teaches that one must draw out the arrow of grief, and then become calm and gain peace of mind.
    Q3. How does the Buddha teach Kisa Gotami the truth?
    The Buddha does not scold or argue with Kisa Gotami. He gives her a task that makes her find the truth herself. When she searches for mustard seed and finds that every home has known death, she realises that death is a universal truth. This gentle method shows his wisdom and compassion, and the lesson is understood more deeply because she has experienced it.
