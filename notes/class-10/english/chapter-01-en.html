A Letter to God

Class 10 · English (First Flight) · Prose · Chapter 1 — Detailed Notes · by G.L. Fuentes

2. Summary in Simple Words

Lencho is a hard-working farmer who lives in the only house in a valley, on top of a low hill. His field of ripe corn, with bean flowers scattered in it, promises a good harvest. The only thing it needs is rain. One morning he studies the north-eastern sky, and soon big drops begin to fall. His wife is cooking, his older sons work in the field and the younger ones play. Lencho thinks the drops are like new coins: the big ones are ten-centavo pieces and the small ones five-centavo pieces.

Suddenly a strong wind rises and large hailstones begin to fall, hitting the roof and the field. They look like new silver coins, and the children run out to collect them. But within minutes the whole field is white as if covered with salt. Not a single leaf is left on the trees, and the corn is completely ruined. Lencho says even a plague of locusts would have left more behind. That night the family is full of sorrow, since they now have no food for the year.

Yet Lencho is a man of great faith. His only hope is God, so he writes a letter. He tells God that the hail has destroyed everything, and asks for one hundred pesos to sow the field again and to live until the next crop. On the envelope he writes 'To God', walks to town, buys a stamp and posts it.

The postman laughs at the address and shows the letter to the postmaster. The fat, friendly postmaster laughs too, but soon becomes serious. He admires Lencho's faith and wishes he himself had such faith. To keep that faith alive, he decides to reply. He collects money from his employees and friends and gives part of his own salary, but cannot gather the full amount. He sends Lencho a little more than half and signs the letter 'God'.

The next Sunday Lencho comes to ask for his letter. He is not surprised at all to see the money, because he was sure God would send it. But when he counts it, he becomes angry: it is less than he asked for. He writes a second letter asking God to send the rest, but not through the post office, because he thinks its employees are a bunch of crooks. The story ends with this irony: the very people who helped him are called thieves.

5. Part-wise Explanation

Part 1 — The hopeful morning

Lencho's house stands alone on a hill. He knows the land well and understands that the field can give a good crop only if it gets rain. He keeps watching the sky; his wife agrees that, God willing, the rain will come.

Key point: farmers depend completely on nature, so Lencho's hope is also his livelihood.

Part 2 — Rain turns into hail

Lencho sees the raindrops as coins, which shows how much the rain means to him. But the hailstorm that follows destroys the crop in an hour. The comparison with locusts shows how total the damage is.

Key point: sudden change from joy to disaster; the coin image is repeated with hail ('silver coins') in a cruel contrast.

Part 3 — The night of sorrow

The field looks like it is covered with salt. Lencho's soul is full of sadness. Still, he reminds himself that nobody dies of hunger while God watches, which is a lesson he learned from his own belief.

Key point: despair does not break his faith; it turns his thoughts to God.

Part 4 — The letter

Lencho writes with complete trust that God will read it. He asks for 100 pesos, a small sum for God but very large for him. He is confident enough to address the envelope only 'To God'.

Key point: his simple, childlike belief that God acts like a person who reads and answers letters.

Part 5 — The postmaster's kindness

The postmaster first laughs, then becomes serious and moved. He wants to reply, but realises he needs money too, not only 'goodwill, ink and paper'. He collects what he can and signs 'God'.

Key point: human kindness and the wish to protect another person's faith.

Part 6 — Lencho's anger

Lencho takes the money without surprise. Only the shortfall upsets him; he cannot imagine that God would make a mistake, so he blames the post office employees.

Key point: irony — ungrateful suspicion towards the people who actually helped him.

6. Character Sketches

Lencho

A hard-working, honest farmer who knows his land well. He is a man of great faith, simple and innocent. He loves his family and feels their pain. His faith is so strong that he writes directly to God and is not surprised when the money arrives. But his blind trust makes him suspicious and ungrateful towards people, and he calls honest helpers crooks.

The Postmaster

A fat, friendly and kind-hearted man with a sense of humour. He first laughs at the letter but then admires Lencho's faith and wants to protect it. He is generous and resourceful: he collects money from his employees and friends and gives from his own salary. He also shows great sensitivity by signing the reply 'God'.

Lencho's family and the post office staff

His wife and children share his sorrow and hope; his wife says 'God willing'. The postman and employees contribute money gladly, showing that ordinary people can be generous.

7. Themes and Message

Faith and hope

Lencho keeps his faith even after losing everything. Faith gives him strength in a time of despair.

Human kindness

God's help reaches Lencho through people. The postmaster's kindness is the real 'answer' to the prayer.

Irony of life

The people who help Lencho are called thieves, and the man who trusts God completely mistrusts people.

Cruelty of nature

Farmers depend on weather; one storm can destroy a year's work.

Moral

Have faith, but also remember to value the kindness of people around us. Never judge helpers without knowing the facts.

10. Short Answer Questions

Q1. What was Lencho's hope and why?
He hoped for rain because his field of ripe corn needed it to give a good harvest.
Q2. Why did Lencho call raindrops 'new coins'?
To a poor farmer rain meant a good crop and money, so each drop seemed as valuable as a coin.
Q3. How did the hailstorm affect the field and family?
It destroyed the whole crop and left the family with no food for the year; they were deeply sad.
Q4. Who did Lencho trust and why?
He trusted only God, believing God knows everything and helps those who are in trouble.
Q5. Why did the postmaster send money?
He admired Lencho's faith and did not want it to be shaken, so he decided to answer the letter.
Q6. Did the postmaster collect the full amount?
No. He gathered only a little more than half, from his own salary and from friends and employees.
Q7. Why was Lencho angry?
The money was less than he asked for, so he thought the post office staff had kept the rest.
Q8. What did Lencho say about the post office employees?
He called them a bunch of crooks and asked God not to send the money by post.

11. Long Answer Questions

Q1. Write a character sketch of Lencho.
Lencho is a hard-working farmer who knows his land well. He has great faith in God and believes that God knows every need. When the hail destroys his crop, he does not lose hope; he writes a letter to God. He is simple and innocent, so he is not surprised when money arrives. But his belief that God never makes mistakes makes him suspicious of the post office staff, and he wrongly calls them crooks. Thus he is faithful and sincere, but also ungrateful and blind in trust.
Q2. How is the story ironic?
The story is full of irony. Lencho trusts God completely and is certain that help will come, yet he distrusts the very people who helped him. The postmaster and his staff give their own money to protect his faith, but Lencho calls them thieves. The reader knows the truth while Lencho does not, and that gap between what is and what he believes makes the ending ironic.
Q3. What message does the story give?
The story teaches that faith can give strength in the hardest times. It also shows that God's help often comes through human kindness. The postmaster's act proves that charity is a real way of serving God. Finally, we should not judge people without knowing the facts, and we should be grateful to those who help us.
