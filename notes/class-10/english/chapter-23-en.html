A Question of Trust ✍️

Class 10 · English · Footprints Without Feet (Supplementary) · Author: Victor Canning · Chapter 23 as per your list

1. Story in short

GenreProse / Crime story with a twist.

Horace Danby is a 50-year-old, respectable locksmith who makes safes. He lives alone and loves gardening and collecting rare books.

Once a year he robs one safe to pay for his hobby. He has never been caught.

This year he plans to rob Shipton Grange, where jewels worth a fortune are kept in a library safe.

He studies the house, picks the time when the owners are away, and breaks in. He wears gloves, but he has a bad cold.

While he works, a young woman walks in and calmly says she is a member of the family who has forgotten the safe's combination.

Danby is shocked, and she asks him to open it. Danby, flustered, trusts her, opens the safe and, for convenience, takes off his gloves.

She takes the jewels, thanks him, and tells him to leave. Soon the police arrive. His fingerprints are everywhere, and she has vanished.

Danby is arrested and sent to prison. The thief who tricked the thief was never found.

5. Themes & message

  • Irony: a professional thief is tricked by another thief.
  • Crime never pays; no one is as clever as he thinks.
  • Blind trust in strangers can be dangerous.
  • A person with two faces (respectable + criminal) is finally exposed.
  • Greed and overconfidence lead to downfall.

7. Important Q & A

Q1. Why did Danby rob safes once a year?

To earn money for buying rare, expensive books for his library.

Q2. How did the woman trick him?

She acted like a family member, spoke confidently, and asked him to open the safe. He believed her.

Q3. What is the irony in the story?

A clever thief, who trusted a stranger, is caught while the real thief escapes.

Q4. Why is the title apt?

The whole plot turns on one question: whom to trust? Danby trusted the wrong person.
