1. Story in short

GenreBiography / non-fiction prose. This is the true story of Richard Ebright, a young American who became a scientist.

Richard was a curious child. His mother encouraged him to ask questions and pursue hobbies, which gave him confidence and freedom to explore.

He started by collecting butterflies as a boy, and soon his collection grew. His interest in butterflies turned into a deep scientific curiosity.

He read widely, joined a butterfly association, and learned about the Monarch butterfly and its amazing long migration.

He wrote to the scientist Dr. Urquhart, who studied Monarch migration. The scientist replied and invited young Ebright to help tag butterflies.

Richard raised caterpillars and noticed gold spots on the chrysalis (pupa). He wondered: what are they for?

He designed an experiment: he removed the spots from some chrysalises and observed what happened. The butterflies did not develop properly, so the spots must be vital.

His project won recognition at a science fair. Later he studied at a famous university and became a professional scientist.

6. Themes & message

  • Curiosity is the seed of all science.
  • Parents' encouragement shapes a child's future.
  • A hobby can turn into a career if pursued seriously.
  • Science needs observation, patience and experiments.
  • Learn from experts, but also think for yourself.

8. Important Q & A

Q1. What inspired Ebright to become a scientist?

His mother's encouragement and his butterfly collection awakened his curiosity.

Q2. Who was Dr. Urquhart?

A scientist who studied Monarch butterfly migration and guided Ebright.

Q3. What did Ebright discover about the gold spots?

They were essential for the pupa to grow properly into a butterfly.

Q4. What is the message of the chapter?

Curiosity, hard work and support can make a scientist.
