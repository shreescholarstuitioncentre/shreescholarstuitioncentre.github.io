The Necklace

Class 10 • Interesting Prose • Chapter 26 (Supplementary) • by Guy de Maupassant

✏️ About the Author & Story

Guy de Maupassant (1850–1893) was a French writer, a master of the short story. His stories are famous for realistic detail and a surprise (twist) ending. The Necklace (original: La Parure, 1884) shows how vanity and pride can ruin a life.

📖 Summary (Step by Step)

  1. Mathilde Loisel is a pretty girl born in a family of clerks. She marries a petty clerk in the Ministry of Education. She feels she was born for luxury and suffers over her plain home.
  2. M. Loisel brings an invitation to the Minister’s ball. She cries, as she has no dress. He gives her 400 francs (saved to buy a gun) for a gown.
  3. She still needs jewels. On her husband’s advice she borrows a diamond necklace from her rich friend Madame Forestier.
  4. At the ball Mathilde is the prettiest woman, admired by all, and dances with joy till 4 a.m.
  5. On reaching home she finds the necklace is lost. Search, police and newspaper adverts fail.
  6. They buy a similar necklace for 36,000 francs: 18,000 from his father’s legacy, the rest borrowed from moneylenders at ruinous interest. Madame Forestier does not notice the change.
  7. For ten years they live in poverty: dismiss the maid, live in a garret, Mathilde does hard housework, M. Loisel works extra jobs. She grows old and rough.
  8. One day on the Champs-Élysées she meets Madame Forestier and tells everything. Forestier says: “My necklace was only paste, worth at most 500 francs!” (Twist ending)

💡 Themes

  • Vanity & discontent: wishing for what one lacks brings ruin.
  • Appearance vs reality: the “diamond” was fake.
  • Honesty: telling the truth to Forestier early would have saved ten years.
  • Irony of fate: loss and hardship came from a false object.
  • Dignity of labour: hardship made Mathilde strong.

❓ Important Q & A

Q1. Why was Mathilde unhappy?
She felt she deserved a rich life but was married to a poor clerk.
Q2. Why did the Loisels not tell Madame Forestier about the loss?
They were afraid of being called careless and dishonest, so they hid it and replaced the necklace.
Q3. What is the moral?
Be content with what you have and be honest; pride and show-off lead to suffering.
Q4. How does the story end?
Forestier reveals the necklace was paste worth 500 francs; ten years of sacrifice were for nothing.
