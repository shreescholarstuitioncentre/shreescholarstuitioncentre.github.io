Dust of Snow

Class 10 · English (First Flight) · Poem 1 — Detailed Notes · by Robert Frost

Central Idea (as expected in the CBSE Board Exam)

The poem 'Dust of Snow' shows how a tiny, unexpected event in nature can bring a big change in a person's mood. The poet is feeling sad and regrets how the day is passing. Suddenly a crow, which is generally seen as a bird of ill omen, shakes some snow off a hemlock tree onto him. This small incident lifts his spirits, and he feels that a part of the day has been saved from being wasted.

The poet wants to tell us that nature has a healing power, that joy can come from the most unexpected things, and that we should not let gloom and regret rule our hearts. A positive outlook can turn even a bad day into a good one.

  • Nature heals: even a simple natural event can cheer a troubled mind.
  • Joy comes from unexpected things: the crow and the hemlock (symbols of sorrow) bring happiness.
  • Mood can change in a moment: a small incident turns sadness into joy.
  • Do not waste a day in regret: a positive attitude saves our time and our peace of mind.

2. Summary of the Poem in Simple Words

The poet is walking on a cold winter day, feeling low and regretful. He has been having a bad day and is lost in sad thoughts. He is walking beneath a hemlock tree, on which a crow is sitting.

Suddenly, the crow moves on the branch and shakes some snow down from the tree. The snow falls lightly, like fine dust, on the poet. This small, unexpected touch of nature surprises him and breaks the heavy mood he was in.

The poet feels a change in his heart. His sadness lifts and he starts to feel cheerful. Because of this small happening, a part of the day that he was about to regret as wasted is saved, and the day becomes better. The poem shows how a tiny moment in nature can bring hope, joy and a fresh outlook.

    5. Stanza-wise Explanation

    Stanza 1 (lines 1–4)

    The poet describes how a crow, sitting on a hemlock tree, shook down a little snow on him. The snow was fine and light, like dust. The crow and the hemlock are normally seen as signs of sadness, yet it is these gloomy things that bring the surprise.

    Key point: an unexpected touch of nature; the crow and the hemlock tree are usual symbols of sorrow.

    Stanza 2 (lines 5–8)

    The poet says that this small event changed his heart and mood. He had been feeling that the day was wasted and he regretted it, but now a part of the day is saved because of this small incident.

    Key point: nature can change our mood; a tiny moment can bring hope.

    Overall meaning

    The poem teaches that happiness can come from little, unexpected things, and that we should stay open to small joys in nature to overcome sorrow.

    Key point: positivity and a change of mood.

    6. Elements of the Poem (Speaker and Symbols)

    The speaker (poet)

    A thoughtful person who is sad and regretful at the start. He is observant and open to nature, so a small incident lifts his spirit. He represents anyone who feels low but can be cheered by small joys.

    The crow

    Usually a symbol of sorrow and bad luck, but here it becomes an unlikely bringer of joy by shaking snow on the poet.

    The hemlock tree

    A dark evergreen tree linked with sadness and death; it provides the setting for the surprising change of mood.

    The dust of snow

    Soft, light snow that stands for purity, joy and the small gifts of nature.

    11. Long Answer Questions

    Q1. Explain the poem 'Dust of Snow' in your own words.
    The poet is walking in a sad and regretful mood. As he passes under a hemlock tree, a crow sitting on it shakes the branch and a little snow falls on him like fine dust. This unexpected touch of nature changes his mood and makes him cheerful. A day he thought was wasted is saved by this small incident. The poem tells us that small joys in nature can lift our spirits.
    Q2. What do the crow and hemlock symbolise, and why does the poet use them?
    The crow is traditionally a symbol of sorrow and bad luck, and the hemlock is a dark tree linked with gloom and death. By choosing these gloomy symbols as the source of the change, the poet shows the contrast between expectation and result: something that seems sad brings joy. This makes the message about unexpected happiness stronger.
    Q3. How does the poem show a change in mood?
    The poem begins with sadness and regret, shown by the poet's feeling that the day is wasted. After the snow falls on him, his heart feels lighter and he becomes cheerful. The words show this movement from gloom to joy, and the poem ends with the idea that part of his day is saved. The change shows the healing power of nature.
