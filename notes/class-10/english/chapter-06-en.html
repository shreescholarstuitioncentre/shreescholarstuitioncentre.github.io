Two Stories About Flying

2. Summary in Simple Words

PART I — HIS FIRST FLIGHT: A young seagull is alone on his ledge. His two brothers and his sister have already flown, but he is too afraid. He cannot bring himself to jump into the air, and he watches them from the ledge. His parents try to persuade him, and they even scold him and threaten that he will go hungry unless he flies.

Hungry and frightened, the young seagull is lured by his mother. She flies near with a piece of fish in her beak and hovers just beyond his reach. Overcome by hunger, he gives a cry and leaps towards the food. For a moment he falls with fear, but then his wings open, he flaps them, and he finds himself flying. He dives, rises and glides, and his family cheers and praises him, bringing him food. He is thrilled that he can fly.

PART II — THE BLACK AEROPLANE: A pilot is flying his old Dakota aeroplane through the night on his way home. He is tired and dreams about a good breakfast. Suddenly he runs into thick, dark storm clouds. His compass and radio stop working, he has very little fuel left, and he cannot see anything or know where he is going. He is helpless and frightened.

Then he sees another aeroplane, a black one, flying close to him. He is relieved and follows its lights through the storm. When the clouds clear, the black plane has led him towards the airport. The pilot lands safely. The air traffic controller is amazed and tells him there was no other plane in the sky, and says that with so little fuel he should not have been able to fly so far. The pilot is left wondering who or what the black aeroplane was, and the story ends in mystery.

    5. Part-wise Explanation

    Part I.1 — The frightened seagull

    The young seagull is the youngest and the most timid. He is afraid of the drop and of failing. His brothers and sister fly with ease, which makes him more ashamed and fearful.

    Key point: fear of the unknown stops him, not lack of ability.

    Part I.2 — The parents' efforts

    The parents scold, threaten and encourage. When nothing works, they hold up food to tempt him. Their tough love is meant to teach him independence.

    Key point: sometimes a gentle push is needed to help a child grow.

    Part I.3 — The leap and the flight

    Hunger overcomes fear. He leaps, and for a moment he feels he will fall, but then his wings spread and he flies. He realises that he could fly all along.

    Key point: we often discover our abilities only when we try.

    Part I.4 — Celebration

    The family gathers to praise him and give him food. He is proud and happy, and the fear is gone.

    Key point: success and encouragement build confidence.

    Part II.1 — Night flight and storm

    The pilot is tired and thinks of breakfast when he suddenly meets thick storm clouds. The compass and radio fail and the fuel is running low.

    Key point: sudden danger tests the pilot's calm and courage.

    Part II.2 — The mysterious black plane

    He sees a black aeroplane and follows its lights because he has no other guide. Soon the clouds clear.

    Key point: help appears when it is needed most.

    Part II.3 — The landing

    The black plane leads him to the airport and he lands safely. The air traffic controller says there was no other plane and that he had almost no fuel left.

    Key point: suspense turns into mystery.

    Part II.4 — The unanswered question

    The pilot is left wondering who guided him. The story does not give an explanation.

    Key point: an open ending that makes readers think about the unexplained.

    6. Character Sketches

    The young seagull

    Timid and fearful at first, he is hungry and stays on the ledge. But he is not weak: when hunger pushes him, he takes the leap and finds that he can fly. In the end he is brave, proud and delighted.

    The seagull's parents

    Caring but firm. They scold and threaten at first, then use food to tempt him. They want their child to become independent and are overjoyed when he flies.

    The pilot (narrator)

    An experienced but tired pilot who keeps his calm in a storm. He is practical enough to follow the only guide available. He is grateful and puzzled by the mystery.

    The air traffic controller

    Alert and surprised. His statement that no other plane was seen deepens the mystery.

    The black aeroplane

    A mysterious, almost supernatural helper that appears at the right time and disappears. It is a symbol of unexpected help.

    7. Themes and Message

    Overcoming fear

    In both stories, fear of the unknown is overcome. The seagull overcomes the fear of falling and the pilot overcomes the fear of the storm.

    Courage and self-confidence

    The seagull discovers he had the ability all along. Courage is acting even when afraid.

    Tough love

    Parents sometimes need to push their children to help them grow.

    Presence of mind

    The pilot stays calm and follows the black aeroplane rather than panicking.

    Mystery and unexpected help

    Help can come from unexpected places, and not everything can be explained.

    Moral

    Face your fears with courage; you can do more than you think.

    10. Short Answer Questions

    Q1. Why was the young seagull afraid to fly?
    He was frightened of the long drop and of falling, even though his brothers and sister had already flown.
    Q2. What did his parents do to make him fly?
    They scolded and threatened him, and finally his mother tempted him with a piece of fish just out of his reach.
    Q3. How did he finally fly?
    Driven by hunger, he leapt for the fish, fell for a moment, then his wings opened and he flew.
    Q4. How did the family react after his first flight?
    They praised him and gave him food, and he was very proud and happy.
    Q5. What does Part I teach us?
    That fear can stop us from using our abilities; with courage and a push we can do more than we think.
    Q6. What problems did the pilot face in Part II?
    A storm, failing compass and radio, and almost no fuel.
    Q7. What guided the pilot through the storm?
    A mysterious black aeroplane whose lights he followed.
    Q8. What did the controller say after landing?
    That there was no other aeroplane in the sky, and that the pilot was lucky to have reached the airport.
    Q9. Why is Part II called a mystery?
    Because no one else saw the black plane and its identity is never explained.
    Q10. What is common to both stories?
    Both are about overcoming fear and facing danger with courage.

    11. Long Answer Questions

    Q1. Describe the young seagull's first flight.
    The young seagull was afraid to fly while his brothers and sister were already flying. His parents scolded and threatened him but he stayed on the ledge, hungry. Then his mother tempted him with a piece of fish held just out of his reach. He leapt, fell for a moment, and then his wings opened. He flapped, dived and rose, and found he could fly. His family praised him. The story shows how fear can hold us back and how a push can help us discover our abilities.
    Q2. Narrate the pilot's experience in The Black Aeroplane.
    The pilot was flying his old Dakota at night and was tired. He suddenly ran into thick storm clouds, and his compass and radio failed while his fuel was running low. He feared for his life. Then a black aeroplane appeared, and he followed its lights through the storm. It led him to the airport, where he landed safely. The controller said no other plane had been seen. The pilot was left wondering who had guided him.
    Q3. What message do both stories give?
    Both stories show that courage is needed to face fear. The seagull overcomes fear through a push from his mother, while the pilot keeps his presence of mind and follows the guide. They teach us that we should face difficulties bravely, trust ourselves, and be grateful for help that comes from unexpected sources.
