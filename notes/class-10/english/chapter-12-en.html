🌳 The Trees

Class 10 · English (First Flight) · Poem – Chapter 12 · By Adrienne Rich

1. About the Poet

Adrienne Rich (1929–2012) was an American poet, essayist and thinker, born in Baltimore, Maryland. She is regarded as one of the most influential English-language poets of the twentieth century. Her work often deals with freedom, identity, women's lives, and the relationship between people and the world around them. She won many honours, including the National Book Award for her collection Diving into the Wreck.

  • Style: simple, quiet language with strong hidden meanings.
  • Her poems often show people or things breaking free from confinement.
  • In The Trees, she imagines house plants leaving the house to return to the forest.

4. Detailed Explanation (Part by Part)

Part 1 — The Trees Are Moving Out

The poem opens with a surprising and magical statement: the trees that have been kept inside the house are moving out into the forest. These are house plants, perhaps trees in pots or tubs, that people keep indoors for decoration. The poet pictures them as if they have a will of their own and are slowly leaving. They are leaving at night when everyone is quiet, and we can almost hear the leaves rustling and the roots tearing free from the soil of their pots. The scene is told calmly, as if it were a natural event.

Part 2 — The Forest Has Been Empty

The poet then tells us about the forest. For all these days, the forest has been empty. There were no trees to give birds a place to sit, no leaves to hide an insect, and no shade where the sun could rest. In other words, a forest without trees is not a forest at all. The idea that it has been empty "all these days" is repeated, so that we feel the long loneliness of the forest. The trees now moving out of the house will bring the forest back to life, and birds, insects and sunlight will once again find a home.

Part 3 — The Poet Inside the House

While all this happens, the poet is sitting inside the house with the doors open to the veranda, writing long letters. She says she hardly mentions the departure of the trees in her letters. This shows how people are busy with their own small concerns, and do not notice the great events of nature happening around them. It also shows that she is a quiet watcher who understands what is happening, but does not interfere.

Part 4 — The Night and the Final Mood

The poem ends in a fresh, bright night. The moon is shining in a wide open sky, and the trees stand confident and free. The poet's mind is full of whispers, the soft sounds of the leaves, which tomorrow, when the trees have gone, will be silent. The house will feel empty. So the ending brings together two feelings: joy for the trees and the forest, and a quiet sadness for what the house will lose.

💡 Key insight: The trees are brought indoors by humans, but they belong to the forest. The poem says that nature wants to be free and in its natural place, and also hints at the deeper human wish to break free from confinement.

6. Central Idea (Board Answer)

Central Idea / Theme: In The Trees, Adrienne Rich imagines the plants and trees kept inside a house moving out at night into the forest. The forest, which had been empty without them, has no shelter for birds, no hiding place for insects and no shade for the sun. The trees' departure shows nature's longing for freedom and its wish to return to its natural home. The poet, meanwhile, sits inside writing letters and hardly notices, which shows how human beings are absorbed in their own world and forget nature. The poem highlights the conflict between nature and human life: people confine nature for their own comfort, but nature must be free. At a deeper level, the trees also symbolise anyone who wants to break free from confinement, including women who long to break out of narrow domestic roles.

Key Points to Remember

  • The trees are personified: they decide to leave the house.
  • The forest is empty without trees; the trees' return restores life.
  • Nature belongs in its natural place and should be free.
  • The poem can also be read symbolically as a longing for freedom from confinement.
  • The ending mixes joy and a quiet sadness.

7. Literary Devices

DeviceExplanation
PersonificationThe trees are shown as if they can think, decide and move, leaving the house on their own.
SymbolismThe trees stand for freedom and nature; the house for confinement and human control; the forest for the natural home.
RepetitionThe idea that the forest has been empty all these days is repeated to stress its long loneliness.
ImageryPictures of the rustling leaves, roots leaving the soil, the moonlit night and the open sky appeal to our senses.
ContrastThe inside of the house (closed, quiet, human) is contrasted with the forest outside (open, alive, natural).
MetaphorThe forest "leaving the house" is a metaphor for nature returning to its true place.
EnjambmentSentences run over the line breaks, giving a flowing, quiet, dreamlike rhythm.
Free verseThere is no fixed rhyme or metre, which suits the natural, unforced movement of the trees.

10. Important Questions & Answers

A. Short Answer Questions (30–40 words)

Q1. What are the trees doing in the poem?

The trees that were kept inside the house are moving out into the forest. Their roots are leaving the soil of the pots, and they are going back to their natural home, as if they had a will of their own.

Q2. Why is the forest described as empty?

The forest is empty because its trees are missing. Without trees, birds have nowhere to sit, insects have nowhere to hide and there is no shade. A forest without trees has no life or shelter.

Q3. What is the poet doing while the trees leave?

The poet is sitting inside the house with the doors open to the veranda, writing long letters. She hardly mentions the trees' departure in them, showing how people are busy with their own affairs and overlook nature.

Q4. What does the poem suggest about the relationship between nature and humans?

The poem suggests that humans keep nature confined for their comfort, but nature longs to be free and return to its natural place. It shows the conflict between human life and the needs of nature.

Q5. What do the trees symbolise?

The trees symbolise nature's freedom and the longing to return to one's roots. In a deeper reading, they also stand for anyone, such as women, who wants to break free from confinement.

Q6. What is the mood at the end of the poem?

The ending is fresh and hopeful, with a bright moonlit night and trees standing free, but it also carries a quiet sadness, as the house will be left silent once the trees have gone.

B. Long Answer Questions (100–120 words)

Q1. Explain the central idea of the poem "The Trees".

In "The Trees", Adrienne Rich imagines the trees kept inside a house moving out into the forest at night. The forest has been empty without them: there was no place for birds to sit, insects to hide or the sun to rest in shade. The trees' departure shows nature's longing for freedom and its desire to return to its natural home. Meanwhile the poet writes letters inside and barely notices, showing how humans forget nature. The poem highlights the conflict between humans and nature and suggests that nature must be free. It can also symbolise anyone who wishes to break free from confinement.

Q2. How does the poet use personification and symbolism in the poem?

The poet gives the trees human qualities: they decide, move and leave the house, as if they were alive in the human sense. This personification makes the reader feel their longing for freedom. The trees are also symbols: they stand for nature, freedom and the wish to return to one's roots. The house is a symbol of confinement and human control, while the forest represents the natural home. At a deeper level the trees can symbolise people, especially women, who wish to escape restrictions. Thus the simple scene carries layers of meaning beyond the literal picture.

Q3. Is the departure of the trees a happy or a sad event? Explain.

The poem presents both feelings. For the trees and the forest it is a happy event: the trees are free, and the empty forest becomes full of life again, with shelter for birds and insects. The moonlit night is fresh and bright. But for the house and the poet there is a quiet sadness, because the soft whispers of the leaves will be gone and the house will be silent and empty. This mixed mood shows that freedom for one may mean loss for another, and the poet accepts it calmly, without protest.
