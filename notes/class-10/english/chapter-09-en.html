2. Summary in Simple Words

In her first entry on Saturday, 20 June 1942, thirteen-year-old Anne Frank says it is a strange idea for someone like her to keep a diary. She has never done it, and she thinks nobody would be interested in the thoughts of a schoolgirl. But she wants to write and to get things off her chest. She says paper has more patience than people. Though she has loving parents, a sixteen-year-old sister, about thirty friends, admirers and a good home, she feels she has no real friend with whom she can share her deepest thoughts. So she decides to make her diary her friend and calls it Kitty.

To help Kitty understand her, Anne gives a short family background. Her father married at thirty-six and her mother was twenty-five. Her sister Margot was born in Frankfurt in 1926, and Anne in 1929. Because they were Jewish, the family moved to Holland in 1933, where her father became a businessman. Her grandmother came to live with them. The family was happy until the Germans occupied Holland in 1940.

After the occupation, life for Jews became harder and harder. They had to wear a yellow star, hand in their bicycles and could not travel by tram or car. They could shop only between 3 and 5 pm and only at Jewish shops. They had to stay indoors from 8 pm to 6 am and could not go to theatres, cinemas, swimming pools, tennis courts or hockey fields. They were not allowed to visit Christians and had to attend Jewish schools. Anne says that life went on in spite of these restrictions, but they could not do this or that.

The second part of the lesson shows Anne's lively school life. Her maths teacher, Mr Keesing, is angry that she talks too much in class and gives her a punishment essay titled 'A Chatterbox'. Anne argues that talking is a student's trait and that she cannot cure it because she has inherited it from her talkative mother. Mr Keesing laughs, but when she continues to chatter, he gives a second essay, 'An Incorrigible Chatterbox', and later a third one, 'Quack, quack, quack, said Mistress Chatterbox'.

For the third essay Anne and her friend Sanne, who writes poetry, compose a funny poem about a mother duck and a father swan who bite their three ducklings to death because they quack too much. Mr Keesing takes the joke in good humour, reads it to the class and other classes, and after that never punishes Anne for talking again. He even makes jokes about her chatter.

    5. Part-wise Explanation

    Part 1 — Why Anne writes a diary

    She says it is odd for a 13-year-old to keep a diary, but she wants to unburden herself. She values paper because it has more patience than people.

    Key point: writing becomes a way of sharing thoughts when there is no one to listen.

    Part 2 — Loneliness in spite of friends

    Anne has parents, a sister, friends and admirers, yet she lacks a close friend who understands her deepest feelings. So she makes her diary her friend and names it Kitty.

    Key point: a person can be lonely even in a crowd.

    Part 3 — Family background

    She describes her parents' ages, her sister Margot, her own birth and the move to Holland to escape persecution.

    Key point: the family were refugees because they were Jews.

    Part 4 — Life under German rule

    The Germans' arrival changes everything. Jews are forced to wear a yellow star, give up their bicycles, follow a curfew and stay out of public places.

    Key point: gradual loss of freedom and dignity under the Nazis.

    Part 5 — Mr Keesing and the chatterbox essays

    Mr Keesing punishes Anne for talking with essays. She replies with reasons and humour. Each new punishment is also taken lightly by Anne.

    Key point: Anne's wit and spirit even under authority.

    Part 6 — The duck poem

    Anne and Sanne's funny poem wins over the teacher, who reads it out and stops punishing her.

    Key point: humour can solve problems better than anger.

    6. Character Sketches

    Anne Frank

    A bright, talkative, observant and witty girl. She is lonely and sensitive, longing for a true friend. She is honest and expresses herself clearly. Even in difficult times she keeps her humour and spirit. Her diary shows her courage and her mature understanding of events.

    Mr Keesing

    The maths teacher. He is strict about discipline but has a sense of humour. He accepts Anne's joke and thereafter stops punishing her.

    Margot

    Anne's older sister, sixteen at the time. She is part of the family background Anne describes.

    Sanne

    Anne's friend who writes poetry. She helps Anne compose the poem in the third essay.

    Kitty

    Anne's diary, treated as a dear friend and confidante.

    7. Themes and Message

    Loneliness and friendship

    Anne is lonely and needs a friend to share her feelings. The diary becomes her friend.

    Effects of war and persecution

    Jewish families lose freedom and normal life. The lesson hints at the larger tragedy of the Holocaust.

    Resilience and humour

    Anne uses humour and wit to face difficulties, both at school and in life.

    Writing as expression

    A diary can be a place to release feelings and record history.

    Moral

    Even in the darkest times we must keep hope, humour and our human spirit.

    10. Short Answer Questions

    Q1. Why did Anne decide to keep a diary?
    She wanted to unburden her thoughts and feelings, and she felt she had no real friend to confide in.
    Q2. What did she mean by 'paper has more patience than people'?
    Paper silently accepts whatever is written and never judges or interrupts, unlike people.
    Q3. Why did she name her diary Kitty?
    She wanted to treat it as a dear friend to whom she could tell everything.
    Q4. Why was Anne lonely though she had family and friends?
    Because she had no one who understood her deepest feelings, a true friend.
    Q5. Why did the family move to Holland?
    They were Jews, and they left Germany in 1933 to escape Nazi persecution.
    Q6. What restrictions were imposed on Jews?
    They wore a yellow star, gave up bicycles, could not use trams or cars, had curfew from 8 pm, and could not visit theatres, cinemas or pools.
    Q7. Why did Mr Keesing punish Anne?
    Because she talked too much in his class.
    Q8. What were the three essay topics?
    'A Chatterbox', 'An Incorrigible Chatterbox' and 'Quack, quack, quack, said Mistress Chatterbox'.
    Q9. How did Anne win over Mr Keesing?
    With a funny poem written with her friend Sanne; he laughed and stopped punishing her.
    Q10. What did Anne say about her talking habit?
    That it was a student's trait and she inherited it from her talkative mother.

    11. Long Answer Questions

    Q1. Write a character sketch of Anne Frank.
    Anne is a thirteen-year-old girl who is lively, talkative and witty. She is intelligent and honest, and she understands the world around her. Although she has family and friends, she is lonely and wants a true friend, so she makes her diary her friend, Kitty. Her humour is seen in the chatterbox essays and the duck poem. Even when the Nazis take away the freedom of Jews, she keeps her spirit. Thus she is both cheerful and thoughtful.
    Q2. Describe the restrictions placed on Jews and their effect.
    After Germany occupied Holland, Jews had to wear a yellow star, hand in bicycles and could not use trams or cars. They could shop only at Jewish shops between 3 and 5 pm, had to stay indoors from 8 pm to 6 am and could not go to cinemas, theatres, swimming pools, tennis courts or hockey fields. They could not visit Christians and had to go to Jewish schools. These rules took away their freedom and dignity, but Anne says life went on.
    Q3. What does the chatterbox episode tell us about Anne and Mr Keesing?
    The episode shows that Anne is witty, confident and fond of talking, and she faces punishment with humour. Mr Keesing is strict but has a sense of humour. He gives essays as punishment, but Anne's last essay, a funny poem, makes him laugh. He then stops punishing her. It shows that humour and creativity can turn a punishment into a happy ending.
