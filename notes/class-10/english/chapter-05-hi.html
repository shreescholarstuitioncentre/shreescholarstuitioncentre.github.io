🐯 A Tiger in the Zoo

Class 10 · English (First Flight) · Poem – Chapter 5 · By Leslie Norris

1. About the Poet

Leslie Norris (1921–2006) was a Welsh poet, born in Merthyr Tydfil, Wales. He worked as a teacher and later as a university lecturer, and he spent the later part of his life in the United States. His poems often deal with nature, animals, memory and the countryside, written in clear and simple language.

  • His writing shows deep sympathy for living creatures.
  • In this poem he contrasts the free life of a tiger in the wild with its lonely life in a zoo.

4. Stanza-wise Detailed Explanation

Stanza 1 — The Caged Tiger

The poet introduces a tiger in a zoo, moving up and down inside his cage. His body is marked with bright, vivid stripes, so he looks beautiful and powerful. But he can take only a few steps before reaching the end of the cage. His paws are soft and padded, so he walks silently. His movement is quiet, yet inside him is suppressed anger. This silent anger shows that the tiger is furious at being imprisoned but can do nothing about it.

Stanza 2 — Where He Should Be (Hunting in the Wild)

Now the poet tells us where the tiger should be. He ought to be hiding in the shadows and creeping through the tall grass. He ought to be near a water hole where plump deer come to drink, waiting to hunt. This is his natural life: free, alert and in control. The poet draws a sharp contrast between what the tiger is and what he should be.

Stanza 3 — Where He Should Be (Power and Terror)

The poet continues. In the wild, the tiger would roam at the edge of the jungle near human villages, showing his white fangs and sharp claws, and the villagers would be afraid of him. This shows the tiger's true strength and majesty, which has been completely taken away from him in the zoo. Notice that the poet does not blame the tiger for being dangerous. He accepts it as part of the tiger's nature.

Last Stanza — The Sad Reality

The poem returns to the present. The tiger is locked inside a concrete cell with his strength behind bars. He keeps pacing along the length of the cage and ignores the visitors who come to stare at him. His mind is far away from them. At night he hears the last sounds of the day and the patrolling cars. Finally, he stares at the brilliant stars with his bright eyes. The stars stand for the freedom and wilderness that he longs for. The poem ends on a note of silent protest and sadness.

🌿 Key insight: The poem is built on a contrast: what the tiger is (caged, quiet, ignored) versus what he should be (free, hunting, feared).

6. Central Idea (Board Answer)

Central Idea / Theme: In A Tiger in the Zoo, Leslie Norris contrasts the free, powerful life of a tiger in the jungle with the confined, helpless life he leads in a zoo. The tiger, beautiful and strong, paces silently inside a small cage, filled with suppressed anger, while his mind is on the forests he should be roaming, hunting and spreading fear in. The visitors who come to stare cannot see his pain, so he simply ignores them and gazes at the stars, which symbolise the freedom he has lost. The poem conveys the poet's sympathy for caged animals and criticises humans for taking away wild creatures' natural homes and freedom. It is a plea for freedom and respect for all living beings.

Key Points to Remember

  • The poem is built on contrast: caged tiger vs tiger in the wild.
  • The tiger's suppressed anger and silent protest are the emotional centre.
  • Stars are a symbol of freedom; cage and concrete cell stand for captivity.
  • The poet criticises humans for exploiting animals and invites readers to protect wildlife and their habitats.

10. Important Questions & Answers

A. Short Answer Questions (30–40 words)

Q1. Why does the tiger walk quietly in the cage?

The tiger has soft padded paws, so he naturally moves without noise. Though he seems calm and silent outside, he is full of anger inside because he is locked in a small cage and cannot hunt or roam.

Q2. Where should the tiger be, according to the poet?

The tiger should be in the jungle, hiding in shadows, creeping through tall grass near a water hole to hunt deer, and prowling near villages to frighten people, showing his fangs and claws.

Q3. Why does the tiger ignore the visitors?

The tiger is angry and heartbroken at being caged. The visitors only stare at him for entertainment and cannot understand his pain, so he does not pay them any attention. His mind is far away in the jungle.

Q4. What do the stars symbolise in the poem?

The stars symbolise the freedom and the wild open world the tiger longs for. They are far and bright, just like the freedom that he has lost and which he can only look at.

Q5. Why does the poet describe the cage as a concrete cell?

It means the zoo cage is as hard and lifeless as a prison cell. It has no grass, trees or water, which shows that the tiger is treated like a prisoner by humans.

Q6. What does the poet want to convey through the poem?

The poet wants to say that wild animals belong to their natural habitat and should not be kept in cages. He asks us to respect animals' freedom and feel sympathy for them.

B. Long Answer Questions (100–120 words)

Q1. How does the poet show the contrast between the tiger's life in the zoo and in the wild?

Leslie Norris shows the contrast by describing the tiger's present and his natural life side by side. In the zoo, the tiger paces slowly inside a small cage, on soft paws, in silent anger, in a concrete cell with iron bars. In the wild, he should be lurking in the shadows, creeping through the long grass near the water hole to hunt deer, and snarling near the villages and frightening people. The repeated use of "should be" stresses what the tiger has lost. This contrast makes the reader feel sympathy for the tiger and realise the cruelty of keeping wild animals in captivity.

Q2. Describe the poet's attitude towards the caged tiger.

The poet's attitude is one of deep sympathy and respect. He describes the tiger's vivid stripes, his soft padded steps and his powerful nature with admiration. He shows that the tiger is not meant to be locked in a concrete cell, where his strength is wasted behind bars. Words such as "rage" show that the poet understands the tiger's suppressed anger. The tiger ignores the visitors and stares at the stars, showing his dignity and longing for freedom. Through this the poet criticises humans who imprison wild animals for entertainment and appeals to the reader to respect nature and its creatures.

Q3. What message does the poem "A Tiger in the Zoo" give?

The poem gives a strong message about freedom, wildlife and humane treatment. It tells us that animals like the tiger belong to their natural habitat, where they can live freely according to their nature. Locking them in cages for human amusement takes away their dignity and strength and causes them suffering. The poet wants readers to understand the pain of caged creatures and to protect forests and wildlife instead of destroying them. The poem encourages us to be sensitive towards all living beings and to value freedom, both for animals and for humans.
