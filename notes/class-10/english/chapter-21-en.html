2. Central Idea / Theme (as expected in the CBSE Board Exam)

'The Thief's Story' shows how trust and kindness can reform even a habitual thief. Hari Singh, a young thief, meets Anil, a writer, and plans to rob him. But Anil treats him with trust, gives him food, a place to stay and promises to teach him to read and write. This kindness slowly touches Hari's heart, and when he has the chance to steal Anil's money, his conscience stops him.

The story tells us that people are not born criminals; circumstances make them so, and love, trust and education can bring out the good in them. It also shows the power of conscience: even a thief who is used to cheating can feel guilty when someone believes in him.

  • Trust can reform a person: Anil's faith makes Hari rethink his life.
  • Kindness is stronger than punishment: Anil never scolds or threatens Hari.
  • Conscience wins over temptation: Hari cannot betray the one person who trusted him.
  • Education and honest work give a new life: reading and writing open a new path for Hari.

3. Summary in Simple Words

Hari Singh is a fifteen-year-old boy who has been a thief for years. One day he sees a young man named Anil watching a wrestling match. Hari strikes up a conversation and pretends to be an orphan looking for work. Anil is a writer who lives alone and who is kind and trusting. He tells Hari that he can work for him as a cook and promises to pay him, and also to teach him to read and write.

Hari thinks that Anil is a simple and easily fooled person, and he plans to rob him when the time is right. For some time he cooks for Anil, and Anil begins his lessons, teaching him to read and write. Hari finds himself enjoying the lessons, though he continues to look for a chance to steal.

One day Anil sells a story and earns some money. He keeps it in a place that Hari knows about. That night, Hari takes the money and runs towards the railway station, planning to go far away.

But at the station he cannot feel happy. He thinks of Anil's trust in him, the food, the lessons and the kindness that no one had ever shown him. His conscience troubles him, and he decides that he cannot cheat the man who believed in him. He gives up the idea of running away and returns, choosing a new, honest way of life. The story closes with Hari's change of heart.

    6. Part-wise Explanation

    Part 1 — Hari the thief

    Hari Singh is a young thief, clever at tricking people. When he sees Anil, a gentle and trusting young man, he thinks he has found an easy target.

    Key point: Hari's cunning nature and his view that Anil is a fool.

    Part 2 — Anil's kindness

    Anil accepts Hari's story that he is an orphan, offers him a job as cook, and promises to teach him to read and write. He never doubts or scolds him.

    Key point: trust and kindness without conditions.

    Part 3 — Lessons and growth

    Hari begins to study, and he enjoys it. He feels something new: someone cares for his future. Yet he still watches for a chance to rob Anil.

    Key point: education begins to change Hari, but he has not yet changed fully.

    Part 4 — The temptation

    Anil sells a story and earns money. Hari sees his chance, takes the money and runs towards the railway station.

    Key point: the pull of old habits against new feelings.

    Part 5 — The conscience at the station

    At the station, Hari feels no joy. He remembers Anil's trust, the food and the lessons. His conscience troubles him.

    Key point: the turning point of the story; conscience wins.

    Part 6 — The return

    Hari gives up the idea of running away and goes back, choosing to be honest. A thief becomes a better person through love and trust.

    Key point: reform through kindness.

    7. Character Sketches

    Hari Singh

    A fifteen-year-old thief and the narrator. He is clever, cunning and proud of his skill at deceiving people. But he is also young, hungry for affection and capable of change. Anil's kindness awakens his conscience, and he chooses honesty over theft.

    Anil

    A young, kind and trusting writer, about twenty. He is generous, patient and unselfish: he feeds Hari, gives him work and teaches him to read and write. He sees the good in Hari and treats him with respect, which brings about Hari's reform.

    Other people

    The crowd at the wrestling match and the world of petty crime that Hari came from form the background of his life.

    8. Themes and Message

    Trust and reform

    Faith in a person can bring out the good in him.

    Power of kindness

    Love and kindness change people more than punishment.

    Conscience

    Even a thief has a conscience that can win over temptation.

    Education

    Learning to read and write opens a path to a better life.

    Circumstances make criminals

    A boy like Hari becomes a thief because of his surroundings, not by nature.

    Moral

    Trust and kindness can turn a wrong-doer into a good person.

    12. Long Answer Questions (5–6 marks)

    Q1. Write a character sketch of Hari Singh.
    Hari Singh is a fifteen-year-old thief who has lived by cheating people. He is clever and cunning and believes Anil is a fool who can be robbed. But he is also young and hungry for affection. Anil's trust, food and lessons slowly touch him, and he starts to enjoy reading and writing. When he steals the money, his conscience does not let him enjoy it, and at the station he decides to give up theft. So Hari is cunning but also sensitive and capable of change.
    Q2. How does Anil change Hari Singh?
    Anil accepts Hari without doubting him, gives him food, work and a place to stay, and promises to teach him to read and write. He never scolds or threatens him. This trust and kindness are new to Hari, who has been used to deceiving and being deceived. Gradually, Hari enjoys the lessons and feels cared for. When he tries to steal Anil's money, he cannot bear the guilt and gives up the idea. Thus Anil's kindness reforms him.
    Q3. What is the message of the story 'The Thief's Story'?
    The story teaches that trust, love and kindness can reform even a habitual wrongdoer. People are not born thieves; their surroundings make them so. When a person is trusted and shown affection, his conscience awakens and he chooses to do right. The story also shows the value of education and the power of conscience over temptation.
