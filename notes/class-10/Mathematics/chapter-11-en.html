Class 10 Maths – Ch 11: Areas Related to Circles

1. Circle – Circumference and Area

Circumference C = 2πr = πd   Area A = πr²
π = 22/7 (when r is a multiple of 7) or 3.14.
Semicircle: area = ½πr², perimeter = πr + 2r.

A circle of radius r rolls along the ground for exactly one full turn. The distance it covers equals its circumference 2πr. For r = 7 cm, C = 44 cm and A = 154 cm².

Wheel problems: number of revolutions = total distance ÷ circumference. Ratios: circumferences ∝ radii; areas ∝ (radii)². Eg radii 2 : 3 ⇒ areas 4 : 9.

2. Area of a Ring (Annulus)

The pink ring lies between two concentric circles of radii R (outer) and r (inner). Area of ring = area of big circle − area of small circle.

Area of ring = π(R² − r²) = π(R + r)(R − r)

3. Sector and Arc

Sector: region between two radii and the arc. Arc: part of the circle's boundary. A sector with angle θ at the centre is the θ/360 fraction of the whole circle. Quadrant θ = 90°, semicircle θ = 180°.
Length of arc l = (θ/360°) × 2πr
Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr² = ½ l r
Perimeter of sector = l + 2r

4. Segment of a Circle

The chord AB cuts off the green minor segment. The yellow sector includes the triangle OAB as well, so remove the triangle to get the segment.

Area of minor segment = Area of sector OAB − Area of ΔOAB
= (θ/360)πr² − ½ r² sin θ   (for θ = 60°, ΔOAB is equilateral: area = (√3/4) r²)
Area of major segment = πr² − Area of minor segment

5. Combinations of Plane Figures

  • Draw the figure and split it into squares, rectangles, triangles, sectors, semicircles.
  • Shaded area = (area of whole) − (area of un-shaded parts), or the sum of the shaded parts.
  • Clock hands: minute hand turns 6° per minute, hour hand 0.5° per minute; area swept = sector area.
  • Cow/horse tied at the corner of a field: the grazing area is a quadrant (if the rope is shorter than the sides).

Q & A – Practice

(Take π = 22/7 unless stated.)

MCQs (NCERT / Balaji / Rajeev / UP Board style)

Circumference of a circle of radius 7 cm is (a) 22 cm (b) 44 cm (c) 154 cm (d) 49 cm
(b) 44 cm: 2 × 22/7 × 7.
Area of a circle of diameter 14 cm is (a) 44 cm² (b) 154 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 308 cm²
(b) 154 cm²: r = 7, πr² = 154.
Area of a sector of angle 90° and radius 14 cm is (a) 154 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 77 cm²
(a) 154 cm²: ¼ × 22/7 × 196.
Length of the arc of a sector of angle 60° and radius 21 cm is (a) 11 cm (b) 22 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 66 cm
(b) 22 cm: (1/6) × 2 × 22/7 × 21.
Area of a ring with radii 14 cm and 7 cm is (a) 462 cm² (b) 154 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 308 cm²
(a) 462 cm²: 22/7 × (196 − 49).
Perimeter of a semicircle of radius 7 cm is (a) 22 cm (b) 36 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 29 cm
(b) 36 cm: πr + 2r = 22 + 14.
Circumferences of two circles are in ratio 2 : 3. The ratio of their areas is (a) 2 : 3 (b) 4 : 9 (c) 9 : 4 (d) 3 : 2
(b) 4 : 9: areas ∝ radii².

Short Answer

A wheel has diameter 42 cm. How many revolutions will it make to cover 13.2 km?
C = 22/7 × 42 = 132 cm. Distance = 13.2 km = 1,320,000 cm. Revolutions = 1,320,000/132 = 10,000.
Find the perimeter of a quadrant of a circle of radius 14 cm.
2r + πr/2 = 28 + 22 = 50 cm.
Find the area of a sector of angle 60° in a circle of radius 6 cm.
(60/360) × 22/7 × 36 = 132/7 ≈ 18.86 cm².
The minute hand of a clock is 14 cm long. Find the area swept in 5 minutes.
5 min = 30°. Area = (30/360) × 22/7 × 14² = 616/12 ≈ 51.33 cm².
Find the area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is 22 cm.
2πr = 22 ⇒ r = 3.5 cm. Area = ¼ × 22/7 × 3.5² = 9.625 cm².

Long Answer

Find the areas of the minor and major segments of a circle of radius 6 cm formed by a chord subtending 60° at the centre. (π = 3.14, √3 = 1.73)
Sector = (60/360) × 3.14 × 36 = 18.84 cm². ΔOAB is equilateral: (√3/4) × 36 = 9√3 = 15.57 cm². Minor segment = 18.84 − 15.57 = 3.27 cm². Circle = 3.14 × 36 = 113.04 cm². Major segment = 113.04 − 3.27 = 109.77 cm².
A circle of radius 7 cm is inscribed in a square. Find the area of the region between the square and the circle.
Square side = diameter = 14 cm. Square area = 196 cm². Circle area = 154 cm². Required area = 42 cm².
A horse is tied to a corner of a 15 m square field with a 5 m rope. (i) Find the area it can graze. (ii) Find the increase if the rope is made 10 m long. (π = 3.14)
(i) Quadrant: ¼ × 3.14 × 25 = 19.625 m². (ii) New area ¼ × 3.14 × 100 = 78.5 m². Increase = 78.5 − 19.625 = 58.875 m².
The inner circumference of a circular track is 352 m and the track is 14 m wide. Find the outer circumference and the area of the track.
2πr = 352 ⇒ r = 56 m, R = 70 m. Outer circumference = 2 × 22/7 × 70 = 440 m. Area = 22/7 × (70² − 56²) = 22/7 × 1764 = 5544 m².
An arc of length 22 cm belongs to a circle of radius 21 cm. Find the angle of the sector and its area.
22 = (θ/360) × 2 × 22/7 × 21 = (θ/360) × 132 ⇒ θ = 60°. Area = ½ l r = ½ × 22 × 21 = 231 cm².
