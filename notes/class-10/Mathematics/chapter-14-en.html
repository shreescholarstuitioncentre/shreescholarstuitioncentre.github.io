Class 10 Maths – Ch 14: Probability

1. Basic Terms

Experiment / trial: an action with uncertain outcome (tossing a coin). Outcome: one possible result. Sample space: the set of all possible outcomes. Event E: a collection of outcomes. Equally likely outcomes: each outcome has the same chance (a fair coin or unbiased die).

2. Theoretical (Classical) Probability

P(E) = Number of outcomes favourable to E / Total number of possible outcomes
valid only when all outcomes are equally likely.

The probability scale runs from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). A fair coin giving heads is exactly halfway, 1/2. A probability can never be negative or greater than 1.

3. Important Properties

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
Sure (certain) event: P = 1. Impossible event: P = 0.
P(E) + P(not E) = 1 ⇒ P(Ē) = 1 − P(E)
Sum of the probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment = 1.

4. Sample Spaces to Remember

  • One coin: {H, T}. Two coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT} (4 outcomes). Three coins: 8 outcomes.
  • One die: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. Primes: 2, 3, 5. Perfect squares: 1, 4.
  • Two dice: 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes.

Each cell shows the sum of the two dice. Sum 7 appears 6 times ((1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1)), so P(7) = 6/36 = 1/6. Sum 2 and 12 occur only once each (1/36).

5. Pack of 52 Playing Cards

4 suits (spades, clubs, hearts, diamonds) × 13 cards. Black cards = 26 (spades + clubs), red cards = 26 (hearts + diamonds). Face cards: King, Queen, Jack in each suit = 12. Aces = 4. Number cards = 36 (including aces as 1 – check your book's convention).

6. Steps to Solve

  • Write the total number of outcomes.
  • Count the favourable outcomes carefully (list them if needed).
  • Divide and reduce the fraction.
  • For "at least one" or "not", use P(Ē) = 1 − P(E).
  • With replacement total stays same; without replacement it reduces by 1 each draw.

Q & A – Practice

MCQs (NCERT / Balaji / Rajeev / UP Board style)

The probability of an impossible event is (a) 1 (b) 0 (c) 1/2 (d) −1
(b) 0.
A die is thrown once. P(getting a prime number) = (a) 1/3 (b) 1/2 (c) 2/3 (d) 1/6
(b) 1/2: primes 2, 3, 5 ⇒ 3/6.
A coin is tossed once. P(head) = (a) 0 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/2 (d) 1
(c) 1/2.
If P(E) = 0.05, then P(not E) = (a) 0.05 (b) 0.95 (c) 0.5 (d) 1.05
(b) 0.95: 1 − 0.05.
A card is drawn from a pack of 52. P(king) = (a) 1/52 (b) 1/26 (c) 1/13 (d) 1/4
(c) 1/13: 4/52.
Two dice are thrown. P(sum = 7) = (a) 1/12 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/9 (d) 7/36
(b) 1/6: 6/36.
A bag has 3 red and 5 black balls. P(red ball) = (a) 3/5 (b) 5/8 (c) 3/8 (d) 1/8
(c) 3/8.

Short Answer

A die is thrown once. Find P(number less than 3) and P(multiple of 3).
Less than 3: {1, 2} ⇒ 2/6 = 1/3. Multiples of 3: {3, 6} ⇒ 2/6 = 1/3.
Two coins are tossed together. Find P(at least one head) and P(no head).
Sample space HH, HT, TH, TT. At least one head: 3 ⇒ 3/4. No head: TT ⇒ 1/4.
A bag has 5 red, 8 white and 4 green balls. Find P(white) and P(not green).
Total = 17. P(white) = 8/17. P(not green) = 13/17 = 13/17.
Find the probability of drawing a red face card from a pack of 52 cards.
Red face cards: J, Q, K of hearts and diamonds = 6. P = 6/52 = 3/26.
What is the probability that a leap year has 53 Sundays?
366 days = 52 weeks + 2 extra days. The pair of extra days can be (Mon-Tue, Tue-Wed, Wed-Thu, Thu-Fri, Fri-Sat, Sat-Sun, Sun-Mon): 7 equally likely, 2 contain Sunday. P = 2/7.

Long Answer

Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that (i) the sum is 8 (ii) both numbers are equal (iii) the sum is at most 4.
Total = 36. (i) Sum 8: (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) = 5 ⇒ 5/36. (ii) Doubles: 6 ⇒ 1/6. (iii) Sum 2: 1, sum 3: 2, sum 4: 3 ⇒ 6 ⇒ 1/6.
A box has 12 balls, x of them black. One ball is drawn. If 6 more black balls are added, the probability of a black ball becomes double of before. Find x.
P₁ = x/12. After adding: P₂ = (x + 6)/18. P₂ = 2P₁ ⇒ (x + 6)/18 = x/6 ⇒ x + 6 = 3x ⇒ x = 3.
A jar contains 24 marbles, some green and others blue. If P(green) = 2/3, find the number of blue marbles.
Green = (2/3) × 24 = 16. Blue = 24 − 16 = 8.
A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck. Find the probability of (i) king of red colour (ii) a face card (iii) a red face card (iv) jack of hearts (v) a spade (vi) queen of diamonds.
(i) 2/52 = 1/26. (ii) 12/52 = 3/13. (iii) 6/52 = 3/26. (iv) 1/52. (v) 13/52 = 1/4. (vi) 1/52.
Three coins are tossed together. Find the probability of (i) exactly 2 heads (ii) at least 2 heads (iii) all heads.
8 outcomes: HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT. (i) HHT, HTH, THH ⇒ 3/8. (ii) 3 + 1 = 4 ⇒ 1/2. (iii) HHH ⇒ 1/8.
