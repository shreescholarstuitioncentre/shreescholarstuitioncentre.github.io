Class 10 Maths – Ch 6: Triangles

1. Similar Figures

Similar figures have the same shape but not necessarily the same size (a photo and its enlargement). Congruent figures are similar and of equal size.
Two polygons are similar if (i) corresponding angles are equal and (ii) corresponding sides are in the same ratio. Symbol: ∼
All circles are similar; all squares are similar; all equilateral triangles are similar.

2. Similarity of Triangles

ΔABC ∼ ΔPQR if ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R and AB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RP. The order of letters matters.

The green triangle is the red-lined one doubled: sides 3-4-5 become 6-8-10, and the angles θ and φ stay the same.

Criteria for Similarity

AA (AAA): two angles equal ⇒ similar (third automatically equal).
SSS: all three pairs of sides in the same ratio ⇒ similar.
SAS: one angle equal and the sides including it in the same ratio ⇒ similar.

3. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales)

BPT: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides at distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
Converse: If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

Triangle ABC is drawn first, then the red line DE is drawn parallel to BC. With AD : DB = 2 : 3, the same ratio holds on the other side: AE : EC = 2 : 3. Converse: if the ratios are equal, DE must be parallel to BC.

4. Areas of Similar Triangles

If ΔABC ∼ ΔPQR then ar(ABC)/ar(PQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)².
Ratio of areas = square of ratio of corresponding sides (also of altitudes and medians).

5. Pythagoras Theorem

Theorem: In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
Converse: If in a triangle the square of one side equals the sum of squares of the other two, the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.
Pythagorean triples: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17), (7,24,25).

Squares drawn on the sides of the 3-4-5 right triangle have areas 9, 16 and 25. The two smaller squares together equal the big one: 9 + 16 = 25.

Short Answer

In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC, AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Find EC.
AD/DB = AE/EC ⇒ 1.5/3 = 1/EC ⇒ EC = 2 cm.
Is a triangle with sides 7, 24, 25 right-angled?
7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25² ⇒ yes, right angle opposite 25.
ΔABC ∼ ΔDEF, areas 64 cm² and 121 cm². If EF = 15.4 cm, find BC.
BC/EF = √(64/121) = 8/11 ⇒ BC = 15.4 × 8/11 = 11.2 cm.
A 15 m ladder reaches a window 12 m high. How far is its foot from the wall?
√(15² − 12²) = √81 = 9 m.
DE ∥ BC with AD = x, DB = x − 2, AE = x + 2, EC = x − 1. Find x.
x/(x − 2) = (x + 2)/(x − 1) ⇒ x² − x = x² − 4 ⇒ x = 4.

Long Answer

State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
Given: ΔABC, DE ∥ BC, D on AB, E on AC. To prove: AD/DB = AE/EC.
Join BE and CD. ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB (same height from E). ar(ADE)/ar(CDE) = AE/EC (same height from D). ΔBDE and ΔCDE are on the same base DE between the same parallels DE and BC, so ar(BDE) = ar(CDE). Hence AD/DB = AE/EC. ∎
Prove that the ratio of areas of two similar triangles equals the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
Let ΔABC ∼ ΔPQR; draw altitudes AM ⟂ BC, PN ⟂ QR. ar(ABC)/ar(PQR) = (½·BC·AM)/(½·QR·PN) = (BC·AM)/(QR·PN). In ΔABM and ΔPQN: ∠B = ∠Q and ∠M = ∠N = 90° ⇒ similar (AA), so AM/PN = AB/PQ. Also AB/PQ = BC/QR. So ratio = (BC/QR)(AB/PQ) = (BC/QR)² = (AB/PQ)² = (CA/RP)². ∎
Prove Pythagoras theorem using similar triangles.
ΔABC right-angled at B; draw BD ⟂ AC. ΔADB ∼ ΔABC (AA) ⇒ AD/AB = AB/AC ⇒ AB² = AD·AC. ΔBDC ∼ ΔABC (AA) ⇒ CD/BC = BC/AC ⇒ BC² = CD·AC. Adding: AB² + BC² = AC(AD + DC) = AC². ∎
A vertical pole 6 m long casts a shadow 4 m long. At the same time a tower casts a 28 m shadow. Find the tower's height.
Triangles formed by the sun's rays are similar (AA): 6/4 = h/28 ⇒ h = 6 × 28/4 = 42 m.
In right ΔABC, right angle at C, P and Q are points on CA and CB. Prove PB² + QA² = AB² + PQ².
PB² = PC² + BC² (ΔPCB), QA² = QC² + AC² (ΔQCA). Add: PB² + QA² = (PC² + QC²) + (BC² + AC²) = PQ² (ΔPCQ) + AB² (ΔABC). ∎
