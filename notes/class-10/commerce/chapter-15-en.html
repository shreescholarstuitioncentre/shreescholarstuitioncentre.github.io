Part A – Export and Import Trade

1. Meaning

Foreign trade = buying and selling of goods and services between two or more countries. Also called international or external trade.

Easy example: India sends tea to the USA (export) and brings crude oil from abroad (import) ✔

2. Types

  • Import – bringing goods from another country
  • Export – sending home-country goods abroad
  • Entrepot (re-export) – importing goods and sending them on to a third country

3. Flow of Import-Export (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: Country A sends goods (orange dot) and receives payment from Country B (green dot). What is export for one country is import for the other.

4. Importance of Foreign Trade

  • Gets goods scarce at home
  • Earns foreign exchange
  • Growth of industries and jobs
  • Benefit of division of labour
  • International friendship

5. Export Procedure (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: There are six steps from enquiry to payment. Each box lights up in turn and the dot shows the whole journey. Payment is received when the bank gets the documents.

6. Import Procedure (in short)

  • Import licence and arranging foreign exchange
  • Placing order and opening a letter of credit
  • Receiving shipment advice
  • Filing bill of entry and paying customs duty
  • Clearing the goods (taking delivery)

Part B – Banking: Origin and Functions

1. Meaning

A bank = an institution that accepts deposits from the public and gives loans to those in need. It is the centre of savings and capital formation.

Easy example: you deposit money in a bank and the bank lends it to a trader ✔

2. Origin

  • The word "bank" comes from the Italian word "banco" (bench) – money-lenders in Italy sat on benches to do business.
  • Modern banking began in Italy.

3. Growth of Banking in India

YearEvent
1770Bank of Hindustan (first)
1935Reserve Bank of India (RBI) set up
1955Imperial Bank → State Bank of India (SBI)
196914 big banks nationalised
19806 more banks nationalised

📝 Explanation: Depositors put in money (orange dot) and the bank lends it out. Borrowers pay higher interest (green dot) while the bank pays depositors lower interest. This difference is the bank's profit.

7. Types of Banks

  • Central bank – RBI (issues notes, controls banks)
  • Commercial banks – SBI, PNB etc.
  • Co-operative banks – for farmers / small traders
  • Development banks – long-term loans to industry
⚡ Quick Revision: Bank = deposits + loans | Primary functions = accept deposits, give loans | RBI = 1935 | Nationalisation = 1969 and 1980
