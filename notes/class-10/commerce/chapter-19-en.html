Part A – Indigenous Bankers

1. Meaning

Indigenous bankers = private individuals or firms that accept deposits, give loans and deal in hundis. They are known as sahukars, mahajans, seths, shroffs, chettiars etc.

Easy example: the village mahajan who lends to a farmer for cultivation ✔

2. Features

  • Private ownership, few rules
  • Personal relations with customers
  • Little paperwork, quick loans
  • Interest rates often high
  • Do trade along with banking
  • Use of hundis

3. How an Indigenous Banker Works (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: The banker takes deposits and quickly lends to the needy (orange dot). Borrowers pay higher interest (green dot). The process is easy, but the interest is costly.

4. Hundi (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: A hundi is an indigenous bill of exchange. The drawer gives a written order to another banker to pay the payee. After acceptance, it is paid. Orange dot = journey of the hundi.

TypeFeature
Darshani hundiPayable on sight (on demand)
Muddati hundiPayable after a fixed period

5. Main Functions

  • Accepting deposits
  • Loans to farmers, traders and artisans
  • Issuing and discounting hundis
  • Money changing
  • Remitting money for trade

6. Defects

  • High interest, chance of exploitation
  • No proper accounts
  • No government/RBI control
  • Mixing of banking and trade

Part B – Meaning and Scope of Economics

1. Meaning

The word "Economics" comes from the Greek "oikonomia" (oikos = house, nomos = management), i.e. management of the resources of a household/society. Economics studies how people earn and use wealth.

Easy example: a mother divides limited money among ration, fees and clothes = economic thinking ✔

2. Main Definitions

EconomistApproachEssence
Adam SmithWealthStudy of the wealth of nations (1776)
MarshallWelfareStudy of man's material welfare
RobbinsScarcityStudy of choice between unlimited wants and limited resources
SamuelsonGrowthStudy of efficient use of resources and economic growth

3. Basic Problem – Scarcity (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: Our wants are unlimited (the heavier pan) while resources are limited (the lighter pan). So we must make a choice about which want to satisfy first. This is the basic problem of economics.

4. Scope of Economics (animated diagram)

📝 Explanation: Economics has five divisions – production, exchange, distribution, consumption and public finance. The orange dot shows goods moving from production to consumption, while public finance (the government's income and spending) influences all of them.

  • Consumption – using goods to satisfy wants
  • Production – creating utility in goods
  • Exchange – buying and selling
  • Distribution – sharing wages, rent, interest, profit
  • Public finance – government income and expenditure

5. Micro vs Macro Economics

BasisMicroMacro
Study ofIndividual / firm / industryWhole economy
ExamplePrice of one goodNational income, inflation

6. Positive and Normative

  • Positive – "what is" (facts) – e.g. when price rises, demand falls.
  • Normative – "what ought to be" – e.g. the rich should be taxed more.
⚡ Quick Revision: Economics = choice with limited resources and unlimited wants | 5 parts = consumption, production, exchange, distribution, public finance | Micro = individual, Macro = whole country
