1. Meaning of Filing

DefinitionFiling is the systematic arrangement and safe keeping of letters, documents and papers in a proper order so that they can be found quickly whenever they are needed.
TermMeaning
FileA folder, cover or box in which papers are kept (also the bundle of papers itself)
FilingThe process of arranging and storing papers in files
Filing systemThe method and equipment used to arrange files
📌 Filing is part of office record-keeping. Without it, papers get lost, work is delayed and legal proof is missing.

2. Importance of Filing

Learn the six main benefits with the mind-map:

Animated mind-map – Importance of filing

🔍 Explanation

  1. Easy retrieval – the paper is found within seconds.
  2. Safe custody – protected from loss, theft, dust and damage.
  3. Legal evidence – letters, bills and contracts can be shown in court or to tax officers.
  4. Saves time – staff do not waste time searching.
  5. Reference – past decisions, prices and dealings can be consulted.
  6. Continuity – a new employee can carry on the work from the records.

3. Essentials of a good filing system

  • Simplicity – easy to understand and operate by everyone.
  • Economy – low cost of equipment, space and staff.
  • Flexibility (elasticity) – room to add new files without disturbing the old.
  • Accessibility – papers can be reached quickly.
  • Safety – protection from fire, damp, insects and theft.
  • Proper classification – papers grouped logically.
  • Cross-reference – a second route to papers that fit under two heads.
  • Regular weeding – old, useless papers removed on time.
  • Proper indexing – a guide to locate files.
Memory word: S-E-F-A-S-C-C-W-I… or simply think: Simple, Cheap, Elastic, Quick, Safe!

4. Types of files & equipment

Papers are kept in different kinds of files depending on their size and use:

Animated – common types of files

🔍 Explanation

  • Spike (bill) file – a metal spike on a base; used for bills and vouchers kept temporarily.
  • Box file – a box-shaped file with a spine label; keeps papers loose and in a large amount.
  • Arch lever file – papers are punched and held by a lever; holds papers securely.
  • Ring binder – papers can be added or removed easily; good for manuals and price lists.

5. Steps in filing (How a paper reaches the file)

Every paper that arrives is processed through the following steps:

Animated flow – 7 steps of filing

🔍 Explanation

  1. Receiving – papers are collected from post, staff and customers.
  2. Inspecting – the officer reads and marks it "released" (ready to be filed).
  3. Indexing – deciding under which heading to file (name, subject, number).
  4. Sorting – papers are arranged heading-wise, usually in alphabetical or numerical order.
  5. Cross-reference – a slip placed in a second file if the paper concerns two heads.
  6. Storing – the paper is put in its file, latest on top.
  7. Retrieval – taken out when needed; an out-card is left in its place.

6. Methods of classification (arrangement of files)

Classification means grouping papers on a common basis. Start with the most common method – alphabetical.

A. Alphabetical order (A–Z)

  • Files are arranged by the name (surname first) from A to Z.
  • If the first letters are the same, compare the second letter, and so on.
  • Titles such as Mr., Dr., Shri are ignored.

8. Indexing & cross-reference

IndexingMaking a guide or list that shows where a file is kept. Types: Page index (a book), Card index (cards in a tray) and Visible index (cards whose edges stay visible).
Cross-referenceWhen a paper concerns two or more heads, the original is kept in one file and a cross-reference slip ("see also…") is placed in the other.

Example: A letter from Ram & Sons about the Delhi branch.

Animated – how a cross-reference works

🔍 Explanation

  • The letter fits two heads – a firm name and a place.
  • The original (blue arrow) is filed under the main head.
  • The slip (red arrow) in the second file points to the main file.

9. Retrieval, transfer & weeding

  • Retrieval – taking out a paper when needed; the borrower signs a requisition slip and an out-card replaces the file.
  • Follow-up (tickler) file – reminds staff of papers to be dealt with on a certain date.
  • Transfer – moving files that are rarely used to a storeroom to make room.
  • Weeding out – removing old, useless papers (destroy) as per legal rules and policy; important ones may be archived.
Animated – life cycle of a file

🔍 Explanation

  • Active – current files are used every day and stay in the office.
  • Semi-active – when use falls, files are transferred to a storeroom.
  • Dead – after the legal retention period they are weeded out.

10. Differences & common mistakes

BasisFilingIndexing
MeaningArranging & storing papersPreparing a guide to locate papers
PurposeSafe keepingQuick finding
ResultFilesIndex (page/card)

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Filing papers without a release mark from the officer.
  • Forgetting the out-card when a file is taken out.
  • No cross-reference for papers with two heads.
  • Placing the oldest paper on top (latest must be on top).
  • Ignoring weeding – cabinets get overloaded.

11. Q&A and exam tips

Q1. What is filing?
A: Systematic arrangement and safe keeping of papers so that they can be found quickly when needed.
Q2. State any four advantages of filing.
A: Easy retrieval, safe custody, legal evidence, saves time, helps reference.
Q3. Name the methods of classification.
A: Alphabetical, numerical, alpha-numeric, chronological, geographical, subject.
Q4. What is cross-referencing?
A: Placing a slip in a second file pointing to the main file where a paper concerning two heads is kept.
Q5. What is weeding out?
A: Removing old, useless papers from the files regularly.
