Partnership Accounts + Bank Reconciliation Statement

3 Parts: ① Partnership Basics ② Profit & Loss Appropriation Account ③ Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)
Part 1

① Partnership – Basics

Definition (Indian Partnership Act 1932, Sec. 4): Relation between persons who agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.

Features

  • Minimum 2 partners (max 50)
  • Written/oral agreement – Partnership Deed
  • Profit-loss sharing in agreed ratio
  • Unlimited liability · Mutual agency

Partnership Deed contains

  • Names, capital, profit ratio
  • Interest on capital & drawings
  • Salary/commission to partners
  • If silent → Sec. 13: equal share, no salary, no interest on capital, 6% interest on loan from partner

Capital Accounts

  • Fixed: capital stays same; profit, drawings go to Current A/c
  • Fluctuating: everything adjusted in Capital A/c
Part 2

② Profit & Loss Appropriation A/c

Account prepared after Profit & Loss A/c to divide net profit among partners.
Interest on Capital = Capital × Rate% × Time  |  Interest on Drawings = Drawings × Rate% × Time
  • Interest on capital → charge (Dr. side, reduces profit)
  • Partner's salary / commission → charge (Dr.)
  • Interest on drawings → income (Cr. side, increases profit)
  • Remaining profit → shared in profit ratio

Example

Net profit ₹1,00,000. Capital: A ₹2,00,000, B ₹1,00,000. Ratio 3:2. Interest on capital 5%. B's salary ₹10,000.
  • Interest: A 10,000 + B 5,000 = 15,000
  • Salary: B = 10,000
  • Divisible profit = 1,00,000 – 15,000 – 10,000 = 75,000
  • A = 75,000×3/5 = 45,000; B = 75,000×2/5 = 30,000

🎞 Animated Diagram: Profit Appropriation

Explanation: Net profit flows down like a waterfall. Charges are deducted first, then what is left (divisible profit) is split between partners.
Journal: P&L Appropriation A/c Dr. To Partners' Capital/Current A/c
Part 3

③ Bank Reconciliation Statement

Meaning: A statement prepared to find the reasons for the difference between the bank balance in the Cash Book and the Pass Book, and to match them.

Why prepare?

  • Find errors & frauds
  • Know items not yet recorded
  • Know true bank balance

Reasons for difference

  • Cheque issued but not presented
  • Cheque deposited but not yet collected
  • Direct bank charges / interest / dividend
  • Cheque dishonoured
  • Errors in books

🎞 Animated Diagram: Balancing the Scale

Explanation: At first the two balances are unequal (scale tilted). After adding/deducting reconciling items, both become equal – scale balanced.

Rules (starting from Cash Book Dr. balance)

ADDLESS
Cheques issued, not presentedCheques deposited, not collected
Direct deposit / interest / dividend by bankBank charges, direct payments
Dishonoured cheque
Starting from Pass Book balance → do the opposite.

Example

Cash Book balance ₹10,000; cheque issued not presented 2,000; cheque deposited not collected 3,000; bank charges 100; interest credited 200.
10,000 + 2,000 + 200 – 3,000 – 100 = ₹9,100 (Pass Book balance)
Remember: if the bank column shows overdraft (credit balance), rules are reversed. ✔ Best of luck! 🍀
