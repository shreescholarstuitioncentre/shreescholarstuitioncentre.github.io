Depreciation + Bills of Exchange, Promissory Notes & Hundi

5 Parts: ① Depreciation Basics ② Methods (SLM & WDV) ③ Bill of Exchange ④ Promissory Note & Hundi ⑤ Quick Revision
Part 1

① Depreciation – Basics

Meaning: Permanent, gradual fall in the value of a fixed asset due to use, time or obsolescence.

Causes

  • Wear & tear (use)
  • Passage of time / expiry (lease, patent)
  • Obsolescence (new technology)
  • Accident & depletion (mines, wells)

Objectives

  • Find true profit (charge cost of asset over its life)
  • Show asset at true value in Balance Sheet
  • Build funds to replace asset
  • Tax saving
Depreciation is a non-cash expense: no money leaves the business, but profit reduces.

Journal Entries

  • Depreciation A/c Dr. To Asset A/c
  • Profit & Loss A/c Dr. To Depreciation A/c
Part 2

② Methods of Depreciation

A) Straight Line Method (SLM / Fixed Instalment) – same amount every year.
Annual Dep. = (Cost – Scrap Value) ÷ Life in years
B) Written Down Value (WDV / Diminishing Balance) – fixed % on the reducing book value.
Dep. = Opening Book Value × Rate %

Example: Machine cost ₹1,00,000, scrap ₹10,000, life 5 yrs

  • SLM = (1,00,000 – 10,000) ÷ 5 = ₹18,000 every year
  • WDV @20%: Yr1 = 20,000; Yr2 = 80,000×20% = 16,000; Yr3 = 64,000×20% = 12,800

🎞 Animated Diagram: SLM vs WDV

Label: orange bars = SLM (equal), blue bars = WDV (falling). Explanation: In WDV, dep. is high early and low later, so repair cost (rising) + dep. (falling) stays balanced. In WDV, asset value never becomes zero.
Part 3

③ Bill of Exchange (Hundi-Vipatra)

Definition (Sec. 5, N.I. Act 1881): A written, unconditional order, signed by the maker (drawer), directing a certain person to pay a certain sum to a certain person or bearer – on demand or at a fixed time.

Features

  • In writing & signed by drawer
  • Unconditional order to pay
  • Fixed amount, fixed time
  • Needs acceptance by drawee
  • Revenue stamp is required

3 Parties

  • Drawer – seller/creditor who writes the bill
  • Drawee – buyer/debtor who must pay
  • Payee – receives money (often the drawer himself)

🎞 Animated Diagram: Life of a Bill

Explanation: ① Seller draws bill on buyer. ② Buyer accepts by signing – now it is a legal liability. ③ On due date buyer pays.

Important Terms

  • Due date = bill term + 3 days of grace. e.g. 5 Jan, 2 months → 5 Mar + 3 = 8 March
  • Endorsement: signing at the back to transfer
  • Discounting: getting cash from bank before due date (bank cuts discount)
  • Dishonour: drawee refuses to pay on due date
  • Noting: notary records dishonour
  • Renewal: old bill cancelled, new bill drawn
  • Retiring: paying before due date (rebate)
Part 4

④ Promissory Note & Hundi

Promissory Note (Sec. 4): A written, unconditional promise signed by the maker to pay a fixed sum to a certain person or bearer. Bank notes & currency notes are not promissory notes.

2 Parties:

  • Maker (debtor) · Payee (creditor)

🎞 Animated Diagram: Promissory Note

Explanation: Debtor himself writes the promise, so no acceptance is needed. The creditor gets the money on due date.
Hundi: Indian traditional (indigenous) credit instrument written in local language, used by traders.
Darshani Hundi – payable on sight (demand). Muddati Hundi – payable after a fixed time.

Part 5

⑤ Quick Revision ⭐

SLM = (Cost – Scrap) ÷ Life  |  WDV = Book Value × Rate%  |  Due Date = Term + 3 days
  • Depreciation = non-cash expense, reduces profit.
  • SLM: equal each year; WDV: decreasing each year.
  • Bill = order (3 parties, acceptance); Note = promise (2 parties).
  • Hundi = indigenous instrument: Darshani (sight) / Muddati (time).
  • Dishonour → Noting; Cash early → Discounting.
