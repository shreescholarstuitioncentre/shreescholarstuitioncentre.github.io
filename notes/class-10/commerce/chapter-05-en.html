1. Negotiable Instruments

MeaningA negotiable instrument is a written document that creates a right to receive money and can be freely transferred from one person to another by delivery (bearer) or by endorsement and delivery (order) – Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.

Features

  • Free transferability – ownership passes by simple delivery or endorsement.
  • Holder in due course gets a good title even if the previous holder's title was defective.
  • The holder can sue in his own name.
  • Presumption – it is presumed that it was made for a lawful consideration.
  • Always in writing and for a certain sum of money.

Main types: Promissory Note (Sec. 4), Bill of Exchange (Sec. 5) and Cheque (Sec. 6). Hundi is the traditional Indian instrument.

2. Promissory Note (PN)

Definition (Sec. 4)A promissory note is an instrument in writing (not a bank note or currency note) containing an unconditional undertaking, signed by the maker, to pay a certain sum of money to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer.
PartyWho?
Maker (promisor)The debtor who promises to pay and signs the note
Payee (promisee)The creditor to whom the amount is to be paid

Essentials

  • In writing; unconditional promise ("I promise to pay")
  • Signed by the maker; certain sum; certain payee
  • Properly stamped; payable on demand or at a fixed time
  • Cannot be made payable to the bearer on demand
  • No acceptance needed – only two parties
Animated, labelled Promissory Note

🔍 Label explanation

  1. Stamp – a PN must carry the proper stamp.
  2. Place & date – show when it was made.
  3. Promise – "I promise to pay" – clear, unconditional, with the time (3 months).
  4. Payee – Mr. Ram Kumar, the creditor.
  5. Sum – fixed ₹10,000.
  6. Signature – by Shyam Lal, the maker (debtor).

3. Bill of Exchange (BoE)

Definition (Sec. 5)A bill of exchange is an instrument in writing containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer.
PartyWho?
DrawerThe creditor who writes (draws) the bill and orders payment
DraweeThe debtor on whom the bill is drawn; becomes Acceptor after he signs "Accepted"
PayeeThe person who receives the money (often the drawer himself)

Essentials

  • In writing; unconditional order ("pay…")
  • Signed by the drawer; certain sum; certain parties
  • Must be accepted by the drawee (a time bill)
  • Properly stamped; three parties (can be two if drawer = payee)
Animated, labelled Bill of Exchange

🔍 Explanation

  1. Ram sells goods to Shyam on credit.
  2. Ram draws a bill ordering Shyam to pay after 3 months.
  3. Shyam accepts (signs) and returns it – now he is the Acceptor.
  4. On the due date Shyam pays Ram (arrow along the bottom green line).

Instead of waiting, Ram may discount the bill with a bank or endorse it to one of his creditors (red arrows).

Types: Trade bill (for goods sold) / Accommodation bill (to help raise money); Inland / Foreign; After date / At sight.

5. Important terms

TermMeaning
DrawingWriting the bill by the drawer
AcceptanceDrawee's signature + word "Accepted" – he agrees to pay
Term / Tenor / UsanceThe period after which the bill is payable (e.g. 3 months)
Date of maturityDate on which the term ends
Days of grace3 extra days allowed for payment (not for bills on demand/sight)
Due date (legal due date)Date of maturity + 3 days of grace
EndorsementSigning on the back of the bill to transfer it to another person; endorser → endorsee
DiscountingSelling the bill to a bank before due date; bank deducts discount
DishonourDrawee/maker refuses to pay (or accept) on due date
NotingNotary Public records the dishonour; fee = noting charges
RetirementPayment by acceptor before due date, usually with a rebate
RenewalCancelling the old bill and drawing a new one (with interest) when the drawee cannot pay
Holiday rule: if the due date is a public holiday, the bill is due on the preceding business day (Sec. 25).
Animated timeline – how due date is found

🔍 Explanation

  • Blue bar – the 3-month term from the date of the bill (1 Jan → 1 Apr).
  • Red bar – 3 days of grace, so the bill can be paid on 4 April.
Example (term in days): Bill dated 10 Jan 2025 for 60 days. Days left in Jan = 21, Feb = 28 → 49; +11 days of March = 60 → date of maturity 11 March; add 3 days → due date 14 March 2025.

6. What can the holder do with an accepted bill?

After acceptance, the drawer (holder) has 4 choices:

Animated – 4 choices for the holder of a bill

🔍 Explanation

  1. Retain – no loss; the full amount is received later.
  2. Endorse – no cash, but a debt is cleared.
  3. Discount – cash early, but discount is a loss to the drawer. Discount = Amount × Rate × Time ÷ 100 → 10,000 × 12% × 3/12 = ₹300.
  4. Dishonour – the holder can claim the bill amount plus noting charges from the drawee.

8. Hundi

MeaningHundi is an indigenous (traditional) Indian negotiable instrument written in a local language, used by traders for centuries to transfer money safely over long distances – the ancestor of the modern bill of exchange.

Hundis are governed by local customs; the NI Act applies only when the parties agree. Still, hundis are negotiable by endorsement.

TypeMeaning
Darshani (Sight) HundiPayable on presentation (on demand)
Muddati (Time) HundiPayable after a fixed period
Dhani-jogPayable to any holder / bearer
Shah-jogPayable only to a respectable person (Shah), a known businessman
Firman-jogPayable to the order of a named person
Jokhmi HundiDrawn on goods in transit; payable only if goods reach safely (a marine risk hundi)

9. Differences, formulas & common mistakes

BasisBill of ExchangeCheque
Drawn onAny personA banker only
AcceptanceRequired (time bills)Not required
Days of grace3 daysNone (payable on demand)
StampRequiredNot required
CrossingNot possiblePossible
  • Due date = Date of bill + Term + 3 days
  • Discount = Bill amount × Rate × Unexpired time ÷ 100
  • Cash from bank = Bill amount – Discount
  • Dishonoured bill: drawee account is debited with bill amount + noting charges

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the 3 days of grace when finding the due date.
  • Confusing Bills Receivable (asset) with Bills Payable (liability).
  • Debiting Bills Receivable instead of crediting when the bill is discounted or endorsed.
  • Calculating discount on the full year instead of the unexpired period.
  • Making an entry in the drawee's books when the bill is discounted.

10. Q&A and exam tips

Q1. What is a negotiable instrument?
A: A written document that creates a right to money and can be freely transferred by delivery or endorsement.
Q2. Define a promissory note.
A: A written unconditional promise by the maker to pay a certain sum to a certain person or order or bearer (Sec. 4).
Q3. Name the parties to a bill of exchange.
A: Drawer, Drawee (Acceptor) and Payee.
Q4. What are days of grace?
A: Three additional days allowed after the date of maturity before a time bill must be paid.
Q5. What is hundi?
A: A traditional Indian instrument for transferring money, written in a local language.

✅ Exam tips

  • Write the definition with the keywords: unconditional, written, signed, certain sum.
  • Draw the diagram of the bill and label the parties.
  • Always compute the due date carefully; show your working.
  • For entries, write the narration and identify whose books are asked.
