1. Meaning of Final Accounts

DefinitionFinal Accounts are the financial statements prepared at the end of the accounting year from the Trial Balance, to find out profit or loss and the financial position of the business.

Final Accounts have 3 parts

  • Trading Account → finds Gross Profit / Gross Loss
  • Profit & Loss Account → finds Net Profit / Net Loss
  • Balance Sheet → shows Assets & Liabilities (financial position)
📌 Trading A/c and P&L A/c are accounts, but the Balance Sheet is only a statement (it is not an account).

2. Objectives – Why do we prepare them?

  • To know the profit or loss earned during the year.
  • To know the financial position – what the business owns (assets) and owes (liabilities).
  • To compare with previous years and with other firms.
  • To help owner & management take decisions (control costs, plan future).
  • Needed for income tax / GST returns.
  • Needed by banks and lenders for giving a loan, and by investors.

3. Journey: from Journal to Final Accounts

Accounting is a chain. Each step feeds the next one:

  1. Journal – record every transaction daily.
  2. Ledger – post entries to account-wise pages.
  3. Trial Balance – list all ledger balances to check Total Debit = Total Credit.
  4. Final Accounts – prepared from the Trial Balance.
Animated flow chart – how Final Accounts are born
Rule
  • Nominal accounts (expenses & incomes) → go to Trading A/c & P&L A/c
  • Real & Personal accounts (assets, liabilities, capital) → go to Balance Sheet

4. Capital vs Revenue items (very important!)

BasisCapitalRevenue
NatureLong-term benefit (more than 1 year)Short-term benefit (within 1 year)
ExamplesPurchase of building, machinery, furniture; Capital; LoanWages, salaries, rent, purchases, sales, commission
Where shownBalance SheetTrading A/c or P&L A/c
Trick: Capital = asset that stays for years. Revenue = day-to-day running cost / income.

5. Trading Account (to find Gross Profit)

MeaningAn account prepared to find the Gross Profit or Gross Loss from buying and selling goods during the year. It contains direct items only.

Debit side (Dr)

  • Opening Stock
  • Purchases (– Purchase Returns)
  • Direct expenses: Wages, Carriage Inwards, Freight, Fuel & Power
  • Gross Loss (if any)

Credit side (Cr)

  • Sales (– Sales Returns)
  • Closing Stock
  • Gross Profit (if any)
Formula
Gross Profit = Net Sales + Closing Stock – (Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses)
If the answer is negative, it is Gross Loss.

Example data: Opening Stock ₹10,000; Purchases ₹60,000; Purchase Returns ₹2,000; Wages ₹5,000; Carriage Inwards ₹1,000; Sales ₹90,000; Sales Returns ₹3,000; Closing Stock ₹15,000.

Animated Trading Account (Dr | Cr)

🔍 Label explanation

  1. Opening Stock – goods left unsold at start of year.
  2. Net Purchases = 60,000 – 2,000 returns = 58,000.
  3. Wages – direct expense (paid to make goods ready).
  4. Carriage Inwards – transport cost for bringing goods in.
  5. Gross Profit – the balancing figure (28,000), written on the debit side to make both sides equal.
  6. Net Sales = 90,000 – 3,000 returns = 87,000.
  7. Closing Stock – goods unsold at end of year (valued at cost or market price, whichever is lower).
Check: Cr 87,000 + 15,000 = 1,02,000. Dr 10,000 + 58,000 + 5,000 + 1,000 = 74,000. 1,02,000 – 74,000 = 28,000 GP ✔

6. Profit & Loss Account (to find Net Profit)

MeaningPrepared to find the Net Profit or Net Loss. It starts with Gross Profit (from Trading A/c) and then deducts all indirect expenses and adds other incomes.

Debit side (Dr)

  • Gross Loss b/d
  • Salaries, Rent, Insurance
  • Advertisement, Printing
  • Interest paid, Bank charges
  • Discount allowed, Bad debts
  • Depreciation, Repairs
  • Net Profit (to Capital)

Credit side (Cr)

  • Gross Profit b/d
  • Commission received
  • Interest received
  • Discount received
  • Rent received
  • Other incomes / gains
  • Net Loss (to Capital)
Formula
Net Profit = Gross Profit + Other Incomes – Indirect Expenses

Example: Gross Profit ₹28,000; Salaries ₹8,000; Rent ₹3,000; Advertisement ₹2,000; Depreciation ₹1,000; Commission received ₹2,000.

Animated Profit & Loss Account

🔍 Label explanation

  1. Salaries – office staff pay (indirect expense).
  2. Rent – office rent.
  3. Advertisement – promotion cost.
  4. Depreciation – fall in value of assets due to use.
  5. Net Profit – balancing figure 16,000 → added to Capital in Balance Sheet.
  6. Gross Profit b/d – brought down from Trading A/c.
  7. Commission received – extra income.
Check: 28,000 + 2,000 – (8,000+3,000+2,000+1,000) = 16,000 NP ✔

7. Balance Sheet (financial position)

MeaningA statement showing all assets and liabilities (including capital) of the business on a particular date (last day of the year).

Liabilities side (left)

  • Capital (+ Net Profit – Drawings)
  • Long-term loans
  • Sundry Creditors
  • Bills Payable
  • Bank Overdraft

Assets side (right)

  • Fixed: Land, Building, Machinery, Furniture
  • Current: Closing Stock, Debtors, Bills Receivable, Cash, Bank
Formula
Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Net Profit – Drawings  |  Assets = Capital + Liabilities

Example: Opening Capital ₹50,000; Drawings ₹6,000; Loan ₹8,000; Creditors ₹12,000; Building ₹30,000; Furniture ₹10,000; Debtors ₹14,000; Cash ₹6,000; Bank ₹5,000; Closing Stock ₹15,000.

Animated Balance Sheet (Liabilities | Assets)

🔍 Label explanation

  1. Capital 60,000 = 50,000 + 16,000 NP – 6,000 drawings.
  2. Loan – long-term liability.
  3. Creditors – amount payable to suppliers.
  4. Building – fixed asset.
  5. Furniture – fixed asset (after depreciation).
  6. Closing Stock – comes from Trading A/c.
  7. Debtors – amount receivable from customers.
  8. Cash
  9. Bank
Both sides total ₹80,000 – the Balance Sheet always balances! If not, there is an error.
ArrangementLiquidity order (cash first, fixed assets last) or Permanence order (fixed assets first, cash last). Remember: Liquidity = how quickly it turns into cash.
Accounting equation – the scale always balances

Use the scale to remember: left pan = Assets, right pan = Capital + Liabilities. They are always equal.

8. Adjustments (preview)

Some items are not in the Trial Balance but must be adjusted at the year end:

  • Closing Stock – Cr of Trading A/c and asset in Balance Sheet.
  • Outstanding expenses – add to expense, show as liability.
  • Prepaid expenses – deduct from expense, show as asset.
  • Accrued income – add to income, show as asset.   Income received in advance – deduct, show as liability.
  • Depreciation and Bad debts – debit in P&L A/c, reduce the asset.
Golden rule: every adjustment is shown twice – once in Trading/P&L A/c and once in Balance Sheet.

9. Summary – all formulas in one place

  • Net Purchases = Purchases – Purchase Returns
  • Net Sales = Sales – Sales Returns
  • Cost of goods sold = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Exp. – Closing Stock
  • Gross Profit = Net Sales – Cost of goods sold
  • Net Profit = Gross Profit + Other Incomes – Indirect Expenses
  • Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Net Profit – Drawings
  • Assets = Capital + Liabilities

11. Q&A and exam tips

Q1. What are Final Accounts?
A: Statements prepared at year end from the Trial Balance to find profit/loss and financial position.
Q2. Name the three parts.
A: Trading A/c, Profit & Loss A/c, Balance Sheet.
Q3. Why is Closing Stock on the credit side of Trading A/c?
A: Because it reduces the cost of goods sold – it is unsold stock still owned by the business.
Q4. Where does Net Profit go?
A: Added to Capital on the liabilities side of the Balance Sheet.

✅ Exam tips

  • Always write the heading: "Trading A/c of ___ for the year ended ___".
  • Start with "To" on the debit side and "By" on the credit side.
  • Show working notes for Net Purchases / Net Sales.
  • Never forget Drawings – it reduces Capital!
  • Check that totals of both sides agree.

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Putting Wages in P&L instead of Trading A/c.
  • Writing Closing Stock only in Balance Sheet – it must be in both (adjusted).
  • Mixing capital and revenue items.
