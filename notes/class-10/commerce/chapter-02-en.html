1. Meaning & Definition

DefinitionAs per Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932: "Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all."
  • Partners – the persons who form the partnership (individually). Firm – the partnership collectively. Firm name – the name under which business is done.
  • Partnership is formed by agreement, not by birth (unlike Hindu Undivided Family) and not by law.

2. Features of Partnership

Learn the 6 main features with the mind-map below:

Animated mind-map – 6 features of partnership

🔍 Explanation

  1. Two or more persons – minimum 2; maximum 50 (banking business: 10).
  2. Agreement – oral or written (written Partnership Deed is best).
  3. Profit sharing – partners share profit and loss in the agreed ratio.
  4. Unlimited liability – partners are personally liable for firm's debts, jointly and severally.
  5. Mutual agency – every partner is both agent and principal; the act of one binds all.
  6. Lawful business – the firm must run a legal business.
Extra point: A firm is not a separate legal person – it has no existence apart from its partners.

3. Partnership Deed

MeaningA written agreement among partners that states the terms and conditions of the partnership.

Usual contents

  • Name & address of the firm and partners; nature of business
  • Capital contributed by each partner
  • Profit-sharing ratio
  • Interest on capital, interest on drawings, interest on loan
  • Salary / commission to partners
  • Rules of admission, retirement, death of a partner
  • Settlement of accounts on dissolution; arbitration
If the deed is silent on a point, the rules of the Partnership Act (Section 13) apply.

4. If there is no deed – Rules of Sec. 13

ItemRule if deed is silent
Profit-sharing ratioEqual (irrespective of capital)
Interest on capitalNot allowed
Interest on drawingsNot charged
Salary / commission to partnerNot allowed
Interest on partner's loan to firm6% per annum
Memory trick: Only loan gets interest (6%). Everything else = nil, and profit = equal.

6. Interest on Capital & Interest on Drawings

Interest on Capital

A reward to partners for investing money. It is an expense of the firm but is appropriated out of profit.

= Capital × Rate/100 × Time
Allowed only if profit is sufficient and deed permits.

Interest on Drawings

Charged on money partners take out for personal use – a gain to the firm.

= Drawings × Rate/100 × Time

Time period for drawings (when dates are not given)

Drawings madeTime (months)
At the beginning of the year12
In the middle of the year6
At the end of the year0
Equal amounts at the beginning of every month6½ (6.5)
Equal amounts at the end of every month5½ (5.5)
Equal amounts at start / end of every quarter7½ / 4½
Date/period not given for one amount6 (average)

7. Profit & Loss Appropriation Account

MeaningAn account that follows the P&L A/c. It shows how the net profit is appropriated (divided) among partners after interest, salary and commission.

Debit side (Dr)

  • Interest on capital
  • Salary to partner
  • Commission to partner
  • Transfer to reserve (if any)
  • Share of profit to each partner (→ Capital/Current A/c)

Credit side (Cr)

  • Net Profit (from P&L A/c)
  • Interest on drawings
Formula
Divisible Profit = Net Profit – Interest on Capital – Salary – Commission + Interest on Drawings

Worked example (used in all diagrams)

A and B are partners (ratio 3 : 2). Capitals: A ₹1,00,000, B ₹60,000. Interest on capital 10% p.a.; Salary to A ₹12,000 p.a.; Drawings (middle of year): A ₹20,000, B ₹10,000; interest on drawings 6% p.a.; Net profit ₹1,00,000.

  • Interest on capital: A = 10,000; B = 6,000
  • Interest on drawings: A = 600; B = 300
Animated flow – from Net Profit to Partners' shares

🔍 Explanation

  • Pink box = amounts given to partners before splitting (reduce divisible profit).
  • Green box = amounts recovered from partners (increase divisible profit).
  • Yellow box = what is left – shared in the profit ratio.

🔍 Explanation

The bar shows 5 equal parts: A gets 3 parts (72,900 × 3/5 = 43,740) and B gets 2 parts (72,900 × 2/5 = 29,160).

Remember
  • Interest, salary & commission are paid even if the profit is small only if the deed says so – but never create/increase a loss (unless deed provides).
  • Interest on capital → Dr; Interest on drawings → Cr of Appropriation A/c.
  • Salary & commission to partners are appropriations – never charge them to P&L A/c (unless the question says "to be charged").

8. Partner's Current Account

MeaningUnder the fixed-capital method, a Current Account records all yearly changes – drawings, interest, salary, share of profit.

Debit side (Dr)

  • Balance b/d (if debit)
  • Drawings
  • Interest on drawings
  • Share of loss

Credit side (Cr)

  • Balance b/d (if credit)
  • Interest on capital
  • Salary / Commission
  • Share of profit

Example: A's Current A/c opening balance ₹5,000 (Cr). Use figures from the appropriation account; A's drawings ₹20,000.

10. Formulas & Differences

  • Interest on Capital = Capital × Rate × Time / 100
  • Interest on Drawings = Drawings × Rate × Time / 100
  • Divisible Profit = Net Profit – Int. on Capital – Salary – Commission + Int. on Drawings
  • Partner's share = Divisible Profit × Partner's ratio
  • Closing Current A/c = Opening ± Int. on Capital + Salary + Share of Profit – Drawings – Int. on Drawings
BasisP&L A/cP&L Appropriation A/c
PurposeNet profit of firmDivision of net profit among partners
ItemsBusiness expenses & incomesInterest, salary, commission, share of profit
OrderPrepared firstPrepared after P&L A/c

11. Q&A and exam tips

Q1. Define partnership.
A: Relation between persons who agreed to share profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Sec. 4).
Q2. What is the maximum number of partners?
A: 50 (10 in banking business).
Q3. What is mutual agency?
A: Each partner is an agent of the firm and of other partners; one partner's act binds all.
Q4. How are profits shared if no deed exists?
A: Equally, irrespective of capital.
Q5. Why is interest on drawings credited in the Appropriation A/c?
A: It is income/gain for the firm, recovered from partners.

✅ Exam tips

  • Write the profit ratio first; compute interest before sharing.
  • Show working notes for interest on capital and drawings.
  • Check both sides of every account tally.
  • Read carefully: "interest on opening capital" or "closing capital"?

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Charging partner's salary to P&L A/c instead of Appropriation A/c.
  • Giving interest on capital when the deed is silent.
  • Forgetting to add interest on drawings.
  • Showing a debit Current A/c on liabilities side.
