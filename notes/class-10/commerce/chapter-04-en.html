1. Meaning of Depreciation

DefinitionDepreciation is the permanent, continuous and gradual decrease in the book value of a fixed asset due to wear and tear, passage of time, obsolescence or other causes.
  • It applies to fixed assets only (machinery, building, furniture, vehicles) – not to land (usually) and not to current assets.
  • It is a loss in value, but no cash goes out – so it is a non-cash expense.
  • It is different from repairs (repairs keep the asset working; depreciation spreads its cost over its life).
📌 A temporary fall in market price is not depreciation. Depreciation is a permanent decrease.

2. Causes of Depreciation

Remember the 6 causes with the mind-map:

Animated mind-map – 6 causes of depreciation

🔍 Explanation

  1. Wear & tear – physical use reduces the life and value of machines, vehicles.
  2. Passage of time – even an unused building or furniture ages.
  3. Obsolescence – asset becomes out-of-date due to new inventions or fashion (e.g. old computers).
  4. Accident – sudden damage permanently reduces value.
  5. Exhaustion – natural resources (mines, quarries) are used up (called depletion).
  6. Expiry of legal rights – patents, copyrights, leases end after fixed years (called amortisation).

3. Objectives & features

Objectives

  • To find the true profit (charge a fair cost of using assets every year)
  • To show the true value of assets in the Balance Sheet
  • To provide funds for replacing the asset later
  • To calculate correct tax and selling price

Features

  • Charged every year in the Profit & Loss A/c
  • Reduces the book value of the asset
  • Non-cash expense
  • Based on cost, life and scrap value

5. Factors affecting depreciation

  • Cost of the asset (including installation).
  • Estimated useful life – judgement of experts.
  • Scrap value at the end of life.
  • Additions or improvements made later.
  • Method chosen (SLM or WDV) and the rate.
  • Legal or tax rules.

6. Straight Line Method (SLM) – Fixed Instalment

IdeaThe same amount of depreciation is charged every year. The book value falls in a straight line.
Formula
Annual Depreciation = (Cost – Scrap Value) ÷ Useful Life
Rate % = Annual Depreciation ÷ Cost × 100

Example: (1,00,000 – 10,000) ÷ 5 = ₹18,000 per year; rate = 18,000 ÷ 1,00,000 × 100 = 18% p.a. on cost.

Merits: simple; asset reaches scrap value exactly. Demerits: ignores that repairs rise in later years, so total cost (depreciation + repairs) is higher later.

7. Written Down Value Method (WDV) – Diminishing Balance

IdeaDepreciation is charged at a fixed % on the book value at the beginning of each year. So the amount is high in early years and falls later.
Formula
Depreciation = Rate % × Opening Book Value

Example: Same machine, rate 20% p.a. on WDV (scrap value is not used in this method).

YearOpening ValueDepreciation @20%Closing Value

Merits: equal total charge (dep. + repairs) every year; accepted for tax. Demerits: book value never becomes zero; calculation is longer.

9. Part-year depreciation & sale of an asset

A. Asset bought during the year

Charge depreciation only for the months used (= annual dep. × months/12).

Machine bought on 1st October 2024, year ends 31st March 2025 → 6 months.
SLM: 18,000 × 6/12 = ₹9,000  |  WDV: 20,000 × 6/12 = ₹10,000

B. Sale of the asset

FormulaProfit / Loss on Sale = Sale Price – Book Value at the date of sale

Book value of the machine at end of Year 2 (SLM) = ₹64,000.

CaseEntry
Sold for ₹70,000 → profit ₹6,000Bank A/c Dr 70,000   To Machinery A/c 64,000   To Profit on Sale A/c 6,000

10. Differences, formulas & common mistakes

BasisSLM (Fixed Instalment)WDV (Diminishing Balance)
Amount per yearSame every yearDecreases every year
Charged onOriginal costBook value at start of year
Uses scrap value?YesNo (rate is given)
Book value at endReaches scrap valueNever becomes zero
GraphStraight lineCurve
CalculationSimpleLittle complex
  • SLM = (Cost – Scrap) ÷ Life
  • WDV = Rate × Opening Book Value
  • Book Value = Cost – Total depreciation so far
  • Part-year = Annual dep × months/12
  • Profit/Loss on sale = Sale price – Book value

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Using cost instead of book value from Year 2 in WDV.
  • Forgetting to subtract scrap value in SLM.
  • Charging a full year's depreciation for an asset bought mid-year.
  • Treating depreciation as a cash expense.
  • Charging depreciation on land or current assets.

11. Q&A and exam tips

Q1. What is depreciation?
A: The permanent, gradual fall in the book value of a fixed asset due to use, time, obsolescence etc.
Q2. Name the main causes.
A: Wear & tear, passage of time, obsolescence, accident, exhaustion, expiry of legal rights.
Q3. Why is depreciation called a non-cash expense?
A: Because no cash is paid; it is only an accounting adjustment.
Q4. Where is depreciation shown?
A: Debit side of P&L A/c, and as a deduction from the asset in the Balance Sheet.
Q5. Difference between depreciation and repairs?
A: Repairs maintain the asset's working condition (expense when incurred); depreciation spreads the cost over the asset's life.

✅ Exam tips

  • Write the formula first, then substitute.
  • Make a small table (opening value, depreciation, closing value) for each year.
  • Check months used if the asset is bought or sold during the year.
  • Show the journal entry and ledger accounts with proper headings.
