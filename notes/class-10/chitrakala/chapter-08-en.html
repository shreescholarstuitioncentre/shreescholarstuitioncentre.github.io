Class 10 Chitrakala - Chapter 8: History of Indian Art (Theory) - Notes

1. Introduction

Indian painting has a history of thousands of years - from rock shelters to wall murals, manuscript miniatures and modern art. It is mostly religious, narrative and decorative, with rhythmic lines, symbolic colours and emotion (Rasa/Bhava).

2. Prehistoric Painting

Found at Bhimbetka (near Bhopal, M.P.; UNESCO site 2003), Mirzapur (U.P.), Hoshangabad, Singhanpur, Lakhudiyar (Uttarakhand). Colours: red and white ochre, green, yellow from minerals. Themes: hunting, animals, dance, daily life; simple, flat figures.

3. Indus Valley

No big paintings survive. Art seen in seals (bull, unicorn), pottery with black designs on red, and terracotta figures.

4. Ajanta

30 Buddhist rock-cut caves on river Waghora near Aurangabad, Maharashtra (UNESCO 1983; found by British officers in 1819). Two phases: Satavahana (Hinayana, 2nd c BCE - 1st c CE; caves 9, 10) and Vakataka/Gupta (Mahayana, 5th-6th c; caves 1, 2, 16, 17). Technique: clay and rice-husk plaster, lime coat, red outline, tempera colours (red ochre, yellow, green earth, lapis blue, lamp black, white). Famous: Padmapani and Vajrapani (Cave 1), Dying Princess (Cave 16), Mother and Child (Cave 17), Mahajanaka, Shibi and Chhaddanta Jatakas. Features: rhythmic lines, soft shading, graceful gestures, emotion.

5. Bagh, Sittanavasal, Ellora

Bagh (Dhar, M.P., 5th-6th c) - Buddhist, with worldly scenes like processions and music. Sittanavasal (Tamil Nadu, Jain, 7th c). Ellora (Maharashtra) - Kailasa temple, some paintings in Jain caves.

6. Pala School

East India (Bihar-Bengal), 8th-12th c, under Pala kings at Nalanda and Vikramshila. Palm-leaf Buddhist manuscripts (Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita), small illustrations, delicate lines, soft colours; artists Dhiman and Vitapala.

7. Jain / Western Indian School

Gujarat-Rajasthan, 11th-16th c. Illustrated Kalpasutra and Kalakacharya Katha first on palm leaf, later paper. Features: angular figures, large eyes with the far eye projecting, sharp noses, bold red/blue, gold and silver.

8. Mughal School

Under Humayun Persian masters came; Akbar: Hamzanama (about 1400 paintings), Akbarnama, Razmnama, Tutinama; Jahangir: realistic birds, animals, flowers, portraits; Shah Jahan: Padshahnama, rich and formal. Features: miniature size, portraits, court and hunting scenes, naturalism, fine brush, Persian + Indian blend. Decline under Aurangzeb.

9. Deccan School

Ahmednagar, Bijapur, Golconda (16th-17th c). Rich colours, Persian-Turkish influence, Ragamala, portraits; Chand Bibi hawking is famous.

10. Rajasthani School

Centres: Mewar, Bundi, Kota, Kishangarh, Jaipur, Bikaner, Marwar. Themes: Ragamala, Barahmasa, Radha-Krishna, Ramayana, hunting. Bold, bright flat colours. Bani Thani by Nihal Chand (Kishangarh).

11. Pahari School

Basohli (earliest, bold colours; Rasamanjari), Guler (Nainsukh, Manaku), Kangra (most lyrical, Gita Govinda; patron Sansar Chand), Garhwal (Mola Ram). Soft nature, delicate faces, romance and devotion.

12. Company School & Patna Kalam

Indian artists painted for British East India Company officers: flora, fauna, monuments, daily life, in a semi-European realistic style; Patna Kalam (Bihar) is a branch.

13. Bengal School & Modern Art

Raja Ravi Varma (1848-1906) - oil painting, mythology (Shakuntala). Abanindranath Tagore (Bharat Mata, wash technique) with E.B. Havell revived Indian style; Nandalal Bose (Shantiniketan, Constitution art), Gaganendranath Tagore, Jamini Roy (folk style), Amrita Sher-Gil. Progressive Artists' Group (1947): M.F. Husain, F.N. Souza, S.H. Raza.

14. Folk Art

Madhubani (Bihar), Warli (Maharashtra), Pattachitra (Odisha), Phad (Rajasthan), Kalamkari (Andhra), Gond (M.P.). Natural colours, bold lines, myths and rural life.

Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Where are Bhimbetka paintings?
Near Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh.
Q2. Which colours were used in prehistoric painting?
Red, white, green, yellow from minerals.
Q3. How many caves are at Ajanta?
30.
Q4. Technique of Ajanta?
Tempera on prepared clay-lime plaster.
Q5. Name two Ajanta paintings.
Padmapani (Cave 1), Dying Princess (Cave 16).
Q6. Where are Bagh caves?
Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh.
Q7. What is Pala painting known for?
Palm-leaf Buddhist manuscript illustrations.
Q8. What is Kalpasutra?
A Jain text illustrated in Western Indian school.
Q9. Which Mughal ruler started the school?
Humayun brought the masters; it grew under Akbar.
Q10. What is Hamzanama?
Akbar's large illustrated book of Amir Hamza's adventures.
Q11. Who painted Bani Thani?
Nihal Chand.
Q12. Which is the earliest Pahari school?
Basohli.
Q13. Who is the father of oil painting in India?
Raja Ravi Varma.
Q14. Who painted Bharat Mata?
Abanindranath Tagore.
Q15. What is Madhubani art?
Folk painting of Bihar, with bold lines and bright natural colours.
