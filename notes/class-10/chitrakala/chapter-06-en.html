Class 10 Chitrakala - Chapter 6: Memory Drawing OR Indian Art - Notes

PART A: MEMORY DRAWING

Memory drawing (Smriti Chitran) means drawing a scene, event or object you have seen, observed and remembered - without looking at it. It trains observation, memory, imagination and creativity.

A1. Common Themes

Village fair (mela), market, festival (Diwali, Holi), railway station, bus stand, farm work, rainy day, wedding procession, playground, river ghat, school function.

A2. Steps to Draw

1) Recall the scene and plan. 2) Draw the horizon line (eye level). 3) Divide space into foreground, middle ground and background. 4) Light pencil outline of main objects. 5) Add people/animals in correct proportion. 6) Details. 7) Colour - warm colours in front, cool and light colours far away. 8) Final outlines.

A3. Elements to Remember

Composition (balance, main subject near a focal point, ), Perspective (far things look smaller and lighter), Proportion of figures, Colour harmony, Rhythm and Movement, Story/mood. Fill the page - avoid empty corners.

PART B: INDIAN ART (Bharatiya Chitrakala)

B1. Prehistoric & Ajanta

Prehistoric: Bhimbetka (M.P.) rock shelters - hunting, dance, animals in red/white ochre. Ajanta (near Aurangabad, Maharashtra; 2nd c. BCE to 6th c. CE; 30 caves): Buddhist wall paintings in tempera on plaster; Jataka tales; famous Padmapani and Vajrapani (Cave 1), Mahajanaka Jataka. Features: flowing lines, soft shading, graceful gestures, natural colours. Also Bagh caves (M.P.).

B2. Mughal Painting

Started under Humayun (Persian masters Mir Sayyid Ali, Abdus Samad), grew under Akbar (Hamzanama, Razmnama; artists Basawan, Daswant), peaked under Jahangir (realistic birds, animals, flowers; Ustad Mansur, Abul Hasan), Shah Jahan (fine, rich, formal portraits). Features: miniature size, portraits, court scenes, hunting, fine brushwork, naturalism, Persian + Indian blend.

B3. Rajasthani School

Schools: Mewar, Bundi, Kota, Kishangarh, Jaipur, Bikaner. Themes: Ragamala, Radha-Krishna, Ramayana, hunting, court life. Bright flat colours, bold lines. Bani Thani (Kishangarh, by Nihal Chand) - long eyes, sharp nose, elegant look.

B4. Pahari School

Hill states: Basohli (earliest; bold colours), Guler, Kangra (most lyrical; soft colours, Gita Govinda, Radha-Krishna), Garhwal. Artists: Nainsukh, Manaku, Mola Ram. Features: soft nature, delicate faces, romance and devotion.

B5. Bengal School & Modern

Revival of Indian style by Abanindranath Tagore (Bharat Mata, wash technique), E.B. Havell as promoter; Nandalal Bose (Sati, illustrated Constitution), Asit Haldar, Kshitindranath Majumdar. Raja Ravi Varma - oil painting on mythological themes (Shakuntala, Damayanti-Hamsa).

B6. Shadanga (Six Limbs)

From Yashodhara's commentary on Vatsyayana's Kamasutra.

1 Roopbheda - knowledge of forms; 2 Pramana - proportion; 3 Bhava - emotion; 4 Lavanya Yojana - grace; 5 Sadrishya - resemblance; 6 Varnika Bhanga - use of colours.

Short Questions & Answers

Q1. What is memory drawing?
Drawing from remembered observation and imagination, not from the object in front.
Q2. Name three themes of memory drawing.
Village fair, market, railway station (also festival, farm).
Q3. What is horizon line?
The line where earth and sky appear to meet; it is the viewer's eye level.
Q4. What is perspective?
The art of showing depth - distant things look smaller, lighter.
Q5. Where are Ajanta caves?
Near Aurangabad, Maharashtra.
Q6. Which technique was used at Ajanta?
Tempera on prepared plaster walls.
Q7. Name two Mughal painters.
Basawan and Ustad Mansur (also Daswant, Abul Hasan).
Q8. Which Mughal ruler loved bird and animal painting?
Jahangir.
Q9. Who painted Bharat Mata?
Abanindranath Tagore.
Q10. Which is the earliest Pahari school?
Basohli.
Q11. What is Shadanga?
Six limbs of Indian painting given by Yashodhara.
Q12. Who is Bani Thani painted by?
Nihal Chand of Kishangarh.
