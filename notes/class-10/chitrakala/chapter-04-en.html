Chapter 4: Ornamental Design (Aalekhan)

Class 10 • Drawing & Painting (Chitrakala) • UP Board

1. Meaning of Ornamental Design

Aalekhan (Ornamental Design / Alankaran) means decorating a surface with beautiful lines, shapes, flowers, leaves, birds, animals or geometric forms arranged in a pleasing pattern. Its purpose is beauty and decoration, not copying nature exactly.

Definition: A design made by repeating or arranging simple lines, shapes and motifs in a balanced, rhythmic way to decorate a surface is called an Ornamental Design.

It is used on floors, walls, borders of sarees, pottery, books, cloth, metal work, invitation cards and festival decoration.

2. Types of Ornamental Design

TypeDescription
GeometricalMade from circles, squares, triangles, stars, straight and curved lines.
FloralBased on flowers, leaves, buds, creepers (lotus, marigold, rose).
Animal / BirdPeacock, parrot, fish, elephant shown in simplified, stylised form.
MixedCombination of the above.

By place of use

  • Border (Kinara / Patti): a long strip in which one unit repeats in a line.
  • Corner design: fills the corner of a square/rectangular surface.
  • Central design: placed in the middle, evenly spread around the centre point.
  • All-over design: a motif repeated across the whole surface.

4. Principles of Design

  • Rhythm (Lay): repetition of shapes at regular gaps gives movement to the eye.
  • Balance (Santulan): equal visual weight on both sides of the centre.
  • Symmetry: one half is the mirror image of the other.
  • Repetition: same unit repeated again and again.
  • Harmony: shapes and colours suit each other.
  • Proportion: correct size relation between parts.
  • Contrast: light and dark, big and small shapes for interest.
  • Unity: all parts together look like one complete design.

5. Materials Required

Drawing paper/sheet, HB and 2B pencil, eraser, sharpener, scale, set-square, compass, protractor, poster colours, flat and round brushes, palette, water pot, cloth.

6. Diagram 1 – Floral Central Design

Steps to draw

  1. Draw a square; join diagonals and mid-lines to find the centre.
  2. With a compass draw circles of different radii from the centre.
  3. Draw one petal on the vertical line.
  4. Divide the circle into 8 equal parts (every 45°) and repeat the petal.
  5. Fill corners with small motifs, then add outline.
  6. Colour: use contrast (warm petals, cool leaves), keep it neat, erase pencil marks.

8. Colour Scheme Tips

  • Use 3–4 colours only. Keep one main colour and others as support.
  • Place light colours beside dark ones for contrast.
  • Fill colour evenly inside the outline, no gaps.

10. Short Answer Questions (2–3 marks)

Q1. Write the types of ornamental design.

Geometrical, floral, animal/bird and mixed designs. By placement: border, corner, central and all-over.

Q2. What is a border design?

A strip design in which one unit (motif) is repeated at equal gaps between two parallel lines. Used in sarees, books, walls and certificates.

Q3. Explain rhythm in design.

Rhythm is the regular repetition of shapes, lines or colours which leads the eye smoothly across the design, like beats in music.

Q4. Name any four folk designs with regions.

Alpana – Bengal; Kolam – Tamil Nadu; Mandana – Rajasthan; Aipan – Uttarakhand.

Q5. What materials are needed for a design?

Paper, pencil, eraser, scale, compass, poster colours, brushes, palette and water.

11. Long Answer Questions (5–6 marks)

Q1. What is ornamental design? Explain its importance and types.

Ornamental design decorates a surface with arranged lines, shapes and motifs. Importance: it adds beauty, shows culture and festivals, develops creativity, patience and sense of colour, and is used in textile, pottery, architecture and printing. Types: geometrical, floral, animal/bird, mixed; also border, corner, central and all-over designs.

Q2. Describe the steps to make a floral central design with colours.

(1) Draw a square and find the centre with diagonals. (2) Draw guide circles with a compass. (3) Draw one petal. (4) Divide into equal parts and repeat. (5) Add corner motifs and border. (6) Outline neatly. (7) Colour with 3–4 contrasting shades. (8) Erase the guide lines. Label and draw a neat final figure.

Q3. Explain the principles of design.

Rhythm, balance, symmetry, repetition, harmony, proportion, contrast and unity. Write 1–2 lines on each, as in Section 4 above.

Q4. Write a note on folk ornamental arts of India.

Alpana (Bengal), Rangoli (Maharashtra/UP), Kolam (Tamil Nadu), Mandana (Rajasthan), Chowk Purana (UP), Aipan (Uttarakhand), Sathiya (Gujarat), Muggu (Andhra). Made with rice paste, coloured powder and petals on festivals and auspicious days.
