Class 10 Chitrakala - Chapter 7: Memory Drawing (Pencil Shading Practice) - Notes

1. What is Shading?

Shading means showing light, shadow and depth in a drawing with pencil tones, so that a flat outline looks solid and three-dimensional. In memory drawing you recall a scene (pot, fruits, tree, hut, mela) and give it form using shading.

2. Materials

  • Pencils: H grades (2H) hard and light; HB medium; B grades (2B, 4B, 6B, 8B) soft and dark.
  • Drawing paper (slightly rough), kneaded eraser (lifts graphite for highlights), blending stump / tissue, sharpener, drawing board.

3. Parts of Light and Shade

Light comes from one source. On a round object we see: Highlight (brightest spot), Light tone, Half-tone (middle), Core shadow (darkest band), Reflected light (slightly lighter edge in shadow, bounced from surface) and Cast shadow (on ground, away from light, darkest near the object).

4. Shading Techniques

Hatching - parallel lines; Cross-hatching - crossing layers for deeper tone; Stippling - dots; Scribbling - free circular/zigzag strokes; Blending - smooth gradation using stump or finger. Rule: closer strokes and more pressure = darker tone.

5. Shading Basic Forms

Sphere: circular gradation, highlight top-left if light is there. Cylinder: tone changes across the width, a vertical highlight strip, curved cast shadow. Cube: each face flat tone - top lightest, lit side medium, shadow side darkest. Cone: like cylinder, narrowing to the tip.

6. Steps of Pencil Shading

1) Light outline in H/HB. 2) Decide the light direction and mark it. 3) Mark light, half-tone, shadow areas. 4) Shade lightest to darkest in layers with the stroke following the form. 5) Add core shadow and cast shadow with 4B-6B. 6) Blend and lift highlights with eraser. 7) Final sharp details.

7. Shading in Memory Drawing

Use shading on still life (pot, fruits, matka), trees, huts, animals and figures. Keep one light direction for the whole picture, strong contrast in front, soft tones far away, and keep the main subject darkest/brightest.

8. Tips & Precautions

Do not press hard in the beginning; keep pencil sharp; shade in one direction; do not smudge with hand; keep paper clean (use a spare sheet under the hand); keep highlight as untouched paper.

Short Questions & Answers

Q1. What is shading?
Use of pencil tones to show light, shadow and depth.
Q2. Why do we use shading?
To give volume, depth and realism to a flat drawing.
Q3. Name the pencil grades.
2H, H, HB, B, 2B, 4B, 6B, 8B.
Q4. Which pencil is darkest?
Highest B number, such as 8B.
Q5. What is a highlight?
The brightest spot where light falls directly.
Q6. What is core shadow?
The darkest band on the object, away from light.
Q7. What is cast shadow?
Shadow thrown by the object on the surface, opposite to light.
Q8. What is reflected light?
Light bounced from the surroundings, giving a lighter edge in the shadow.
Q9. What is hatching?
Shading with parallel lines.
Q10. What is stippling?
Making tone with small dots.
Q11. Use of kneaded eraser?
Lifting graphite to make highlights and correct tones.
Q12. How can you make a tone darker?
Bring strokes closer, layer again, or use a softer pencil.
