1. What is a Drone (UAV)?

UAV = Unmanned Aerial Vehicle. A drone is an aircraft that flies without a pilot on board. It is controlled by a person with a remote controller or flies automatically by a pre-set route (autopilot).
  • UAS (Unmanned Aircraft System) = UAV + ground control station + communication link.
  • Drones were first made for military use; today they are used by farmers, photographers, rescuers and delivery companies.
  • Remotely piloted drone: a person controls it. Autonomous drone: it decides and flies using sensors and software (AI).

3. How a Drone Flies

A drone flies by changing the speed of its motors. Faster propellers give more lift. There are four basic movements:

  • Throttle – all motors faster/slower → go up/down.
  • Pitch – tilt forward/back → move forward/backward.
  • Roll – tilt left/right → move sideways.
  • Yaw – rotate left/right around its centre.

7. Drone Safety and Rules

In India, drones are regulated by DGCA under the Drone Rules 2021, using the Digital Sky platform. (Rules can change, so check the latest ones.)

ZoneMeaning
GreenOpen airspace: flying allowed up to 400 ft (120 m) without special permission
YellowControlled airspace (e.g. near airports): permission needed
RedNo-fly zone (e.g. borders, military and sensitive sites)
  • Keep the drone in visual line of sight.
  • Do not fly over crowds or near airports.
  • Respect privacy: do not film people without permission.
  • Check battery, weather and propellers before flying.

8. Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Reach dangerous or far places safely.
  • Saves time and cost; quick data collection.
  • Gives a bird’s-eye view.

Disadvantages

  • Short battery life and limited range.
  • Bad weather (wind, rain) affects flight.
  • Privacy and safety risks; needs rules.

9. Remember

  • UAV = flies without a pilot on board.
  • Main parts: frame, propellers, motors, ESC, flight controller, battery, GPS, camera.
  • Drone moves by changing motor speeds: throttle, pitch, roll, yaw.
  • Multirotor can hover; fixed-wing flies long distance.
  • Fly only within rules (DGCA zones: Green, Yellow, Red).
