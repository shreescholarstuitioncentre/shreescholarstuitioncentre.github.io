1. What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that makes machines think, learn and act like humans. The term was coined by John McCarthy in 1956.

Abilities of an AI system

  • Learning from data and experience.
  • Reasoning and problem solving.
  • Perception (seeing and hearing) and language understanding.
  • Decision making with little human help.

Milestones

  • 1950 – Alan Turing proposes the Turing Test.
  • 1956 – the term “AI” is born.
  • 1997 – IBM Deep Blue beats chess champion Kasparov.
  • 2016 – AlphaGo beats a Go champion.
  • 2022 onwards – Generative AI chatbots become popular.

2. AI, Machine Learning and Deep Learning

  • AI – the big idea: smart machines.
  • Machine Learning (ML) – a part of AI where machines learn from data instead of fixed rules.
  • Deep Learning (DL) – a part of ML that uses many-layered neural networks (inspired by the brain).

5. Branches of AI

  • Machine Learning – learns patterns from data.
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP) – understands human language (translators, chatbots).
  • Computer Vision – understands images and video (face recognition).
  • Speech Recognition – converts voice to text (voice assistants).
  • Robotics – machines that sense and act in the real world.
  • Expert Systems – copy the decisions of a human expert (medical diagnosis).

7. Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Works 24×7 without tiredness.
  • Fast and accurate; fewer errors.
  • Does dangerous jobs (mining, bomb disposal).

Disadvantages / Concerns

  • Costly to build; some job loss.
  • No real emotions or creativity.
  • Bias if the training data is unfair.
  • Privacy risk and misuse (deepfakes).

8. Remember

  • AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL.
  • Cycle: Input → Processing → Output → Feedback.
  • Today’s AI is only Narrow AI (ANI).
  • Capability types: ANI, AGI, ASI. Functional types: Reactive, Limited memory, Theory of mind, Self-aware.
  • Use AI responsibly: check facts, protect privacy.
