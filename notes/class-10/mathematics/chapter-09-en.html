Class 10 Maths – Ch 9: Some Applications of Trigonometry

1. Important Terms

Line of sight: the line from the observer's eye to the object viewed.
Horizontal level: the horizontal line through the observer's eye.
Angle of elevation: angle between the horizontal and the line of sight when the object is above the horizontal level (we look up).
Angle of depression: angle between the horizontal and the line of sight when the object is below the horizontal level (we look down).

The observer on the ground looks up at the top of the tower. The line of sight rises at θ = 30° above the horizontal. The tower (height h), the ground (distance d) and the line of sight form a right triangle, so tan θ = h/d.

2. Angle of Depression

From the top of the building the boat is below the horizontal line, so the angle is of depression. The horizontal line at the top is parallel to the ground, so the angle of depression equals the angle of elevation from the boat (alternate interior angles). Never confuse the two: depression is measured from the horizontal, not from the vertical.

3. How to Solve a Problem

Steps:
1. Read carefully and draw a neat figure (right triangle).
2. Mark known and unknown lengths and angles.
3. Choose the ratio that contains the known and unknown sides: tan = height/distance (most common), sin = height/line of sight, cos = distance/line of sight.
4. Solve; use √2 = 1.414, √3 = 1.732.
5. Write the answer with units.
If the observer's eye is at height e above the ground, add e to the height found: total height = (height above eye level) + e.

4. Two-Triangle Problems

An observer at A sees the top of a tower at 30°, then walks 20 m towards the tower to B and sees it at 60°. Let BC = x. Then tan 60° = h/x ⇒ h = √3 x, and tan 30° = h/(x + 20) ⇒ h = (x + 20)/√3. Equating: 3x = x + 20 ⇒ x = 10, h = 10√3 m.

5. Common Types

  • Height of a tower/pole/tree from a known distance and angle.
  • Shadow problems: the sun's elevation θ, tan θ = height/shadow.
  • Ladder against a wall: sin θ = height/length of ladder.
  • Kite problems: string length is the hypotenuse.
  • Depression problems: boat, car, lighthouse, river width.

Q & A – Practice

MCQs (NCERT / Balaji / Rajeev / UP Board style)

Angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 30 m from its base is 45°. Height of tower is (a) 15 m (b) 30 m (c) 30√3 m (d) 60 m
(b) 30 m: tan 45° = h/30 ⇒ h = 30.
A 10 m tower casts a shadow 10√3 m long. Sun's elevation is (a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 90°
(a) 30°: tan θ = 10/(10√3) = 1/√3.
A 10 m ladder makes 60° with the ground. It reaches a height of (a) 5 m (b) 5√3 m (c) 10 m (d) 10√3 m
(b) 5√3 m: 10 sin 60°.
Angle of elevation of the sun is 45° when the shadow of a pole equals (a) half its height (b) its height (c) twice its height (d) √3 × height
(b) its height: tan 45° = 1.
A kite string of 100 m is at 60° to the ground. Height of kite (assume no slack) is (a) 50 m (b) 50√3 m (c) 100 m (d) 100√3 m
(b) 50√3 m ≈ 86.6 m.
Angle of depression of a point from the top of a building equals (a) angle of elevation of the top from that point (b) 90° minus it (c) double it (d) none
(a): alternate interior angles.
If shadow of a pole is 1/√3 of its height, sun's elevation is (a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 75°
(c) 60°: tan θ = √3.

Short Answer

A 6 m pole has a shadow 2√3 m long. Find the sun's elevation.
tan θ = 6/(2√3) = √3 ⇒ θ = 60°.
A 6 m ladder makes 60° with the ground. How high does it reach?
sin 60° = h/6 ⇒ h = 6 × √3/2 = 3√3 ≈ 5.2 m.
A kite string 100 m long is inclined 60° to the ground. Find the kite's height.
sin 60° = h/100 ⇒ h = 50√3 ≈ 86.6 m.
From a point 20 m from the base of a tower the elevation of its top is 60°. Find the height.
tan 60° = h/20 ⇒ h = 20√3 ≈ 34.64 m.
From the top of a 75 m lighthouse the angle of depression of a ship is 30°. Find the distance of the ship from the lighthouse.
tan 30° = 75/d ⇒ d = 75√3 ≈ 129.9 m.

Long Answer

From the top of a 7 m building the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Find the height of the tower.
Distance d: tan 45° = 7/d ⇒ d = 7. Height of tower above building = d tan 60° = 7√3. Total height = 7 + 7√3 = 7(√3 + 1) ≈ 19.12 m.
The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point is 30°. On moving 20 m towards the tower it becomes 60°. Find the height of the tower.
Let BC = x. tan 60° = h/x ⇒ h = √3 x; tan 30° = h/(x + 20) ⇒ h = (x + 20)/√3. So 3x = x + 20 ⇒ x = 10. h = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m.
A 1.5 m tall boy stands 30 m from a building. The elevation of the top from his eyes is 30°. Find the height of the building.
Height above his eyes = 30 tan 30° = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m. Total = 17.32 + 1.5 = 18.82 m.
The shadow of a tower is 40 m longer when the sun's elevation is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.
Let shadow at 60° be x. tan 60° = h/x ⇒ h = √3 x; tan 30° = h/(x + 40) ⇒ h = (x + 40)/√3. 3x = x + 40 ⇒ x = 20. h = 20√3 ≈ 34.64 m.
From a point on a bridge 3 m above water, the angles of depression of the two banks are 30° and 45°. Find the width of the river.
Horizontal distances: 3/tan 30° = 3√3 and 3/tan 45° = 3. Width = 3√3 + 3 = 3(√3 + 1) ≈ 8.196 m.
