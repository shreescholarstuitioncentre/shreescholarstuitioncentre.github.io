Class 10 Maths – Ch 7: Coordinate Geometry

1. Cartesian Plane – Recap

Two perpendicular number lines meet at the origin O(0, 0): horizontal x-axis, vertical y-axis. A point is P(x, y): x = abscissa (distance from y-axis), y = ordinate (distance from x-axis).
Quadrants: I (+,+), II (−,+), III (−,−), IV (+,−). A point on the x-axis is (x, 0); on the y-axis is (0, y).

2. Distance Formula

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
Distance of P(x, y) from the origin = √(x² + y²).

Watch: the segment AB is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 4 (horizontal) and 3 (vertical). By Pythagoras, AB = √(16 + 9) = 5. The formula is simply Pythagoras on the grid.

Uses of the distance formula

  • Collinear: three points are collinear if the sum of two distances = the third.
  • Isosceles: two sides equal. Equilateral: three sides equal. Right-angled: sum of squares of two sides = square of the third.
  • Square: four equal sides and equal diagonals. Rhombus: four equal sides, unequal diagonals. Rectangle: opposite sides equal and diagonals equal. Parallelogram: opposite sides equal (diagonals bisect each other).
  • Equidistant point: for P equidistant from A and B put PA² = PB² (no square roots needed).

3. Section Formula

Point P dividing A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in ratio m : n (AP : PB = m : n):
P = ( (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n) , (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n) )
Midpoint (m = n): ( (x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2 )
Centroid of triangle: ( (x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3 , (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3 )

A(1,1) and B(7,4) with AP : PB = 1 : 2 gives P = ((7 + 2)/3, (4 + 2)/3) = (3, 2). The midpoint M = (4, 2.5) lies further along AB.

Finding the ratio: assume the ratio k : 1, apply the section formula and equate the coordinate (e.g. x = 0 for the y-axis, y = 0 for the x-axis). Negative k means external division.

4. Area of a Triangle

Area = ½ |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|
Three points are collinear if this area = 0.

For A(2,1), B(6,1), C(4,5): base AB = 4 and height 4, so area = ½ × 4 × 4 = 8. The formula gives ½|2(1−5) + 6(5−1) + 4(1−1)| = ½ × 16 = 8 ✓. (Area of triangle is in UP Board and older NCERT syllabus; CBSE has rationalised it, so check your syllabus.)

(b) (−7, 0).

Area of triangle with vertices (0,0), (4,0), (0,3) is (a) 12 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 3.5
(b) 6: ½ × 4 × 3.
Point (−2, −3) lies in the (a) I (b) II (c) III (d) IV quadrant
(c) III: both negative.

Short Answer

Are (1, 5), (2, 3), (−2, −11) collinear?
AB = √5, BC = √212, AC = √265. √5 + √212 ≠ √265 ⇒ not collinear.
Find y if distance between (2, −3) and (10, y) is 10.
64 + (y + 3)² = 100 ⇒ (y + 3)² = 36 ⇒ y + 3 = ±6 ⇒ y = 3 or −9.
Find the point dividing (−1, 7) and (4, −3) in the ratio 2 : 3.
x = (2·4 + 3·(−1))/5 = 1; y = (2·(−3) + 3·7)/5 = 3 ⇒ (1, 3).
In what ratio does the y-axis divide (−4, −6) and (10, 8)?
x = 0: (10m − 4n)/(m + n) = 0 ⇒ m : n = 2 : 5. Point: y = (16 − 30)/7 = −2 ⇒ (0, −2).
Show that (0, 0), (4, 0), (2, 2√3) form an equilateral triangle.
Sides: 4, 4, √(4 + 12) = 4 ⇒ all equal ⇒ equilateral.

Long Answer

Show that (1, 7), (4, 2), (−1, −1), (−4, 4) are the vertices of a square.
AB = √(9 + 25) = √34; BC = √(25 + 9) = √34; CD = √(9 + 25) = √34; DA = √(25 + 9) = √34. Diagonals AC = √(4 + 64) = √68, BD = √(64 + 4) = √68. Four equal sides and equal diagonals ⇒ square.
Find a point on the x-axis which is equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9).
Let P(x, 0). PA² = PB²: (x − 2)² + 25 = (x + 2)² + 81 ⇒ −4x + 29 = 4x + 85 ⇒ −8x = 56 ⇒ x = −7. P = (−7, 0).
Find the ratio in which the x-axis divides A(1, −5), B(−4, 5). Also find the point.
Ratio k : 1, y = 0: (5k − 5)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 1. Ratio 1 : 1; point = (−3/2, 0) (midpoint).
Find the area of the triangle with vertices (1, −1), (−4, 6), (−3, −5).
½|1(6 + 5) + (−4)(−5 + 1) + (−3)(−1 − 6)| = ½|11 + 16 + 21| = 24 sq. units.
For what k are (7, −2), (5, 1), (3, k) collinear?
Area = 0: 7(1 − k) + 5(k + 2) + 3(−2 − 1) = 0 ⇒ 8 − 2k = 0 ⇒ k = 4.
