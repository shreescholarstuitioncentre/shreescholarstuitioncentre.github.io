Class 10 Maths – Ch 10: Circles

1. Circle – Recap of Terms

Circle: all points in a plane at a fixed distance (radius) from a fixed point (centre).
Chord: segment joining two points on the circle. Diameter: longest chord = 2r. Arc: part of the circle. Secant: a line cutting the circle at two points. Tangent: a line touching the circle at exactly one point, the point of contact.

2. A Line and a Circle

The three cases are: the line does not meet the circle; it touches at one point (tangent); it cuts at two points (secant). A tangent is the limiting position of a secant when its two points of intersection come together.

  • At every point of a circle there is exactly one tangent.
  • From a point inside the circle: no tangent. On the circle: one tangent. Outside: exactly two tangents.
  • A circle has exactly two parallel tangents in any given direction (at the ends of a diameter).
  • The length of a tangent is the segment from the external point to the point of contact.

3. Theorem 10.1: Tangent ⟂ Radius

The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

The red tangent touches the circle at P and the green radius OP meets it at a right angle. Proof idea: take any other point Q on the tangent; Q lies outside the circle, so OQ > OP. Thus OP is the shortest distance from O to the line, and the shortest distance is the perpendicular. So OP ⟂ tangent. ∎

4. Theorem 10.2: Equal Tangents

The lengths of the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Proof: In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°, OA = OB (radii), OP common ⇒ ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (RHS) ⇒ PA = PB. ∎ Also ∠OPA = ∠OPB, so the line OP bisects the angle between the tangents.

Useful results:
• Tangent length = √(d² − r²), d = distance of the point from the centre (Pythagoras on right triangle OAP).
• ∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°.
• If a quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle, then AB + CD = AD + BC.
• Tangents at the ends of a diameter are parallel.

Q & A – Practice

MCQs (NCERT / Balaji / Rajeev / UP Board style)

The angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact is (a) 45° (b) 60° (c) 90° (d) 180°
(c) 90°.
Number of tangents from an external point to a circle is (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) infinite
(c) 2.
A point is 5 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 3 cm. Length of the tangent is (a) 3 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 8 cm
(b) 4 cm: √(25 − 9).
Number of tangents at a point on a circle is (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) infinite
(b) 1.
If the angle between two tangents from P is 60°, the angle subtended at the centre by the points of contact is (a) 60° (b) 90° (c) 120° (d) 150°
(c) 120°: 180° − 60°.
Number of parallel tangents to a circle with given direction is (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
(b) 2.
PA and PB are tangents with ∠APB = 80°. ∠POA = (a) 40° (b) 50° (c) 60° (d) 100°
(b) 50°: ∠OPA = 40°, so ∠POA = 90° − 40°.

Short Answer

A point is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of the tangent.
√(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm.
The tangent length from Q is 24 cm and OQ = 25 cm. Find the radius.
r = √(25² − 24²) = √49 = 7 cm.
Tangents from P are inclined at 70°. Find the angle subtended at the centre by the chord joining the points of contact.
∠AOB = 180° − 70° = 110°.
A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle. If AB = 6, BC = 7, CD = 4, find AD.
AB + CD = AD + BC ⇒ 6 + 4 = AD + 7 ⇒ AD = 3 cm.
Prove that tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter are parallel.
Each tangent is perpendicular to the radius (diameter) at the point of contact, so both make 90° with the diameter. Equal alternate angles ⇒ the tangents are parallel. ∎

Long Answer

Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
Let the tangent XY touch the circle (centre O) at P. Take any point Q ≠ P on XY. Q lies outside the circle (tangent meets circle only at P), so OQ > OP. This holds for every Q, so OP is the shortest distance from O to XY. The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular. Hence OP ⟂ XY. ∎
Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
Let PA, PB touch the circle (centre O) at A, B. Join OA, OB, OP. OA ⟂ PA and OB ⟂ PB (tangent ⟂ radius). In ΔOAP and ΔOBP: ∠A = ∠B = 90°, OA = OB, OP = OP ⇒ ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (RHS) ⇒ PA = PB. ∎
Prove that if a quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle then AB + CD = AD + BC.
Let the circle touch AB, BC, CD, DA at P, Q, R, S. Tangents from a point are equal: AP = AS, BP = BQ, CR = CQ, DR = DS. AB + CD = (AP + PB) + (CR + RD) = AS + BQ + CQ + DS = (AS + DS) + (BQ + CQ) = AD + BC. ∎
Two concentric circles have radii 5 cm and 3 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.
Let the chord touch the smaller circle at P. OP = 3 and OP ⟂ chord, which bisects it. Half chord = √(5² − 3²) = 4. Chord = 8 cm.
TP and TQ are two tangents from T to a circle with centre O. Prove ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ.
Let ∠PTQ = θ. TP = TQ ⇒ ∠TPQ = (180° − θ)/2 = 90° − θ/2. OP ⟂ TP ⇒ ∠OPT = 90°. So ∠OPQ = 90° − (90° − θ/2) = θ/2. Hence ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ. ∎
