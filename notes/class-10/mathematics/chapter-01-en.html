Class 10 Maths – Ch 1: Real Numbers

1. Euclid's Division Lemma

Statement: For positive integers a and b there exist unique integers q and r with a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b.
a = dividend, b = divisor, q = quotient, r = remainder.

Watch: 17 is divided into jumps of 5. Three full jumps fit (q = 3) and 2 is left over (r = 2). So 17 = 5×3 + 2. If r = 0 then b divides a exactly.

Euclid's Division Algorithm (finds HCF)

Steps: (1) Take bigger number a, smaller b. Apply a = bq + r. (2) If r = 0, HCF = b. (3) Else replace a by b and b by r and repeat.
Example: HCF(135, 225): 225 = 135×1 + 90; 135 = 90×1 + 45; 90 = 45×2 + 0 → HCF = 45.
Uses: Every positive odd integer is 2q+1; any positive integer is of form 3q, 3q+1, 3q+2; squares are 3m or 3m+1; cubes are 9m, 9m+1, 9m+8.

2. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique apart from the order of the primes.

Splitting 60 repeatedly gives leaves 2, 2, 3, 5 (green = primes). So 60 = 2²×3×5.

HCF = product of smallest power of each common prime. LCM = product of greatest power of each prime present.
Key result: HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b (for two numbers only, not three).
Example: 6 = 2×3, 20 = 2²×5 → HCF = 2, LCM = 2²×3×5 = 60; 2×60 = 120 = 6×20 ✓.

Why 4ⁿ never ends in 0? 4ⁿ = 2²ⁿ has only prime 2; a number ending in 0 needs prime 5 too. Impossible by uniqueness.

3. Irrational Numbers

Irrational: a number that can't be written as p/q (q ≠ 0). Decimal is non-terminating, non-repeating. Eg √2, √3, π.
Theorem: If prime p divides a², then p divides a.
Proof that √2 is irrational: Suppose √2 = a/b, a,b co-prime. Then 2b² = a², so 2 | a², so 2 | a. Let a = 2c: 2b² = 4c² → b² = 2c² → 2 | b. Now 2 divides both a and b — contradicts co-primality. Hence √2 is irrational. ∎
  • Rational + irrational = irrational. Rational (non-zero) × irrational = irrational.
  • Sum/product of two irrationals may be rational: √2·√2 = 2.

4. Decimal Expansion of Rationals

Let x = p/q in lowest terms. If q = 2ᵐ5ⁿ → decimal terminates. Otherwise → non-terminating repeating.
Eg 13/3125: 3125 = 5⁵ → terminates (0.00416). 17/6: 6 = 2×3 → repeating.

Q & A – Practice

MCQs (NCERT / Balaji / UP Board style)

HCF of 96 and 404 is (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 12
(b) 4. 96=2⁵×3, 404=2²×101; common 2² = 4.
LCM of 12 and 18 is (a) 6 (b) 36 (c) 72 (d) 216
(b) 36. 12=2²×3, 18=2×3² → 2²×3²=36.
√5 is (a) rational (b) irrational (c) integer (d) whole
(b) irrational, 5 is not a perfect square.
7/(2³·5²) has decimal expansion that (a) terminates (b) repeats (c) never ends (d) none
(a) terminates; denominator is 2³5².
If HCF(a,b)=12 and ab=1800 then LCM = (a) 150 (b) 1800 (c) 12 (d) 21600
(a) 150 since LCM = 1800/12.

Short Answer

Find HCF of 867 and 255 by Euclid's algorithm.
867=255×3+102; 255=102×2+51; 102=51×2+0 → HCF=51.
Express 140 as product of primes.
140=2×70=2×2×35=2²×5×7.
Show that 5−√3 is irrational.
Assume 5−√3 = r rational. Then √3 = 5−r is rational, contradicting that √3 is irrational. So 5−√3 is irrational.
Can 6ⁿ end with digit 0?
6ⁿ=2ⁿ3ⁿ has no factor 5, so it can never end in 0.

Long Answer

Prove √3 is irrational.
Assume √3=a/b, co-prime. 3b²=a² ⇒ 3|a ⇒ a=3c. Then 3b²=9c² ⇒ b²=3c² ⇒ 3|b. Both divisible by 3 — contradiction. So √3 is irrational.
Show that the square of any positive integer is of form 3m or 3m+1.
Let n=3q+r, r=0,1,2. r=0: n²=9q²=3(3q²). r=1: n²=9q²+6q+1=3(3q²+2q)+1. r=2: n²=9q²+12q+4=3(3q²+4q+1)+1. Hence 3m or 3m+1.
Two numbers have HCF 9, LCM 360; one is 72. Find other.
Other = (9×360)/72 = 45.
Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes. When will they ring together again?
LCM(6,8,12)=24. They ring together after 24 minutes.
