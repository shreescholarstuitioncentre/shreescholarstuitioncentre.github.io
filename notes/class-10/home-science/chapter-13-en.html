Chapter 13: Kitchen Management, Care and Cleaning

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and Importance

Kitchen management means planning, arranging and running the kitchen so that cooking is done with less time, effort and money, in a clean and safe way.

  • Food is prepared hygienically, which keeps the family healthy.
  • Saves time, energy and fuel.
  • Reduces fatigue and accidents.
  • Makes cooking pleasant and the kitchen attractive.
  • Good use of space and equipment.

2. Characteristics of a Good Kitchen

  • Location: near the dining room and store, with a fresh air and sun side (preferably east or south-east); away from toilets, drains and garbage.
  • Light and ventilation: windows, exhaust fan and chimney to remove smoke and smell.
  • Floor: smooth, hard, non-slippery, easy to wash, with a slope to the drain.
  • Walls: light colour, washable tiles up to a height above the platform.
  • Platform (counter): at a comfortable height (about 80–90 cm), made of stone or tile, easy to clean.
  • Water and drainage: sink with tap and proper outlet.
  • Storage: cupboards, shelves and racks within easy reach; airtight containers.
  • Safety: fire extinguisher, safe wiring and gas pipe, first-aid box; fly-proof windows.

4. Work Triangle and Work Centres

A kitchen has three work centres: store/refrigerator (food storage), sink (washing and cutting) and cooking range (cooking). The imaginary triangle joining them is the work triangle.

  • Work should flow in order: store → sink (wash, cut) → cooking → serving.
  • The triangle should be neither too big (more walking) nor too small (crowded).
  • No door or passage should cut across the triangle.
  • Keep things near where first used: pots near the stove, knives near the work counter, masala near the stove.

Equipment: gas stove, pressure cooker, mixer-grinder, refrigerator, microwave/oven, chimney and exhaust fan, kitchen tools (knives, chopping board, ladles). Storage: store grains and pulses in clean, dry airtight containers; keep heavy things low and daily-use items at arm level; use labels; keep the oldest stock in front.

6. Kitchen Cleaning Schedule

WhenWork
DailyWash utensils; wipe platform and stove after cooking; clean sink and remove waste; sweep and mop floor; empty and wash dustbin; keep leftovers covered or in refrigerator
WeeklyWash shelves and containers; clean refrigerator, exhaust fan and windows; scrub tiles, sink and drain with disinfectant
Monthly / periodicClean chimney; clear cupboards, check stock for insects; defrost refrigerator; pest control; clean gas pipe and burners

Cleaning tips: use hot water and detergent for grease; baking soda or vinegar for stains; scrub the sink and drain daily; keep dustbin covered and lined.

7. Care of Kitchen Equipment

  • Gas stove: wipe after use; clean burner holes with a pin; check for leaks with soap solution, never with a flame; turn off regulator at night.
  • Pressure cooker: check gasket (rubber ring) and vent regularly; keep clean; do not overfill (two-thirds).
  • Refrigerator: defrost regularly, wipe shelves, do not overload, keep cooked and raw foods apart, close door properly.
  • Mixer-grinder: switch off before opening, wash jars and dry; never overload.
  • Knives: keep sharp, wash and dry at once, store safely.
  • Chopping board: scrub and dry; separate boards for raw meat and vegetables.
  • Dish cloths and sponges: wash and dry daily; replace often.

8. Hygiene, Safety and First Aid in the Kitchen

  • Hygiene: wash hands with soap before cooking; tie hair; wear clean clothes; cover food; wash fruits and vegetables; keep flies, cockroaches and rats out.
  • Safety: turn pan handles inward; keep children away from stove; do not wear loose clothes near fire; keep hands dry near switches; keep inflammables away from fire.
  • Gas leak: close the regulator, open windows, do not switch on lights or fans, call the gas agency.
  • Oil fire: cover with a lid or a wet cloth, switch off gas; never pour water on an oil fire.
  • Burns: hold under cool running water for at least 10 minutes; do not apply butter or toothpaste; see a doctor for large burns.
  • Cuts: wash, press with clean cloth, and bandage.
  • Keep a first-aid box in the kitchen.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is kitchen management?

Planning, arranging and running the kitchen so cooking is done easily, safely and hygienically with less time and effort.

Q2. What is the work triangle?

The imaginary triangle joining store/refrigerator, sink and cooking range.

Q3. Name four types of kitchen layout.

One-wall, L-shaped, U-shaped and parallel (galley).

Q4. Why is exhaust fan or chimney needed?

It removes smoke, heat and smell and keeps the kitchen clean.

Q5. How is a minor burn treated?

Hold it under cool running water for at least 10 minutes and cover it with a clean cloth.

Q6. Why should the platform be at a proper height?

It prevents bending and backache and reduces fatigue.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the characteristics of a good kitchen.

A good kitchen is near the dining room and store, well lit and ventilated with a chimney or exhaust fan, has a smooth non-slippery floor, light washable walls, a platform at a comfortable height, a sink with drainage, enough storage, and safety features such as fire extinguisher and first-aid box.

Q2. Explain the types of kitchen layout and the work triangle.

One-wall layout has all units on one wall, L-shaped on two, U-shaped on three and parallel on two facing walls. In all, store, sink and cooker form the work triangle. Work flows from store to sink to cooker. The triangle should be compact and free from traffic.

Q3. Write a note on cleaning, hygiene and safety in the kitchen.

Daily cleaning includes washing utensils, wiping platform and stove, cleaning the sink and mopping the floor; weekly and monthly cleaning cover shelves, refrigerator, chimney and pest control. Wash hands, cover food, turn pan handles inward, keep children away, never use water on oil fire, and close the gas regulator at night.
