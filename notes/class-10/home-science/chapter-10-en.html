Chapter 10: Some Common Diseases, Causes and Prevention

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Health, Disease and Causes

Health (WHO) is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not just absence of disease. Disease is any disturbance of the normal working of the body.

Causes: germs (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, worms), poor nutrition, unclean water, food and surroundings, heredity, lack of exercise and sleep, harmful habits (tobacco, alcohol), stress, accidents and pollution.

2. Types and Spread of Diseases

TypeMeaningExamples
Communicable (infectious)Caused by germs; spread from person to personTB, measles, malaria, cholera
Non-communicableNot spread by germsDiabetes, heart disease, cancer, deficiency diseases

Modes of spread: air (cough, sneeze droplets), water and food, direct contact (touch, towels, wounds), vectors (mosquito, fly, rat flea) and blood or body fluids (unsafe needles, from mother to child).

5. Contact and Blood-borne Diseases

  • Tetanus: bacteria from soil or rusty objects enter a wound; stiff jaw and muscles. Prevent with TT/DPT vaccine and cleaning wounds.
  • Scabies and ringworm: mite and fungus; itching and rashes; do not share towels and clothes, keep skin clean and dry.
  • Conjunctivitis (red eye): germs; red, watery, sticky eyes; keep a separate towel, wash hands, see a doctor.
  • Hepatitis B and HIV/AIDS: spread by infected blood, unsafe needles and blades, unprotected sexual contact and from mother to child. They do not spread by touching, hugging, sharing food or mosquito bites. Use new sterile syringes, safe blood transfusion; HIV causes AIDS by weakening the body's defence.

7. Non-communicable (Lifestyle) Diseases

  • Diabetes: high blood sugar; thirst, frequent urination, tiredness. Control by diet, exercise, medicines.
  • High blood pressure: risk of heart attack and stroke; reduce salt and stress, exercise.
  • Heart disease: due to fat, smoking, obesity.
  • Cancer: abnormal growth of cells; tobacco and gutkha are major causes; early check-up saves lives.
  • Obesity: too much weight from overeating and inactivity.

Prevention: balanced diet, daily exercise or yoga, no tobacco or alcohol, enough sleep, regular health check-ups.

8. General Prevention and Immunization

  • Immunization gives the body strength to fight germs in advance. Vaccines protect against TB, polio, diphtheria, whooping cough, tetanus, measles, hepatitis B and more. Keep the child's vaccination card.
  • Isolate the patient and keep his things separate; disinfect.
  • Wash hands, drink safe water, eat clean food, cover cough and sneeze.
  • Control flies, mosquitoes and rats; keep surroundings clean.
  • Eat balanced diet and take rest, exercise.
  • Consult a doctor early; do not self-medicate; complete the course of medicines.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Define health.

A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely absence of disease.

Q2. Differentiate communicable and non-communicable diseases.

Communicable diseases are caused by germs and spread between people. Non-communicable diseases are not spread by germs, e.g. diabetes.

Q3. Name four modes of spread of diseases.

Air, water and food, contact and vectors (also blood).

Q4. How can dengue be prevented?

Do not let clean water stand in coolers, tyres and pots, use nets, repellents and full-sleeve clothes.

Q5. What is immunization?

Giving vaccines so that the body can fight a particular disease in future.

Q6. Write two symptoms of tuberculosis.

Cough lasting more than two weeks and weight loss (also blood in sputum, fever).

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe any three air-borne diseases with cause, symptoms and prevention.

Tuberculosis is caused by bacteria and gives long cough, fever and weight loss; BCG, covering mouth and full treatment prevent it. Measles is a virus disease with fever, red eyes and rash; measles vaccine and isolation prevent it. Diphtheria is bacterial and gives throat membrane and breathing trouble; DPT vaccine prevents it.

Q2. Explain malaria and dengue: cause, vector, symptoms and control.

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium through the bite of female Anopheles at night, with fever and chills. Dengue is a virus spread by day-biting Aedes that breeds in clean stagnant water, with high fever, headache, joint pain and rash. Control: remove stagnant water, nets, repellents, full clothing, spraying and early treatment.

Q3. Write a note on deficiency diseases and their prevention.

Lack of protein causes kwashiorkor and marasmus, vitamin A night blindness, vitamin C scurvy, vitamin D rickets, iron anaemia, iodine goitre and calcium weak bones. Prevention is a balanced diet with milk, pulses, green leafy vegetables, fruits, iodised salt and sunlight, and supplements as advised by a doctor.
