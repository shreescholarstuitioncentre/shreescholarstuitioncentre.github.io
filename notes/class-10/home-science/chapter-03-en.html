Chapter 3: Home Cleanliness

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and Importance

Home cleanliness means keeping the house, its articles and surroundings free from dust, dirt, waste, germs and pests.

  • Prevents diseases (cholera, typhoid, dengue, malaria).
  • Gives a fresh, pleasant and healthy environment.
  • Protects furniture, floors and clothes, so they last longer.
  • Saves medical expenses and improves efficiency.
  • Builds good habits in children and gives a good impression on guests.

2. Dirt and Cleaning Agents

Type of dirtExampleCleaning agent
Loose dirtDust, cobwebsBroom, duster, vacuum
Greasy dirtKitchen oil, smokeHot water, soap, detergent, soda
StainsTea, ink, rustLemon, vinegar, salt, bleach
GermsToilet, drainPhenyl, bleaching powder, Dettol

Cleaning agents are of 3 kinds: soaps and detergents (remove grease), abrasives (ash, sand, scrubber, for utensils) and disinfectants (kill germs).

3. Cleaning Equipment

  • Broom (jhadu) – sweeping floors; soft broom for rooms, hard for courtyard.
  • Duster / wet cloth – for furniture; wet cloth stops dust flying.
  • Mop – wet cleaning of floors.
  • Brush – toilet, bathroom, walls and corners.
  • Vacuum cleaner – sucks dust from carpets, sofas, curtains.
  • Dustpan and dustbin – collect and hold waste.

Clean and dry equipment after use and store it at a fixed place.

5. Cleaning of Different Rooms

  • Kitchen: wipe platform and stove daily, clean sink, keep dustbin covered, store food covered, clean chimney and shelves weekly.
  • Bathroom/toilet: scrub daily with brush and disinfectant, keep floor dry, good ventilation.
  • Bedroom: air bedding, change sheets weekly, dust under bed.
  • Drawing room: dust furniture, clean carpets, wipe glass and decorative items.
  • Courtyard and drains: sweep daily, no water to stand, so mosquitoes cannot breed.

6. Waste (Garbage) Disposal

Household waste is of two kinds: wet/biodegradable (vegetable peel, leftover food, leaves) and dry/non-biodegradable (plastic, paper, glass, metal).

  • Keep two separate covered bins (segregation).
  • Make compost from wet waste for plants.
  • Give dry waste for recycling; avoid burning plastic.
  • Do not throw garbage in drains or on roads.
  • Use municipal vans and dustbins; reduce, reuse, recycle.

7. Pest Control

PestHarmControl

Keep chemicals away from children and food.

8. Good Habits and Precautions

  1. Clean a little every day; do not let dirt pile up.
  2. Wash hands before eating and after toilet.
  3. Do not spit or litter inside or outside the home.
  4. Keep windows open for light and fresh air.
  5. Make a work chart so all members share cleaning.
  6. Use cleaning chemicals carefully, never mix two cleaners.
  7. Keep surroundings green with plants.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is home cleanliness?

Keeping the house and surroundings free from dust, dirt, waste, germs and pests.

Q2. Name the types of cleaning.

Daily, weekly and periodic (special) cleaning.

Q3. What are abrasives? Give examples.

Rough materials that scrub off dirt, such as ash, sand, scrubber.

Q4. What is waste segregation?

Keeping wet and dry waste in separate bins.

Q5. Why should water not stand in the courtyard?

Stagnant water is a breeding place for mosquitoes.

Q6. Write two uses of a vacuum cleaner.

It removes dust from carpets, sofas and curtains quickly without raising dust.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe daily, weekly and periodic cleaning of a house.

Daily cleaning is sweeping, mopping, dusting, washing utensils, cleaning kitchen and toilet and removing garbage. Weekly cleaning covers fans, shelves, cupboards, bathroom scrubbing and changing bed sheets. Periodic cleaning is done once or twice a year: whitewashing, washing curtains, moving furniture, cleaning the water tank and pest control.

Q2. Explain the importance of home cleanliness and cleaning agents.

Cleanliness prevents disease, gives a pleasant environment, protects articles and saves money. Cleaning agents are chosen by dirt: soap/detergent for grease, abrasives for utensils, lemon/vinegar for stains and disinfectants like phenyl for germs.

Q3. How are household pests controlled? Explain with examples.

Pests are controlled by cleanliness, covering food, closing cracks and removing stagnant water. Flies by fly nets and clean garbage, mosquitoes by nets and repellents, cockroaches by boric acid, bedbugs by sun-drying bedding and rats by traps. Chemicals must be kept away from children.
