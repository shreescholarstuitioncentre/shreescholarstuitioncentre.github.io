Ch. 14: Cooking, Serving Food & Preservation of Nutrients

1. Meaning of Cooking

Cooking means preparing raw food by applying heat (and other processes) so that it becomes tasty, digestible, safe and attractive.

Objects of cooking: ① Makes food easy to digest ② Improves taste, flavour, colour ③ Kills harmful germs ④ Softens fibre/cellulose ⑤ Increases variety ⑥ Food keeps longer ⑦ Makes food appetising.

2. Methods of Cooking

MethodMeaning / ExampleMeritsDemerits
A. Moist heat
1) Boiling		Cooking in boiling water – dal, rice, potato, eggEasy, soft, digestibleVitamin B & C and minerals lost in water; fuel/time more
2) SteamingCooking by steam – idli, dhokla, momosLeast nutrient loss, light foodNeeds special utensil; less flavour
3) Stewing / SimmeringSlow cooking in little water below boiling point; water is also eaten – kheer, stewNutrients retained, tastyTakes long time
4) Pressure cookingCooking in steam under high pressure – dal, rice, rajmaSaves time, fuel; nutrients savedCan't check/add during cooking; cost; accident risk
B. Dry heat
Roasting / Baking / Grilling		Direct fire or hot air – roti, papad, bhutta, cake, bread, tandooriGood flavour, crisp, no fat neededFood may burn; some Vitamin loss; hard to control
C. In fat
Frying (shallow / deep), Sautéing		Puri, pakora, tikki, parathaCrisp, tasty, quick, varietyHard to digest; fat absorbed, overheated oil is harmful; vitamin loss

3. Diagram 1 – Pressure Cooker (animated)

Explanation: Heat turns water into steam. The lid is airtight, so steam pressure rises and temperature goes above 100°C (about 120°C). Food cooks fast. Extra steam leaves through the weight (vent). Benefits: saves time & fuel, nutrients are saved. Care: keep water enough, clean vent, open only after steam is out.

4. Loss of Nutrients During Cooking & Prevention

Causes of loss: washing cut vegetables, peeling thick, cutting small & keeping open, excess water & throwing it, overcooking, soda use, open pan, reheating, high flame, frying.
Prevention:
  1. Wash whole vegetables, then cut.
  2. Peel thin; keep fibre-rich skin where possible.
  3. Cut big pieces just before cooking.
  4. Use little water & use leftover water in dal/soup.
  5. Cook covered, on medium flame; don't overcook.
  6. Don't add baking soda (destroys Vit. B & C).
  7. Prefer steaming/pressure cooking.
  8. Soak & sprout pulses; mix cereal + pulse (supplementary value).
  9. Don't wash rice repeatedly; don't throw starch water (maad).

5. Serving Food

Serving means presenting cooked food attractively, hygienically and at the right temperature so that it is enjoyed.

Rules: ① Hot food hot, cold food cold ② Clean utensils, cloth, hands ③ Attractive garnish & colour combination ④ Quantity as per age/appetite ⑤ Serve in order: soup→main→sweet ⑥ Serve from left (hand-served), clear plates from right ⑦ Don't touch food with fingers; use serving spoon ⑧ Cheerful, clean, airy place ⑨ Serve elders/guests first.

Table manners: sit straight, chew with mouth closed, don't talk with food in mouth, use napkin, say thanks, don't waste food, wash hands before & after.

6. Preservation of Nutrients – Points

Store food in clean, covered, dry containers; keep away from heat/sunlight; use fresh seasonal produce; refrigerate leftovers; eat cooked food soon; cook with least loss (see Sec. 4).

7. Short Questions (Q & A)

Q1. What is cooking?Preparing raw food with heat so that it is tasty, digestible and safe.
Q2. Name two moist-heat methods.Boiling and steaming (also stewing, pressure cooking).
Q3. Which method loses the least nutrients?Steaming and pressure cooking.
Q4. Why should baking soda not be used?It destroys Vitamin B-complex and Vitamin C.
Q5. Which vitamins are water-soluble & easily lost?Vitamin B-complex and Vitamin C.
Q6. What is supplementary value of food?Eating two foods together (e.g., dal + rice) so one covers the other's amino-acid shortage.
Q7. Why is food garnished?To look attractive and increase appetite.

8. Long Questions (Q & A)

Q1. What are the objects of cooking? Explain.Cooking (i) makes food digestible by softening fibre and starch, (ii) improves taste, colour and aroma, (iii) kills germs & makes food safe, (iv) gives variety, (v) improves keeping quality, (vi) makes food appetising. Hence raw food becomes useful for the body.
Q2. Describe moist-heat methods with merits and demerits.See table in Section 2: boiling (simple but vitamin loss), steaming (best retention, light food), stewing (nutrients retained, slow), pressure cooking (fast, fuel saving, but cannot check mid-way).

Q4. Explain the rules of serving food.Write rules of Section 5 – temperature, cleanliness, attractive look, quantity, order, manners – with one line each.
Q5. Draw & explain a pressure cooker.Draw Diagram 1; label lid, gasket, weight/vent, handle, food, water, flame; explain high pressure raises boiling point, so quick cooking; write 3 merits and 2 precautions.
