Chapter 5: Home Decoration

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and Importance

Home decoration means arranging and beautifying the house with colour, furniture, furnishings and accessories so that it is comfortable, attractive, useful and reflects the family's taste.

  • Makes the home pleasant and restful.
  • Shows the personality and culture of the family.
  • Good use of space, light and money.
  • Develops creativity of members.
  • Does not need to be costly; it needs planning and imagination.

2. Elements of Design

ElementMeaning and effect
LineVertical gives height and dignity, horizontal gives rest and width, curved gives grace, zigzag gives excitement
Form/shapeOutline of objects; shapes in a room should go together
ColourMost powerful element; creates mood and size effect
TextureSmooth, rough, shiny or dull surface; shiny looks bright, rough looks warm
LightNatural and artificial; brings out colour and form
Space and patternEmpty area and designs on cloth, walls and floor; small room needs small patterns

3. Colour in Home Decoration

  • Primary: red, yellow, blue (cannot be made by mixing).
  • Secondary: orange, green, violet (mix of two primaries).
  • Tertiary: mix of a primary and neighbouring secondary, e.g. yellow-green.
  • Warm colours (red, orange, yellow): cheerful, make a room look smaller and cosy. Cool colours (blue, green, violet): calm, make a room look bigger.
  • Qualities: hue (name of colour), value (lightness or darkness), intensity (brightness or dullness).
Colour schemeMeaning
MonochromaticShades of one colour
AnalogousNeighbouring colours, e.g. yellow, green, blue
ComplementaryOpposite colours, e.g. red and green
TriadThree colours equally apart, e.g. red, yellow, blue

4. Principles of Design

  • Harmony (unity): all things fit together in colour, style and purpose.
  • Balance: equal visual weight. Formal (symmetrical): same on both sides. Informal (asymmetrical): different objects balanced by size, colour, distance.
  • Rhythm: eye moves smoothly through repetition, gradation, radiation, contrast.
  • Proportion: size relation of parts, e.g. large sofa in a small room is out of proportion.
  • Emphasis: one centre of interest, like a painting or fireplace; other things support it.

5. Decoration of Rooms

  • Living room: light cheerful colours, comfortable seating in a group, family photos, flowers, enough light.
  • Bedroom: soft cool colours, simple furniture, bed away from direct draught, soft lighting.
  • Kitchen: light washable colours, work triangle (stove, sink, fridge), good ventilation, tidy shelves.
  • Children's room: bright colours, safe low furniture, space to play, study table with light.
  • Study room: quiet colours, good light from left, comfortable chair, no clutter.

6. Furniture and Accessories

  • Furniture: choose by need, size and comfort; keep walking space and do not block doors and windows.
  • Curtains: give privacy, control light, add colour; match walls and keep washable.
  • Carpets and mats: warmth and colour; plain with patterned walls.
  • Pictures and mirrors: hung at eye level; mirror makes room look larger.
  • Lighting: general, task (reading lamp) and accent lights.
  • Indoor plants: freshness and beauty.

7. Flower Arrangement and Traditional Art

Flower arrangement adds beauty. Use a suitable vase, fresh flowers, cut stems slanting under water, keep tall flowers in the middle and small ones in front, change water daily. Styles: Ikebana (Japanese), mass, line arrangement.

Traditional decoration: rangoli, alpana, torans, diyas on festivals and welcome occasions.

8. Low-cost Decoration and Precautions

  1. Use waste: bottles, jars, old sarees, cartons for craft.
  2. Paint old furniture and rearrange for new look.
  3. Make cushions covers and wall hangings at home.
  4. Avoid overcrowding; fewer things look better.
  5. Keep decoration safe: no loose wires, sharp edges or slippery rugs.
  6. Keep everything clean and dust-free.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is home decoration?

Arranging and beautifying a house with colour, furniture and accessories to make it comfortable and attractive.

Q2. Name the elements of design.

Line, form, colour, texture, light, space and pattern.

Q3. What are warm and cool colours?

Warm colours are red, orange, yellow. Cool colours are blue, green, violet.

Q4. Define balance.

Equal visual weight on both sides of a centre; it can be formal or informal.

Q5. What is a monochromatic scheme?

A colour scheme that uses different shades of one colour.

Q6. Write two uses of curtains.

They give privacy and control light, and add colour to the room.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the elements of design used in home decoration.

Line gives feeling: vertical for height, horizontal for rest, curved for grace. Form is the shape of objects. Colour creates mood. Texture is surface quality. Light shows colour and form. Space and pattern decide how crowded or open a room looks. Together they make the room attractive.

Q2. Describe the colour wheel and colour schemes.

The wheel has primary colours red, yellow, blue; secondary orange, green, violet; and tertiary colours between them. Schemes are monochromatic (one colour shades), analogous (neighbours), complementary (opposites) and triad (three equally apart). Warm colours cheer and cool colours calm.

Q3. Explain the principles of design with examples.

Harmony joins all things; balance gives equal weight (formal or informal); rhythm moves the eye by repetition or gradation; proportion relates sizes, like furniture to room size; emphasis creates one centre of interest like a painting. Good decoration uses all five.
