1. Natural Respiration

Natural respiration is the automatic breathing that goes on day and night. It is controlled by the respiratory centre in the brain (medulla), which responds to the level of carbon dioxide in the blood. Normal rate: adult 12–20 per minute. It brings oxygen to cells and removes carbon dioxide (see Ch 17).

2. When Does Breathing Stop?

Causes: drowning, electric shock, suffocation by smoke or poisonous gas (carbon monoxide), choking, strangulation, heart attack, snake or drug poisoning, head injury or lightning.

Signs: no chest or stomach movement, no air felt at the nose, blue lips, face and nails (cyanosis), unconsciousness, dilated pupils. The brain can be damaged in 4–6 minutes without oxygen, so act at once.

3. What is Artificial Respiration?

Artificial respiration (rescue breathing) is a first-aid method that forces air into the lungs of a person who has stopped breathing, until natural breathing returns or medical help arrives. Start immediately; do not wait for the ambulance.

4. Before You Start

  1. Call for help (in India, 112 or 108 ambulance).
  2. Remove the person from danger (water, gas, live wire after switching off power).
  3. Loosen tight clothing and clear the mouth of mud, vomit or false teeth.
  4. Lay the person on the back (or face down for Schafer) on firm ground, with the airway open (head tilted back, chin lifted).

5. Methods

(a) Mouth-to-mouth (best and most used): Lay the person on the back, tilt the head back, pinch the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, and blow steadily for about 1 second until the chest rises. Let the chest fall, then repeat about every 5 seconds (12 per minute) for adults, more gently and faster for children. If the chest does not rise, re-tilt the head.

(b) Schafer's (prone pressure) method: The person lies face down, head turned to the side, arms forward. Kneel astride the thighs, place both hands on the lower ribs, lean forward to press (air out) and release (air in), about 12 times per minute.

(c) Sylvester's (arm lift) method: The person lies on the back with a pad under the shoulders. Kneeling at the head, hold the wrists and swing the arms up over the head (air in), then press them against the chest (air out), 12 times per minute.

(d) Holger-Nielsen method: The person lies face down, forearms folded under the head. Press the shoulder blades, then lift the arms at the elbows, alternately.

(e) CPR: If there is no pulse or movement as well, give 30 chest compressions (centre of chest, 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 per minute) then 2 rescue breaths, and repeat until help arrives.

6. Aftercare & Precautions

Continue until breathing returns or a doctor takes over, even if it takes an hour. When breathing resumes, place the person in the recovery position (on the side), keep warm and watch breathing. Don't give food or drink until fully conscious. Don't use the prone methods on someone with a back or chest injury. For drowning, don't waste time draining water. For poison on the mouth, use a face shield or cloth.

Questions & Answers (tap to see answer)

MCQ: Which centre controls natural respiration? (a) cerebrum (b) medulla

(b) Medulla in the brain.

MCQ: Best method of artificial respiration? (a) mouth-to-mouth (b) Sylvester

(a) Mouth-to-mouth.

MCQ: Rescue breaths per minute for adult?

About 12 (one every 5 seconds).

MCQ: CPR ratio compressions:breaths?

30 : 2.

MCQ: Blue colour of lips shows? (a) fever (b) lack of oxygen

(b) Lack of oxygen (cyanosis).

Short: Define artificial respiration.

A first-aid method of forcing air into the lungs of a person who has stopped breathing.

Short: Name four causes of respiratory failure.

Drowning, electric shock, poisonous gas, choking (also heart attack).

Short: Why must artificial respiration start at once?

Brain cells die after about 4–6 minutes without oxygen.

Short: Why tilt the head back?

It lifts the tongue from the throat and opens the airway.

Short: Why pinch the nose in mouth-to-mouth?

So the blown air goes into the lungs, not out of the nose.

Short: Which method uses arm lifting?

Sylvester's (and Holger-Nielsen) method.

Short: What is the recovery position?

Lying on the side so the tongue and fluids drain and the airway stays clear.

Short: Signs that a person is not breathing?

No chest movement, no air at the nose, blue lips, unconsciousness.

Long: Describe the steps of mouth-to-mouth respiration.

Call for help; lay the person on the back, clear the mouth, tilt the head and lift the chin; pinch the nose, seal your mouth and blow for 1 second until the chest rises; release and watch the chest fall; repeat every 5 seconds; continue till natural breathing returns or help comes.

Long: Explain Schafer's prone pressure method with precautions.

Person face down, head to side, arms forward; rescuer kneels astride thighs, hands on lower ribs, presses forward to push air out and releases to let air in at 12 per minute. Precautions: clear the airway first, press gently, avoid in chest or back injury, continue until breathing returns.

Long: Differentiate natural and artificial respiration.

Natural: automatic, controlled by the brain, 12–20 per minute, by diaphragm and rib muscles. Artificial: outside help forces air into the lungs after breathing stops, rate about 12 per minute, a first-aid skill to save life.
