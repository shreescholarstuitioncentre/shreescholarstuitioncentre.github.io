1. The Skeleton & Why It Matters

An adult has 206 bones. Bones support the body, protect organs (skull protects brain, ribs protect heart and lungs), allow movement with muscles, and store minerals. A joint is where two bones meet; ligaments join bone to bone and tendons join muscle to bone.

2. Fracture (Broken Bone)

A fracture is a break or crack in a bone, usually from a fall, accident or heavy blow. Weak bones (calcium/vitamin D deficiency, old age) break easily.

Types:

  • Closed (simple): bone breaks, skin stays intact.
  • Open (compound): broken bone pierces the skin; risk of bleeding and infection.
  • Greenstick: bone bends and cracks partly, common in children.
  • Comminuted: bone shatters into many pieces.
  • Complicated: bone also damages a nerve, vessel or organ.

Signs: severe pain, swelling, bruising, deformity or odd angle, inability to move the part, grating sound, sometimes bleeding or shock.

3. First Aid for Fracture

  1. Keep the person calm and do not move the injured part.
  2. Stop any bleeding with a clean cloth; cover an open wound with a sterile dressing.
  3. Immobilise with a splint (stick, rolled newspaper, board) padded with cloth, extending beyond the joint above and below the break.
  4. Tie with bandages above and below the break, not on it, firm but not tight.
  5. Support the arm with a sling; raise the injured leg slightly if possible.
  6. Treat for shock (keep warm, lying down) and take the person to a hospital quickly. Suspect a neck or back injury? Do not move them; call for help.

Never try to straighten the bone, push it back or give food or drink if surgery may be needed.

4. Sprain

A sprain is a stretch or tear of a ligament at a joint, commonly ankle or wrist, caused by a sudden twist. Signs: pain, swelling, bruising, tenderness and difficulty moving the joint. A strain is the similar injury of a muscle or tendon; a dislocation is when a bone comes out of its joint.

5. First Aid for Sprain: RICE

Rest: stop use of the joint. Ice: apply an ice pack wrapped in cloth for 15–20 minutes, several times a day, for the first 24–48 hours. Compression: wrap a firm (not tight) elastic bandage. Elevation: raise the joint above heart level. Do not massage or force it. If it cannot bear weight or pain is severe, see a doctor, since a fracture may be hidden.

6. Prevention & Care

Eat calcium-rich food (milk, ragi, green leafy vegetables), get vitamin D from sunlight, exercise, wear proper footwear, keep floors dry and use safety gear. After a fracture the bone is set (plaster cast) and heals in weeks.

Short: What is a fracture?

A break or crack in a bone caused by a fall, accident or blow.

Short: Difference between sprain and strain?

Sprain is injury to a ligament at a joint; strain is injury to a muscle or tendon.

Short: Why must a splint extend beyond the joints above and below the fracture?

So that the broken ends and neighbouring joints cannot move and cause more pain or damage.

Short: Why not tie the bandage over the fracture itself?

It would press and move the broken bone and can stop blood flow.

Short: Why use ice in a sprain?

Cold reduces swelling, pain and bleeding under the skin.

Short: Name two signs of a fracture.

Severe pain with swelling, and deformity or inability to move the limb.

Short: What is a dislocation?

The bone end is forced out of its normal position in a joint.

Short: Which foods help build strong bones?

Calcium-rich: milk, curd, ragi, green leafy vegetables; vitamin D from sunlight.

Long: Describe types of fractures.

Closed: skin intact. Open: bone through the skin, infection risk. Greenstick: partial bend/crack in children. Comminuted: many fragments. Complicated: damages nerve, vessel or organ. Each needs immobilisation and medical care.

Long: Explain first aid for a fractured forearm.

Keep the person calm, do not move the arm. Control bleeding if any. Place a padded splint along the forearm covering the wrist and elbow. Bandage above and below the break. Support in a sling with the hand slightly higher than the elbow. Treat for shock and go to a hospital.

Long: Describe signs and first aid (RICE) of a sprained ankle.

Signs: pain, swelling, bruise, can't walk properly. Rest the foot, apply wrapped ice for 15–20 minutes repeatedly, compress with an elastic bandage and elevate above heart level. Avoid massage. If pain stays severe, consult a doctor for a possible fracture.
