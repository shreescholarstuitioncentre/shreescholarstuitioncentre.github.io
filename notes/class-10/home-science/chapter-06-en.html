Chapter 6: Household Mathematics (Home Arithmetic)

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and Importance

Household mathematics is the use of simple arithmetic (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, percentage, area, interest) in daily home work.

  • Helps in making budget and keeping accounts.
  • Checks shop bills, electricity bills and milk bills.
  • Helps buy the right quantity: no waste, no shortage.
  • Saves money by comparing prices and discounts.
  • Needed for room decoration (carpet, paint, tiles), food planning and savings.

4. Shopping, Food and Percentage Calculations

  • Cost = quantity × rate. 3 kg at ₹40/kg = ₹120.
  • Discount: price ₹500 with 10% off → discount = 500 × 10/100 = ₹50; pay ₹450.
  • Profit/Loss by seller = selling price – cost price.
  • Food quantity (example): 250 g cereal per person per day for 5 persons for 30 days = 250 × 5 × 30 = 37,500 g = 37.5 kg.
  • Milk bill: 1.5 L/day at ₹60/L = ₹90/day; in 30 days = ₹2700.
  • Unit rate to compare: ₹90 for 1.5 kg = ₹60/kg is cheaper than ₹35 for 500 g = ₹70/kg.

5. Electricity Bill

Electricity is measured in units (kilowatt-hours, kWh).

Units = Watts × Hours ÷ 1000

  • 100 W bulb for 10 hours = 100 × 10 ÷ 1000 = 1 unit.
  • 40 W tube light, 5 h daily, 30 days = 40 × 5 × 30 ÷ 1000 = 6 units.
  • Bill = units × rate per unit (+ fixed charges, tax). If rate is ₹6 (example), 6 units cost ₹36.

Rates differ by state and slab, so check your bill. Save power: use LED, switch off unused fans/lights, use star-rated appliances.

6. Area, Perimeter and Volume at Home

NeedFormula
Floor area (rectangle)length × breadth
Perimeter (border, skirting)2 × (length + breadth)
Walls area2 × (l + b) × height
Water tank volumelength × breadth × height (1 m³ = 1000 L)

Example: Room 5 m × 4 m. Area = 20 m². Carpet at ₹150/m² = ₹3000. Tile 0.5 m × 0.5 m = 0.25 m², so tiles = 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80. Perimeter = 2 × 9 = 18 m. Walls (height 3 m) = 18 × 3 = 54 m²; minus doors and windows 6 m² = 48 m²; paint at ₹20/m² = ₹960.

The floor is divided into 1 m² squares. 5 columns × 4 rows give 20 squares, so area = 5 × 4 = 20 m².

7. Simple Interest and Savings

Simple Interest (SI) = P × R × T ÷ 100

Example: P = ₹5000, R = 8% per year, T = 2 years. SI = 5000 × 8 × 2 ÷ 100 = ₹800. Amount = 5000 + 800 = ₹5800.

Recurring deposit or fixed deposit grows by interest, so calculate it before choosing a scheme.

8. Tips for Accurate Household Calculation

  1. Write down the formula and units first.
  2. Convert all units to the same unit before calculating.
  3. Check bills against the weight and rate.
  4. Round off carefully and add a small margin (5–10%) for waste while buying cloth, tiles, paint.
  5. Keep receipts and accounts of every month.
  6. Compare unit rates, not just the packet price.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain how an electricity bill is calculated, with an example.

Units = watts × hours ÷ 1000. A 40 W tube light used 5 hours daily for 30 days uses 40 × 5 × 30 ÷ 1000 = 6 units. If the rate is ₹6 per unit, the energy cost is ₹36. Fixed charges and taxes are added to get the final bill. Using LED lights and switching off unused appliances lowers the bill.

Q2. A room is 5 m × 4 m and 3 m high. Find floor area, tiles of 0.5 m × 0.5 m needed, and cost of painting walls at ₹20/m² if doors and windows cover 6 m².

Floor area = 5 × 4 = 20 m². Tile area = 0.25 m², so tiles = 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80. Wall area = 2 × (5 + 4) × 3 = 54 m². Minus 6 m² = 48 m². Paint cost = 48 × 20 = ₹960.

Q3. Describe the importance and method of keeping household accounts.

Accounts record receipts and payments with date and item in a cash book. Balance is received minus spent. At month end, total income minus total expenditure gives savings. They control waste, help budgeting and give proof of payment.
