1. What is First Aid?

First aid is the immediate and temporary help given to an injured or suddenly ill person before a doctor or hospital care arrives. Aims (3 Ps): Preserve life, Prevent the condition from worsening, Promote recovery.

A good first-aider is calm, quick, observant, gentle, resourceful, and does only what they know. First aid is never a replacement for a doctor.

2. General Rules

  1. Stay calm and look for danger to yourself and the patient.
  2. Call for medical help (112 / 108) at once.
  3. Check breathing, bleeding and consciousness; treat the most serious first (breathing, then bleeding, then shock).
  4. Move the patient only if unsafe; handle gently.
  5. Loosen tight clothes, give fresh air, keep the crowd away, and reassure.
  6. Don't give food or drink to an unconscious person.

3. First Aid Box

Keep it at home, school and in vehicles, in a clean, dry box marked with a red cross, away from children yet easy to reach.

Contents: sterile gauze and cotton, adhesive bandages, roller and triangular bandages, antiseptic liquid or cream, scissors, tweezers, safety pins, tape, disposable gloves, thermometer, ORS packets, a pain relief and fever tablet, torch, and a list of emergency numbers. Check and replace expired items regularly.

4. Common First Aid

Cuts and bleeding: wash hands, press a clean pad firmly on the wound, raise the limb, bandage firmly. If blood soaks through, add another pad over it. Seek a doctor for deep wounds.

Burns: hold under cool running water for 10–20 minutes; remove rings or tight items near the burn; cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy cloth. Do not apply butter, ointment, toothpaste or burst blisters. Go to hospital for large or deep burns.

Fainting: lay the person flat, raise the legs, loosen clothing, give fresh air; give water only after waking. Place on the side if vomiting.

Nosebleed: sit leaning forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes, breathe through the mouth. Don't tilt the head back.

Snake bite: keep the patient calm and still, immobilise the limb below heart level, remove rings and tight items, hurry to a hospital for antivenom. Do not cut, suck, tie tightly or apply ice.

Dog bite: wash thoroughly with soap and running water for 15 minutes, cover with a clean cloth and get an anti-rabies injection.

Foreign body in the eye: do not rub; wash with clean water. Poisoning: call the hospital or poison centre; do not induce vomiting unless told. Electric shock: switch off power first; use a dry wooden stick to separate the person; give artificial respiration if needed. Fracture and sprain: see Chapter 16.

5. Care of the Sick (Home Nursing)

Sick room: clean, airy, well-lit, quiet, with a comfortable bed, and a separate set of utensils and clothes for infectious patients.

Duties of the nurse/attendant: keep the patient clean (sponge bath, clean bedding, mouth and hair care), give medicines on time as prescribed, give a nutritious, light diet and plenty of fluids, keep records, be cheerful and patient, wash hands before and after, and prevent bed sores by changing the patient's position every two hours.

6. Observing the Patient

Temperature is measured with a clinical thermometer (normal 37 °C or 98.6 °F). Pulse at the wrist: adult about 72 per minute. Respiration: 12–20 per minute. Note colour, appetite, sleep and pain.

For high fever: rest, plenty of fluids, a light cotton sheet, sponging with lukewarm water, and consult a doctor.

Questions & Answers (tap to see answer)

MCQ: Normal body temperature? (a) 35 °C (b) 37 °C (c) 40 °C

(b) 37 °C (98.6 °F).

MCQ: First aid symbol? (a) red cross (b) blue star

(a) Red cross.

MCQ: Pulse rate of a healthy adult per minute?

About 72.

MCQ: For a burn, first step is? (a) apply butter (b) cool under running water

(b) Cool under running water for 10–20 minutes.

MCQ: A person has fainted. Place them?

Flat, with the legs raised.

Short: Define first aid.

Immediate, temporary help to an injured or ill person before medical aid arrives.

Short: Write the aims of first aid.

Preserve life, prevent worsening, promote recovery.

Short: List five items in a first aid box.

Sterile gauze, bandage, antiseptic, scissors, thermometer (also gloves, ORS).

Short: What should not be applied to a burn?

Butter, ointment, toothpaste, and do not burst the blisters.

Short: First aid for a nosebleed?

Sit leaning forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes; don't tilt the head back.

Short: First aid for a dog bite?

Wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes, cover and take an anti-rabies injection.

Short: What is a bed sore and how to prevent it?

Skin damage from long pressure on one spot; change position every 2 hours and keep skin and bedding clean and dry.

Short: Why isolate an infectious patient?

To stop spread of disease to other family members.

Long: Write the qualities of a good first-aider and the general rules of first aid.

Qualities: calm, quick, observant, gentle, resourceful, knows limits. Rules: stay safe, call for help, check breathing/bleeding/consciousness, treat the most serious first, reassure, handle gently, no food or drink for unconscious people.

Long: Explain first aid for snake bite.

Keep the patient calm and still; immobilise the bitten limb, keep it below heart level; remove rings and tight items; wash the area gently; reach a hospital quickly for antivenom. Do not cut, suck, tie tightly, apply ice or give alcohol.

Long: Describe the duties of a person nursing a patient at home.

Keep the room clean, airy and quiet; bathe and change bedding; give medicines and a light nutritious diet on time; measure temperature, pulse and respiration and record; prevent bed sores; wash hands; be cheerful; call a doctor if the condition worsens.
