Chapter 2: Investment of Savings

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Savings and Investment

Saving = income not spent (Income – Expenditure). Investment = putting savings in a scheme or asset so that it earns interest, profit or growth.

Money kept at home earns nothing and loses value through inflation, theft and fire. Invested money grows.

Save first, then invest wisely.

2. Importance of Savings and Investment

  • Security in illness, accidents, job loss.
  • Funds for children's education and marriage.
  • Comfortable old age.
  • Buying house, vehicle, land without heavy loans.
  • Habit of thrift and self-reliance.
  • Helps national development, because banks lend these savings for industries and roads.

3. Principles of a Good Investment

PrincipleMeaning
SafetyPrincipal amount must not be lost
LiquidityMoney can be withdrawn quickly when needed
ProfitabilityGood rate of interest/return
Tax benefitIncome tax exemption, e.g. PPF
StabilitySteady, regular return
ConvenienceEasy to start, nearby, simple rules

No investment gives highest safety, liquidity and return together. Choose by family need.

4. Post Office Schemes

  • Savings account – small deposits, easy withdrawal.
  • Recurring deposit (RD) – fixed sum every month.
  • Time deposit (TD) – lump sum for fixed years.
  • Monthly Income Scheme – regular monthly interest.
  • National Savings Certificate (NSC) – safe, tax benefit.
  • Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP) – money grows over fixed period.
  • Public Provident Fund (PPF) – long-term, tax-free interest.
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana – for girl child's education and marriage.
  • Senior Citizen Savings Scheme – for elders.

Backed by Government of India, so very safe. Interest rates are revised by the government, so check current rates.

5. Bank Investments

  • Savings account – safe, liquid, low interest, cheque/ATM facility.
  • Current account – for traders, no interest.
  • Fixed deposit (FD) – lump sum for fixed time, higher interest, premature withdrawal gives penalty.
  • Recurring deposit – monthly instalments, builds saving habit.

6. Insurance

Insurance gives protection plus saving. The insured pays a premium; the insurer pays the sum assured on death, maturity or loss.

  • Life insurance (LIC, private companies) – term, endowment, money-back plans.
  • Other: health, accident, vehicle, crop and fire insurance.

7. Shares, Mutual Funds and Others

  • Shares – part ownership of a company; dividend and price gain, but high risk.
  • Debentures – loan to a company at fixed interest.
  • Mutual funds – many people's money pooled, managed by experts, spread over many shares.
  • Gold, land, house – value usually rises, but liquidity is low.
  • Chit funds and unknown schemes – avoid unregistered ones, fraud risk.

8. Precautions While Investing

  1. Fix your goal and time period first.
  2. Invest in government or registered institutions.
  3. Do not put all money in one place (diversify).
  4. Never believe offers of very high returns in a short time.
  5. Read all terms and keep receipts, passbook, policy papers.
  6. Nominate a person (nominee) in every investment.
  7. Review the investments regularly.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is investment?

Using savings in a scheme or asset to earn interest, profit or growth.

Q2. Why is saving important?

It gives security in emergencies, funds for education and old age, and reduces dependence on loans.

Q3. Define liquidity and safety.

Liquidity is quick availability of cash; safety means the principal is not lost.

Q4. What is a mutual fund?

A pool of money from many investors, managed by experts and invested in shares and bonds.

Q5. What is a nominee?

The person named by an investor to receive the money after the investor's death.

Q6. Name four post office schemes.

RD, NSC, Kisan Vikas Patra, PPF (also Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana).

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the principles of a good investment.

A good investment should be safe, liquid, profitable, give tax benefit, offer stability and be convenient. A family should weigh these and choose according to its goals. For example, a retired person prefers safety and regular income, while a young earner may accept some risk for growth.

Q2. Describe the post office and bank schemes for investing savings.

Post office offers savings account, RD, TD, MIS, NSC, KVP, PPF and Sukanya Samriddhi. Banks offer savings, current, fixed and recurring deposits. Both are very safe; post office schemes are government-guaranteed and reach villages. Choice depends on amount, period and need for liquidity.

Q3. What precautions should be taken while investing savings?

Fix the goal, invest in registered or government institutions, diversify, avoid get-rich-quick offers, read terms, keep documents, appoint a nominee and review the investment regularly. These steps protect savings from fraud and loss.
