Chapter 12: Washing and Maintenance of Clothes

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Importance of Washing and Care

  • Removes dirt, sweat and germs, so protects health and skin.
  • Clothes look fresh, bright and neat, and give a good impression.
  • Proper care makes clothes last longer, which saves money.
  • Prevents damage from moths, fungus and stains.

Dirt on clothes is of three kinds: loose dust (removed by shaking and brushing), sweat and body oil and stains (food, ink, rust etc.).

Soap and detergent compared: soap is made from fat or oil and alkali; it makes scum in hard water. Detergent is a synthetic cleaner that foams even in hard and cold water and cleans better, but some types pollute water.

3. How Soap and Detergent Remove Dirt

Dirt sticks to cloth with oil. A soap molecule has a water-loving head and an oil-loving tail. The tails go into the oily dirt and the heads stay in water. Rubbing breaks the dirt into small drops that surround themselves with soap molecules and float away. Rinsing then removes them.

4. Steps of Washing

  1. Sort white, coloured, woollen and silk clothes separately; keep new coloured clothes apart.
  2. Mend tears and loose buttons; remove stains first.
  3. Soak in soap or detergent solution for 15–30 minutes (not for silk and wool).
  4. Wash by rubbing, kneading and squeezing, brushing or machine; start with the cleaner clothes, and wash collars and cuffs with extra care.
  5. Rinse in clean water until no soap remains.
  6. Blue, bleach or starch if needed.
  7. Dry in the correct place.
  8. Iron, fold and store.

5. Methods of Washing

  • Rubbing and brushing: for strong cotton and linen.
  • Kneading and squeezing: for delicate silk, wool and synthetics; no hard rubbing.
  • Suction washing: woollens are pressed up and down with a plunger or hand in lukewarm soapy water.
  • Machine washing: saves time and effort; load correctly, use the proper programme.
  • Dry cleaning: uses organic solvents instead of water, for suits, silk and heavily decorated clothes that shrink or lose colour in water.

7. Stain Removal

Rules: treat the stain at once; find its kind; test the remedy on a hidden corner; place absorbent cloth below and work from the wrong side; rinse well; do not iron a stained cloth, since heat sets the stain.

StainRemedy
Tea, coffeeStretch over a bowl and pour hot water; borax; then wash
Oil, grease, currySprinkle talcum or chalk powder to absorb; wash with hot soapy water
Blood, milk, eggCold water or cold salt water; never hot, it sets the stain
RustLemon juice and salt; dry in sun
InkRub with lemon and salt or ink remover; wash
Fruit juiceRinse at once; salt, lemon, or borax and hot water
MudLet it dry, brush off, then wash
Chewing gum, waxHarden with ice, scrape; blot wax with paper and warm iron

Kinds of stain removers: absorbents (talcum), solvents (spirit, turpentine, petrol), acids (lemon, vinegar), alkalis (borax, soda), bleaches. Acid stains go with alkali; alkaline stains with acid. Do not use strong bleach on silk and wool.

8. Drying, Ironing, Storage and Care

  • Drying: turn clothes inside out; white cotton in sun, coloured, silk, wool and synthetics in shade; use clothes line and pegs.
  • Starching and blueing: use thin starch for cotton; add blue to the last rinse for whites.
  • Ironing: sort by heat setting from low (synthetics, silk) to high (cotton, linen); iron on the wrong side for dark and delicate cloth.
  • Storage: store only clean and dry clothes; fold neatly; use neem leaves, naphthalene balls or camphor against moths and silverfish; air and sun woollens before packing.
  • Care labels: read the wash instructions, as they tell temperature, bleaching, ironing and dry-cleaning.
  • Keep cupboards clean and dry; mend damage early.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Why should clothes be sorted before washing?

To wash white, coloured, woollen and silk clothes in the proper way and stop colour from spreading.

Q2. What is the role of washing soda?

It softens hard water and helps remove grease from cotton clothes.

Q3. What is dry cleaning?

Cleaning clothes with organic solvents instead of water.

Q4. Name two stain removers for rust.

Lemon juice and salt (and sunlight).

Q5. Why are clothes dried inside out?

It protects the colour from fading in sun.

Q6. Write two precautions in storing clothes.

Store only clean and dry clothes, and keep neem leaves or naphthalene balls against insects.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the steps of washing clothes.

Sort clothes, mend and remove stains, soak, wash with a suitable method, rinse well, apply blue, bleach or starch if needed, dry in the correct place, then iron, fold and store. Each step protects the fabric and gives clean clothes.

Q2. Explain the washing of cotton, silk and woollen clothes.

Cotton can take hot water, strong detergent, sun drying and a hot iron. Silk needs cold or lukewarm water, mild soap, no wringing, shade drying and a cool iron on the wrong side. Wool needs lukewarm water, mild soap, no rubbing or twisting, flat drying in shade and pressing through a damp cloth.

Q3. Write the general rules of stain removal and remedies for any five stains.

Treat stains at once, identify the kind, test on a hidden corner, work from the wrong side and rinse well. Tea: hot water or borax. Grease: talcum then hot soapy water. Blood: cold salt water. Rust: lemon and salt in sun. Ink: lemon and salt or ink remover.
