Chapter 1: Family Budget

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning of Budget

Budget is a plan of expected income and planned expenditure of a family for a fixed period (usually one month or one year).

Family budget = estimating income first, then dividing it among needs, comforts, savings and emergencies so that the family lives happily within its means.

Remember: Income → Plan → Spend → Save → Review

2. Family Income (3 types)

  • Money income – salary, wages, rent, interest, profit received in cash.
  • Real income – goods and services enjoyed without paying, e.g. own house, kitchen garden, free school/hospital, vehicle given by employer.
  • Psychic income – satisfaction and happiness from using money and resources, e.g. joy of a well-kept home, respect in society.

3. Types of Budget

TypeConditionMeaning
SurplusIncome > ExpenditureSavings possible – best budget
BalancedIncome = ExpenditureNo saving, no loan
DeficitIncome < ExpenditureLoan/debt needed – harmful

4. Importance of Family Budget

  • Controls unnecessary spending.
  • Helps in saving for future and emergencies.
  • Fulfils needs by priority.
  • Avoids debt and tension.
  • Teaches all members cooperation and responsibility.
  • Gives a feeling of security and a better standard of living.

5. Steps in Making a Budget

  1. Find the total family income (all sources).
  2. List the needs of all members.
  3. Decide priority: necessities → comforts → luxuries.
  4. Check last year's expenses.
  5. Divide income among heads: food, clothing, housing, education, health, savings, miscellaneous.
  6. Keep daily accounts of spending.
  7. Review and correct the budget.

6. Factors Affecting the Budget

  • Size of family and age of members.
  • Level of income and its regularity.
  • Occupation and social status.
  • Place of living (city/village), climate.
  • Prices of goods, inflation.
  • Special needs: marriage, illness, education.
  • Habits, tastes and values of family.

7. Heads of Expenditure & Engel's Law

Main heads: food, clothing, housing (rent, fuel, light), education, health, recreation, savings, miscellaneous.

Engel's Law: as income rises, the percentage spent on food falls, while percentage on education, recreation and savings rises. Poor families spend most of their income on food.

Diagram 2: Sample monthly budget (animated donut)

Food takes the biggest share, as Engel's Law says. Savings (10%) keep the family secure.

8. Savings – Why and How

Saving = Income – Expenditure. Methods: Post Office savings, bank account/FD/RD, PPF, insurance, Kisan Vikas Patra, Sukanya Samriddhi, mutual funds, chit funds (with caution).

Good saving is regular, safe and goal-based.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is a family budget?

A written plan that estimates family income and divides it among expenses and savings for a fixed period.

Q2. Define real income.

Goods and services a family enjoys without payment, such as own house or kitchen garden.

Q3. Write any four benefits of a budget.

It controls overspending, encourages saving, avoids debt and fulfils needs by priority.

Q4. State Engel's Law.

With rising income the percentage of income spent on food decreases.

Q5. Why is keeping accounts necessary?

It shows where money was spent, helps compare with the budget and correct mistakes.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the types of family income with examples.

Money income is cash received (salary, rent). Real income is goods and services used without paying (own house, free hospital). Psychic income is satisfaction felt from using resources (pride in a neat home). All three together decide the real standard of living of a family.

Q2. Describe the steps in preparing a family budget.

First find total income. Next list family needs and fix priorities. Study past expenses. Divide income into heads – food, clothing, housing, education, health, savings. Keep daily accounts. At the end of the period review, find the difference and revise the plan.

Q3. Discuss factors that affect the family budget.

Family size, income level, occupation, place of residence, price level, special occasions such as marriage or illness, and the family's habits and values all change how money is divided. A large family with low income must give priority to necessities.
