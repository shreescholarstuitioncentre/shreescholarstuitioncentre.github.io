Chapter 9: Environmental Pollution and its Effect on Human Life

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning of Environment and Pollution

Environment is everything around us: air, water, soil, plants, animals and humans. Pollution is the addition of harmful substances (pollutants) to the environment so that its natural quality is spoiled and living beings are harmed.

Pollutants may be biodegradable (sewage, food waste, rot naturally) or non-biodegradable (plastic, DDT, heavy metals, remain for long). Main causes: population growth, industrialization, vehicles, urbanization, deforestation and careless habits.

3. Air Pollution

  • Causes: smoke from vehicles and factories, burning of crop residue, garbage and wood, dust from construction, CFCs from refrigerators and ACs, forest fires.
  • Effects on humans: cough, asthma, bronchitis, lung disease, eye irritation, headache; carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin and reduces oxygen in blood; long exposure can cause lung cancer and heart disease. Children and old people suffer most.
  • Control: use CNG/electric vehicles, regular vehicle checks, chimneys with filters, smokeless chulha or LPG, plant trees, do not burn waste.

4. Water Pollution

  • Causes: sewage and sullage, factory effluents, fertilizer and pesticide run-off, dumping of garbage, idols and ashes, bathing and washing clothes in ponds, oil spills.
  • Effects: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, jaundice; heavy metals like lead and mercury poison the body; fish and aquatic life die; excess fertilizers make algae grow thickly and use up oxygen (eutrophication).
  • Control: treat sewage and factory waste before release, use less chemicals, keep wells, ponds and rivers clean, boil or filter drinking water, laws.

5. Soil Pollution

  • Causes: excess chemical fertilizers and pesticides, plastic and polythene, industrial waste, mining, open dumping, deforestation which causes erosion.
  • Effects: soil loses fertility; poisons enter crops and the food chain; plastic blocks water entry and harms cattle; breeding of flies and rats.
  • Control: organic manure and compost, bio-pesticides, ban or reduce polythene, segregate and recycle waste, plant trees.

6. Noise Pollution

Sound is measured in decibels (dB). Normal talk is about 60 dB; long exposure to sound above about 85 dB can damage hearing.

  • Causes: vehicle horns, loudspeakers, DJs, factory machines, aircraft, firecrackers, TV and music at high volume.
  • Effects: hearing loss, headache, irritation, stress, high blood pressure, sleeplessness, poor concentration and poor studies.
  • Control: avoid unnecessary horns, limit loudspeaker use, silencers on machines, sound-proof rooms, trees along roads, low volume of TV, rules for crackers.

7. Global Effects of Pollution

  • Greenhouse effect and global warming: gases like CO₂, methane and nitrous oxide trap the Earth's heat. Extra gases from burning fuel and cutting forests raise temperature, melt glaciers, raise sea level and change climate.
  • Ozone layer depletion: ozone shields us from ultraviolet rays. CFCs destroy it, causing skin cancer, cataract and crop damage. The Montreal Protocol (1987) limits CFCs.
  • Acid rain: SO₂ and nitrogen oxides mix with rain water; it harms crops, forests, lakes and corrodes marbles and buildings such as the Taj Mahal.

Diagram 2: Greenhouse effect and global warming (animated)

Sunlight reaches the Earth. The Earth sends back heat, but greenhouse gases trap much of it and send it down again. More gases mean more warming.

8. Prevention, Laws and Our Role

  • Laws in India: Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981, Environment (Protection) Act 1986.
  • Individual role: plant trees, save water and electricity, use public transport or bicycle, use cloth bags, segregate waste, avoid crackers and loud noise, do not burn waste.
  • Remember the 3R: reduce, reuse, recycle.
  • Community: clean-up drives, awareness, tree plantation. World Environment Day is on 5 June.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Define pollutant.

A substance that is added to the environment and harms its quality or living beings.

Q2. Name the types of pollution.

Air, water, soil and noise pollution.

Q3. What is the greenhouse effect?

Trapping of the Earth's heat by gases like carbon dioxide and methane, which warms the Earth.

Q4. Write two effects of noise pollution.

Hearing loss and stress or high blood pressure (also headache and sleeplessness).

Q5. What is acid rain?

Rain mixed with acids formed from sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides in the air.

Q6. Write two ways of controlling air pollution.

Use CNG or electric vehicles and plant more trees (also fit filters in chimneys).

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the causes, effects and control of air pollution.

Causes are smoke from vehicles and factories, burning waste and crop residue, dust and CFCs. Effects are asthma, bronchitis, eye irritation, lung disease and reduced oxygen in blood by carbon monoxide. Control by CNG or electric vehicles, filters, LPG or smokeless chulha, trees and not burning waste.

Q2. Explain the causes and effects of water and soil pollution.

Water is polluted by sewage, factory effluents, fertilizers and garbage, causing diseases like cholera and typhoid and killing aquatic life. Soil is polluted by excess chemicals, plastic and waste, which reduce fertility and enter crops and the food chain. Treating waste, organic manure and less plastic are the remedies.

Q3. Write a note on global warming, ozone depletion and acid rain.

Greenhouse gases trap heat and raise Earth's temperature, melting glaciers and raising sea level. CFCs deplete the ozone layer, letting UV rays cause skin cancer and cataract. SO₂ and nitrogen oxides cause acid rain which harms crops, lakes and monuments. Reducing fuel burning, CFC use and planting trees helps.
