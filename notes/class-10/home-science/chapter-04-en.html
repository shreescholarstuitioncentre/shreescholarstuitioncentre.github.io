Chapter 4: Waste Disposal and Cleanliness

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and Types of Waste

Waste is any unwanted or useless material thrown out from homes, farms, industries and hospitals. Waste disposal is its safe removal so that health and environment are not harmed.

2. Harms of Improper Disposal

  • Heaps of garbage breed flies, mosquitoes, rats, so diseases spread (cholera, typhoid, dengue, malaria).
  • Bad smell and ugly surroundings.
  • Drains choke and cause waterlogging.
  • Water, soil and air get polluted.
  • Plastic harms animals and blocks sewers.
  • Open defecation contaminates water and spreads worms and diarrhoea.

3. Disposal of Solid Waste

  • Dumping: in open fields away from houses; the cheapest but unhygienic.
  • Sanitary landfill: waste is put in a pit in layers, each covered with soil; it rots safely.
  • Incineration: burning in closed furnaces; reduces volume, but plastic burning gives poisonous gases.
  • Composting: wet waste is buried in a pit; bacteria turn it into manure in 2–3 months.
  • Vermicomposting: earthworms convert waste into rich vermicompost.
  • Recycling: paper, glass, metal and plastic are reprocessed into new goods.

4. Disposal of Liquid Waste

Waste water from bathroom, kitchen and washing is called sullage. Water with human excreta is sewage.

  • Open drains: cheap, but smell and breed mosquitoes; keep sloping and clean.
  • Closed (underground) drains: hygienic, carry water to sewers.
  • Soak pit: a pit filled with stones and brick pieces; the water soaks into the ground (used in villages).
  • Kitchen garden: sullage can water plants.

5. Disposal of Human Excreta and Sewage

  • Sanitary latrine / flush toilet: water seal stops smell and flies.
  • Septic tank: underground closed tank; bacteria digest the solids as sludge, and the liquid goes to a soak pit.
  • Biogas plant: cattle dung (and latrine waste) gives methane gas for cooking and slurry for manure.
  • Sewage treatment plant (cities): screening → settling → bacterial treatment → chlorine disinfection → clean water released.

Never defecate in the open.

6. Gaseous Waste and Air Cleanliness

  • Source: chulha smoke, vehicles, factories, burning garbage.
  • Use smokeless chulha, LPG, solar cooker and biogas.
  • Keep proper ventilation and a chimney or exhaust fan in the kitchen.
  • Plant trees, and service vehicles.
  • Do not burn waste.

7. Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (3R)

  • Reduce: buy only what is needed; avoid plastic bags and packaging.
  • Reuse: use cloth bags, old jars, old clothes as rags, one-side-used paper.
  • Recycle: give paper, plastic, metal and glass to recyclers; compost wet waste.

3R reduces the load on dumps, saves resources and energy and lowers pollution.

Diagram 2: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle cycle (animated)

The three Rs work in a cycle. First Reduce waste, then Reuse things, and finally Recycle what remains.

8. Role of Individual and Government

  • Individual: segregate waste, use dustbin, keep surroundings clean, build and use a toilet.
  • Community: clean drains, village ponds and streets together, form a cleanliness committee.
  • Government: sewers, garbage collection and treatment plants, laws and awareness.
  • Swachh Bharat Abhiyan (launched 2 October 2014) aims at clean villages and cities and toilets for all.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is waste disposal?

Safe removal of waste so that health and environment are not harmed.

Q2. Differentiate between sullage and sewage.

Sullage is waste water from kitchen, bath and washing. Sewage contains human excreta as well.

Q3. What is a soak pit?

A pit filled with stones and brick pieces that lets waste water seep into the ground.

Q4. What is composting?

Burying wet waste in a pit so that bacteria change it into manure.

Q5. Write the full form idea of 3R.

Reduce, Reuse and Recycle.

Q6. Name two harms of open defecation.

It pollutes water and soil and spreads diarrhoea, worms and other diseases.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the methods of disposing solid waste.

Dumping puts waste far away but is unhygienic. Sanitary landfill buries layers of waste under soil. Incineration burns waste in closed furnaces. Composting and vermicomposting turn wet waste into manure. Recycling reprocesses paper, glass, metal and plastic into new products, and is the best method as it saves resources.

Q2. Explain how liquid waste and human excreta are disposed of.

Sullage is carried by open or closed drains, or soaked in a soak pit or used in kitchen garden. Human excreta is disposed in sanitary latrines connected to septic tanks, where bacteria digest solids and the liquid goes to a soak pit. Cities use sewers and treatment plants. Biogas plants give fuel and manure from dung.

Q3. Explain the 3R principle and the role of people in keeping the surroundings clean.

Reduce means using less, Reuse means using things again, Recycle means making new things from waste. Everyone should segregate waste, use dustbins, keep drains clean, use toilets and join community cleanliness work. Government supports through sewers, collection and Swachh Bharat Abhiyan.
