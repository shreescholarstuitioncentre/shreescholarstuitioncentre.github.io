Chapter 8: Water-Borne Diseases

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Meaning and How Water Gets Contaminated

Water-borne diseases are diseases caused by germs (bacteria, viruses, protozoa) and worms that enter the body through contaminated water or food.

Contamination happens when:

  • Human or animal faeces mix with drinking water (open defecation, leaking sewers, toilets near wells).
  • Drains, garbage and dead animals pollute ponds, wells and rivers.
  • Dirty hands, utensils or containers touch water; flies sit on food.
  • Floods mix sewage with drinking water.

Most spread by the faecal-oral route: germs from faeces reach another person's mouth.

Diagram 1: Cycle of faecal-oral infection (animated)

Germs from faeces reach water and food through flies, hands and soil, enter another person's mouth, and the cycle continues. Breaking any link stops the disease.

Malaria and dengue are not water-borne. They are spread by mosquitoes that breed in stagnant water.

5. Protozoal and Worm Diseases

  • Amoebic dysentery: by Entamoeba histolytica; stools with mucus and blood, abdominal pain. Spreads via contaminated water, vegetables and flies. Treated with anti-amoebic medicine.
  • Giardiasis: by Giardia; foul-smelling loose motions, gas, weight loss.
  • Roundworm (Ascaris): eggs enter with unwashed vegetables or dirty hands; stomach pain, weakness, anaemia in children. Treated with deworming tablets.

6. Diarrhoea, Dehydration and ORS

Diarrhoea is three or more loose watery motions in a day. Loss of body water and salts is called dehydration, the real danger, especially in children.

Signs of dehydration: thirst, dry mouth, sunken eyes, little urine, weakness; in babies a sunken soft spot on the head and no tears.

ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution): packet ORS in clean water as per pack, or home-made: 1 litre clean (boiled and cooled) water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + ½ level teaspoon salt. Give small sips often, continue breast-feeding and normal food, and use the solution within 24 hours. Take the patient to a doctor if there is blood in stool, repeated vomiting, high fever or no urine.

Diagram 2: Making home-made ORS (animated)

Add sugar and salt in the right amount to one litre of clean water and stir well. Give sips often.

7. Prevention and Control

  • Personal: drink boiled/filtered water; wash hands with soap before eating and cooking and after toilet; eat fresh, well-cooked food; wash vegetables and fruits; cut nails.
  • Home: cover food and water; keep water containers clean; control flies.
  • Community: sanitary latrines, proper sewage and garbage disposal, covered wells, chlorination of public water, no open defecation.
  • Vaccines: typhoid, hepatitis A, polio, cholera (in outbreak areas) as advised by health workers.
  • Government: health centres, ORS and chlorine tablets, awareness drives.

8. During an Outbreak: Precautions

  1. Drink only boiled or chlorinated water.
  2. Avoid street food, cut fruit and ice of unknown source.
  3. Report cases quickly to the health centre.
  4. Keep the patient's utensils and clothes separate and clean; wash hands after nursing.
  5. Dispose of the patient's excreta safely and disinfect the toilet.
  6. Do not take medicines without a doctor's advice; start ORS immediately.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What are water-borne diseases?

Diseases caused by germs or worms that enter the body through contaminated water or food.

Q2. Name two bacterial and two viral water-borne diseases.

Bacterial: cholera, typhoid. Viral: hepatitis A, polio.

Q3. What is dehydration?

Loss of too much water and salts from the body, usually through loose motions and vomiting.

Q4. Write the home-made ORS recipe.

1 litre clean water, 6 level teaspoons sugar and ½ level teaspoon salt.

Q5. What is the faecal-oral route?

Germs from faeces reach another person's mouth through water, food, hands or flies.

Q6. Why is malaria not called a water-borne disease?

It is spread by mosquito bites, not by drinking contaminated water.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the causes, symptoms and prevention of cholera and typhoid.

Cholera is caused by Vibrio cholerae and gives sudden watery motions, vomiting and dehydration. Typhoid is caused by Salmonella Typhi and gives rising fever, headache and weakness. Both spread by contaminated water and food. Prevention is boiled water, hand washing, safe food, sanitation, vaccines, and prompt ORS and medical care.

Q2. Explain diarrhoea, dehydration and the use of ORS.

Diarrhoea is frequent loose watery motions causing loss of water and salts, i.e. dehydration, with dry mouth, sunken eyes and little urine. ORS replaces them. Mix 1 litre clean water, 6 tsp sugar and ½ tsp salt, give small sips often, continue feeding and see a doctor for blood in stool, repeated vomiting or high fever.

Q3. Discuss measures to prevent water-borne diseases at personal, home and community level.

Personally: drink boiled or filtered water, wash hands, eat fresh cooked food. At home: cover food and water, clean containers, control flies. In the community: latrines, sewage and garbage disposal, covered wells, chlorination, no open defecation. Vaccines and health education by government complete the protection.
