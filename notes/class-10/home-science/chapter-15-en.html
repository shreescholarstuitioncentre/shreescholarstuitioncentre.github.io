Ch. 15: Human Skeleton and Joints

1. Introduction – Human Skeleton

The skeleton is the framework of bones and cartilage that supports the body. An adult human skeleton has 206 bones; a newborn baby has about 270–300 bones, which join (fuse) as the child grows. Bones are hard because of calcium phosphate and flexible because of the protein collagen; inside long bones is soft bone marrow. The study of bones is osteology.

2. Functions of the Skeleton

  1. Shape and support: gives body shape and holds it upright.
  2. Protection: skull protects brain; rib cage protects heart & lungs; vertebral column protects spinal cord; pelvis protects bladder and reproductive organs.
  3. Movement: bones act as levers and muscles attached to them pull to produce movement at joints.
  4. Blood formation: red bone marrow makes red & white blood cells and platelets.
  5. Storage of minerals: calcium and phosphorus are stored and released when needed.
  6. Attachment: surface for muscles, tendons, ligaments.
  7. Hearing: tiny ear bones (ossicles) pass sound vibrations.

Skull

Cranium (8 fused bones – frontal, 2 parietal, occipital, 2 temporal, sphenoid, ethmoid) encloses the brain; face has 14 bones (e.g., maxilla, nasal, zygomatic); the mandible (lower jaw) is the only movable skull bone. Skull bones are joined by immovable sutures.

Vertebral Column (Backbone) – 26 bones

Explanation: 33 vertebrae at birth → 26 bones in adult as sacral (5) and coccygeal (4) vertebrae fuse. Its S-shape absorbs shocks; discs of cartilage between vertebrae allow bending. First two cervical vertebrae: atlas (holds skull) and axis (allows head to turn).

4. Joints

A joint (articulation) is the place where two or more bones meet. Joints allow movement and give flexibility and stability to the skeleton.

TypeMovementExamples
1. Immovable (fixed/fibrous)No movementSutures in skull, joints between teeth & jaw
2. Slightly movable (cartilaginous)Little movementBetween vertebrae, pubic joint, ribs with sternum
3. Freely movable (synovial)Wide movementShoulder, hip, elbow, knee, wrist, neck

Structure of a Synovial Joint

  • Cartilage: smooth tissue covering bone ends; reduces friction, absorbs shocks.
  • Synovial membrane & capsule: encloses the joint.
  • Synovial fluid: lubricating fluid (like oil) – reduces friction.
  • Ligaments: tough elastic bands joining bone to bone, stabilise joint.
  • Tendons: join muscle to bone.

5. Common Disorders & Care

DisorderMeaning / Cause
FractureBreak or crack in a bone due to injury; needs plaster/immobilisation
DislocationBone displaced from joint
SprainLigament tear/stretch at joint
ArthritisInflammation of joints – pain, swelling, stiffness
OsteoporosisBones become weak and brittle (calcium loss, old age)
RicketsSoft, bent bones in children due to lack of Vitamin D/calcium
Care of bones & joints: diet rich in calcium, phosphorus, vitamin D (milk, curd, ragi, green vegetables, sunlight); regular exercise & yoga; correct posture (sitting, standing, carrying school bags); avoid obesity & injury; proper footwear; first aid (rest, ice, compression, elevation) and doctor for fractures.
