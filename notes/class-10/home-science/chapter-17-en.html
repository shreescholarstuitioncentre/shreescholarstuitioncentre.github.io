1. Breathing vs Respiration

Breathing is the physical taking in of air (inhalation) and giving out (exhalation). Respiration is the chemical process in cells that releases energy:

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

Breathing supplies oxygen for cellular respiration and removes carbon dioxide.

2. Organs of the Respiratory System

Path of air: nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.

  • Nose/nasal cavity: hairs and mucus trap dust and germs; air is warmed and moistened.
  • Pharynx: common passage for food and air.
  • Larynx (voice box): has vocal cords; the epiglottis closes it during swallowing.
  • Trachea (windpipe): about 10–12 cm tube kept open by C-shaped cartilage rings; lined with cilia and mucus.
  • Bronchi: two branches, one to each lung, dividing into bronchioles.
  • Alveoli: tiny, thin-walled air sacs surrounded by capillaries; the site of gas exchange. About 300 million in the lungs, giving a huge surface area.
  • Lungs: spongy organs in the chest, covered by pleura; right lung has 3 lobes, left has 2 (space for the heart).
  • Diaphragm and ribs: muscles that drive breathing.

3. Mechanism of Breathing

Inhalation: diaphragm contracts and flattens, rib muscles lift ribs up and out, chest volume increases, pressure drops, and air rushes in.

Exhalation: diaphragm relaxes and rises, ribs move down and in, chest volume decreases, pressure rises, and air is pushed out.

Rate: adult 12–20 breaths per minute at rest, more in exercise; infants breathe much faster.

4. Gas Exchange

At the alveoli, oxygen diffuses from air into the blood (haemoglobin in red cells carries it) and carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into air to be exhaled.

Composition: inhaled air has about 21% O₂ and 0.04% CO₂; exhaled air has about 16% O₂ and 4% CO₂.

5. Common Disorders

  • Common cold: viral infection of the nose and throat.
  • Asthma: airways narrow and swell; wheezing, breathlessness; triggered by dust, pollen, smoke.
  • Bronchitis: inflammation of bronchi, cough with mucus.
  • Pneumonia: infection of the lungs, fever, chest pain.
  • Tuberculosis (TB): bacterial lung disease with long cough, weight loss; curable with a full course of medicines.

See a doctor for a cough lasting more than two weeks or for breathing difficulty.

6. Care of the Respiratory System

Breathe through the nose, not the mouth; avoid smoking and smoky kitchens; keep rooms ventilated; cover the mouth when coughing; exercise and practise deep breathing or pranayama; keep away from dust and pollution; eat nutritious food and get vaccinations.

Short: Differentiate breathing and respiration.

Breathing is the physical intake/output of air; respiration is the chemical release of energy from food in cells.

Short: Why is the trachea supported by C-shaped cartilage rings?

They keep the windpipe open so air passes freely, while the gap lets the food pass down the oesophagus behind it.

Short: How does the nose protect the lungs?

Hairs and mucus trap dust and germs, and the air is warmed and moistened.

Short: What is the function of the epiglottis?

It covers the larynx during swallowing so food does not enter the windpipe.

Short: Why are alveoli efficient for gas exchange?

Millions of thin-walled, moist sacs with dense capillaries give a large surface area and short diffusion distance.

Short: Name any three respiratory diseases.

Asthma, bronchitis, pneumonia (also TB and common cold).

Short: Why is breathing through the nose better?

The nose filters, warms and moistens the air; the mouth does not.

Short: Which gas does haemoglobin carry?

Oxygen (forming oxyhaemoglobin).

Long: Describe the organs of the respiratory system in order of air flow.

Nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, two bronchi, many bronchioles and finally alveoli in the lungs, with the roles stated in the notes above (filtering, passage, voice, windpipe, branching, gas exchange).

Long: Explain the mechanism of breathing.

Inhalation: diaphragm contracts and moves down, ribs rise and move outward, the chest cavity enlarges, pressure falls and air enters the lungs. Exhalation: the diaphragm relaxes and rises, ribs fall, the cavity shrinks, pressure rises and air leaves.

Long: Write the causes, symptoms and care of asthma.

Causes: allergens such as dust, pollen, smoke, cold air. Symptoms: wheezing, cough, breathlessness, chest tightness. Care: avoid triggers, keep surroundings clean, take the doctor's prescribed inhaler or medicine and seek help in a severe attack.
