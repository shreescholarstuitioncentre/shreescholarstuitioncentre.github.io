Chapter 7: Water Sources: Use and Purification

Class 10 · Home Science · UP Board / NCERT style notes

1. Importance and Uses of Water

Water is essential for life. About two-thirds of the human body is water.

  • Drinking and cooking.
  • Cleanliness: bathing, washing clothes and utensils, cleaning the house.
  • Digestion, blood circulation, removal of waste (sweat, urine) and control of body temperature.
  • Agriculture, industry, power generation and fire-fighting.
  • Gardening, animals and recreation.

An adult needs about 2–3 litres of drinking water daily (more in summer).

3. Safe Water and Impurities

Safe (potable) water is colourless, odourless, tasteless, clear and free from germs, harmful chemicals and excess salts.

ImpurityExampleRemoved by
SuspendedMud, sand, leavesSettling, straining, filtration
DissolvedSalts, minerals, gasesBoiling (some), RO, distillation
BiologicalBacteria, viruses, wormsBoiling, chlorination, UV

4. Water-borne Diseases

Prevention: drink safe water, wash hands, cover food, keep surroundings clean. For loose motions give ORS (oral rehydration solution).

5. Purification of Water on a Large Scale

Done by the municipal waterworks in these steps:

  1. Storage and sedimentation: water is kept in tanks; heavy mud settles down.
  2. Coagulation: alum (phitkari) is added; fine particles stick together and settle.
  3. Filtration: water passes through sand and gravel filter beds and removes remaining particles.
  4. Chlorination: chlorine is added in correct amount to kill germs.
  5. Storage and supply: clean water is stored in overhead tanks and sent through pipes.

Water from the source settles, gets alum, is filtered and then chlorinated before reaching homes through pipes.

6. Purification of Water at Home

  • Straining: through clean thick cloth removes big particles.
  • Boiling: boil for 10–20 minutes, cool and cover; kills germs. Most reliable simple method.
  • Filtration: candle filter or sand-gravel filter removes suspended impurities and some germs.
  • Alum: rub or hang alum in turbid water, particles settle.
  • Chlorine tablets / drops: follow instructions on the pack.
  • RO and UV purifiers: remove dissolved salts and kill germs; need regular servicing.
  • Solar disinfection: clear plastic bottles in strong sun for hours.

Diagram 2: Sand and gravel filter (animated)

Water trickles down through fine sand, coarse sand, gravel and pebbles, leaving dirt in the layers, and clean water comes out of the pipe.

7. Hard and Soft Water

Hard water has calcium and magnesium salts; it does not make lather with soap easily and leaves scum. Soft water (rain water) lathers easily.

  • Temporary hardness: removed by boiling.
  • Permanent hardness: removed by adding washing soda or using ion-exchange.

8. Conservation and Safe Storage

  1. Do not waste water: close taps, repair leaks, use buckets, reuse washing water for plants.
  2. Do rainwater harvesting: collect roof water in tanks or recharge pits.
  3. Do not pollute water bodies: no waste, soap or dung in ponds, rivers, wells.
  4. Keep wells covered with a parapet and clean regularly.
  5. Store drinking water in clean covered containers with a tap or ladle; do not dip hands.
  6. Clean water tanks every few months.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is safe water?

Water that is clear, odourless, tasteless and free from germs and harmful substances.

Q2. Name four sources of water.

Rain, river/lake, well/tube well and sea.

Q3. Why is alum added to water?

It makes fine suspended particles stick together and settle at the bottom.

Q4. Name four water-borne diseases.

Cholera, typhoid, dysentery and jaundice (also diarrhoea).

Q5. What is hard water?

Water containing calcium and magnesium salts that does not lather easily with soap.

Q6. Why should water be boiled?

Boiling kills bacteria and other germs and makes water safe to drink.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Describe the steps of water purification on a large scale.

Water is stored for sedimentation so heavy mud settles. Alum is added for coagulation so fine particles settle. Water is filtered through sand and gravel beds. Chlorine is added to kill germs. Finally clean water is stored in overhead tanks and supplied through pipes.

Q2. Explain the methods of purifying water at home.

Straining removes large particles; boiling for 10–20 minutes kills germs; candle or sand filters remove suspended impurities; alum settles mud; chlorine tablets disinfect; RO and UV remove salts and germs. The method is chosen by the type of impurity and the source.

Q3. Write a note on water-borne diseases and their prevention.

Cholera, typhoid, dysentery, jaundice and diarrhoea spread through contaminated water. They are prevented by drinking boiled or purified water, covering stored water, washing hands, proper sewage disposal and keeping wells clean. ORS prevents dehydration in loose motions.
