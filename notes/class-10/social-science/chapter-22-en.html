Ch. 22: Consumer Rights

1. Consumer in the Marketplace

A consumer is a person who buys goods/services for personal use. In the market, producers (especially large ones) are powerful, while individual consumers are scattered and weak, so consumers may be exploited: short weights and measures, adulteration, hidden charges, selling above MRP, unsafe products, false advertisements, poor after-sale service, lack of information. E.g., a shopkeeper giving less quantity, spurious medicines, misleading ads.

Therefore rules and regulations are needed to protect consumers, and consumers must be aware of their rights and use them.

2. Consumer Movement

  • In India, the consumer movement arose from consumers' dissatisfaction: food shortages, hoarding, black marketing, adulteration of food and edible oil in the 1960s. It became organised in the 1970s with many consumer organisations writing articles and creating awareness.
  • The movement pressed for legal protection: the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA), 1986 was enacted on 24 December – celebrated as National Consumer Day. It was replaced by the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (in force from July 2020) which also covers e-commerce, product liability, misleading ads and created the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA).
  • 15 March is World Consumer Rights Day. Consumer International (1985 UN guidelines) helps consumer groups globally.
  • India has many (thousands of) consumer groups – some work with government.

3. Consumer Rights

Diagram 1 – Rights of a consumer (animated)

RightMeaning / Example
1. Right to SafetyProtection against goods and services hazardous to life and property – e.g., ISI-marked pressure cooker, safe electrical goods, medicines; faulty gas cylinder injuries.
2. Right to be InformedKnow details of goods – MRP, batch number, date of manufacture, expiry date, ingredients, quality, quantity, purity; so consumer is not cheated. (RTI Act 2005 extends right to information on government services.) Packaged goods must show these.
3. Right to ChooseFreedom to choose from a variety of goods at competitive prices; no pressure to buy a certain brand or tie-up of products.
4. Right to Represent (to be heard)Right to have consumer interests represented in forums, to give views, and to form consumer organisations.
5. Right to Seek RedressalRight to get relief against unfair trade practices, defective goods or exploitation – compensation, replacement, refund through consumer courts.
6. Right to Consumer EducationRight to acquire knowledge and skill to be an informed consumer throughout life (consumer awareness).
NCERT lists five main rights (Safety, Information, Choice, Representation, Redressal); the UN guidelines and many UP Board books also add Consumer Education – so mention all six in the long answer.

4. Consumer Redressal – Consumer Courts

A consumer who feels cheated can file a complaint in a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery (consumer commissions/forums) set up under the Act. A complaint can be filed on a simple paper, with a nominal fee, and even without a lawyer; it can also be filed online. If dissatisfied, one can appeal to the next higher level.

Diagram 2 – Three-tier consumer courts (animated)

Explanation: A consumer first goes to the District Commission; if unhappy, appeals to the State Commission, then the National Commission (in Delhi); final appeal to the Supreme Court. The Commission can order refund, replacement, removal of defects, compensation, costs, and withdrawal of hazardous goods.

Steps to complain: keep cash memo/bill, warranty card → write complaint with facts → send to consumer commission (or call National Consumer Helpline 1915) → hearing → order. Time limit: normally within 2 years of the cause of action.

6. Duties of a Consumer

  1. Buy only after checking quality marks, MRP, expiry date.
  2. Always take cash memo/bill and warranty/guarantee card.
  3. Be aware of rights; compare before buying.
  4. Do not buy fake/unmarked goods; avoid misleading ads.
  5. Report cheating; complain; join consumer groups.
  6. Use goods carefully and avoid waste.
