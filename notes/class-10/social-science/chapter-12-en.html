✎ Lifelines of National Economy
Class 10 SST · Geography · Ch 12 (NCERT · UP Board)

1. Introduction

Goods and services must move from where they are produced to where they are needed. Transport, communication and trade do this job, so they are called the lifelines of the economy – like blood vessels carrying blood in the body. They bind the nation together, link villages with cities and make local markets national and international.

Remember: The pace of a country's development depends on the production of goods and services and their movement. Efficient transport + communication = faster development.

2. Transport

(a) Roadways

  • India has one of the largest road networks in the world – about 54.7 lakh km.
  • Why roads are important: cheaper to build; can cross dissected, rugged hills (Himalayas); suit short distances and small loads; give door-to-door service; link with railways, ports and airports.

Road problems: too much traffic for too few roads; about half of roads are unmetalled and become unusable in rains; national highways are ~2% of roads but carry ~40% of traffic; narrow bridges/culverts and city congestion. Road density = km of road per 100 sq km (J&K ~10 vs Kerala ~375).

(b) Railways

  • Main mode for freight and passengers; first train 1853, Mumbai–Thane. Now 16 zones.
  • Dense network in northern plains (flat, dense population, rich agriculture). Hard in Himalayas, deserts, forests and marshes.
  • Konkan Railway (1998): Roha (Maharashtra) – Mangaluru (Karnataka), 760 km; crosses many rivers, bridges and tunnels (Karbude tunnel 6.5 km) – an engineering feat.
  • Problems: ticketless travel, theft, damage to property, chain pulling.

(c) Pipelines

Carry crude oil, petroleum products, natural gas and even coal slurry; low running cost, no transshipment loss. Key lines: Upper Assam → Kanpur (via Guwahati, Barauni, Prayagraj); Salaya (Gujarat) → Jalandhar (via Viramgam, Mathura, Delhi); HVJ gas pipeline Hazira–Vijaipur–Jagdishpur (UP) – fertiliser & power plants.

(d) Waterways

  • Cheapest, fuel-efficient, eco-friendly, ideal for heavy and bulky goods.
  • National Waterways: NW-1 Ganga (Prayagraj–Haldia, 1,620 km); NW-2 Brahmaputra (Sadiya–Dhubri, 891 km); NW-3 West Coast Canal (Kottapuram–Kollam, 205 km).
  • India has 12 major ports and ~200 minor ports handling about 95% of foreign trade by volume.

(e) Airways

Fastest, most comfortable, overcomes difficult terrain (NE, J&K, Himalaya). Nationalised in 1953; Air India runs international and domestic flights; Pawan Hans helicopters serve ONGC and remote regions. Costly, so used for passengers, mail and valuable cargo.

3. Communication

  • Personal: post, telegram, telephone, email, internet. India Post is the largest postal network in the world (6-digit PIN, Speed Post, Rajdhani/Metro/Green channels).
  • Mass communication: radio (All India Radio), television (Doordarshan), newspapers (published in ~100 languages; Hindi has the largest circulation), films, satellites.
  • Telecom network, STD/ISD and the internet = the "information highway"; IT has helped villages too.

4. International Trade

Exchange of goods between countries. Exports = selling; imports = buying. If exports > imports → favourable balance of trade; if imports > exports → unfavourable. Trade is the barometer of a nation's economy and promotes peace and goodwill. India's exports: gems & jewellery, engineering goods, chemicals, software, textiles. Imports: petroleum, pearls & precious stones, fertilisers, machinery. Trading partners: Asia/ASEAN, Europe, America; WTO regulates global trade.

Tourism as a trade

Brings foreign exchange, jobs, supports handicrafts and local culture, and helps national integration. Types: heritage, medical, eco, adventure tourism.

B. Short Answer

1. Why are transport and communication called lifelines?

They move goods, people and information, link producers with markets and bind regions together, so the economy cannot grow without them.

2. Give two advantages of roadways over railways.

Cheaper construction, can cross steep and rugged terrain, and offer door-to-door service for short distances.

3. What is the Konkan Railway?

A 760 km rail line of 1998 from Roha to Mangaluru along the west coast, with many bridges and tunnels – a notable engineering feat.

4. Why is trade called the barometer of the economy?

The volume and nature of a country's exports and imports show its economic strength and level of development.

5. What is PMGSY?

Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana – a scheme to link villages with all-weather roads.

C. Long Answer

1. Describe the classification of roads in India.

Roads are classed as Golden Quadrilateral and NS-EW super highways (NHAI), National Highways (CPWD), State Highways (State PWD), District Roads (Zila Parishad), Other/rural roads (PMGSY) and Border Roads (BRO). Each links places at a different level – metros, states, districts, villages or borders.

2. Discuss the importance and problems of railways.

Importance: carries heavy freight and large passenger numbers cheaply over long distances; aids industry, agriculture and national integration. Problems: ticketless travel, theft, damage to property, chain pulling, hard terrain in hills and deserts, and need for modernisation.

3. Explain the role of international trade and tourism in India's economy.

Trade earns foreign exchange, spreads goods, technology and ideas, and reflects economic health. Tourism creates jobs, boosts handicrafts, promotes cultural understanding and national integration, and earns foreign exchange; heritage, eco and medical tourism are growing.
