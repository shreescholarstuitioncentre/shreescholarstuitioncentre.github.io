Ch. 18: Development

1. What Development Promises – Different People, Different Goals

Every person wants a better life, but development goals differ for different people. A landless rural labourer wants more days of work and better wages, regular income, local school for children, no caste discrimination. A prosperous farmer wants high support prices and cheap labour/exports. A girl from a rich family wants freedom and equal opportunity, while a girl from a poor family wants safety and education. So:

① People have different developmental goals. ② What is development for one may not be development (or even be harmful) for another. ③ Goals can be conflicting (e.g., a dam brings power but displaces tribal people). ④ Hence people look at income and other things that matter to them – regular work, decent wages, security, respect, freedom, equal treatment.

National development: What is development for country as a whole is decided by a mix of the goals of all. Gandhiji: "The earth has enough resources to meet the needs of all, but not enough for any one's greed."

2. Income and Other Goals

Income is important – more income means more things and services one can buy. But other goals matter too: equal treatment, freedom, security, respect of others (e.g., a job in a far city may give more income but less security/family life). Such qualitative things can't be measured in rupees, but they are valuable. Therefore development is judged by income and non-material goals.

3. How to Compare Countries or States? – Average Income

Average income (per capita income) = Total income of the country ÷ Total population. It is used to compare countries. The World Bank classifies countries by per capita income: high-income (rich) countries (above about US$ 12,000 per year) and low-income countries (about US$ 1,000 or less). India falls in the lower-middle income group (figures are revised yearly – check the latest World Bank report).

4. Income and Other Criteria

Besides income, countries are compared on: Literacy rate, Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) (deaths of children under 1 year per 1000 live births), Life expectancy, Net Attendance Ratio (NAR) (% of 14–18 age children attending school), Body Mass Index (BMI) (nutrition level), access to drinking water, health care.

  • Kerala vs Haryana: Haryana has higher per capita income than Kerala, but Kerala is ahead in literacy, low infant mortality and school attendance – since it spends more on basic health and education. So higher income does not guarantee better quality of life.
  • Bihar: low in both income and social indicators.
  • Public facilities: What money cannot buy individually (good schools, clean water, health care, safe environment) must be provided collectively by government. Kerala's functioning Public Distribution System (PDS) keeps malnutrition low; Tamil Nadu's mid-day meal improves attendance & nutrition of poor children.

5. Human Development Index (HDI)

The UNDP (United Nations Development Programme) publishes the Human Development Report, ranking countries on health (life expectancy), education (literacy/enrolment/schooling) and per capita income. India's rank is lower than some smaller, poorer-looking countries (e.g., Sri Lanka has better health & education), proving income alone isn't enough.

Diagram 2 – What development includes (animated)

Explanation: Development = rising income + good health + education + equality & freedom, and it must be sustainable for future generations.

6. Sustainability of Development

Sustainable development means development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

  • Non-renewable resources (crude oil, coal, minerals) will be exhausted; renewable ones (water, forests, solar) must not be used faster than they regenerate.
  • Groundwater overuse (e.g., in Punjab, parts of Gujarat, Rajasthan) lowers water tables – a threat to future farming and drinking water.
  • Environmental degradation (pollution, deforestation, climate change) harms health and livelihoods but is not counted in national income.
  • Remedies: use renewable energy, conserve water, afforestation, recycling, care for resources, individual and government action.

Q2. Which organisation classifies countries by income?The World Bank.
Q3. What is Infant Mortality Rate?Number of children dying before age one per 1000 live births in a year.
Q4. What does HDI measure?Health, education and per capita income of a country.
Q5. Who publishes the Human Development Report?UNDP.
Q6. What is sustainable development?Development that meets present needs without harming future generations' needs.
Q7. What is Net Attendance Ratio?Percentage of children of a given age group (14–18) attending school.
Q8. Why do Kerala's indicators beat Haryana's despite lower income?Kerala spends more on health, education and PDS.
