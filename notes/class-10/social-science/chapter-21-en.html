Ch. 21: Globalisation and the Indian Economy

1. Production Across Countries – MNCs

Earlier, production was largely confined within countries; only raw materials, food and finished goods crossed borders (trade). Now, production is spread across countries by Multinational Corporations (MNCs).

MNC = a company that owns or controls production in more than one nation. It sets up factories/offices where it can get cheap labour and other resources, to cut costs and earn more profit. E.g., Ford Motors (USA) set up a plant near Chennai and sells cars in India and exports small cars; Coca-Cola, Nokia, Samsung, Hyundai.
Why MNCs set up units in a particular place:
  • Closeness to markets.
  • Availability of skilled and unskilled labour at low cost.
  • Availability of other inputs (raw material, land).
  • Government policies that look after their interests – tax concessions, relaxed labour laws, assured power, roads, ports (Special Economic Zones – SEZs).

Explanation: The MNC keeps design and brand in the home country, produces in cheaper countries and sells across the world. Production is thus split into parts across countries.

2. How MNCs Spread Production & Interlink Markets

  • Setting up own production units (investment) – in SEZs or elsewhere.
  • Buying local companies – e.g., Cargill Foods bought Parakh Foods.
  • Joint production with local companies – local partner provides land & knowledge of market; MNC brings technology & money.
  • Outsourcing – placing orders with small producers (garments, footwear, sports goods) who supply at low cost; MNC sells with its brand; also call centres & services.
Foreign trade & integration of markets: Foreign trade is the main channel connecting markets of different countries. It gives producers a choice beyond domestic markets, and buyers a choice of goods from other countries. Prices of goods tend to become similar across countries; producers in distant places compete with each other.

3. What Is Globalisation?

Globalisation = rapid integration or interconnection between countries through foreign trade and foreign investment by MNCs. More and more goods, services, investment and technology move between countries; MNCs are the major agents.

4. Factors That Enabled Globalisation

  1. Technology: improved transport (containers, ships, aircraft) lowers cost and time; information & communication technology (telecom, computers, internet) lets design, accounts, call-centre services be done across the world.
  2. Liberalisation of trade and investment policy: Liberalisation = removal of government barriers/restrictions on foreign trade & investment. Trade barriers = taxes on imports (tariffs) and quotas (limit on number of imported goods). India removed many barriers after 1991; MNCs could import & export freely; companies could import machinery and raw materials more cheaply.
  3. Pressure from international organisations – WTO (World Trade Organisation): started 1995 by developed countries; sets rules of international trade and ensures free trade; has many member countries including India. Critics say developed countries kept their own barriers (e.g., on agriculture subsidies) while asking developing countries to remove theirs – an unfair practice.

Effect of competition: Indian producers who invested in new technology and improved quality (e.g., in auto parts) benefited. Competition forced them to improve; but small units lacking resources suffered.

6. Struggle for a Fair Globalisation

Fair globalisation creates opportunities for all and ensures benefits are shared better. How?

  • Government must protect interests of all – ensure labour laws are implemented, workers get fair wages & rights; support small producers to improve performance (technology, credit).
  • Use trade barriers where required (but WTO rules limit this).
  • Negotiate at WTO for fairer rules; developing countries unite to demand fair trade rules (e.g., end of unfair subsidies by rich countries).
  • Consumers and groups campaign; small producers need support to compete.
Special Economic Zones (SEZs): industrial areas with world-class facilities – electricity, water, roads, transport, storage, recreation. Companies setting up units get tax holidays (no taxes for an initial period) and relaxed labour laws.
