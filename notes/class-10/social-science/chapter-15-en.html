Part A – Gender and Politics

Gender division of labour: Work is divided so that women do household work (cooking, cleaning, child care) and men do "outside" work. Women also work outside, but it is often unpaid or underpaid. On average Indian women work more hours daily than men, yet their work is not valued or counted.

Patriarchy = male-dominated society where men are given more value & power. Feminist movements = demand equal rights in public & personal life (vote, education, jobs, equal wages).
Women's condition in India:
  • Literacy: women (~54% in 2001) lower than men (~76%).
  • Fewer women in high-paid jobs; equal wages not always given (Equal Remuneration Act 1976).
  • Sex ratio ≈ 940 females per 1000 males (2011); sex-selective abortion, dowry, harassment, domestic violence.

Diagram 1 – Women's political representation (animated)

Explanation: Women are about half the population but a small share of Lok Sabha and state assemblies. To fix this, 1/3 seats are reserved for women in Panchayats and Municipalities (73rd & 74th Amendments). A Women's Reservation Act (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) for 33% in Lok Sabha and Assemblies was passed in 2023; its implementation follows the census and delimitation.

Part B – Religion and Politics

Gandhiji: religion can never be separated from politics – he meant moral values drawn from all religions should guide politics. Human rights groups say many religious minorities suffer discrimination; so the issue is religion-based domination, not religion itself.

Communalism: Idea that religion is the main basis of social community; followers of one religion belong to one community, with same interests, and different religions cannot live together as equals.
Forms of communalism:
  1. Daily beliefs – religious prejudices, stereotypes, belief in superiority of own religion.
  2. Majority dominance (majoritarianism) / minority fear of separate political unit.
  3. Political mobilisation on religion – sacred symbols, leaders, emotional appeals.
  4. Separate political fold – demand for separate electorates (Partition 1947).
  5. Communal violence – riots, massacre (Partition, 1984, 2002 examples).
Secular state: India has no official religion; all religions equal; freedom of faith; no discrimination on religion (Constitution: Arts. 25–28, 15). State can interfere to ensure equality (e.g., banning untouchability).

Part C – Caste and Politics

Caste inequality continues but is weakening because of urbanisation, growth of literacy & education, occupational mobility, weakening hold of landlords, and reformers: Jyotiba Phule, Gandhiji, B.R. Ambedkar, Periyar E.V. Ramaswami Naicker. Constitution bans caste discrimination (Art. 15, 17).

Diagram 2 – Caste and politics (animated)

Caste in politics:
  • Parties choose candidates keeping caste composition of constituency in mind.
  • Appeals to caste sentiments for votes; caste alliances formed.
  • Universal adult franchise made leaders seek support of all castes.
Limits: No constituency has one caste majority; no party wins all votes of a caste; voters also see performance, development, leaders; ruling parties often lose.
Positive effectsNegative effects
Marginalised castes get voice, participation and claims for dignityPolitics around caste can divide society, create tension, violence; other issues (poverty, development) get ignored

Politics in caste: Caste groups become politicised – neighbouring castes/sub-castes join, and new caste groups emerge (Dalits, OBC), so caste itself changes.

Short Questions

Q1. What is the sexual division of labour?System where household work is done by women and outside work by men.
Q2. What is patriarchy?Male-dominated social system where men get more value and power.
Q3. What is communalism?Using religion as the basis of politics/community, claiming religious identity is the main interest.
Q4. What is a secular state?A state with no official religion that treats all religions equally.
Q5. Name four caste reformers.Jyotiba Phule, Gandhiji, B.R. Ambedkar, Periyar.
Q6. What reservation exists for women in local bodies?At least one-third seats in Panchayats and Municipalities.
Q7. Why can't a party win all votes of a caste?Each caste has rich and poor, many parties/candidates; voters also consider other issues.
