Class 10 Science – Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations

Sources covered: NCERT, Balaji, Rajeev Publications, UP Board pattern

1. Chemical Reaction

A process in which one or more substances (reactants) change into new substances (products) with new properties is a chemical reaction. Example: rusting of iron, curdling of milk, burning of magnesium.
Signs of a reaction: (i) change of state (ii) change of colour (iii) evolution of a gas (iv) change of temperature (v) formation of a precipitate.
Burning Mg ribbon in air gives a dazzling white flame and white powder MgO. Clean the ribbon with sandpaper first to remove the oxide layer.

2. Chemical Equation

Shortest way to represent a reaction using symbols and formulae.
Word: Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide
Skeletal: Mg + O2 → MgO
Symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous solution, ↑ gas released, ↓ precipitate, Δ heat, conditions (catalyst, light, temperature) are written above/below the arrow.

3. Balancing a Chemical Equation

Law of conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed, so atoms of each element must be equal on both sides.
Steps (hit and trial): 1) Write the skeletal equation. 2) Count atoms of each element. 3) Start with the compound having most atoms. 4) Put coefficients only (never change subscripts). 5) Balance H and O last. 6) Re-check.
Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2
Balanced: 3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2

4. Types of Chemical Reactions

(a) Combination: two or more substances form one product.
CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + heat (slaked lime)
2Mg + O2 → 2MgO   C + O2 → CO2
Exothermic reactions release heat (burning, respiration, combination of CaO and water). Endothermic reactions absorb heat (photosynthesis, decomposition of CaCO3).
(b) Decomposition: one compound breaks into two or more simpler substances.
Thermal: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2
2FeSO4 → Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3 (green → brown)
2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2 + O2 (brown fumes)
Electrolytic: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2
Photolytic: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 (white → grey in sunlight)
(c) Displacement: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive one from its compound.
Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu
Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu   Pb + CuCl2 → PbCl2 + Cu
(d) Double displacement: ions are exchanged between two compounds; usually a precipitate forms (precipitation reaction).
Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4↓ (white) + 2NaCl
(e) Redox: oxidation = gain of oxygen/loss of hydrogen; reduction = loss of oxygen/gain of hydrogen. Both occur together.
CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O (CuO reduced, H2 oxidised)

7. Effects of Oxidation in Everyday Life

Corrosion: metals are slowly eaten away by air and moisture, e.g. rusting of iron (Fe2O3·xH2O, reddish-brown), green coating on copper, black coating on silver. Prevention: painting, oiling, galvanisation (zinc coat), alloying, electroplating.
Rancidity: fats and oils oxidise, giving bad smell and taste. Prevention: add antioxidants, airtight containers, refrigeration, flush chips packets with nitrogen.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. What is a balanced chemical equation? Why is it needed?An equation with equal atoms of each element on both sides. It is needed to obey the law of conservation of mass.
Q2. Why is Mg ribbon cleaned before burning?It has a layer of MgO on the surface which stops the burning, so sandpaper removes it.
Q3. Why is respiration an exothermic reaction?Glucose is oxidised in cells to CO2 and water, releasing energy as heat: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy.
Q4. Why does the colour of copper sulphate change when an iron nail is dipped in it?Iron displaces copper, forming green FeSO4, so the blue colour fades.
Q5. Why are chips packets flushed with nitrogen?Nitrogen is unreactive and prevents oxidation (rancidity) of oil and fat.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the types of decomposition reactions with equations.Decomposition splits one compound into simpler ones. Thermal (heat): CaCO3 → CaO + CO2. Electrolytic (electricity): 2H2O → 2H2 + O2. Photolytic (light): 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2; 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br2 (used in B/W photography). They are endothermic, so energy is needed.
Q2. Describe the activity to show a precipitation (double displacement) reaction.Take dil. Na2SO4 in one test tube and BaCl2 solution in another. Mix them. A white precipitate of BaSO4 appears. Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4↓ + 2NaCl. The ions SO42- and Ba2+ combined, and Na+ and Cl- exchanged partners.
Q3. What is corrosion? Explain how it can be prevented.Corrosion is the gradual eating away of a metal by moisture, air or acids, e.g. iron rusts, copper turns green and silver turns black. It damages bridges, ships and railings. Prevention: painting, greasing, galvanising, alloying (stainless steel), electroplating.
