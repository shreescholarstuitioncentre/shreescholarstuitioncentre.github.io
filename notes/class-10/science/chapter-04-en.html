Class 10 Science – Chapter 4: Carbon and its Compounds

Sources covered: NCERT, Balaji, Rajeev Publications, UP Board pattern

1. Bonding in Carbon: Covalent Bond

Carbon has 4 valence electrons (2,4). To get a noble gas configuration, it would have to gain or lose 4 electrons, which is difficult (C4- holds 6 protons for 10 electrons; C4+ needs a huge energy). So it shares electrons, forming a covalent bond.
Covalent bond: bond formed by sharing of electron pairs between atoms. Single bond (1 pair): H2, Cl2, CH4. Double bond (2 pairs): O2, CO2, C2H4. Triple bond (3 pairs): N2, C2H2.

Properties of covalent compounds: (1) low melting and boiling points as the intermolecular forces are weak; (2) poor conductors, since no ions are formed; (3) mostly insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents; (4) many are volatile and inflammable.

2. Versatile Nature of Carbon

(i) Catenation: carbon atoms join each other to form long chains, branched chains and rings, due to the strength of the C–C bond. (ii) Tetravalency: 4 valencies allow bonding with C, H, O, N, S, Cl. This gives a huge number of compounds.
Allotropes: diamond (each C bonded to 4 C, rigid 3D, hardest, non-conductor), graphite (each C bonded to 3 C, hexagonal layers, slippery, conducts, used as lubricant and in pencil), fullerene (C-60, football-shaped, buckminsterfullerene).

Homologous series: a series of compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties, in which successive members differ by –CH2 (14 u). Physical properties show a gradation (mp/bp rise with mass).
Structural isomers: same molecular formula, different structures. Butane C4H10 has two: n-butane (straight chain) and isobutane (branched). Pentane has 3 isomers.
Functional groups: Cl/Br (halo), –OH (alcohol), –CHO (aldehyde), >C=O (ketone), –COOH (carboxylic acid).

4. Nomenclature (IUPAC)

Steps: (1) count the longest carbon chain for the word root (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-); (2) add suffix for saturation: -ane, -ene, -yne; (3) functional group: alcohol -ol, aldehyde -al, ketone -one, carboxylic acid -oic acid (drop the final 'e'); halogen as prefix chloro-, bromo-. Examples: CH3CH2OH = ethanol; CH3CHO = ethanal; CH3COOH = ethanoic acid; CH3COCH3 = propanone; CH3CH2Cl = chloroethane.

5. Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds

(a) Combustion:
CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + heat + light
C2H5OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O + heat
Saturated hydrocarbons burn with a clean blue flame; unsaturated burn with a yellow sooty flame (incomplete combustion, carbon soot). Limited air gives a sooty flame (LPG stove, blackened vessels): adjust the air holes. Coal and petroleum are formed from decayed plant/animal matter.
(b) Oxidation: alcohol → carboxylic acid using alkaline KMnO4 or acidified K2Cr2O7 (oxidising agents).
CH3CH2OH → CH3COOH
(c) Addition (unsaturated only): hydrogenation with Ni/Pd catalyst: CH2=CH2 + H2 → CH3CH3 Used to convert vegetable oils (unsaturated) into ghee (saturated). Animal fats have saturated fatty acids, which are harmful to health, so cooking oil is preferred.
(d) Substitution (saturated): one H replaced by another atom in sunlight: CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl

6. Ethanol and Ethanoic Acid

Ethanol (C2H5OH): colourless liquid, soluble in water, neutral to litmus.
With Na: 2CH3CH2OH + 2Na → 2CH3CH2ONa + H2↑
Dehydration (conc. H2SO4, 443 K): CH3CH2OH → CH2=CH2 + H2O
Uses: solvent, medicines (tincture of iodine, cough syrup), fuel, alcoholic drinks. Denatured alcohol: ethanol made unfit for drinking with methanol, pyridine and blue dye. Harm: alcohol slows metabolism and harms the liver; methanol is oxidised to methanal in the liver, which damages it and can cause blindness or death.
Ethanoic acid (CH3COOH) (acetic acid): 5–8% solution in water is vinegar. Pure acid freezes at 290 K as ice-like crystals, hence glacial acetic acid. It is a weak acid.
NaOH + CH3COOH → CH3COONa + H2O
Na2CO3 + 2CH3COOH → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2
Esterification: CH3COOH + C2H5OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O (conc. H2SO4, ethyl ethanoate, fruity smell)
Saponification: ester + NaOH → alcohol + sodium ethanoate

7. Soaps and Detergents

Soap: sodium or potassium salt of long-chain carboxylic (fatty) acids, e.g. C17H35COO-Na+. Made by saponification of oil/fat with NaOH. Molecule: ionic hydrophilic head (dissolves in water) + hydrocarbon hydrophobic tail (dissolves in oil).
Cleansing action: the tails stick into the oily dirt and the heads stay in water, forming a spherical micelle that traps dirt. Micelles do not coalesce due to ion–ion repulsion between the heads, so dirt is rinsed away when the cloth is rubbed.
Hard water has Ca2+/Mg2+ salts, which form insoluble scum with soap, so more soap is wasted. Detergents (sodium salts of sulphonic acids or ammonium salts with long chain) do not form scum, so they work in both hard and soft water. Non-biodegradable detergents pollute water.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Why does carbon form covalent compounds?Carbon has 4 valence electrons, so it cannot easily gain or lose 4 electrons. It completes its octet by sharing electrons.
Q2. What is catenation?Self-linking property of carbon atoms to form long chains, branched chains or rings.
Q3. Draw the electron dot structure of ethane and explain the number of bonds.In C2H6 the two carbon atoms share one pair with each other and each shares three pairs with three H atoms. Total 7 single covalent bonds (1 C–C and 6 C–H).
Q4. Why do covalent compounds have low melting points?Their intermolecular forces are weak, so little energy is needed to separate the molecules.
Q5. Why is graphite a conductor while diamond is not?Graphite has one free electron per carbon atom (3 bonds each), but in diamond all four valence electrons of each carbon are bonded.
Q6. What is denatured alcohol?Ethanol mixed with poisonous substances like methanol, pyridine and a dye so that it cannot be drunk, to prevent misuse.
Q7. Why is soap ineffective in hard water?Ca2+ and Mg2+ salts react with soap to form insoluble scum, so soap does not lather and cleaning fails.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the cleansing action of soap with diagram description.Soap molecules have a hydrophilic ionic head and a hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail. In water the tails attach to the oil/dirt while the heads stay in water, so the molecules arrange into a micelle with tails inside, holding the dirt, and the heads outside. Micelles remain suspended as they repel each other, and dirt is washed away on rinsing. (See the animated Diagram 2.)
Q2. Write the chemical properties of ethanol and ethanoic acid with equations.Ethanol: with Na gives sodium ethoxide and H2; dehydration with hot conc. H2SO4 gives ethene; oxidation gives ethanoic acid; burns to CO2 and H2O. Ethanoic acid: reacts with NaOH to form sodium ethanoate and water; with Na2CO3 or NaHCO3 to evolve CO2; with ethanol to form the sweet-smelling ester (esterification): CH3COOH + C2H5OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O. The ester on heating with NaOH gives alcohol and sodium salt (saponification).
Q3. What are isomers? Draw the structural isomers of butane and pentane.Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures are isomers. Butane (C4H10): n-butane CH3–CH2–CH2–CH3 and isobutane CH3–CH(CH3)–CH3. Pentane (C5H12) has three: n-pentane, 2-methylbutane (isopentane) and 2,2-dimethylpropane (neopentane).
Q4. Differentiate between addition and substitution reactions with examples. Why do unsaturated hydrocarbons burn with a sooty flame?Addition reaction occurs in unsaturated hydrocarbons, where the double or triple bond breaks and atoms add: CH2=CH2 + H2 → CH3CH3 (Ni). Substitution occurs in saturated hydrocarbons, where one H is replaced: CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl (sunlight). Unsaturated hydrocarbons have a higher percentage of carbon and burn incompletely in air, leaving unburnt carbon particles, so the flame is yellow and sooty.
Q5. What is a homologous series? List its characteristics.A series of organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula, in which any two successive members differ by –CH2 (14 u). Characteristics: same general formula; same functional group and similar chemical properties; gradual change in physical properties (mp, bp, density); can be prepared by the same method. Example: CH4, C2H6, C3H8.
