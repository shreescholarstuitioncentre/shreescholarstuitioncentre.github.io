Class 10 Science – Ch 11: Electricity

1. Electric Current & Charge

Charge (Q) is measured in coulomb (C). 1 electron carries 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, so 1 C = 6.25×10¹⁸ electrons. Electric current is the rate of flow of charge:

I = Q / t (unit: ampere, A)

1 A = 1 C flowing per second. Conventional current flows from + to −; electrons actually move from − to +. Current is measured by an ammeter, connected in series.

2. Potential Difference

Potential difference is the work done to move 1 coulomb of charge between two points.

V = W / Q (unit: volt, V)

1 V = 1 J of work per coulomb. A voltmeter is connected in parallel. A cell or battery maintains the potential difference that drives current.

3. Ohm's Law

At constant temperature, current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

V = I R   (R in ohm, Ω)

The V–I graph is a straight line through the origin; its slope is R. 1 Ω = 1 V/A.

4. Resistance & its Factors

Resistance is the opposition a conductor offers to the flow of current.

R = ρ l / A

R increases with length l, decreases with area A, and depends on material and temperature. ρ (resistivity) is a property of the material, unit Ω·m. Metals and alloys have low resistivity (alloys like nichrome have higher than pure metals and do not oxidise easily, so heaters use them). Rubber and glass are insulators.

5. Series & Parallel

Series: Rs = R1+R2+R3 (same I, V divides)
Parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R1+1/R2+1/R3 (same V, I divides)

Parallel is used at homes: each appliance gets full voltage, works independently, and total resistance falls.

6. Heating Effect, Power & Energy

Joule's law: H = I²Rt
Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R (watt, W)
1 kWh = 3.6×10⁶ J = 1 unit

Uses: heater, toaster, iron, bulb (tungsten, high melting point), fuse (low melting point wire, protects circuits).

Questions & Answers (tap to see answer)

MCQ: SI unit of potential difference? (a) A (b) V (c) Ω (d) W

(b) Volt.

MCQ: Ammeter is connected in? (a) series (b) parallel

(a) Series, as it has very low resistance.

MCQ: Resistance of a wire is doubled in length and radius halves. New R = ? times

Length ×2, area ×1/4, so R becomes 8 times.

MCQ: 1 kWh equals? (a) 3.6×10⁶ J (b) 3.6×10³ J

(a) 3.6×10⁶ J.

Short: Why is tungsten used for bulb filaments?

High melting point (3380°C) and it glows without melting.

Short: Why are alloys used in heating devices?

Higher resistivity and they do not oxidise (burn) easily at high temperature.

Short: What does a fuse do?

It melts when current exceeds a safe limit and breaks the circuit, protecting appliances.

Numerical: Current 0.5 A flows for 10 min. Find charge.

Q = It = 0.5×600 = 300 C.

Numerical: Find R if 12 V gives 3 A.

R = V/I = 12/3 = 4 Ω.

Numerical: 100 W bulb runs 5 h daily for 30 days. Units used?

E = 0.1 kW×5×30 = 15 kWh = 15 units.

Long: State Ohm's law, describe the experiment to verify it.

Connect cell, ammeter (series), resistor, voltmeter (parallel) and a key. Vary the voltage by adding cells, note V and I each time. The V–I graph is a straight line through the origin, so V ∝ I, and V/I = R is constant.

Long: Compare series and parallel combinations.

Series: one path, same current, voltages add, Rs largest, if one fails all stop. Parallel: many paths, same voltage, currents add, Rp smaller than smallest R, appliances work independently, so homes use parallel.
