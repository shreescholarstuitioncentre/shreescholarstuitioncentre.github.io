Exceptions: Na, K, Li are soft and cut with a knife. Mercury is liquid at room temperature. Gallium and caesium melt on the palm. Lead and mercury are poor conductors. Non-metals: graphite conducts electricity, diamond is the hardest natural substance with very high melting point, iodine is a lustrous non-metal. Bromine is the only liquid non-metal.

2. Chemical Properties of Metals

(a) With oxygen: Metal + O2 → Metal oxide (basic).
4Na + O2 → 2Na2O   2Mg + O2 → 2MgO
2Cu + O2 → 2CuO (black coating on heating)
4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3
Na and K react so vigorously that they catch fire, so they are stored in kerosene. Au and Ag do not react. Amphoteric oxides (Al2O3, ZnO) react with both acids and bases: Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O; Al2O3 + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO2 + H2O. Anodising thickens the protective oxide layer on aluminium.
(b) With water:
2K + 2H2O → 2KOH + H2 + heat (fire, violent)
2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2 + heat
Ca + 2H2O → Ca(OH)2 + H2 (bubbles make Ca float)
Mg + 2H2O (hot) → Mg(OH)2 + H2
2Al + 3H2O (steam) → Al2O3 + 3H2   3Fe + 4H2O (steam) → Fe3O4 + 4H2
Cu, Ag, Au do not react with water.
(c) With acids: Metal + dil. acid → Salt + H2 (e.g. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2). Cu, Ag, Au do not react. With dil. HNO3 metals do not release H2 as HNO3 is a strong oxidiser (only Mg and Mn give H2). Aqua regia (3 parts conc. HCl : 1 part conc. HNO3) dissolves gold and platinum.
(d) With salt solutions: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal.
Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu   Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu   Cu + 2AgNO3 → Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag

3. Reactivity Series

Arrangement of metals in decreasing order of reactivity: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au. Mnemonic: Kya Na Ca Mag Al Zn Fe Pb H Cu Hg Ag Au.

4. Reaction of Non-metals

Non-metals do not give H2 with acids. Their oxides are acidic (CO2 + H2O → H2CO3), while metal oxides are basic. Non-metals gain electrons to form negative ions.

5. Ionic Compounds

Formed by transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal to complete the octet.
Na (2,8,1) → Na+ (2,8) + e-; Cl (2,8,7) + e- → Cl- (2,8,8) ⇒ NaCl
Mg (2,8,2) → Mg2+ + 2e-; 2Cl gain 2e- ⇒ MgCl2
Properties: (1) Hard, brittle solids due to strong force of attraction between ions. (2) High melting and boiling points (needs much energy to break strong bonds). (3) Soluble in water, insoluble in kerosene/petrol. (4) Conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state (ions free to move) but not in solid state.

6. Occurrence and Extraction of Metals

Mineral: natural material in which a metal or its compound occurs. Ore: mineral from which metal can be extracted profitably. Gangue: impurities (soil, sand) in the ore. Steps: enrichment of ore → extraction of metal → refining.
(i) Low reactivity (Hg, Cu): heat the sulphide ore in air.
2HgS + 3O2 → 2HgO + 2SO2 (roasting)
2HgO → 2Hg + O2 (heat)
(ii) Medium reactivity (Zn, Fe, Pb): convert to oxide, then reduce with carbon. Roasting (sulphide ore in excess air): 2ZnS + 3O2 → 2ZnO + 2SO2. Calcination (carbonate ore in limited air): ZnCO3 → ZnO + CO2. Reduction: ZnO + C → Zn + CO.
Thermit reaction: Fe2O3 + 2Al → 2Fe + Al2O3 + heat (molten iron welds rail tracks, cracked machine parts)
(iii) High reactivity (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): carbon cannot reduce them, so use electrolytic reduction of the molten compound. Cathode: Na+ + e- → Na. Anode: 2Cl- → Cl2 + 2e-.
Refining by electrolysis: impure metal is the anode, pure metal strip is the cathode, electrolyte is an acidified salt solution of the metal (CuSO4). Pure copper deposits on the cathode, impurities settle under the anode as anode mud (contains Au, Ag).

7. Corrosion and Alloys

Rusting of iron requires both air (oxygen) and moisture: Fe2O3·xH2O (a reddish-brown flaky substance). Silver turns black (Ag2S), copper turns green (basic copper carbonate). Prevention: painting, oiling/greasing, galvanisation (zinc coating protects even if scratched), electroplating, alloying.
Alloy: homogeneous mixture of a metal with other metals/non-metals. Pure iron is soft; adding carbon makes steel hard; stainless steel = Fe + Ni + Cr (no rust). Brass = Cu + Zn; Bronze = Cu + Sn; Solder = Pb + Sn (low melting point, welding wires). Amalgam = alloy with mercury. 24 carat gold is pure; 22 carat gold is alloyed with copper or silver for hardness.

Short Answer Questions

Q1. Why are Na, K and Li stored in kerosene?They are highly reactive, catch fire in air and react violently with water. Kerosene protects them from air and moisture.
Q2. Why is aluminium foil used to wrap food even though it is reactive?A thin protective oxide layer forms on aluminium, which stops further reaction.
Q3. Why does a copper vessel lose its shine and turn green?Copper reacts with CO2 and moisture of air to form a green coating of basic copper carbonate.
Q4. Why is carbon not used to extract sodium from its oxide?Na has a greater affinity for oxygen than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce it. Electrolysis of the molten chloride is used.
Q5. What is galvanisation? State its advantage.Coating iron with a thin layer of zinc. Zinc protects iron even if the layer is scratched, because zinc is more reactive and corrodes in place of iron (sacrificial protection).
Q6. Why do ionic compounds conduct only in molten or aqueous state?In the solid state the ions are fixed in the lattice. On melting or dissolving, they become free to move and carry current.
Q7. Why does a non-metal not displace hydrogen from dilute acid?Non-metals cannot lose electrons to form positive ions, so they cannot replace H+.

Long Answer Questions

Q1. Explain the reaction of metals with water and rank them in activity.K and Na react violently even with cold water giving hydroxide and H2, enough heat to burn H2: 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2. Ca reacts less violently, floating due to H2 bubbles. Mg reacts only with hot water. Al, Zn and Fe react with steam to form oxide and H2: 3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2. Pb, Cu, Ag, Au do not react with water. Order: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe.
Q2. Describe the extraction of metals of medium reactivity, e.g. zinc from its ores.Zinc occurs as ZnS or ZnCO3. Sulphide ore is roasted in air: 2ZnS + 3O2 → 2ZnO + 2SO2. Carbonate ore is calcined: ZnCO3 → ZnO + CO2. ZnO is then reduced with carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO. Metals of the middle group are first converted into oxides since oxides are easier to reduce than sulphides or carbonates.
Q3. Explain electrolytic refining of copper with a diagram description.Impure copper is the anode and thin pure copper strip is the cathode in acidified CuSO4 solution. On passing current, Cu from the anode dissolves as Cu2+ ions, which move to the cathode and deposit as pure Cu: Cu2+ + 2e- → Cu. Impurities like Au and Ag settle below the anode as anode mud. The anode becomes thinner and the cathode thicker.
Q4. How is an ionic compound formed? Describe properties of NaCl and MgCl₂.Na (2,8,1) loses one electron to become Na+ (2,8) and Cl (2,8,7) gains it to become Cl- (2,8,8). The opposite ions attract strongly and form NaCl. Mg (2,8,2) loses two electrons, and two chlorine atoms accept one each, forming MgCl2. Properties: hard and brittle, high mp/bp, soluble in water, conduct in molten and aqueous state, but not in solid state.
