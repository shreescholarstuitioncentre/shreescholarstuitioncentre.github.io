Class 10 Science · Ch 12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

1. Magnetic Field & Field Lines

Magnetic field = region around a magnet where its force is felt (unit: tesla, T). Field lines are imaginary lines showing it.

  • Outside the magnet they go N → S; inside S → N (closed curves).
  • Two lines never cross (a compass needle can't point two ways at one point).
  • Crowded lines = strong field.
  • A compass needle is a tiny magnet; it lines up along the field.

2. Oersted's Experiment (1820)

A compass needle near a wire deflects when current flows, and deflects the opposite way when current reverses. So electricity and magnetism are related — a current produces a magnetic field.

3. Straight wire & Right-Hand Thumb Rule

Hold the wire in your right hand, thumb along current: curled fingers show the field direction. Field ∝ current and ∝ 1/distance. A circular loop gives concentric circles that become straight at the centre; n turns give n times the field.

4. Solenoid & Electromagnet

Solenoid = coil of many circular turns. Its field resembles a bar magnet; inside it is uniform. Putting a soft iron core inside makes an electromagnet. Strength increases with turns, current and core. Soft iron is used because it loses magnetism quickly; steel keeps it (permanent magnet).

5. Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

A conductor in a magnetic field feels a force (Andre-Marie Ampere). It is maximum when current ⟂ field, zero when parallel. Reversing current or field reverses the force. F = BIL sinθ.

6. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Stretch thumb, forefinger, middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular.
Forefinger = Field · Middle finger = Current · Thumb = Force/motion

7. Electric Motor

Converts electrical → mechanical energy. Parts: armature coil, strong magnet, split-ring commutator, brushes, battery. The commutator reverses current every half turn, so the coil keeps turning in the same direction. Used in fans, mixers, washing machines.

8. Electromagnetic Induction (Faraday)

Moving a magnet in/out of a coil (or changing the field through it) induces a current; the galvanometer deflects only while the magnet moves. Faster motion or more turns = more current.

9. Fleming's Right-Hand Rule

Right hand: Thumb = motion of conductor, Forefinger = Field, Middle finger = Induced current.

10. Generator, AC & DC

Generator converts mechanical → electrical energy: a coil rotates in a magnetic field; slip rings and brushes carry current out. AC reverses direction periodically (India: 50 Hz, reverses every 1/100 s); DC flows one way (cell). AC suffers less loss in long-distance transmission.

11. Domestic Electric Circuits

India supplies 220 V, 50 Hz. Wires: live (red), neutral (black), earth (green). The earth wire gives leakage current a safe path (metal bodies of geyser, fridge). Fuse melts on excess current (5 A lighting, 15 A appliances). Short circuit = live and neutral touch; overloading = too many appliances on one circuit.

Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Why does a compass needle deflect near a current wire?

Answer: Current creates a magnetic field around the wire, which exerts force on the needle.

Q2. Why can't field lines cross?

Answer: At the crossing point the field would have two directions, which is impossible.

Q3. Function of the commutator?

Answer: It reverses current direction every half rotation so the torque stays in one direction.

Q4. Why is earthing necessary?

Answer: It carries leakage current to the ground, preventing shock when a metal body becomes live.

Q5. Difference between AC and DC?

Answer: AC changes direction periodically (50 Hz in India); DC flows in one direction only.

Q6. What is a short circuit?

Answer: Live and neutral wires touch directly; resistance drops, huge current flows, fuse should blow.

Long Questions & Answers

Q1. Explain the working of an electric motor.

Answer: Current from the battery flows through coil ABCD between the poles. By Fleming's left-hand rule, sides AB and CD feel opposite forces, so the coil rotates. After half a turn the split-ring commutator swaps contacts, reversing the current, so forces still turn the coil the same way. Continuous rotation converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Q2. Explain the working of an AC generator.

Answer: A coil rotates between N and S poles, cutting field lines. The changing flux induces an EMF (Fleming's right-hand rule). Slip rings and brushes connect the coil to the outside circuit. In each half turn the current direction reverses, giving alternating current.

Q3. Describe Oersted's experiment and conclusion; state two ways to increase an electromagnet's strength.

Answer: Place a compass beside a wire; closing the circuit deflects the needle, reversing current reverses deflection. Conclusion: a current produces a magnetic field. Strength increases by more turns, more current, or a soft iron core.
