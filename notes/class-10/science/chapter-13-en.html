Class 10 Science · Ch 13: Our Environment

1. Ecosystem

Ecosystem = all living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic: air, water, soil, light, temperature) things interacting in an area. Natural: forest, pond, lake. Artificial: garden, crop field, aquarium.

  • Producers: green plants, algae; make food by photosynthesis.
  • Consumers: herbivores (deer), carnivores (lion), omnivores (human), parasites (lice).
  • Decomposers: bacteria, fungi; break dead matter into simple substances, recycling nutrients.

2. Food Chain & Trophic Levels

Food chain = linear sequence of organisms in which each eats the one before it. Each step is a trophic level. Example: Grass → Insect → Frog → Snake.

3. Food Web

Many food chains interlink in an ecosystem to form a food web. It is more stable: if one species falls, a consumer can switch to other food.

4. Flow of Energy & 10% Law

Sun is the source. Energy flow is unidirectional (never returns to the sun). Producers capture ~1% of sunlight. Lindeman's 10% law: only ~10% of energy passes to the next level; the rest is lost as heat and in respiration. So food chains have only 3–4 trophic levels, and eating plants (lower level) gives more usable energy.

5. Biological Magnification

Non-biodegradable chemicals (pesticides like DDT) enter food chains via soil and water. They are not broken down, so concentration increases at each higher trophic level; humans, at the top, have the maximum amount (found in wheat, vegetables, milk, meat).

6. Biodegradable & Non-biodegradable

Biodegradable: broken by bacteria/fungi (vegetable waste, paper, cotton, dung). Non-biodegradable: not broken (plastic, DDT, glass, metals, polythene) and persist, harming the environment.

7. Ozone Layer

Ozone (O₃) in the stratosphere absorbs harmful UV radiation. Formed: O₂ → O + O (by UV); O + O₂ → O₃. Cs (refrigerators, ACs, aerosol sprays, fire extinguishers) destroy it. UN agreed in 1987 to freeze C production. UV causes skin cancer, cataract and weakens immunity.

8. Garbage Disposal

Methods: landfill, composting (biodegradable waste → manure), recycling (paper, metal, glass, plastic), incineration. Plastic is dangerous because it blocks drains and harms animals. Follow the 3 Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (and Refuse).

Short Questions & Answers

Q1. What are abiotic components?

Answer: Non-living parts of an ecosystem: air, water, soil, sunlight, temperature.

Q2. Define trophic level.

Answer: Each step in a food chain where energy is transferred is a trophic level.

Q3. Why do food chains have only 3–4 steps?

Answer: Only 10% energy passes on, so too little remains for further levels.

Q4. What are decomposers? Give examples.

Answer: Organisms that break dead plants and animals into simple substances, e.g. bacteria and fungi.

Q5. What is biological magnification?

Answer: Increasing concentration of non-biodegradable chemicals at higher trophic levels.

Q6. Name the chemical that harms the ozone layer.

Answer: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

Q7. Why is energy flow unidirectional?

Answer: Energy from producers passes to consumers and is lost as heat; it never returns to the sun.

Q8. Give two biodegradable and two non-biodegradable wastes.

Answer: Biodegradable: paper, vegetable peels. Non-biodegradable: plastic, DDT.

Long Questions & Answers

Q1. Explain the flow of energy in an ecosystem with the 10% law.

Answer: The sun's energy is trapped by producers through photosynthesis (about 1%). When a herbivore eats a plant, only about 10% of this energy is stored in its body; the rest is lost as heat and respiration. The same happens at each next level. Example: 10,000 J in producers → 1000 J in herbivores → 100 J in carnivores → 10 J in top carnivores. Flow is one-way, and because energy shrinks fast, food chains have 3–4 levels.

Q2. What is the ozone layer? Why is it depleting and what are its effects?

Answer: Ozone (O₃) layer in the stratosphere absorbs UV rays of the sun. CFCs released from refrigerators, ACs and aerosols reach the stratosphere and break ozone down, creating a hole over Antarctica. More UV reaches Earth, causing skin cancer, cataract, weaker immunity and harm to crops and marine life. UNEP's 1987 agreement froze CFC production.

Q3. Describe biological magnification with an example.

Answer: Pesticides like DDT sprayed on crops wash into soil and water and are absorbed by plants. Being non-biodegradable, they pass to herbivores and then carnivores, becoming more concentrated at each step. Humans at the top accumulate the most through grains, vegetables, milk and meat.

Q4. Discuss the problems from non-biodegradable waste and how to manage garbage.

Answer: Non-biodegradable waste like plastic stays for centuries, chokes drains, pollutes soil and kills animals. Management: segregate waste, compost biodegradable waste, recycle paper/metal/glass/plastic, reduce use of plastic, reuse items, and dispose of the rest in landfills safely.
