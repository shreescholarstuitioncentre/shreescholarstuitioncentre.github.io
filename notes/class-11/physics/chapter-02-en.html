Physics Class 11 – Ch 2: Units and Measurements

2A Units, Measurements & Errors • 2B Dimensional Analysis

2A.1 Need for Measurement

Physics is an experimental science. A law is accepted only if numbers measured in experiments agree with it. Measurement lets us compare a quantity with a standard, and so describe nature exactly, e.g. “the room is 4 m long”, not “quite long”.

2A.2 Measurement and its Components

Physical quantity = numerical value (n) × unit (u).   Q = n u
Example: length = 5 m → n = 5, u = metre.
If unit becomes smaller, n becomes larger: n₁u₁ = n₂u₂.

Fundamental (base) quantities: independent of others (length, mass, time…). Derived quantities: made from base ones (speed, force…). A good unit is well defined, invariable, easy to reproduce, and convenient in size.

2A.4 Measurement of Length

Ordinary: metre scale (LC 1 mm). Small: vernier callipers (LC 0.1 mm), screw gauge (LC 0.01 mm). Very large (stars/planets): parallax method: d = b/θ, where b = distance between two observation points, θ = parallax angle (rad).
Very small: electron microscope, X-ray diffraction.

Vernier constant (LC) = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = (value of 1 MSD) / (no. of vernier divisions).
If 10 VSD = 9 MSD and 1 MSD = 1 mm → LC = 0.1 mm = 0.01 cm.
Screw gauge: LC = pitch / no. of circular divisions; reading = PSR + (CSR × LC). Correct for zero error.

2A.5 Measurement of Mass

Common balance for ordinary masses; mass spectrograph for atoms. Atomic scale unit: 1 amu (u) = 1/12 mass of C-12 atom = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg. Large: 1 quintal = 100 kg, 1 tonne = 1000 kg, 1 slug = 14.59 kg, 1 CSL (Chandrasekhar limit) = 1.4 × solar mass.

2A.6 Measurement of Time Interval

Sundial → pendulum clock → quartz clock → atomic (caesium) clock, accuracy 1 s in 10⁹ years. 1 day = 86 400 s; 1 solar year ≈ 3.16×10⁷ s.

2A.7 Accuracy and Precision

Accuracy: closeness of measured value to the true value.
Precision: closeness of repeated measurements to each other (depends on instrument resolution).
Precise ≠ accurate (a clock showing consistently 5 min fast is precise, not accurate).

2A.8 Errors in Measurement

Error = difference between true and measured value.

Systematic errors (tend one direction)

Instrumental (zero error, faulty scale), Personal (parallax, reaction time), Imperfection in technique/method, Environmental (temperature). Remedy: calibrate, correct zero error, improve method.

Random errors

Irregular, due to unknown causes; reduced by taking many readings and the mean.

Gross errors

Carelessness: wrong reading/recording; avoided by care.

Least count error = LC of instrument; reduced by using a finer instrument.

2A.9 Absolute, Relative & Percentage Error

Mean a_m = (a₁+a₂+…+aₙ)/n
Absolute error: Δaᵢ = a_m − aᵢ
Mean absolute error: Δa_mean = (|Δa₁|+…+|Δaₙ|)/n
Relative (fractional) error = Δa_mean / a_m
Percentage error = (Δa_mean / a_m) × 100 %
Result written a = a_m ± Δa_mean

2A.10 Combination of Errors and Maximum Error

CaseRule (max error)
Sum / difference: Z = A ± BΔZ = ΔA + ΔB (errors always add)
Product: Z = ABΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B
Quotient: Z = A/BΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B
Power: Z = AⁿΔZ/Z = n ΔA/A
General: Z = AᵖBᵠ/CʳΔZ/Z = pΔA/A + qΔB/B + rΔC/C

Proof (product): Z = AB. Take A±ΔA, B±ΔB. Z±ΔZ = (A±ΔA)(B±ΔB) = AB ± AΔB ± BΔA ± ΔAΔB. Neglect ΔAΔB (very small). Divide by Z=AB: ΔZ/Z = ΔB/B + ΔA/A (max). ∎

2A.11 Error in Calculation of a Physical Quantity

Example: g = 4π²L/T². Then Δg/g = ΔL/L + 2ΔT/T. Note the power multiplies the relative error, so measure the quantity with the highest power most carefully.

2A.12–14 Significant Figures

Rules:
1) All non-zero digits are significant (2.57 → 3).
2) Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (2.07 → 3).
3) Leading zeros are NOT significant (0.0025 → 2).
4) Trailing zeros after decimal point ARE significant (2.500 → 4).
5) Trailing zeros in a whole number without decimal are not significant (2500 → 2, ambiguous). Use scientific notation: 2.50×10³ → 3.
6) Powers of 10 don’t count: 4.70×10⁻³ → 3.
7) Exact numbers/counts have infinite sig figs.

Rounding off: digit <5 → drop; digit >5 → increase previous by 1; digit =5 → increase if previous is odd, keep if even (2.35→2.4, 2.45→2.4).

× and ÷: answer has as many sig figs as the number with the fewest sig figs. + and −: answer has as many decimal places as the number with the fewest decimal places.

2B.3 Principle of Homogeneity

Only quantities of the same dimensions can be added, subtracted or equated. Every term on both sides of a correct equation must have the same dimensions.

2B.4 Uses of Dimensions

① Check correctness of an equation. ② Derive relation between quantities. ③ Convert units: n₂ = n₁ [M₁/M₂]ᵃ [L₁/L₂]ᵇ [T₁/T₂]ᶜ. e.g. 1 N = 10⁵ dyne.

2B.5 Limitations

① Cannot give dimensionless constants (½, 2π…). ② Fails for trigonometric, exponential, log functions. ③ Cannot derive relation having sum/difference of terms. ④ Cannot tell whether a quantity is scalar or vector. ⑤ Needs knowing on which quantities it depends. ⑥ A dimensionally correct equation need not be physically correct.

Solved Examples

Easy Find LC of a vernier where 10 VSD = 9 MSD, 1 MSD = 1 mm.
Given: 10 VSD = 9 MSD. Step 1: 1 VSD = 0.9 MSD = 0.9 mm. Step 2: LC = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1 − 0.9 = 0.1 mm = 0.01 cm.
Easy Convert 1 N to dyne.
1 N = 1 kg m s⁻². kg → g: ×1000; m → cm: ×100. So 1 N = 1000 g × 100 cm s⁻² = 10⁵ g cm s⁻² = 10⁵ dyne.
Medium Readings of time period: 2.63, 2.56, 2.42, 2.71, 2.80 s. Find mean, mean absolute error, relative and % error.
Step 1: mean = 13.12/5 = 2.624 ≈ 2.62 s.
Step 2: |Δa| = 0.01, 0.06, 0.20, 0.09, 0.18. Sum = 0.54.
Step 3: mean abs. error = 0.54/5 = 0.108 ≈ 0.11 s.
Step 4: relative = 0.11/2.62 = 0.042. Step 5: % error = 4.2 %. T = (2.62 ± 0.11) s.
Medium R₁ = (100±3) Ω and R₂ = (200±4) Ω in series. Find R with error.
R = 100+200 = 300 Ω. ΔR = 3+4 = 7 Ω (errors add for sum). R = (300 ± 7) Ω; % error = 7/300×100 = 2.33 %.
Medium Radius of a sphere has 1% error, mass has 2% error. Find % error in density.
ρ = M/(4πr³/3) ∝ M r⁻³. Δρ/ρ = ΔM/M + 3Δr/r = 2 + 3(1) = 5 %.
Hard Derive time period of a simple pendulum using dimensions.
Assume T = k lᵃ mᵇ gᶜ. Dimensions: [T] = [L]ᵃ[M]ᵇ[LT⁻²]ᶜ = MᵇLᵃ⁺ᶜT⁻²ᶜ.
Compare: b = 0; a + c = 0; −2c = 1 → c = −½, a = ½.
So T = k √(l/g) (k = 2π by experiment). Period is independent of mass.
Hard Check dimensionally: s = ut + ½at².
[s] = L. [ut] = LT⁻¹·T = L. [½at²] = LT⁻²·T² = L (½ dimensionless). All terms have dimension L → dimensionally correct.
Hard If v = x + at (x: position), is it correct? [v]=LT⁻¹, [x]=L, [at]=LT⁻¹. L ≠ LT⁻¹ → violates homogeneity → incorrect.
Hard Find dimensions of a and b in (P + a/V²)(V − b) = RT.
By homogeneity [a/V²]=[P] → [a] = [P][V²] = ML⁻¹T⁻² · L⁶ = ML⁵T⁻². [b] = [V] = L³.
Medium Sig figs: 3.0 × 2.50 and 12.11 + 18.0 + 1.012.
Product = 7.5 → 2 sig figs (least from 3.0). Sum = 31.122 → one decimal place (18.0) → 31.1.

Short & Long Q & A

Q1. What is a unit? Name base units of SI.
Ans: Unit is standard used to measure a quantity. m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd.
Q2. Difference between accuracy and precision?
Ans: Accuracy = closeness to true value; precision = closeness of readings to each other.
Q3. What is least count?
Ans: Smallest value measurable by an instrument; error of measurement is ±LC.
Q4. Why do errors always add in sum or difference?
Ans: For maximum possible error, the errors in both quantities can be in same direction, so ΔZ = ΔA + ΔB.
Q5. Define light year, parsec and AU.
Ans: Light year = distance light travels in 1 year = 9.46×10¹⁵ m; parsec = 3.08×10¹⁶ m; AU = mean Earth–Sun distance 1.496×10¹¹ m.
Q6. Write the rules of significant figures.
Ans: See section 2A.12 (all 7 rules).
Q7. State principle of homogeneity.
Ans: Dimensions of every term on both sides of a valid equation are equal.
Q8. Name dimensionless quantities.
Ans: Angle, strain, refractive index, specific gravity, Reynolds number, π.
Q9. Can a dimensionally correct equation be wrong?
Ans: Yes — e.g. K.E. = mv² is dimensionally right but physically wrong (needs ½).
Q10. Are dimensions of work and torque same? Differ how?
Ans: Both [ML²T⁻²], but work is scalar and torque is vector; dimensions cannot tell.
Q11. (Long) Describe systematic, random and gross errors.
Ans: Write definition, causes, remedy of each as in 2A.8.
Q12. (Long) Prove ΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B for Z = A/B.
Ans: Z±ΔZ = (A±ΔA)/(B±ΔB) = (A/B)(1±ΔA/A)(1±ΔB/B)⁻¹ ≈ Z(1±ΔA/A)(1∓ΔB/B); neglect product term → ΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B (max). ∎
