Physics Class 11 – Ch 4: Motion in a Plane

4A Vectors • 4B Motion in a Plane & Relative Velocity

4A.1 Scalar and Vector Quantities

ScalarVector
Only magnitudeMagnitude + direction
Added algebraicallyAdded geometrically (triangle/parallelogram law)
Mass, time, speed, distance, energy, work, temperature, chargeDisplacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, torque, electric field

Note: Current, pressure, area (in some cases) have direction-like properties but obey ordinary addition, so they are treated as scalars (tensors/special).

4A.2 Representation and 4A.3 Definitions

A vector is drawn as a directed line: length = magnitude (to scale), arrowhead = direction. Written as A or A⃗, magnitude |A| = A. Tail = initial point, head = terminal point.

Equal vectors: same magnitude & direction.
Negative vector (−A): same magnitude, opposite direction.
Unit vector: Â = A/|A|, magnitude 1, shows direction only.
Zero (null) vector: magnitude 0, no direction.
Collinear: along same/parallel lines (parallel or antiparallel). Coplanar: in one plane. Co-initial: same starting point.
Position vector r: from origin to the point (x,y,z). Displacement vector: r₂ − r₁.
Free vector: can be shifted parallel to itself. Localised: fixed to a point (force at a point).

4A.4 Addition of Two Vectors – Laws

Triangle law

If two vectors are drawn head-to-tail as two sides of a triangle in same order, the third side in opposite order is the resultant.

Parallelogram law

If two vectors from a point are two adjacent sides of a parallelogram, the diagonal from that point is the resultant.

Polygon law (more than two vectors, 4A.5)

Join vectors head to tail in order; the line from the tail of first to head of last is the resultant. If polygon closes, resultant = 0.

Analytical method (4A.6)

Magnitude: R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)
Direction: tanα = B sinθ / (A + B cosθ) (α with A)
Special cases: θ = 0° → R = A+B (max); θ = 90° → R = √(A²+B²); θ = 180° → R = |A−B| (min); equal magnitudes: R = 2A cos(θ/2).
Condition: |A−B| ≤ R ≤ A+B.

4A.7 Subtraction of Vectors

A − B = A + (−B). Magnitude: |A − B| = √(A² + B² − 2AB cosθ), θ = angle between A and B.

4A.8 Laws of Vector Addition

LawStatement
CommutativeA + B = B + A
Associative(A + B) + C = A + (B + C)
Distributivem(A + B) = mA + mB

Subtraction is not commutative: A − B ≠ B − A (they are negatives).

4A.10 Resolution of a Vector (2D)

Aₓ = A cosθ, A_y = A sinθ; A = Aₓî + A_yĵ
|A| = √(Aₓ² + A_y²); θ = tan⁻¹(A_y/Aₓ).
Resultant of many vectors: Rₓ = ΣAₓ, R_y = ΣA_y → R = √(Rₓ²+R_y²).
Adding: (a₁î+b₁ĵ) + (a₂î+b₂ĵ) = (a₁+a₂)î + (b₁+b₂)ĵ.

4A.11 Resolution (3D)

A = Aₓî + A_yĵ + A_zk̂; |A| = √(Aₓ²+A_y²+A_z²).
Direction cosines: l = cosα = Aₓ/A, m = cosβ = A_y/A, n = cosγ = A_z/A, with l² + m² + n² = 1.
Position vector of (x,y,z): r = xî + yĵ + zk̂.

4A.12 Multiplication by a Scalar

λA has magnitude |λ|A; direction same as A if λ > 0, opposite if λ < 0. Unit changes if λ has unit (e.g. p = mv, F = ma). λ = 0 gives null vector.

4A.13 Dot (Scalar) Product

A · B = AB cosθ (scalar), 0 ≤ θ ≤ 180°.
Commutative A·B = B·A; distributive A·(B+C) = A·B + A·C.
î·î = ĵ·ĵ = k̂·k̂ = 1; î·ĵ = ĵ·k̂ = k̂·î = 0.
A·B = AₓBₓ + A_yB_y + A_zB_z; A·A = A².
Perpendicular ⇔ A·B = 0; parallel: A·B = AB; antiparallel: −AB.
Angle: cosθ = A·B / (AB). Projection of A on B = A·B/B.
Uses: Work W = F·s; Power P = F·v; Flux φ = E·A.

4A.14 Cross (Vector) Product

A × B = AB sinθ n̂ (vector). Direction by right-hand rule (curl fingers from A to B, thumb gives direction) ⊥ plane of A and B.
Not commutative: A × B = − B × A; distributive over addition. A × A = 0; parallel vectors give 0.
î × ĵ = k̂, ĵ × k̂ = î, k̂ × î = ĵ (reverse order gives −).
A × B = | î ĵ k̂ ; Aₓ A_y A_z ; Bₓ B_y B_z | (determinant).
Area of parallelogram = |A×B|; area of triangle = ½|A×B|.
Uses: Torque τ = r × F; angular momentum L = r × p; v = ω × r; force on charge F = q(v × B).

4B.1 Motion in a Plane

Position: r = xî + yĵ. Displacement Δr = r₂ − r₁.
Velocity: v = dr/dt = (dx/dt)î + (dy/dt)ĵ = vₓî + v_yĵ, direction tangent to path; |v| = √(vₓ²+v_y²), tanθ = v_y/vₓ.
Acceleration: a = dv/dt = aₓî + a_yĵ.
Constant acceleration: v = u + at; r = r₀ + ut + ½at²; v² − u² = 2a·s (applied to x and y separately).
Projectile and circular motion are special cases (a = −gĵ; a = centripetal).

4B.2 Relative Velocity in 2D

vAB = vA − vB (vector subtraction). Magnitude = √(v_A² + v_B² − 2v_Av_B cosθ).
Boat–river: v_resultant = v_boat + v_river (vector). Shortest time: head ⊥ to bank. Shortest path: sinθ = v_r/v_b upstream.
Rain–man: v_rain,man = v_rain − v_man. If rain vertical (v_R) and man walks at v_m, apparent rain comes at tanθ = v_m/v_R from vertical (tilt umbrella toward the direction of motion).
Wind–aeroplane: v_ground = v_air + v_wind (vector).

Solved Examples

Easy A = 3î + 4ĵ. Find |A| and unit vector.
|A| = √(9+16) = 5. Â = A/|A| = (3î + 4ĵ)/5 = 0.6î + 0.8ĵ.
Easy A = 2î+3ĵ, B = î−2ĵ. Find A+B, A−B and their magnitudes.
A+B = 3î + ĵ → √10 = 3.16. A−B = î + 5ĵ → √26 = 5.10.
Easy Direction cosines of A = 2î+2ĵ+k̂.
|A| = √(4+4+1) = 3. l=2/3, m=2/3, n=1/3. Check: 4/9+4/9+1/9 = 1 ✓.
Medium Two forces 3 N and 4 N act at 90°. Find resultant and direction.
R = √(9+16+0) = 5 N. tanα = 4 sin90°/(3 + 4cos90°) = 4/3 → α = 53° from the 3 N force.
Medium Two equal forces F at 60°. Find R.
R = 2F cos(60°/2) = 2F cos30° = √3 F (= 1.73F).
Medium Max and min resultant of 5 N and 3 N. Can 2, 3, 7 N give zero?
Max = 5+3 = 8 N; min = 5−3 = 2 N. For 2,3,7: largest 7 > 2+3 = 5 → cannot form closed triangle → no.
Medium Resolve 10 N force at 30° to horizontal.
Fₓ = 10 cos30° = 10(0.866) = 8.66 N. F_y = 10 sin30° = 5 N.
Medium A = î+2ĵ+3k̂, B = 4î−2ĵ+k̂. Find A·B and angle.
A·B = 4 − 4 + 3 = 3. |A|=√14=3.74, |B|=√21=4.58. cosθ = 3/(3.74×4.58) = 3/17.1 = 0.175 → θ ≈ 80°.
Medium Find A × B for A = î+2ĵ+3k̂, B = 2î−ĵ+k̂.
î(2·1 − 3·(−1)) − ĵ(1·1 − 3·2) + k̂(1·(−1) − 2·2) = î(5) − ĵ(−5) + k̂(−5) = 5î + 5ĵ − 5k̂.
Medium Force (3î+4ĵ) N moves body by (2î+ĵ) m. Work? W = F·s = 6 + 4 = 10 J.
Medium r = 3t² î + 2t ĵ. Find v, a and speed at t = 2 s.
v = dr/dt = 6tî + 2ĵ → at 2: 12î + 2ĵ. Speed = √(144+4) = 12.2 m/s. a = dv/dt = 6î m/s².
Medium Boat 4 m/s (east relative to water), river 3 m/s north. Resultant velocity?
v = √(16+9) = 5 m/s, tanφ = 3/4 → 37° north of east.
Hard Derive R = √(A²+B²+2AB cosθ) and tanα.
Let OP = A, OQ = B, θ between, OS = R (diagonal). Draw SN ⊥ OP extended. In △PSN: PN = B cosθ, SN = B sinθ. In △OSN: OS² = ON² + SN² = (A + B cosθ)² + (B sinθ)² = A² + 2AB cosθ + B²(cos²θ+sin²θ) → R² = A²+B²+2AB cosθ ∎. tanα = SN/ON = B sinθ/(A + B cosθ) ∎.
Hard If |A+B| = |A−B|, prove A ⊥ B.
Square: A²+B²+2A·B = A²+B²−2A·B → 4A·B = 0 → A·B = 0 → cosθ = 0 → θ = 90° ∎.
Hard Prove A·(A×B) = 0.
A×B is ⊥ to A, so angle = 90° → A·(A×B) = A|A×B|cos90° = 0 ∎. (Determinant check: Aₓ(A_yB_z−A_zB_y) − A_y(AₓB_z−A_zBₓ) + A_z(AₓB_y−A_yBₓ) = 0.)
Hard Find λ so that A = 2î+λĵ+k̂ ⊥ B = 4î−2ĵ−2k̂.
A·B = 8 − 2λ − 2 = 0 → 6 = 2λ → λ = 3.
Hard Area of parallelogram with sides A = 3î+4ĵ, B = −3î+7ĵ.
A×B = (3·7 − 4·(−3)) k̂ = (21+12)k̂ = 33k̂. Area = 33 sq. units.
Hard Rain falls vertically at 3 km/h; man walks east at 4 km/h. Apparent rain velocity?
v_rm = v_r − v_m: components (−4 east, −3 down). Magnitude = √(16+9) = 5 km/h, tanθ = 4/3 → θ = 53° from vertical, rain appears to come from front (east). Umbrella tilted 53° towards east.
Hard Prove A×B = −B×A and |A×B| = AB sinθ gives area of triangle ½|A×B|.
Right-hand rule: rotating A→B gives thumb up; B→A gives thumb down, same magnitude AB sinθ → A×B = −B×A ∎. Parallelogram area = base × height = A(B sinθ) = |A×B|; triangle is half → ½|A×B| ∎.

Short & Long Q & A

Q1. Why is current not a vector though it has direction?
Ans: It does not obey the vector (parallelogram) law of addition; currents add algebraically.
Q2. Can the resultant of two vectors be smaller than both?
Ans: Yes, e.g. A and B antiparallel: R = |A−B|.
Q3. What is a null vector? Example?
Ans: Vector of zero magnitude; e.g. displacement after a full round trip, A − A.
Q4. Can a vector have zero magnitude but non-zero components?
Ans: No; |A| = √(Aₓ²+A_y²+A_z²) = 0 only if all components are 0.
Q5. Give conditions for A·B = 0 and A×B = 0.
Ans: A·B = 0 when perpendicular (or one is null); A×B = 0 when parallel/antiparallel (or one is null).
Q6. Is A×B = B×A? Why?
Ans: No; cross product is anticommutative since direction reverses.
Q7. Why work is scalar but torque is vector?
Ans: Work = F·s (dot product); torque = r×F (cross product).
Q8. What is the angle between A+B and A−B for |A| = |B|?
Ans: (A+B)·(A−B) = A²−B² = 0 → 90°.
Q9. Why rain appears slanted to a moving man?
Ans: Apparent velocity v_r − v_m has a horizontal component opposite to man’s velocity.
Q10. (Long) State and prove parallelogram law (magnitude and direction).
Ans: See worked example (Hard 1) with figure.
Q11. (Long) Define dot and cross products with properties and uses.
Ans: See 4A.13 and 4A.14, give formulas, unit-vector tables, examples.
Q12. (Long) Explain resolution of vector and give uses.
Ans: See 4A.10; use: inclined plane forces, projectile components.
