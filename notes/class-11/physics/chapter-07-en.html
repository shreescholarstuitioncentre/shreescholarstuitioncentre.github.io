✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 7: System of Particles & Rotational Motion

1. Centre of Mass (COM)

COM is the point where whole mass of a system can be assumed concentrated; it moves as if all external forces act there.

Two particles: xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)/(m₁+m₂)

COM lies on the line joining them, nearer the heavier mass.

2. COM of Many Particles

rcm = Σmᵢrᵢ / M   (x, y, z separately)

For continuous bodies: xcm = (1/M)∫x dm.

3. Motion of COM & Momentum Conservation

M vcm = Σmᵢvᵢ = P  |  Fext = M acm

Internal forces cannot change COM motion. If Fext = 0, P is constant and COM moves with constant velocity (e.g., exploding shell: fragments fly but COM follows the original parabola).

4. COM of Rigid Bodies, Rod; COM vs Centre of Gravity

Uniform rod: at its midpoint (L/2). Ring/disc/sphere: geometric centre. Triangle: centroid (h/3 from base). Semicircular disc: 4R/3π from centre.

COG is the point where total weight acts. COM and COG coincide when g is uniform (small bodies); they differ for very large bodies where g varies.

5. Torque, Rigid Body & Rotational Motion

τ = r × F = r F sinθ   (unit N·m, dimension ML²T⁻²)

Torque is the turning effect of force; direction by right-hand rule. A rigid body keeps fixed distances between particles. Pure rotation: every particle moves in a circle about the axis with same ω.

6. Rotational Equations

ω = ω₀ + αt
θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

Same as linear equations with s→θ, v→ω, a→α. Also v = rω, at = rα.

7. Moment of Inertia (MOI) & Formulae

I = Σmᵢrᵢ²   (unit kg·m², dimension ML²)
Body (axis)I
Ring (centre, ⊥ plane)MR²
Disc (centre, ⊥ plane)½MR²
Rod (centre, ⊥ rod)ML²/12
Rod (end, ⊥ rod)ML²/3
Solid sphere (diameter)2MR²/5
Hollow sphere (diameter)2MR²/3
Solid cylinder (axis)½MR²

8. Parallel & Perpendicular Axis Theorems

Parallel: I = Icm + Md²
Perpendicular (planar bodies): Iz = Ix + Iy

Parallel axis is valid for any body; perpendicular only for flat (lamina) bodies.

9. Torque & MOI

τ = Iα

Rotational analogue of F = ma. MOI is rotational inertia: greater I → harder to change ω.

10. Angular Momentum, Torque & Conservation

L = r × p = Iω  |  τ = dL/dt

If τext = 0, L = Iω = constant. Examples: ice skater pulling arms in spins faster; diver somersault; planet moves faster near Sun.

11. Rotational Kinetic Energy

Krot = ½Iω² = L²/2I
Rolling: K = ½Mv² + ½Iω²

For pure rolling v = Rω.

12. Equilibrium & Couple

Equilibrium: ΣF = 0 (translational) and Στ = 0 (rotational). Couple: two equal, opposite, non-collinear forces: only rotation, no translation.

Moment of couple = F × d

Examples: opening a tap, turning a key, steering wheel.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. COM of 2 kg at x=0 and 3 kg at x=5 m.
Given: m₁=2, x₁=0, m₂=3, x₂=5. Find: xcm.
xcm = (2×0+3×5)/(2+3) = 15/5 = 3 m.
Ex2. F=20 N at 0.5 m from axis, angle 30°. Find τ.
τ = rF sinθ = 0.5×20×0.5 = 5 N·m.
Ex3. MOI of ring M=2 kg, R=0.5 m about its axis.
I = MR² = 2×0.25 = 0.5 kg·m².
Ex4. MOI of disc (M=4 kg, R=0.5 m) about tangent in its plane.
Idiameter = ¼MR² = 0.25. Parallel axis: I = ¼MR² + MR² = 5/4 MR² = 5/4×4×0.25 = 1.25 kg·m².
Ex5. Skater: I=4 kg·m², ω=2 rad/s; pulls arms so I=1. Find ω and KE change.
L conserved: 4×2 = 1×ω → ω = 8 rad/s. KE: ½×4×4=8 J → ½×1×64=32 J (increase 24 J, from skater's work).
Ex6. τ=10 N·m, I=2 kg·m², from rest. Find α and ω at 4 s.
α = τ/I = 5 rad/s²; ω = αt = 20 rad/s.
Ex7. Rod M=3 kg, L=2 m about one end.
I = ML²/3 = 3×4/3 = 4 kg·m².
Ex8. Couple: F=10 N each, separation 0.2 m. Moment?
τ = F×d = 10×0.2 = 2 N·m.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. Define centre of mass.The point where entire mass may be taken as concentrated and where external force effectively acts.
Q2. Can COM lie outside the body?Yes, e.g., ring (centre), hollow sphere, a bent rod.
Q3. Why doesn't a bomb explosion change COM motion?Explosion forces are internal; F_ext=0 so a_cm unchanged.
Q4. Define torque. Give unit and dimensions.τ = r×F; unit N·m; dimension ML²T⁻². It's a vector.
Q5. State and prove parallel axis theorem (outline).I = I_cm + Md². Take particle at x from COM axis; distance from new axis (x+d). Σm(x+d)² = Σmx² + 2dΣmx + Md², and Σmx = 0 about COM → result.
Q6. Why is a spoked wheel's MOI high with mass at rim (flywheel)?I = Σmr²; mass far from axis gives large I, so flywheel resists speed changes and keeps engines smooth.
Q7. Prove τ = dL/dt.L = r×p; dL/dt = (dr/dt×p) + (r×dp/dt) = v×mv + r×F = 0 + τ.
Q8. Why does a diver tuck in mid-air?Tucking reduces I; L conserved so ω increases for more somersaults; extends to slow down before entry.
Q9. Conditions for equilibrium of a rigid body?ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 about any point.
Q10. Why is it easier to open a door by pushing at the handle end?Larger r gives larger torque τ = rF for the same force.
