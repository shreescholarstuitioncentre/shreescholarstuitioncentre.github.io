15A. Waves

1. Wave & Wave Motion

A wave is a disturbance that travels through a medium and transfers energy and momentum without transferring matter. Particles only vibrate about their mean positions; the disturbance moves ahead.

2. Types of Waves

(a) Mechanical (need a medium: sound, string waves) vs Non-mechanical/EM (no medium: light).
(b) Transverse: particles vibrate ⟂ to wave direction → crests & troughs (string, light). Travel in solids and on liquid surfaces.
(c) Longitudinal: particles vibrate ∥ to wave direction → compressions & rarefactions (sound). Travel in solids, liquids, gases.

3. Definitions

Amplitude (A): max displacement. Wavelength (λ): distance between two nearest points in same phase. Time period (T): time for one vibration. Frequency f = 1/T. Angular frequency ω = 2πf. Wave number k = 2π/λ.
Wave speed v = fλ = ω/k = λ/T

4. Progressive Wave Equation

Wave moving in +x: y = A sin(ωt − kx); in −x: y = A sin(ωt + kx).
Particle velocity = ∂y/∂t = Aω cos(ωt−kx), max = Aω.
Particle acceleration = −ω²y, max = Aω².
Wave speed v = ω/k (different from particle velocity!).

5. Phase Difference

Phase difference φ = (2π/λ) × path difference Δx; φ = (2π/T) × time difference Δt.
Δx = λ ⇒ φ = 2π (same phase); Δx = λ/2 ⇒ φ = π (opposite phase).

6. Speed of Sound

Newton's formula: v = √(E/ρ). Solid: √(Y/ρ); liquid: √(B/ρ).
Newton assumed isothermal change ⇒ v = √(P/ρ) = 280 m/s (wrong, actual ≈ 332 m/s).
Laplace's correction: sound changes are rapid ⇒ adiabatic ⇒ v = √(γP/ρ), γ = Cp/Cv = 1.4 for air. Matches experiment.

7. Effect of Pressure, Temperature, Medium

• Pressure: no effect at constant T (P/ρ constant).
• Temperature: v ∝ √T (kelvin); vt ≈ v₀ + 0.61t (m/s, t in °C), v₀ = 332 m/s.
• Humidity: moist air is lighter ⇒ speed increases.
• Gas: v ∝ 1/√M (H₂ is fastest). • Wind adds to speed.

8. Sound Speed & RMS Speed

vrms = √(3P/ρ); v = √(γP/ρ) ⇒ v = √(γ/3) · vrms, so v < vrms.

15B. Superposition of Waves

1. Principle of Superposition

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement = algebraic sum: y = y₁ + y₂ + … Constructive: Imax = (A₁+A₂)²; destructive: Imin = (A₁−A₂)².

2. Beats

Two waves of slightly different frequencies f₁, f₂ produce periodic waxing and waning of loudness. Beat frequency = |f₁ − f₂| (must be ≤ ~10 per second to be heard). Resultant: y = 2A cos(π(f₁−f₂)t) sin(π(f₁+f₂)t).
Uses: tuning musical instruments, finding unknown frequency (loading a fork with wax lowers its f; filing raises it), detecting poisonous gas in mines.

3. Reflection of Waves

Fixed end (denser): phase change π, a crest returns as a trough; node forms. Free end (rarer): no phase change; antinode forms.

4. Stationary (Standing) Waves

y₁ = A sin(ωt − kx), y₂ = A sin(ωt + kx) ⇒ y = 2A cos(kx) sin(ωt).
Nodes at x = λ/2·n; antinodes at x = (2n+1)λ/4. Node-to-node distance = λ/2. Energy is not transported; all particles between two nodes are in same phase; amplitude varies from 0 to 2A.

Progressive vs standing: progressive – energy flows, all amplitudes same, phase changes point to point; standing – no energy flow, amplitude varies, phase same within a loop.

5. Organ Pipes

Closed pipe (one end closed): node at closed end, antinode at open end. f = (2n+1)v/4L ⇒ f₁ = v/4L, only odd harmonics (f₁, 3f₁, 5f₁ …).
Open pipe: antinodes at both ends. f = nv/2L ⇒ f₁ = v/2L, all harmonics (f₁, 2f₁, 3f₁ …). Open pipe sound is richer.
End correction: antinode forms slightly outside; e = 0.6r. Closed: L+e; open: L+2e.
Resonance tube: first resonance L₁+e = λ/4, second L₂+e = 3λ/4 ⇒ v = 2f(L₂ − L₁), e = (L₂ − 3L₁)/2.

15C. Vibrations of Stretched Strings

Speed of transverse wave: v = √(T/μ) (T tension, μ mass per unit length).
Fixed both ends: fn = (n/2L)√(T/μ), n = 1,2,3… (all harmonics).
Laws: (1) f ∝ 1/L; (2) f ∝ √T; (3) f ∝ 1/√μ (or 1/r, 1/d).
Sonometer: wooden box, wire, bridges, weights; used to verify the laws and find an unknown frequency (resonance shown by paper rider falling off). f = (1/2L)√(T/μ).

15D. Doppler Effect

Apparent change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.

f′ = f (v ± v₀)/(v ∓ vs)
Observer moves toward source: +v₀ (numerator). Source moves toward observer: −vs (denominator). Opposite motion reverses signs. Medium (wind) velocity adds to v.

In light: Δλ/λ = v/c (only the relative velocity along the line of sight). Source receding ⇒ red shift; approaching ⇒ blue shift.
Applications: radar speed guns, SONAR, medical Doppler ultrasound (blood flow), expansion of the universe, binary stars, rotation of the Sun/planets.

Solved Examples (Given – To find – Solution)

EasyEx 1. f = 500 Hz, λ = 0.68 m. Find v.
Solution: v = fλ = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s.
EasyEx 2. λ = 1 m. Phase difference between two points 0.25 m apart?
Solution: φ = (2π/λ)Δx = 2π × 0.25 = π/2 rad.
EasyEx 3. Fork A = 256 Hz, B = 260 Hz. Beats?
Solution: |260 − 256| = 4 beats/s.
MediumEx 4. v at 0 °C = 332 m/s. Find v at 27 °C.
Solution: T₁ = 273 K, T₂ = 300 K; v₂ = 332√(300/273) = 332 × 1.048 ≈ 348 m/s.
MediumEx 5. Air: γ = 1.4, P = 1.013×10⁵ Pa, ρ = 1.29 kg/m³. Find v.
Solution: v = √(1.4 × 1.013×10⁵ / 1.29) = √(1.099×10⁵) ≈ 331.6 m/s.
MediumEx 6. Closed pipe L = 0.85 m, v = 340 m/s. Find f₁ and next two harmonics.
Solution: f₁ = v/4L = 340/3.4 = 100 Hz; only odd: 300 Hz, 500 Hz.
MediumEx 7. Wire μ = 0.01 kg/m, T = 100 N, L = 0.5 m. Find v and f₁.
Solution: v = √(100/0.01) = 100 m/s; f₁ = v/2L = 100/1 = 100 Hz.
MediumEx 8. Source 400 Hz moves toward a stationary observer at 34 m/s (v = 340). Find f′.
Solution: f′ = 400 × 340/(340−34) = 400 × 340/306 ≈ 444.4 Hz.
HardEx 9. y = 0.05 sin(100πt − 0.5πx) (SI). Find A, f, λ, v, max particle speed.
Solution: Compare with A sin(ωt − kx): A = 0.05 m; ω = 100π ⇒ f = 50 Hz; k = 0.5π ⇒ λ = 2π/k = 4 m; v = fλ = 200 m/s; vp,max = Aω = 0.05 × 100π = 5π ≈ 15.7 m/s.
HardEx 10. Resonance tube: L₁ = 20 cm, L₂ = 64 cm, f = 384 Hz. Find v and end correction.
Solution: λ/2 = L₂−L₁ = 44 cm ⇒ λ = 0.88 m; v = 384 × 0.88 ≈ 338 m/s; e = (L₂−3L₁)/2 = (64−60)/2 = 2 cm.

Short & Long Q&A

Q1. Why can't sound travel in a vacuum?Sound is a mechanical wave and needs particles of a medium for compressions and rarefactions.
Q2. What was Laplace's correction?Newton used isothermal (v = √(P/ρ) = 280 m/s). Laplace argued the compressions are too rapid for heat exchange, so the process is adiabatic: v = √(γP/ρ) ≈ 332 m/s, in agreement with experiment.
Q3. Why does pressure not affect sound speed in a gas?For fixed T, P/ρ is constant (Boyle's law). If P doubles, ρ doubles, so v is unchanged.
Q4. Distinguish between beats and stationary waves.Beats: two waves of slightly different frequencies in the same direction, causing intensity variation in time. Stationary waves: two identical waves in opposite directions, causing intensity variation in space (nodes and antinodes).
Q5. Why are only odd harmonics in a closed pipe?The closed end must be a node and the open end an antinode. This requires L = (2n+1)λ/4, so only odd multiples of f₁ are possible.
Q6. Derive the beat frequency.y₁ = A sin2πf₁t, y₂ = A sin2πf₂t. y = 2A cos[π(f₁−f₂)t] sin[π(f₁+f₂)t]. The amplitude 2A|cos π(f₁−f₂)t| reaches a maximum twice per cycle of cosine, so beats per second = f₁ − f₂.
Q7. State and explain the laws of vibration of a string.From f = (1/2L)√(T/μ): law of length (f ∝ 1/L, T and μ constant), law of tension (f ∝ √T), law of mass (f ∝ 1/√μ). Verified using a sonometer.
Q8. Explain the Doppler effect with an example.The siren of an approaching ambulance sounds higher in pitch because more wavefronts reach you per second; once it passes, the pitch drops. Formula: f′ = f(v ± v₀)/(v ∓ vs).
