✎ Work, Energy & Power — Class 11 Physics (Ch 6)

1. Work

Work is done when a force produces displacement in its own direction (component). Work is a scalar.

W = F·s = F s cosθ

Constant force: only the component F cosθ along displacement does work. The component F sinθ (perpendicular) does zero work.

2. Nature of Work

θWorkExample
0° ≤ θ < 90°PositivePushing a trolley
θ = 90°ZeroCentripetal force, carrying a bag horizontally
90° < θ ≤ 180°NegativeFriction, braking

3. Variable Force

W = ∫ F dx = area under F–x graph

Spring: F = −kx, work done by us in stretching = ½kx².

4. Units & Dimensions

SI unit joule (1 J = 1 N·m); CGS erg (1 J = 10⁷ erg); 1 kWh = 3.6×10⁶ J; 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J. Dimension: [ML²T⁻²].

5. Conservative & Non-conservative Forces

Conservative: work depends only on end points, zero over a closed path (gravity, spring, electrostatic). Non-conservative: work depends on path (friction, air drag). Conservative force: F = −dU/dx.

6. Kinetic Energy & Momentum

KE = ½mv² = p²/2m  |  p = √(2m·KE)

Derivation: v² = u² + 2as, multiply by m/2 → ½mv² − ½mu² = F·s.

7. Work–Energy Theorem

W(net) = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu²

Valid for all forces (conservative or not) and any path.

8. Potential Energy & its Forms

Gravitational U = mgh  |  Elastic U = ½kx²

Other forms: electrical, chemical, nuclear. PE is defined only for conservative forces.

9. Conservation & Transformation of Energy

For conservative forces KE + PE = constant. Free fall from h: at ground v = √(2gh). Energy changes form (dynamo: mechanical→electrical; bulb: electrical→light+heat) but total is conserved.

10. Power

P = W/t = F·v  |  1 hp = 746 W

11. Collisions

Momentum is conserved always. Coefficient of restitution e = (speed of separation)/(speed of approach): elastic e=1, inelastic 0<e<1, perfectly inelastic e=0.

Elastic 1D: v₁ = [(m₁−m₂)u₁+2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂)
v₂ = [(m₂−m₁)u₂+2m₁u₁]/(m₁+m₂)

Special cases: equal masses → velocities exchange; heavy hits light at rest → heavy continues, light moves ≈2u.

Perfectly inelastic: v = (m₁u₁+m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂)
Loss of KE = ½·[m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂)]·(u₁−u₂)²

2D elastic: conserve momentum along x and y separately plus KE. For equal masses (target at rest) the two bodies move at 90° after collision.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. F=10 N, s=5 m, θ=60°. Find W.
W = F s cosθ = 10×5×½ = 25 J.
Ex2. Car 1000 kg at 20 m/s stops in 50 m. Find retarding force.
W = ΔKE → F×50 = ½×1000×400 → F = 4000 N.
Ex3. Spring k=200 N/m stretched 0.1 m. Find U.
U = ½kx² = ½×200×0.01 = 1 J.
Ex4. 60 kg man climbs 10 m in 20 s (g=10). Power?
P = mgh/t = 60×10×10/20 = 300 W.
Ex5. Momentum rises 50%. % rise in KE?
KE ∝ p² → (1.5)² = 2.25 → 125% increase.
Ex6. 2 kg at 4 m/s hits identical 2 kg at rest, they stick. Find v and KE lost.
v = (2×4)/4 = 2 m/s; KE: 16 J → 4 J; loss = 12 J.

✎ Q & A (tap to open)

Q1. Define work. Is it scalar?Work = dot product of force and displacement, W = F·s. It is a scalar.
Q2. Why does centripetal force do no work?It acts perpendicular to velocity (θ=90°, cos90°=0), so W=0 and speed stays constant.
Q3. State the work–energy theorem and prove it.Net work equals change in KE. Proof: v²−u²=2as; multiply by m/2: ½mv²−½mu²=(ma)s=F·s=W.
Q4. Why can KE never be negative but work can?KE=½mv² with v² ≥ 0. Work depends on cosθ, which is negative for θ>90°.
Q5. Difference between conservative and non-conservative forces?Conservative: path-independent, zero work in closed loop, PE exists. Non-conservative: path-dependent, energy dissipated (friction).
Q6. Show KE = p²/2m.p=mv → v=p/m → ½m(p/m)² = p²/2m.
Q7. Derive v=√(2gh) using energy conservation.At top: mgh + 0. At ground: 0 + ½mv². Equate → v=√(2gh).
Q8. Why is momentum conserved but KE not in inelastic collision?No external force, so momentum conserved. Some KE becomes heat, sound, deformation.
Q9. Show that in 1D elastic collision relative velocity reverses.From momentum and KE equations: u₁−u₂ = −(v₁−v₂), hence e=1.
Q10. Why is it easier to pull a lawn roller than push it?Pulling lifts it (reduces normal reaction and friction); pushing adds F sinθ downward and increases friction.
