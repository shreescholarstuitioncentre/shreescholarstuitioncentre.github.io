✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 13: Kinetic Theory of Gases

1. Molecular Nature of Matter & Avogadro Number

Matter consists of molecules in constant motion. Avogadro number NA = 6.022×10²³ per mole. Avogadro's law: equal volumes of all gases at same T and P contain equal number of molecules; 1 mole occupies 22.4 L at STP.

2. Behaviour of Gases & Gas Laws

Boyle: PV = const (T const)   Charles: V/T = const (P const)
Gay-Lussac: P/T = const (V const)
PV = nRT = NkT   (k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K, R = NAk)

Real gases behave ideally at low pressure and high temperature.

3. Kinetic Theory: Assumptions

1) Gas has a very large number of identical, tiny molecules. 2) Molecular volume is negligible compared to container. 3) No intermolecular forces except during collisions. 4) Motion is random and continuous. 5) Collisions are perfectly elastic and of negligible duration. 6) Molecules obey Newton's laws; gravity effect is ignored.

4. Pressure of a Gas & RMS Speed

P = ⅓ (mN/V) v²rms = ⅓ ρ v²rms = ⅔ (E/V)
vrms = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M) = √(3P/ρ)

Pressure arises from momentum change of molecules hitting the wall. vrms ∝ √T and ∝ 1/√M.

5. Kinetic Meaning of Temperature & Thermal Equilibrium

Average KE per molecule = ½mv²rms = (3/2) kT

Temperature measures average translational KE and is independent of the gas. Two gases in thermal equilibrium have equal average KE per molecule. At T = 0 K molecular motion (classically) stops. Dalton's law and Graham's law of diffusion (rate ∝ 1/√ρ) also follow.

6. Maxwell Speed Distribution

vp = √(2kT/m) < vavg = √(8kT/πm) < vrms = √(3kT/m)
vp : vavg : vrms = 1 : 1.128 : 1.224

Molecules have a range of speeds. On heating, the peak shifts to higher speed and the curve flattens. The area under the curve (total molecules) stays same.

7. Degrees of Freedom & Equipartition of Energy

Degrees of freedom f: monatomic 3; diatomic 5 (3 translational + 2 rotational; 7 with vibration at high T); linear triatomic 7; non-linear triatomic 6.

Each degree of freedom carries ½kT per molecule (½RT per mole)

8. Internal Energy & Specific Heats

U = (f/2) nRT   Cv = (f/2)R   Cp = (f/2 + 1)R   γ = 1 + 2/f

Monatomic: Cv = 3R/2, γ = 5/3. Diatomic: Cv = 5R/2, γ = 7/5.

9. Mean Free Path

λ = 1/(√2 π d² n) = kT/(√2 π d² P)

Average distance between two successive collisions. λ increases with temperature, decreases with pressure and molecular size. For air at STP, λ ≈ 10⁻⁷ m.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. vrms of O₂ at 300 K (M = 32×10⁻³ kg/mol).
v = √(3RT/M) = √(3×8.31×300/0.032) = √233719 ≈ 483 m/s.
Ex2. Ratio vrms(H₂)/vrms(O₂) at same T.
√(MO₂/MH₂) = √(32/2) = 4.
Ex3. At what temperature is vrms double that at 27°C?
v ∝ √T → T = 4×300 = 1200 K = 927°C.
Ex4. Average KE per molecule at 300 K.
(3/2)kT = 1.5×1.38×10⁻²³×300 = 6.21×10⁻²¹ J.
Ex5. Internal energy of 2 mol diatomic gas at 300 K (f = 5).
U = (5/2)nRT = 2.5×2×8.31×300 ≈ 12465 J.
Ex6. γ and Cv for gas with f = 5.
γ = 1 + 2/5 = 1.4; Cv = 5R/2 = 2.5×8.31 ≈ 20.8 J/mol·K.
Ex7. Air ρ = 1.29 kg/m³, vrms = 500 m/s. Pressure?
P = ⅓ρv² = ⅓×1.29×250000 ≈ 1.08×10⁵ Pa.
Ex8. Mean free path: n = 2.7×10²⁵ m⁻³, d = 3×10⁻¹⁰ m.
λ = 1/(√2π d² n) = 1/(1.414×3.14×9×10⁻²⁰×2.7×10²⁵) ≈ 9.3×10⁻⁸ m.
Ex9. Number of molecules in 1 g of H₂ (M = 2 g/mol).
moles = 1/2 = 0.5; N = 0.5×6.02×10²³ = 3.01×10²³.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. State assumptions of kinetic theory.Tiny point molecules, negligible volume, no forces except elastic collisions, random motion, large number.
Q2. Derive P = ⅓ρv²_rms (outline).Momentum change per collision 2mvₓ; collisions per time vₓ/2L; sum over N molecules; P = F/L² = (mN/3V)v², with vₓ² = v²/3.
Q3. Show temperature is a measure of average KE.From P = ⅔E/V and PV = NkT, E/N = (3/2)kT per molecule.
Q4. Why does v_rms of H₂ exceed that of O₂?v_rms ∝ 1/√M and H₂ is lighter.
Q5. Why does the Moon lack atmosphere (kinetic view)?Molecule speeds at its temperature approach escape velocity 2.4 km/s, so gases escape.
Q6. Explain Maxwell distribution.Fraction of molecules with each speed; asymmetric curve with v_p < v_avg < v_rms; peak moves right on heating.
Q7. State law of equipartition of energy.For equilibrium each degree of freedom has ½kT average energy per molecule.
Q8. Find γ for monatomic and diatomic gases from f.γ = 1 + 2/f: 1 + 2/3 = 5/3; 1 + 2/5 = 7/5.
Q9. Why does mean free path increase on heating at constant pressure?n = P/kT falls with T, and λ ∝ 1/n.
Q10. Why do real gases deviate from ideal behaviour?Molecular volume and intermolecular forces are not negligible at high P and low T.
