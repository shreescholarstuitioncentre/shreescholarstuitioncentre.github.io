✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 14: Oscillations

A1. Periodic Motion, Time Period, Frequency

Motion that repeats at regular intervals is periodic; to-and-fro motion about a mean position is oscillatory (all oscillations are periodic, not vice versa).

f = 1/T  |  ω = 2πf = 2π/T (rad/s)

A2. Simple Harmonic Motion: Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration

x = A sin(ωt + φ)   F = −kx   a = −ω²x   ω = √(k/m)
v = ω√(A² − x²): vmax = Aω (mean), 0 (extreme)
amax = ω²A (extreme), 0 (mean)

A is amplitude, (ωt + φ) is phase, φ is initial phase. Sine/cosine are the periodic functions of SHM.

A3. SHM as Projection of Uniform Circular Motion & Graphs

The projection of a particle moving uniformly on a circle (radius A) onto a diameter executes SHM of amplitude A. Graphs: x–t is sinusoidal; v–t leads x by π/2; a–t is opposite to x (phase π).

A4. Energy in SHM

KE = ½mω²(A² − x²)   PE = ½mω²x² = ½kx²
E = ½mω²A² = ½kA² = constant

KE and PE interchange; both vary with frequency 2f; average KE = average PE = E/2; at x = ±A/√2, KE = PE. PE–x graph is a parabola.

B1. Spring–Mass Systems

T = 2π√(m/k) (horizontal or vertical; vertical: T = 2π√(e/g))
Parallel: k = k₁ + k₂   Series: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂

Mass on two springs between walls (both sides) behaves as parallel: k = k₁ + k₂. A spring cut into n equal parts has each part of constant nk. Period is independent of g if k and m are fixed.

B2. Simple Pendulum & Infinite-Length Pendulum

T = 2π√(L/g)  |  seconds pendulum: T = 2 s, L ≈ 0.99 m
Infinite length: T = 2π√(R/g) ≈ 84.6 min

Valid for small angles (θ < 15°). T is independent of mass and amplitude. In accelerating lift geff = g ± a; in free fall T → ∞; T ∝ √L so a clock runs slow if L increases (summer).

C1. Free, Damped & Forced Vibrations

Free: vibrate at natural frequency with constant amplitude (ideal). Damped: amplitude decays exponentially (A = A₀e−bt/2m) due to friction/air drag. Forced: body vibrates at frequency of an external periodic force.

C2. Resonance: Uses & Sharpness

Resonance: when driving frequency equals natural frequency, amplitude is maximum. Uses: tuning radio, musical instruments, MRI, pushing a swing. Harmful: bridge collapse (Tacoma Narrows), soldiers break step on bridges. Sharp resonance: small damping, tall narrow peak. Flat resonance: large damping, low broad peak.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. x = 5 sin(2πt) cm. Find A, T, f.
A = 5 cm; ω = 2π → T = 2π/ω = 1 s; f = 1 Hz.
Ex2. m = 0.5 kg on spring k = 200 N/m. Find T.
T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.5/200) = 2π×0.05 ≈ 0.314 s.
Ex3. A = 0.1 m, T = 2 s. Find vmax, amax.
ω = 2π/T = π; vmax = Aω = 0.314 m/s; amax = ω²A = 9.87×0.1 ≈ 0.987 m/s².
Ex4. A = 0.1 m, ω = 5 rad/s. Speed at x = 0.06 m?
v = ω√(A² − x²) = 5√(0.01 − 0.0036) = 5×0.08 = 0.4 m/s.
Ex5. m = 0.2 kg, ω = 10 rad/s, A = 0.05 m. Total energy?
E = ½mω²A² = 0.5×0.2×100×0.0025 = 0.025 J.
Ex6. Two springs k₁ = k₂ = 100 N/m. Find k in series and parallel.
Series: 1/k = 1/100 + 1/100 → k = 50 N/m. Parallel: k = 200 N/m.
Ex7. Simple pendulum L = 1 m, g = 9.8. Find T.
T = 2π√(1/9.8) = 2π×0.3194 ≈ 2.007 s.
Ex8. Length of seconds pendulum (T = 2 s).
L = gT²/4π² = 9.8×4/39.48 ≈ 0.993 m.
Ex9. Pendulum in lift accelerating up at g/3. Find T′/T.
geff = g + g/3 = 4g/3; T′/T = √(g/geff) = √(3/4) ≈ 0.866.
Ex10. Infinite-length pendulum, R = 6.4×10⁶ m. Find T.
T = 2π√(R/g) = 2π√(6.4×10⁶/9.8) ≈ 5077 s ≈ 84.6 min.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. Define SHM. Give the condition.Motion where restoring force/acceleration is proportional to displacement and opposite to it: F = −kx.
Q2. Is every periodic motion SHM?No; a periodic motion is SHM only if its acceleration is ∝ −x (e.g., circular motion is periodic but not SHM).
Q3. Show total energy in SHM is constant.KE + PE = ½mω²(A² − x²) + ½mω²x² = ½mω²A².
Q4. Derive T = 2π√(L/g) for pendulum.Restoring force −mg sinθ ≈ −mg(x/L); so a = −(g/L)x, ω² = g/L, T = 2π√(L/g).
Q5. Why does a pendulum clock run slow in summer?Length increases, T ∝ √L increases.
Q6. What happens to T of a pendulum on the Moon?g_moon = g/6, so T increases by √6 ≈ 2.45 times.
Q7. What is resonance? Give one use and one harm.Amplitude maximum when driving frequency = natural frequency; radio tuning (use), bridge collapse (harm).
Q8. Why do soldiers break step on a bridge?Their rhythmic steps may match the bridge's natural frequency and cause resonance.
Q9. Difference between sharp and flat resonance?Sharp: low damping, tall narrow peak. Flat: high damping, low broad peak.
Q10. At what displacement are KE and PE equal?½k(A² − x²) = ½kx² → x = ±A/√2.
