✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 12: Thermodynamics

A1. System, Variables, Equation of State, Zeroth Law

A thermodynamic system is the part under study; the rest is surroundings. State variables: P, V, T, n. Equation of state: PV = nRT (ideal gas). Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium when at same temperature.

Zeroth law: if A~C and B~C are in thermal equilibrium, then A~B are also. (It defines temperature.)

A2. Work, Heat & Internal Energy

W = ∫P dV (area under P–V curve)

Heat Q is energy in transit due to temperature difference. Internal energy U is the total molecular KE + PE; it is a state function, and for an ideal gas depends only on T. Sign: Q positive if absorbed; W positive if done by the gas.

A3. First Law of Thermodynamics & Applications

ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW

It is the law of conservation of energy. Applications: melting (ΔU = mL − PΔV), boiling, free expansion (ΔU = 0), cyclic process (ΔU = 0, so Q = W).

A4. Specific Heats of Gas & Mayer's Formula

Cp − Cv = R  |  γ = Cp/Cv

Monatomic: Cv = 3R/2, γ = 5/3. Diatomic: Cv = 5R/2, γ = 7/5. Cp > Cv since at constant pressure some heat does expansion work.

B1. Isothermal, Isobaric, Isochoric Processes

Isothermal (T const): PV = const, ΔU = 0, W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) = 2.303 nRT log(V₂/V₁) = Q
Isobaric (P const): W = PΔV, Q = nCpΔT
Isochoric (V const): W = 0, Q = ΔU = nCvΔT

Isothermal needs slow process and conducting walls.

B2. Adiabatic Process, Work & Curves

PVγ = const   TVγ−1 = const
Q = 0, W = (P₁V₁ − P₂V₂)/(γ − 1) = nR(T₁ − T₂)/(γ − 1)

Needs quick process and insulated walls. Slope of adiabat = −γP/V, of isotherm = −P/V, so adiabatic curve is steeper (γ times).

C1. Reversible & Irreversible, Heat Engine, Carnot Engine

Reversible: can be retraced exactly (ideal, quasi-static, no friction). Irreversible: most real processes (friction, free expansion, diffusion).

Heat engine: η = W/Q₁ = 1 − Q₂/Q₁
Carnot: η = 1 − T₂/T₁ (T in kelvin)

Carnot cycle: isothermal expansion, adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression, adiabatic compression.

C2. Refrigerator, Second Law & Carnot's Theorem

COP = Q₂/W = T₂/(T₁ − T₂)

Limitation of first law: it does not say in which direction heat flows or whether a process is possible. Kelvin–Planck: no engine can convert all heat to work (η < 1). Clausius: heat cannot flow from cold to hot by itself without external work. Carnot's theorem: no engine between two temperatures is more efficient than a reversible one; all reversible engines have equal efficiency.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. System absorbs 500 J and does 200 J work. Find ΔU.
ΔU = Q − W = 500 − 200 = 300 J.
Ex2. Monatomic gas: find Cp and γ.
Cv = 3R/2; Cp = Cv + R = 5R/2; γ = 5/3 ≈ 1.67.
Ex3. 1 mol gas at 300 K expands isothermally to double volume. Work?
W = RT ln2 = 8.31×300×0.693 ≈ 1728 J.
Ex4. 1 mol monatomic gas expands adiabatically from 400 K to 300 K. Work?
W = R(T₁−T₂)/(γ−1) = 8.31×100/(2/3) ≈ 1246 J.
Ex5. Isobaric P = 10⁵ Pa, V from 1 L to 3 L. Work?
W = PΔV = 10⁵×2×10⁻³ = 200 J.
Ex6. Monatomic gas at 300 K compressed adiabatically to 1/8 volume. Final T?
T₂ = T₁(V₁/V₂)γ−1 = 300×82/3 = 300×4 = 1200 K.
Ex7. Carnot engine T₁ = 500 K, T₂ = 300 K. Efficiency?
η = 1 − 300/500 = 0.4 = 40%.
Ex8. Engine takes Q₁ = 1000 J, η = 40%. Find W and Q₂.
W = ηQ₁ = 400 J; Q₂ = Q₁ − W = 600 J.
Ex9. Refrigerator between 300 K and 270 K. COP?
COP = T₂/(T₁−T₂) = 270/30 = 9.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. State the zeroth law. Why is it important?If A and B are each in equilibrium with C, they are in equilibrium with each other; it justifies temperature as a property.
Q2. State the first law. Is it a conservation law?ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW; yes, it is conservation of energy including heat.
Q3. Why is C_p greater than C_v?At constant pressure, heat also supplies work of expansion.
Q4. Derive Mayer's formula.Q_p = nC_pΔT = ΔU + PΔV = nC_vΔT + nRΔT, so C_p − C_v = R.
Q5. Why is ΔU = 0 in isothermal process for ideal gas?U depends only on T for an ideal gas; T is constant.
Q6. Why is adiabatic curve steeper than isothermal?Slope −γP/V is γ times larger than −P/V since γ > 1.
Q7. Why does a gas cool on sudden expansion?No heat enters (Q = 0), so work is done at the cost of internal energy, T falls.
Q8. Can efficiency of heat engine be 100%?No; Kelvin–Planck forbids it; also would need T₂ = 0 K.
Q9. How can Carnot efficiency be increased?Raise source temperature T₁ or lower sink temperature T₂.
Q10. Why can a refrigerator not cool a room with door open?It dumps more heat into room than it removes (Q₁ = Q₂ + W), warming the room.
