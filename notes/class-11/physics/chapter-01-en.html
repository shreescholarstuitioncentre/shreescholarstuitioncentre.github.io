1.1 What is Physics?

Physics comes from Greek “Physis” = nature. It is the study of nature and natural phenomena: matter, energy, and their interactions.

Two main approaches:
① Unification – explaining different phenomena with few laws (e.g. Newton’s gravity explains apple falling + moon’s orbit).
② Reductionism – explaining a complex system via its simple parts (e.g. thermal properties of gas from molecular motion).

Scientific method: Observation → Hypothesis → Experiment → Analysis → Theory/Law → Prediction.

1.5 Basic Forces in Nature

(Bars animate: strength grows from zero; masses m₁, m₂ move toward each other.)

① Gravitational force

Between any two masses. F = Gm₁m₂/r², G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg². Always attractive, long range (∞), weakest, acts through gravitons (proposed). Controls planets, tides, falling bodies.

② Electromagnetic force

Between charged particles. F = kq₁q₂/r². Attractive or repulsive, long range, ~10³⁶ times gravity. Carrier: photon. Causes friction, tension, chemical bonds.

③ Strong nuclear force

Binds protons & neutrons in nucleus. Strongest, short range (~10⁻¹⁵ m), charge-independent, carrier: gluon (π-mesons for nucleons).

④ Weak nuclear force

Responsible for β-decay (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄). Range ~10⁻¹⁶ m, carriers W⁺, W⁻, Z⁰ bosons.

1.6 Nature of Physical Laws

Laws of physics are universal and hold for all times. Conservation laws: energy, linear momentum, angular momentum, charge.
Symmetry: conservation of momentum ↔ homogeneity of space; energy ↔ homogeneity of time; angular momentum ↔ isotropy of space.
Conservation of mass-energy: E = mc². Laws are tested by experiment; a theory is valid until contradicted.

Worked Examples

Easy Find gravitational force between two 1 kg masses 1 m apart.
Given: m₁=m₂=1 kg, r=1 m, G=6.67×10⁻¹¹.
Formula: F=Gm₁m₂/r²
Solution: F=6.67×10⁻¹¹×1×1/1² = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N (tiny – that is why we do not feel it).
Medium Compare electric and gravitational force between two protons.
Given: e=1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, mₚ=1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg, k=9×10⁹, G=6.67×10⁻¹¹. (r cancels.)
Step 1: Fₑ/F_g = ke²/(Gm²)
Step 2: Numerator = 9×10⁹×2.56×10⁻³⁸ = 2.3×10⁻²⁸
Step 3: Denominator = 6.67×10⁻¹¹×2.79×10⁻⁵⁴ = 1.86×10⁻⁶⁴
Step 4: Ratio ≈ 1.2×10³⁶ → EM force is vastly stronger.
Hard If the distance between two masses is tripled and one mass doubled, how does F change?
Solution: F′ = G(2m₁)m₂/(3r)² = (2/9)·F. So force becomes 2/9 times (decreases).

Short & Long Q & A

Q1. Define physics.
Ans: Study of nature and natural phenomena – matter, energy and their interactions.
Q2. What is unification?
Ans: Explaining diverse phenomena by a few basic laws, e.g. Newton’s gravitation for falling apple and planetary motion.
Q3. What is reductionism?
Ans: Deriving properties of a complex system from its simpler constituents, e.g. gas temperature from molecular kinetic energy.
Q4. Why is gravity called weakest yet important?
Ans: Only 10⁻³⁹ of strong force, but always attractive and additive over huge masses, so it rules planets and stars.
Q5. Which force holds nucleus together?
Ans: Strong nuclear force; it overcomes proton–proton repulsion at ~10⁻¹⁵ m.
Q6. Which force causes β-decay?
Ans: Weak nuclear force.
Q7. Which is the first unification in electricity & magnetism?
Ans: Maxwell’s electromagnetic theory (after Oersted, Faraday).
Q8. State two conservation laws.
Ans: Conservation of energy and of linear momentum (also angular momentum, charge).
Q9. Give three examples of physics in technology.
Ans: Optical fibre (total internal reflection), rocket (Newton’s 3rd law), nuclear reactor (fission).
Q10. (Long) Explain the four fundamental forces.
Ans: See section 1.5 – write definition, formula, range, relative strength, carrier and example of each, plus the table.
