✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 8: Gravitation

1. Kepler's Laws

1) Orbits: every planet moves in an ellipse with Sun at one focus.
2) Areas: line joining Sun and planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, so angular momentum is conserved.
3) Periods: T² ∝ a³ (a = semi-major axis).

dA/dt = L/2m = constant  |  T² = (4π²/GM)·a³

2. Universal Law of Gravitation & G

F = G m₁m₂ / r²

G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², dimension M⁻¹L³T⁻² (measured by Cavendish). The force is attractive, central, conservative, and forms an action-reaction pair.

3. Gravity, Mass & Density of Earth

g = GM/R²  |  M = gR²/G ≈ 6×10²⁴ kg
ρ = 3g/(4πGR) ≈ 5.5×10³ kg/m³

g = 9.8 m/s² at surface, independent of the falling body's mass.

4. Variation of g

Height h: g' = g(R/(R+h))² ≈ g(1 − 2h/R)
Depth d: g' = g(1 − d/R)
Latitude λ: g' = g − Rω²cos²λ

g is maximum at poles, minimum at equator, zero at Earth's centre.

5. Gravitational Field, Potential & Potential Energy

Field I = GM/r² (N/kg)
Potential V = −GM/r; on Earth's surface V = −gR
PE U = −GMm/r

For small height h, ΔU = mgh. Zero of potential is at infinity, so bound energies are negative.

6. Maximum Height of Projectile

h = v²R / (2gR − v²)

From energy conservation: ½mv² − GMm/R = −GMm/(R+h). For small v, h = v²/2g.

7. Escape Velocity & No Atmosphere on Moon

ve = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR) = 11.2 km/s (Earth)
Escape energy = GMm/R

Independent of mass and direction of projection. Moon: ve = 2.4 km/s, comparable to gas molecule speeds, so gases escape and there is no atmosphere.

8. Satellites: Orbital Speed & Time Period

v₀ = √(GM/(R+h)) ≈ √(gR) = 7.9 km/s
T = 2π√((R+h)³/GM) ≈ 84 min (near surface)

Higher orbit means lower speed and longer period. Inside a satellite apparent weight is zero (free fall).

9. Geostationary Satellite

Period 24 h, equatorial orbit, west to east, height ≈ 36000 km, speed ≈ 3.1 km/s. It appears fixed over one point, used for communication.

10. Satellite Energy, Binding Energy & v₀ vs ve

KE = GMm/2r   PE = −GMm/r   E = −GMm/2r
Binding energy = GMm/2r  |  ve = √2·v₀

KE = −E and PE = 2E. Negative total energy means the satellite is bound.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. Force between two 1 kg masses 1 m apart.
F = 6.67×10⁻¹¹×1×1/1² = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N.
Ex2. g at height h=R.
g' = g(R/2R)² = g/4 = 2.45 m/s².
Ex3. g at depth R/2.
g' = g(1 − 1/2) = 4.9 m/s².
Ex4. Planet with twice Earth's mass and twice radius: ve?
ve ∝ √(M/R) = √(2/2) = 1 → 11.2 km/s.
Ex5. Orbit radius 4× another's. Ratio of periods?
T² ∝ r³ → T ∝ 43/2 = 8 → T₂ = 8T₁.
Ex6. Weight of 60 kg on Moon (g = 1.63).
W = 60×1.63 ≈ 98 N (Earth: 588 N).
Ex7. Binding energy of 1000 kg satellite at h=R (R=6.4×10⁶ m).
r=2R: E = GMm/2r = mgR²/4R = mgR/4 = 1000×9.8×6.4×10⁶/4 ≈ 1.57×10¹⁰ J.
Ex8. Mass of Earth from g.
M = gR²/G = 9.8×(6.4×10⁶)²/6.67×10⁻¹¹ ≈ 6×10²⁴ kg.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. State Kepler's second law. Which conservation law is behind it?Equal areas in equal times; it follows from conservation of angular momentum (central force).
Q2. Why is G called universal constant?Its value is same everywhere, for all bodies, independent of medium.
Q3. Derive g = GM/R².Weight mg = gravitational force GMm/R², so g = GM/R².
Q4. Why is g less at equator than poles?Earth bulges at equator (larger R) and rotation reduces g by Rω²cos²λ.
Q5. Derive escape velocity.Total energy must be zero: ½mv² − GMm/R = 0, so v = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR).
Q6. Why does the Moon have no atmosphere?Low escape velocity (2.4 km/s) is comparable to molecular speeds, so gases escape.
Q7. Why is a satellite's total energy negative?E = −GMm/2r; negative means it is bound to Earth.
Q8. Conditions for a geostationary satellite?T = 24 h, equatorial orbit, west to east, height about 36000 km.
Q9. Why do astronauts feel weightless?Satellite and astronaut are both in free fall with the same acceleration, so normal reaction is zero.
Q10. Relation between v₀ and v_e?v_e = √2 v₀ (11.2 vs 7.9 km/s).
