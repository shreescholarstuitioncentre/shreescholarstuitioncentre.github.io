3B.1 Frame of Reference, Point Mass, Rest & Motion

Frame of reference: a coordinate system (with clock) from which position of a body is described. Point mass: a body whose size is negligible compared to distances it moves (train between two cities, planet around sun). Rest: position does not change with time w.r.t. surroundings; motion: position changes. Rest and motion are relative (a passenger is at rest w.r.t. train but moving w.r.t. ground).

Uniform motion: equal displacement in equal time (a = 0). Non-uniform: velocity changes. Magnitude of displacement ≤ distance (equal only for straight-line one-direction motion).

3B.4 Equations of Motion (constant acceleration)

① v = u + at   ② s = ut + ½at²   ③ v² = u² + 2as
④ snth = u + (a/2)(2n − 1)   ⑤ s = (u+v)t/2
Sign convention: take direction of u as +; retardation → a negative.

Graphical derivation (from v–t graph, figure above)

① Slope = a = (v−u)/t → v = u + at.
② Area under v–t = trapezoid = ½(u+v)t = ½(u + u + at)t → s = ut + ½at².
③ From ①, t = (v−u)/a; put in s = ½(u+v)t = (v²−u²)/2a → v² = u² + 2as.

Calculus derivation

① a = dv/dt → ∫dv = ∫a dt → v − u = at → v = u + at.
② v = dx/dt = u + at → ∫dx = ∫(u+at)dt → s = ut + ½at² (x=0 at t=0).
③ a = dv/dt = (dv/dx)(dx/dt) = v dv/dx → ∫v dv = a∫dx → ½(v²−u²) = as → v² = u² + 2as.

Distance in nth second: sₙ = s(n) − s(n−1) = [un + ½an²] − [u(n−1) + ½a(n−1)²] = u + a(2n−1)/2.

3B.5 Motion under Gravity

g = 9.8 m/s² (≈10) downward. Use a = −g for upward as +ve.
Thrown up with u: v = u − gt; time of ascent = u/g; total flight = 2u/g; max height H = u²/2g; returns with speed u.
Free fall from rest from height h: t = √(2h/g), v = √(2gh); distances in successive seconds ratio 1:3:5:7…

3B.6 Motion on Inclined Plane (smooth)

Component of weight along plane = mg sinθ → a = g sinθ. Block starting from rest: length L = h/sinθ. Speed at bottom v = √(2gh) (same as free fall!). Time t = √(2L/a) = (1/sinθ)√(2h/g). If rough: a = g(sinθ − μ cosθ).

3B.7 Relative Velocity

vAB = vA − vB (velocity of A relative to B).
Same direction: v_A − v_B. Opposite directions: v_A + v_B.
River–boat: boat speed v_b (in still water), river v_r, width d.
• Shortest time: head perpendicular. t = d/v_b, drift x = v_r t.
• Shortest path (zero drift): head upstream at θ with sinθ = v_r/v_b (to the normal); v_eff = √(v_b²−v_r²); t = d/√(v_b²−v_r²).
Rain–man: rain vertical v_R, man v_m: umbrella tilt from vertical tanθ = v_m/v_R.

3C.1 Nature of Projectile Motion

A body thrown into air at angle θ with horizontal, moving under gravity only. Two independent motions: horizontal (uniform, a=0, uₓ = u cosθ) and vertical (a = −g, u_y = u sinθ). Path = parabola. At highest point vertical velocity = 0; velocity = u cosθ horizontal; acceleration still g downward.

3C.2 Path, Time of Flight, Range, Height

Position: x = u cosθ·t, y = u sinθ·t − ½gt².
Path: y = x tanθ − g x² / (2u²cos²θ) (parabola).
Time of flight: T = 2u sinθ / g
Max height: H = u² sin²θ / 2g
Range: R = u² sin2θ / g
Max range: θ = 45° → R_max = u²/g
R = 4H cotθ; at 45°, R = 4H. For θ and (90−θ) range is same. Hmax = u²/2g at 90°.

3C.3 Projection from a Height (horizontal)

Body thrown horizontally with u from height h: t = √(2h/g); range R = u√(2h/g); vertical velocity at ground v_y = √(2gh); resultant v = √(u² + 2gh); angle with horizontal tanβ = √(2gh)/u. Time depends only on h, not on u.

3C.4 Circular Motion

Angular displacement θ (rad); angular velocity ω = dθ/dt = 2π/T = 2πν; linear speed v = rω.
Uniform circular motion: speed constant, direction changes → accelerated. Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = ω²r, directed towards centre. Force F = mv²/r. Frequency ν = 1/T. rpm × 2π/60 = rad/s.

Solved Examples

Easy x = 3t³ − 2t² + 5t. Find v and a at t = 2 s.
Step 1: v = dx/dt = 9t² − 4t + 5 → at 2: 36 − 8 + 5 = 33 m/s.
Step 2: a = dv/dt = 18t − 4 → at 2: 32 m/s².
Easy a = 6t, starts from rest at origin. Find v and x at t = 2.
v = ∫6t dt = 3t² (C=0) → 12 m/s. x = ∫3t² dt = t³ = 8 m.
Easy Find minimum of y = x² − 4x + 3.
dy/dx = 2x − 4 = 0 → x = 2. d²y/dx² = 2 > 0 → minimum. y = 4 − 8 + 3 = −1.
Medium A car goes half the distance at 40 km/h and the rest at 60 km/h. Average speed?
Let total 2d. t₁ = d/40, t₂ = d/60. Total t = d(1/40+1/60) = d·(5/120) = d/24. v_avg = 2d/(d/24) = 48 km/h (not 50).
Medium Car at 20 m/s is braked with a = −5 m/s². Find stopping distance and time.
v = 0. v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 400 − 10s → s = 40 m. v = u + at → 0 = 20 − 5t → t = 4 s.
Medium u = 5 m/s, a = 2 m/s². Distance in 4th second? s₄ = u + a(2n−1)/2 = 5 + 2(7)/2 = 12 m.
Medium Ball thrown up at 29.4 m/s (g = 9.8). Find max height, time to return.
H = u²/2g = 864.36/19.6 = 44.1 m. Time of ascent = u/g = 3 s → total 6 s.
Medium Smooth incline 30°, length 10 m. Find a, speed at bottom, time (g = 9.8).
a = g sin30° = 4.9 m/s². v = √(2aL) = √98 = 9.9 m/s. t = v/a = 2.02 s.
Medium Boat 5 m/s, river 3 m/s, width 100 m. (a) shortest time, drift (b) shortest path, time.
(a) t = 100/5 = 20 s; drift = 3×20 = 60 m.
(b) sinθ = 3/5 → θ = 37° upstream; v_eff = √(25−9) = 4 m/s; t = 100/4 = 25 s.
Medium Projectile u = 20 m/s, θ = 30°, g = 10. Find T, H, R.
T = 2(20)(½)/10 = 2 s. H = 400×¼/20 = 5 m. R = 400 sin60°/10 = 40×0.866 = 34.6 m.
Medium Stone thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from 80 m cliff (g=10). Find t, range, final speed.
t = √(160/10) = 4 s. R = 15×4 = 60 m. v_y = 10×4 = 40. v = √(225+1600) = √1825 ≈ 42.7 m/s, tanβ = 40/15 = 8/3.
Medium Wheel r = 0.5 m rotates 120 rpm. Find ω, v, a_c.
ν = 2 rps; ω = 2π×2 = 4π = 12.57 rad/s; v = rω = 6.28 m/s; a_c = ω²r = 157.9×0.5 ≈ 79 m/s².
Hard Prove path of projectile is a parabola.
x = u cosθ t → t = x/(u cosθ). y = u sinθ t − ½gt² = x tanθ − g x²/(2u²cos²θ). Form y = ax − bx² → parabola ∎.
Hard Prove R = 4H cotθ and for θ=45° R = 4H.
R = 2u² sinθ cosθ/g; H = u² sin²θ/2g. R/H = 4cosθ/sinθ = 4cotθ → R = 4H cotθ. cot45° = 1 → R = 4H ∎.
Hard Show ranges for θ and (90°−θ) are equal.
R(90°−θ) = u² sin(180°−2θ)/g = u² sin2θ/g = R(θ) ∎.
Hard Prove Rmax = 4H at θ=45° gives max range u²/g.
R = u² sin2θ/g is max when sin2θ = 1 → 2θ = 90° → θ = 45° → R_max = u²/g ∎.
Hard Velocity v = 3t² + 2t. Find displacement from t = 1 to t = 3.
s = ∫₁³(3t²+2t)dt = [t³ + t²]₁³ = (27+9) − (1+1) = 34 m.

Short & Long Q & A

Q1. Distance vs displacement?
Ans: Distance = actual path (scalar, ≥0); displacement = shortest vector from start to end (can be zero).
Q2. Can average velocity be zero while average speed is not?
Ans: Yes, in a round trip: displacement = 0 but distance > 0.
Q3. What does slope of x–t and area of v–t give?
Ans: Slope = velocity; area = displacement.
Q4. Can a body have zero velocity but non-zero acceleration?
Ans: Yes, at the top of a vertical throw v = 0, a = g.
Q5. Why is projectile path parabolic?
Ans: x ∝ t and y has t² term, so y = ax − bx².
Q6. At which angle is range max, and why?
Ans: 45°, because R ∝ sin2θ is largest when 2θ = 90°.
Q7. Two bodies, one dropped and one thrown horizontally from same height. Which lands first?
Ans: Both together; vertical motions are identical (t = √2h/g).
Q8. What is angle between velocity and acceleration at the highest point?
Ans: 90° (v horizontal, a = g vertical).
Q9. Uniform circular motion is accelerated — why?
Ans: Direction of velocity changes continuously, so velocity vector changes; a_c = v²/r towards centre.
Q10. (Long) Derive the three equations of motion graphically and by calculus.
Ans: See 3B.4 (both derivations).
Q11. (Long) Derive T, H and R of a projectile.
Ans: Vertical: 0 = u sinθ T − ½gT² → T = 2u sinθ/g. Top: 0 = u²sin²θ − 2gH → H = u²sin²θ/2g. R = u cosθ·T = u² sin2θ/g.
Q12. (Long) Explain relative velocity and river-boat cases.
Ans: See 3B.7, with both cases and formulas.
