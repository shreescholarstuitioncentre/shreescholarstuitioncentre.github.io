Physics Class 11 – Ch 5: Laws of Motion

5A Newton’s Laws • 5B Friction • 5C Circular Motion

5A.1 Force and 5A.2 Inertia

Force: a push or pull that changes (or tries to change) the state of rest/motion, speed, direction or shape of a body. Unit: newton (N) = kg m s⁻²; CGS: dyne = 10⁻⁵ N; 1 kgf = 9.8 N. Dimension [MLT⁻²]. Vector.

Inertia: property of a body to resist change in its state of rest or uniform motion. Measure of inertia = mass. Types: inertia of rest (passenger jerks backward when bus starts), inertia of motion (passenger falls forward on sudden brake), inertia of direction (mud flies off a turning wheel).

5A.3 Newton’s First Law

A body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.
It defines force (cause of change of state) and inertia; also called Galileo’s law of inertia.

5A.4 Linear Momentum and 5A.5 Second Law

Momentum p = mv, vector, unit kg m s⁻¹, dim [MLT⁻¹].

Second law: rate of change of momentum = applied force (and is in its direction).
F = dp/dt; for constant mass F = ma.
1 N: force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻². Components: Fₓ = maₓ, F_y = ma_y, F_z = ma_z.

5A.6 Impulse

J = F·Δt = Δp = mv − mu (for constant F); in general J = ∫F dt = area under F–t graph. Unit N s (= kg m s⁻¹). Impulsive force: large force for very short time (bat–ball). Cricketer lowers hands while catching → time ↑ → force ↓ (same Δp).

5A.7 Third Law and 5A.8 Conservation of Momentum

Third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction. F₁₂ = −F₂₁. They act on different bodies (so never cancel), simultaneously, along same line.
Conservation of momentum: if net external force = 0, total momentum is constant. m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂.
Proof: F₁₂ = −F₂₁ → m₁(Δv₁/Δt) = −m₂(Δv₂/Δt) → m₁Δv₁ + m₂Δv₂ = 0 → Δ(p₁+p₂) = 0 ∎.
Examples: gun recoil v_gun = −(m/M)v; rocket propulsion; collisions; swimming.

5A.9 Second Law as a Real Law

① Contains the first law (F = 0 → a = 0). ② Vector law, holds along each axis separately. ③ Is local (force at a moment gives acceleration at that moment). ④ Valid in inertial frames; the third law is then a separate statement. ⑤ Net force F is the vector sum of all forces; acceleration direction = net force direction.

5A.12 Inertial and Non-Inertial Frames

Inertial frame: not accelerating (rest or uniform velocity); Newton’s laws valid (Earth, approximately). Non-inertial frame: accelerating frame (lift, turning car); Newton’s laws fail unless a pseudo force F = −m a_frame is added (opposite to frame’s acceleration). Pseudo force has no reaction and no real source.

5A.13 Apparent Weight

Weight reading R (normal reaction) in a lift: up with a → R = m(g+a) (feels heavier); down with a → R = m(g−a) (lighter); uniform speed → R = mg; cable breaks, a = g → R = 0.

5A.14 Concurrent Forces

Forces whose lines of action meet at a single point. Equilibrium: ΣF = 0 (ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣF_y = 0). Lami’s theorem (three forces): F₁/sinα = F₂/sinβ = F₃/sinγ, where α, β, γ are angles opposite to the forces F₁, F₂, F₃.

5B.1 Frictional Force and 5B.2 Self-adjusting Nature

Friction: force opposing relative motion (or its tendency) between two surfaces in contact; acts along the surface, opposite to the motion/tendency. Cause: surface irregularities and molecular adhesion. Static friction is self-adjusting: f_s = applied force F until it reaches its maximum value (limiting friction).

5B.3 Laws of Limiting Friction

① f_L is independent of apparent area of contact.
② f_L ∝ normal reaction N: f_L = μₛN (μₛ = coefficient of static friction, no unit).
③ Depends on nature and roughness of the surfaces.
④ Always opposes the (tendency of) motion. Condition: f_s ≤ μₛN.

5B.4–5 Kinetic Friction and Graph

Kinetic friction f_k = μₖN (μₖ < μₛ), nearly independent of speed and area. Graph above: straight line (f = F) up to f_L, sudden fall to a constant f_k. Net force beyond: a = (F − f_k)/m.

5B.6 Angle of Friction and Angle of Repose

Angle of friction (λ): angle between normal reaction N and the resultant R of N and limiting friction: tanλ = f_L/N = μₛ.
Angle of repose (α): minimum angle of incline at which a body just starts sliding: tanα = μₛ.
So λ = α. Proof: on incline at α: N = mg cosα, f_L = mg sinα → μ = tanα ∎.

5B.7 Types of Kinetic Friction

Sliding friction (largest), rolling friction (much smaller: μ_r ≪ μₖ; reason: less contact, no sliding), fluid/viscous friction. Order: μₛ > μₖ(sliding) > μ_r(rolling). That is why wheels/ball bearings are used.

5B.8 Sliding on an Inclined Plane

Down the rough incline: N = mg cosθ, a = g(sinθ − μₖ cosθ).
Body at rest if tanθ ≤ μₛ. Pushed up (retarding): a = g(sinθ + μₖ cosθ).
Min force to push up at constant speed: F = mg(sinθ + μ cosθ).

5B.9 Pulling and Pushing a Roller / Body

Force F at angle θ with horizontal, body of mass m, coefficient μ:
Pulling (upward): N = mg − F sinθ → f = μ(mg − F sinθ) smaller → easier.
Pushing (downward): N = mg + F sinθ → f larger → harder.
Min pulling force: F = μmg/(cosθ + μ sinθ); least when tanθ = μ: F_min = μmg/√(1+μ²).
That is why a roller is easier to pull than push.

5B.10 Friction: Necessary Evil & 5B.11 Reducing Friction

Necessary: walking, brakes, nails/screws, belts, writing, vehicle grip. Evil: wear and tear, heat loss, energy waste.
Reduce by: lubricants (oil/grease), polishing, ball bearings (sliding → rolling), streamlined shapes (fluid friction), air cushions, wheels.

5C.1–2 Uniform Circular Motion and Definitions

Period T, frequency ν = 1/T, angular velocity ω = 2π/T = 2πν, linear speed v = rω. Speed constant but velocity changes (direction) → accelerated.

5C.3–4 Dynamics and Centripetal Force

Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = ω²r = 4π²r/T², towards centre.
Centripetal force F = mv²/r = mω²r: real force (tension, friction, gravity, normal reaction…) directed to centre. It does no work (⊥ velocity); not a new type of force.

5C.7 Centrifugal Force

A pseudo force seen only in the rotating (non-inertial) frame, magnitude mv²/r (= mω²r) directed away from the centre. In an inertial frame it does not exist. Makes passengers lean outward on a turn.

5C.8 Motion in a Vertical Circle

Mass m on string length r.
Bottom: T_B − mg = mv_B²/r. Top: T_T + mg = mv_T²/r.
Min speed at top (T = 0): v_T = √(gr); then v_B = √(5gr) (from energy: ½mv_B² = ½mv_T² + 2mgr).
T_B − T_T = 6mg. At horizontal level: v² = 3gr, T = 3mg.
On convex bridge: N = mg − mv²/r (leaves ground if v = √(gr)). Rod instead of string: min top speed = 0, bottom √(4gr).

Solved Examples

Easy Force on a 5 kg body accelerating at 4 m/s². F = ma = 5×4 = 20 N.
Easy Force 50 N acts for 0.2 s on a 10 kg rest body. Find impulse and final speed.
J = FΔt = 50×0.2 = 10 N s. J = mv → v = 10/10 = 1 m/s.
Easy Limiting friction for 10 kg block on μₛ = 0.5 (g = 10). N = 100 N, f_L = 0.5×100 = 50 N.
Medium 0.15 kg ball at 20 m/s is hit back at 20 m/s; contact 0.01 s. Find impulse and average force.
Δp = m(v − u) = 0.15[20 − (−20)] = 6 N s. F = Δp/Δt = 6/0.01 = 600 N.
Medium Gun 4 kg fires 20 g bullet at 400 m/s. Recoil speed?
0 = (0.02)(400) + 4v → v = −8/4 = −2 m/s (2 m/s opposite).
Medium Atwood: m₁ = 5 kg, m₂ = 3 kg, g = 10. Find a and T.
a = (5−3)(10)/8 = 2.5 m/s². T = 2(5)(3)(10)/8 = 37.5 N.
Medium 60 kg man in a lift (g = 10): apparent weight if lift moves up/down with a = 2 and in free fall.
Up: 60(12) = 720 N; down: 60(8) = 480 N; free fall: 0.
Medium m₁ = 2 kg, m₂ = 3 kg joined by string on smooth table; 10 N pulls m₁. Find a, T.
a = 10/5 = 2 m/s². T = m₂a = 3×2 = 6 N.
Medium 10 kg block, μₛ = 0.5, μₖ = 0.4, g = 10. Friction when pushed by 30 N and by 60 N?
f_L = 50 N. 30 N < 50 → no motion, f = 30 N (self-adjusting). 60 N > 50 → moves; f_k = 0.4×100 = 40 N; a = (60−40)/10 = 2 m/s².
Medium Angle of repose if μ = 1/√3. tanα = 1/√3 → α = 30°.
Medium Block slides down 30° incline with μ = 0.2. Find a.
a = g(sinθ − μcosθ) = 10(0.5 − 0.2×0.866) = 10(0.327) = 3.27 m/s².
Medium 0.5 kg stone whirled in circle r = 1 m at 4 m/s. Tension? F = mv²/r = 0.5×16/1 = 8 N.
Medium Max speed of car on level curve r = 50 m, μ = 0.5, g = 10. v = √(μrg) = √250 = 15.8 m/s (≈ 57 km/h).
Medium Banking angle for v = 20 m/s, r = 100 m, g = 10. tanθ = v²/rg = 400/1000 = 0.4 → θ ≈ 21.8°.
Hard Prove angle of friction = angle of repose.
Limiting friction f_L = μN; resultant R of N and f_L makes λ with N: tanλ = f_L/N = μ. On incline at repose: N = mg cosα, f_L = mg sinα → tanα = f_L/N = μ. So tanλ = tanα → λ = α ∎.
Hard Prove minimum force to pull a block at angle θ is μmg/√(1+μ²).
Horizontal: F cosθ = μN; vertical: N = mg − F sinθ → F(cosθ + μ sinθ) = μmg → F = μmg/(cosθ + μ sinθ). Denominator max value √(1+μ²) at tanθ = μ (since a cosθ + b sinθ ≤ √(a²+b²)). → F_min = μmg/√(1+μ²) ∎.
Hard 100 N weight hangs from two strings making 60° between them. Find tension (Lami).
Angle between T₁ and T₂ = 60°; between each T and W = 150°. T/sin150° = 100/sin60° → T = 100(0.5)/0.866 = 57.7 N.
Hard Prove banking angle tanθ = v²/rg.
Forces: N (⊥ road, tilted θ from vertical), mg. Vertical: N cosθ = mg. Horizontal (centre): N sinθ = mv²/r. Divide → tanθ = v²/(rg) ∎.
Hard Show min speed at bottom of vertical circle is √(5gr).
Top (T=0): mg = mv_T²/r → v_T² = gr. Energy: ½mv_B² = ½mv_T² + mg(2r) → v_B² = gr + 4gr = 5gr → v_B = √(5gr) ∎.
Hard Prove T_bottom − T_top = 6mg.
T_B = mg + mv_B²/r; T_T = mv_T²/r − mg. Difference = 2mg + m(v_B² − v_T²)/r = 2mg + m(4gr)/r = 2mg + 4mg = 6mg ∎.
Hard Bucket of water swung in vertical circle r = 0.4 m (g = 10). Min speed at top so water doesn’t fall; frequency?
v_T = √(gr) = √4 = 2 m/s. ω = v/r = 5 rad/s → ν = ω/2π = 0.8 rev/s.

Short & Long Q & A

Q1. Why do action–reaction forces not cancel?
Ans: They act on different bodies, so cannot be added on one body.
Q2. Why does a person fall forward when a bus brakes?
Ans: Inertia of motion: body tends to continue forward while feet stop with the bus.
Q3. Why does a cricketer move hands backward while catching?
Ans: Increases time of stopping; F = Δp/Δt reduces for same Δp.
Q4. Why is rolling friction less than sliding?
Ans: Contact area is small and surfaces don’t interlock/drag; deformation is less.
Q5. Is friction always harmful?
Ans: No; it is a necessary evil – needed for walking, brakes, grip; harmful for wear and heat.
Q6. Why is pulling a roller easier than pushing?
Ans: Pulling reduces N (mg − F sinθ) and friction; pushing increases it.
Q7. Does centripetal force do work?
Ans: No; it is perpendicular to displacement, so W = F s cos90° = 0.
Q8. Why are roads banked at turns?
Ans: Horizontal component of N supplies centripetal force, reducing dependence on friction and wear.
Q9. Why is a body in free fall weightless?
Ans: Apparent weight R = m(g − a) = 0 as a = g.
Q10. Differentiate centripetal and centrifugal force.
Ans: Centripetal: real, towards centre, inertial frame. Centrifugal: pseudo, away from centre, rotating frame.
Q11. (Long) Derive law of conservation of momentum; give examples.
Ans: See 5A.8 proof; examples: gun, rocket, collisions.
Q12. (Long) State laws of limiting friction; draw friction graph; define λ and α.
Ans: See 5B.3–5B.6 with figures.
Q13. (Long) Derive tensions and min speeds in vertical circular motion.
Ans: See 5C.8 and the Hard examples.
