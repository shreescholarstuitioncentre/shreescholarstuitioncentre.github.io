✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 11: Thermal Properties of Matter

A1. Temperature, Scales & Ideal Gas Equation

Temperature is the degree of hotness of a body; heat flows from higher to lower temperature.

C/5 = (F − 32)/9 = (K − 273)/5
PV = nRT (R = 8.31 J/mol·K)

A2. Thermal Expansion of Solids, Liquids & Gases

Linear ΔL = αLΔT   Areal ΔA = βAΔT   Volume ΔV = γVΔT
β = 2α   γ = 3α   ρ' = ρ/(1 + γΔT)

Liquids: real expansion γr = γa + γg (apparent + container). Gases expand by 1/273 of volume at 0°C per °C (Charles' law, V ∝ T at constant P).

A3. Anomalous Expansion of Water

From 0°C to 4°C water contracts, so its density is maximum (1000 kg/m³) at 4°C and it expands above 4°C. Hence in cold lakes the bottom stays at 4°C and ice forms on top, so aquatic life survives.

A4. Specific Heat, Calorimetry

Q = m s ΔT  |  heat capacity C = ms
Heat lost = Heat gained

s of water = 4200 J/kg·K = 1 cal/g·°C (high, so used as coolant; sea breeze).

A5. Change of State, Latent Heat & Effect of Pressure

Q = m L   Lf(ice) = 3.36×10⁵ J/kg (80 cal/g)
Lv(steam) = 22.6×10⁵ J/kg (540 cal/g)

Temperature stays constant during change of state. Pressure raises boiling point (pressure cooker) and lowers melting point of ice (regelation, skating).

B1. Conduction, Thermal Conductivity & Resistance

Q/t = K A (T₁ − T₂)/L  |  R = L/KA
Series: R = R₁ + R₂   Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂

Conduction: heat passes through matter without bulk motion (solids). K unit W/m·K; metals have high K, air and wool have low K.

B2. Convection & Convection Currents

Heat carried by actual movement of fluid particles. Heated fluid expands, becomes lighter, rises; cooler fluid sinks, forming convection currents. Examples: sea/land breeze, boiling water, ventilators, trade winds.

B3. Radiation, Emissive & Absorptive Power, Black Body

Radiation needs no medium and travels at speed of light. Emissive power e: energy radiated per unit area per second. Absorptive power a: fraction of incident radiation absorbed. Black body: a = 1, absorbs and emits all wavelengths.

Kirchhoff: e/a = E (black body) at same T. Good absorbers are good emitters.

B4. Stefan's Law, Wien's Law & Greenhouse Effect

E = σT⁴ (σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴)   net loss = σ(T⁴ − T₀⁴)
Wien: λmT = b = 2.9×10⁻³ m·K

Hotter body radiates shorter peak wavelength (red → white → blue). Greenhouse effect: CO₂, CH₄, water vapour let sunlight in but trap Earth's infrared, warming the planet.

B5. Newton's Law of Cooling

dT/dt = −k (T − T₀)
Average form: (T₁ − T₂)/t = k[(T₁ + T₂)/2 − T₀]

Valid for small temperature difference (forced convection); graph T vs t is exponential decay.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. Convert 37°C to °F and K.
F = 37×9/5 + 32 = 98.6°F; K = 37 + 273 = 310 K.
Ex2. Steel rail 30 m, α = 1.2×10⁻⁵/°C, ΔT = 40°C. Find ΔL.
ΔL = αLΔT = 1.2×10⁻⁵×30×40 = 0.0144 m = 1.44 cm.
Ex3. Heat to warm 2 kg water from 20°C to 70°C.
Q = msΔT = 2×4200×50 = 4.2×10⁵ J.
Ex4. 100 g water at 80°C mixed with 200 g water at 20°C. Final T?
100(80 − T) = 200(T − 20) → 8000 − 100T = 200T − 4000 → T = 40°C.
Ex5. Heat to melt 100 g ice at 0°C (Lf = 3.36×10⁵ J/kg).
Q = mL = 0.1×3.36×10⁵ = 33600 J.
Ex6. Copper rod K = 400, A = 10⁻⁴ m², L = 0.5 m, ΔT = 100°C. Heat flow rate?
Q/t = KAΔT/L = 400×10⁻⁴×100/0.5 = 8 W.
Ex7. Black body at 727°C. Energy radiated per m² per s.
T = 1000 K; E = σT⁴ = 5.67×10⁻⁸×10¹² = 5.67×10⁴ W/m².
Ex8. Sun's peak wavelength 500 nm. Surface temperature?
T = b/λm = 2.9×10⁻³/5×10⁻⁷ = 5800 K.
Ex9. Body cools 80°C → 60°C in 10 min (room 20°C). Time for 60 → 40°C?
Case 1: 20/10 = k(70 − 20) → k = 0.04. Case 2: 20/t = 0.04(50 − 20) = 1.2 → t ≈ 16.7 min.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. Why does ice float and lakes freeze from the top?Water has maximum density at 4°C; colder water (lighter) rises and freezes on top.
Q2. Why are gaps left between railway tracks?To allow expansion in summer, otherwise thermal stress buckles rails.
Q3. Why is sea breeze felt in daytime?Land heats faster (low s); warm air rises over land, cooler sea air flows in.
Q4. Why are cooking utensils given copper bottoms?Copper has high K, so heat passes quickly to food.
Q5. Why does a pressure cooker cook faster?Higher pressure raises boiling point above 100°C.
Q6. Why is a black body ideal absorber and emitter?a = 1 so absorbs all radiation; by Kirchhoff's law e/a = E gives maximum emission.
Q7. Why do we feel warmer in woollen clothes?Wool traps air, a poor conductor, which reduces heat loss.
Q8. What is the greenhouse effect?Gases like CO₂ trap Earth's infrared radiation, raising surface temperature.
Q9. State Wien's displacement law.Wavelength of maximum emission is inversely proportional to absolute temperature: λ_m T = b.
Q10. Why does steam burn more than boiling water?Steam releases extra latent heat (540 cal/g) on condensing.
