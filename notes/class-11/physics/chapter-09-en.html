✎ Class 11 Physics Ch 9: Mechanical Properties of Solids

1. Elastic Behaviour

Elasticity is the property of a body to regain its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed. Perfectly elastic: quartz, phosphor bronze. Plastic: putty, wax, lead (does not regain shape). Steel is more elastic than rubber.

2. Stress & Strain

Stress = F/A (N/m² = Pa, dim ML⁻¹T⁻²)
Longitudinal strain = ΔL/L  |  Volume strain = ΔV/V  |  Shear strain = θ

Strain has no unit or dimension. Stress types: tensile, compressive, shearing, hydraulic (volume). Hooke's law: within elastic limit stress ∝ strain, so stress/strain = modulus of elasticity.

3. Elastic Behaviour of a Wire: Stress–Strain Curve

OA: straight line, Hooke's law valid. B: elastic limit. C: yield point, beyond it strain increases with little stress (plastic flow). D: ultimate tensile strength. E: breaking (fracture) point. Ductile materials (copper) have long CE; brittle ones break soon after elastic limit.

4. Young's Modulus

Y = (F/A)/(ΔL/L) = FL/(AΔL) = MgL/(πr²ΔL)

Unit Pa, dimension ML⁻¹T⁻². Steel 2×10¹¹ Pa, copper 1.1×10¹¹ Pa, rubber ~10⁶ Pa. Larger Y means more elastic (harder to stretch).

5. Bulk Modulus & Modulus of Rigidity

B = −p/(ΔV/V) = −pV/ΔV  |  compressibility k = 1/B
η = (F/A)/θ = F/(Aθ)

Bulk modulus applies to solids, liquids and gases; modulus of rigidity only to solids (fluids cannot resist shear, η = 0).

6. Poisson's Ratio

σ = lateral strain / longitudinal strain

No unit. Theoretical range −1 to 0.5; practical 0 to 0.5. When a wire stretches, its diameter decreases.

7. Elastic Potential Energy

U = ½ F ΔL = ½ × stress × strain × volume
Energy density u = ½ Y (strain)² = stress²/2Y

Work done in stretching a wire is stored as elastic PE (area under F–ΔL graph).

8. Force During Cooling (Thermal Stress)

Thermal stress = Y α ΔT  |  F = Y A α ΔT

A rod fixed between rigid walls cannot contract on cooling, so it develops tension. Same for expansion: compression. This is why gaps are left in rails.

9. Atomic Model & Interatomic Force Constant

Atoms in a solid sit at equilibrium separation r₀ and are joined by spring-like bonds. For small displacements the restoring force is F = kΔr.

k = Y r₀

Larger k means stiffer bonds and higher Young's modulus.

10. Factors Affecting Elasticity

Temperature: elasticity decreases as temperature rises (invar is an exception). Impurities: may increase or decrease. Hammering/rolling: increases elasticity. Annealing: decreases. Crystal structure and nature of material.

11. Applications of Elasticity

Cranes use steel ropes of many thin strands; bridges and beams use I-shaped girders (less material, same strength); rubber for shock absorbers; safety factor in design means breaking stress is much more than working load.

✎ Solved Examples

Ex1. Stress in a wire of area 1 mm² under 100 N.
σ = F/A = 100/10⁻⁶ = 10⁸ Pa.
Ex2. Wire L = 2 m, A = 1 mm², F = 100 N, ΔL = 1 mm. Find Y.
Y = FL/(AΔL) = 100×2/(10⁻⁶×10⁻³) = 2×10¹¹ Pa.
Ex3. Steel wire (Y=2×10¹¹), L = 1 m, A = 1 mm², F = 200 N. Find ΔL.
ΔL = FL/(AY) = 200×1/(10⁻⁶×2×10¹¹) = 10⁻³ m = 1 mm.
Ex4. Water (B = 2.2×10⁹ Pa), pressure increased by 10⁷ Pa. Find ΔV/V.
|ΔV/V| = p/B = 10⁷/2.2×10⁹ ≈ 4.5×10⁻³ (0.45%).
Ex5. Shear stress 4×10⁶ Pa, η = 8×10¹⁰ Pa. Find shear angle.
θ = stress/η = 4×10⁶/8×10¹⁰ = 5×10⁻⁵ rad.
Ex6. Energy stored when F = 100 N stretches wire by 1 mm.
U = ½FΔL = ½×100×10⁻³ = 0.05 J.
Ex7. Steel rod (Y=2×10¹¹, α=1.2×10⁻⁵/°C) clamped, heated by 50 °C. Thermal stress?
σ = YαΔT = 2×10¹¹×1.2×10⁻⁵×50 = 1.2×10⁸ Pa.
Ex8. Longitudinal strain 2×10⁻³, lateral strain −5×10⁻⁴. Find σ.
σ = 5×10⁻⁴/2×10⁻³ = 0.25.

✎ Q & A (tap)

Q1. Define elasticity and plasticity.Elasticity: regaining original shape after removing force. Plasticity: permanently deformed.
Q2. State Hooke's law.Within elastic limit, stress is proportional to strain.
Q3. Why is steel more elastic than rubber?For same stress steel shows much smaller strain, hence larger Y (2×10¹¹ vs 10⁶ Pa).
Q4. Why do bridges use I-shaped girders?Bending depression δ = Wl³/4Ybd³ is lowest for large depth d; I-shape saves material.
Q5. Why are liquids lacking modulus of rigidity?Liquids flow under shear, they cannot develop restoring tangential stress.
Q6. Derive elastic energy density.U = ½FΔL = ½(σA)(εL) = ½σε × AL, so u = ½σε = ½Yε².
Q7. Why are railway tracks laid with gaps?To avoid large thermal stress YαΔT that would bend the rails in summer.
Q8. What is Poisson's ratio?Ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain; range 0 to 0.5 practically.
Q9. Why is a thick rope made of many thin strands?Thin wires are more flexible and bear more load per area (fewer defects), easier to bend.
Q10. Does elasticity increase or decrease on heating?Generally decreases (Y falls with temperature).
