The Tale of Melon City — Vikram Seth (Class 11 Snapshots, Poem) Notes

1. About the Poet

Vikram Seth (born 1952) is an Indian poet, novelist and travel writer. He is the author of A Suitable Boy and The Golden Gate. The Tale of Melon City is a humorous narrative poem with a political satire.

2. Form & Setting

  • Form: narrative (story) poem with rhyme and humour.
  • Setting: an imaginary city ruled by a king.
  • Tone: comic, satirical, ironic.

3. Theme

  • Satire on foolish and unjust rulers.
  • Blame-passing in bureaucracy; no one takes responsibility.
  • Absurd justice and abuse of power.
  • Foolishness of people who follow orders blindly.
  • Rule by chance, not wisdom.

4. Summary (step by step)

  1. The arch: A king orders an arch to be built across the main road of the city.
  2. Problem: The arch is built so low that the king, passing under it, knocks his head, and is angry.
  3. Orders: He orders the Chief Mason to be hanged.
  4. Blame game: The Chief Mason blames the workmen, the workmen blame the architect, and the architect blames the king who approved the plan.
  5. The king: The ministers say the king himself must be hanged, as the final person at fault.
  6. Wise man: A wise man points out that whoever is hanged will win honour or fame.
  7. The new king: After the king's end, the next person to pass under the arch is made ruler, and an idiot is chosen.
  8. Melon City: The idiot's rule is accepted as just; the city is named after a melon, and the story ends as an ironic comment on rulers and the ruled.
My memory of this poem's last stanzas is not fully reliable. Check the ending against your textbook.

5. Characters

  • The King – vain, hasty, tyrannical.
  • Chief Mason – first victim; blames others.
  • Workmen / Architect – each passes blame.
  • Ministers / Wise man – cunning, passive or clever.
  • The Idiot – simple-minded, chosen king.

6. Important Words

  • Satire – ridicule of foolishness
  • Tyrant – cruel ruler
  • Mason – builder with stone
  • Absurd – senseless
  • Blame – hold responsible
  • Irony – opposite of what is expected

9. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'The Tale of Melon City'?

Vikram Seth.

Q. What type of poem is it?

A humorous narrative poem, a satire.

Q. What did the king order to be built?

An arch across the main road.

Q. Why was the king angry?

The arch was too low, so he hit his head against it.

Q. Whom did the king order to be hanged first?

The Chief Mason.

Q. Whom did the Chief Mason blame?

The workmen.

Q. Who finally gets blamed in the chain?

The king himself.

Q. Who was made the next ruler?

An idiot, chosen by chance.

Q. What is the central idea?

Satire on foolish rulers and the absurdity of unjust power and the blame game.

Q. Explain the blame game in the poem.

The king blames the Chief Mason, who blames the workmen, who blame the architect, who blames the king. This circle shows that no one accepts responsibility, and that the king, who approved the plan, is the real cause.

Q. Describe the king's character.

He is hasty, vain and tyrannical. He orders hanging without thought, ignoring his own part in the fault.

Q. What is the irony in the poem?

The king who orders others to be hanged finds himself condemned, and a fool is made ruler, so the powerful are ridiculed.

Q. How does the poet use humour?

Through exaggeration, rhyme and the absurd chain of blame and the comic crowning of an idiot.

Q. Is the poem a criticism of society?

Yes. It mocks rulers who act without thought and subjects who obey blindly.

Q. What message does the poem give?

Rulers must be wise and accountable, and people must not follow orders foolishly.
