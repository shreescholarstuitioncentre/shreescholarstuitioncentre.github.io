Mother's Day — J.B. Priestley (Class 11 Snapshots, Ch. 3) Notes

1. About the Author

John Boynton (J.B.) Priestley (1894–1984) was an English novelist, playwright and broadcaster. He is known for plays like An Inspector Calls and for social comedy. Mother's Day is a one-act comedy.

2. Form & Setting

  • Form: a one-act play (comedy with a message).
  • Place: the sitting-room of the Pearson home, a middle-class English house.
  • Time: an ordinary evening; the action is near real time.

3. Theme

  • Role of a mother in a family: love vs. being taken for granted.
  • Respect and assertiveness: a person must claim self-respect.
  • Family selfishness and lack of consideration.
  • Gender roles and the status of women.
  • Comic role-reversal as a method of teaching a lesson.

4. Summary (scene by scene)

  1. Opening: Mrs Pearson and her neighbour Mrs Fitzgerald chat. Mrs Fitzgerald reads cards and tells fortunes.
  2. Complaint: Mrs Pearson says her family does not respect her and orders her around.
  3. Advice: Mrs Fitzgerald says she must be firm and not give in to their demands.
  4. The plan: Mrs Fitzgerald proposes that they exchange personalities so that she can handle the family while Mrs Pearson watches.
  5. Family enters: Doris, Cyril and George Pearson come in, each wanting something, and treat 'Mrs Pearson' as a servant.
  6. Change: 'Mrs Pearson' (now bold) refuses to cook or obey; she announces she will play cards with Mrs Fitzgerald.
  7. Shock: The family are stunned and angry; they cannot cope without her.
  8. Ending: They begin to realise their selfishness; the real Mrs Pearson learns to stand up for herself.

5. Characters

  • Mrs Annie Pearson – gentle, kind, over-worked, unassertive.
  • Mrs Fitzgerald – confident, sharp, practical; a fortune-teller.
  • George Pearson – the husband; self-centred, bossy.
  • Doris – the daughter; lazy, wants to go out.
  • Cyril – the son; careless and demanding.

6. Important Words

  • Assertive – confident in claiming rights
  • Domestic – related to the home
  • Role-reversal – exchanging roles
  • Fortune-teller – person who predicts future
  • Impudence – rudeness
  • Slave – one with no rights

9. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'Mother's Day'?

J.B. Priestley.

Q. What kind of work is it?

A one-act comedy play.

Q. Who is Mrs Fitzgerald?

Mrs Pearson's neighbour and a fortune-teller.

Q. Who are Mrs Pearson's children?

Doris and Cyril.

Q. What is Mrs Pearson's problem?

Her family treats her as a servant and does not respect her.

Q. What does Mrs Fitzgerald suggest?

To be firm and, for a time, to exchange personalities.

Q. What is the main theme?

A mother's place in the family and the need for respect.

Q. Describe Mrs Pearson.

Mrs Pearson is kind and hard-working but timid. She lets her family boss her, so she is taken for granted; at the end she gains confidence.

Q. Describe Mrs Fitzgerald.

Practical, confident and witty. She guides Mrs Pearson and shows her how to deal with her family firmly.

Q. How does Mrs Fitzgerald teach the family a lesson?

By pretending to be Mrs Pearson, she refuses their demands, shocks them and makes them see how much she does for them.

Q. Why does the family react angrily?

Because they are used to Mrs Pearson serving them and cannot accept the change.

Q. Is the play a comedy or a serious drama?

It is a comedy; humour is used for a serious point about respect.

Q. What does the title suggest?

The day is meant to honour mothers, yet the play shows mothers are often ignored; the title is ironic.

Q. Discuss the role of humour in the play.

Humour comes from the swap of personalities and the family's shock. It makes the message about respect easy to accept.

Q. What message does the play give?

Everyone must be treated with respect; mothers deserve equal consideration; a person must assert herself.
