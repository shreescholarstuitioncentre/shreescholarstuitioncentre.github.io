The Address — Marga Minco (Class 11 Snapshots, Ch. 2) Notes

1. About the Author

Marga Minco (born 1920) is a Dutch writer who survived the Nazi persecution of Jews in the Second World War. Her work deals with loss, memory and the aftermath of war. The Address is based on such experiences.

2. Setting & Narrator

  • Place: Holland (the Netherlands).
  • Time: during and after the Second World War, under Nazi occupation.
  • Narrator: an unnamed young woman (daughter), who tells the story as a first-person account.

3. Theme

  • Loss and grief caused by war.
  • The pull of memory and the emotional value of objects.
  • Betrayal and moral failure (those who kept the belongings).
  • Alienation: the narrator cannot reclaim her past or belong to the present.
  • Dignity: she leaves without making a scene.

4. Summary (step by step)

  1. War: The narrator's mother, a Jewish woman, fears deportation. She gives her valuable things to Mrs Dorling, an acquaintance, for safe keeping.
  2. Address: The mother passes on Mrs Dorling's address, which the narrator remembers.
  3. Loss: After the war the narrator is alone; her mother has not returned.
  4. Visit: She finds the address and visits. Mrs Dorling lets her in and she sees her mother's things in the house.
  5. Recognition: Seeing the familiar objects brings back painful memories of her mother and home.
  6. Silence: Mrs Dorling behaves as if she is a stranger and the narrator, hurt, does not demand anything back.
  7. Leaving: She leaves empty-handed, feeling both anger and grief.
  8. Later: Years after, she is drawn back to the house in her thoughts and feels she is part of a past that has been taken from her.

5. Characters

  • Narrator – sensitive, dignified, burdened by memory.
  • Mother – careful, fearful, protective.
  • Mrs Dorling – outwardly polite, cold and guilty; kept the belongings.

6. Important Words

  • Deportation – forced removal from a country
  • Occupation – control of a country by an enemy army
  • Keepsake – object kept as memory
  • Alienation – feeling of not belonging
  • Betrayal – breaking trust
  • Dignity – calm self-respect

9. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'The Address'?

Marga Minco, a Dutch writer.

Q. Who is the narrator?

An unnamed young daughter whose mother is lost in the war.

Q. To whom did the mother give her belongings?

Mrs Dorling.

Q. Why did the mother give the things away?

She feared deportation and wanted them kept safe.

Q. What did the narrator do after the war?

She went to Mrs Dorling's address to see the belongings.

Q. Did the narrator take anything back?

No, she left without claiming anything.

Q. What is the central idea of the story?

The cruelty of war, loss and memory, and the pain of finding one's possessions in a stranger's home.

Q. Describe Mrs Dorling.

She is polite on the surface but cold and selfish; she treats the narrator as a stranger and does not give back the belongings.

Q. Why did the narrator not ask for the belongings?

Grief, shock and pride; she felt she did not wish to beg for what was already hers.

Q. What do the objects symbolise?

They symbolise the lost mother, a happy home and a past that cannot be regained.

Q. Is the title significant?

Yes. The address is a link to the past; it holds both hope and pain, and it leads to disillusionment.

Q. Discuss the theme of alienation.

The narrator feels cut off from her home and past. The house she visits is part of her life, yet she is now an outsider in it.

Q. Characterise the narrator.

She is sensitive, dignified and reserved. She keeps her emotion inside and avoids confrontation.

Q. What is the tone of the story?

Quiet, sad and reflective.

Q. What message does the story give?

War destroys more than lives; it harms memory, trust and human relationships.
