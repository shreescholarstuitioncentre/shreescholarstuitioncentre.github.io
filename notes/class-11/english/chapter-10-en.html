🌼 The Laburnum Top — Ted Hughes 🌼

Class 11 English (Hornbill / UP Board) • Poem Notes

1. About the Poet

Ted Hughes (1930–1998) – English poet, Poet Laureate of Britain (1984–98). Famous for poems about animals and nature (The Hawk in the Rain, Crow). He shows nature as powerful, wild and full of energy. He was married to the poet Sylvia Plath.

2. Central Idea (≈150 words)

The poem shows how a tree that looks silent and lifeless can suddenly burst into energy when life visits it. On a still September afternoon the laburnum tree stands quiet, its yellow leaves hardly moving and its seed-pods rattling faintly. Then a goldfinch lands on it. At once the tree seems to wake up like a machine that has been switched on. The bird moves rapidly, like a lizard, chirping in small, sharp, squeaky notes. Soon its mate arrives and the tree becomes full of movement, colour and sound. When the birds fly away, the tree falls silent and still again. Hughes wants us to see that nature is a single living whole: the tree and the bird depend on each other, and one small visitor can transform a whole scene. The poem celebrates the sudden, mysterious power of life and the poet's sharp observation of nature.

3. Diagram (animated): Laburnum tree & Goldfinch

Explanation: The tree is calm (still sway, rattling pods). When the goldfinch ⑤ arrives, the whole tree ‘wakes’ – this contrast of stillness → activity → stillness is the poem’s structure.

4. Stanza-wise Summary & Explanation

Part 1 – The silent tree

The laburnum is quiet in the yellow afternoon light of September. A few leaves are turning yellow; only the dry seed-pods rattle. The tree’s thick shadow is like a deep well. Mood: calm, motionless.

Part 2 – Arrival of the goldfinch

A goldfinch lands. Its movements are fast and jerky like a lizard. It makes thin, sharp, squeaky sounds. Suddenly the tree is not silent but ‘charged’ with life, like a machine started by a switch. Mood: sudden energy.

Part 3 – The mate & the whole tree alive

The female bird joins. Their tiny cries and quick flits make the whole tree seem like one lively, trembling living thing; the tree and birds seem one organism.

Part 4 – Departure

The birds leave and the tree goes back to stillness, as if nothing had happened. Nature is cyclic and ever-changing.

6. Literary Devices

Simile: bird compared to a lizard. Metaphor: tree as a machine/engine. Personification: tree ‘wakes’ and ‘trembles’. Imagery: visual (yellow light), auditory (rattling, squeaks), motion. Onomatopoeia/sound words: rattling, squeak. Free verse: no fixed rhyme. Alliteration: repeated consonant sounds. Tone: observant, admiring.

8. Short Q&A (click to open)

Q1. What is the mood of the poem at the beginning?Calm and silent. The tree is still in the September sun, with only the seed-pods rattling.
Q2. Why is the laburnum called ‘silent’?There is no bird or wind; nothing moves except the faint rattle of pods.
Q3. Which bird visits the tree?A goldfinch, followed by its mate.
Q4. How does the poet describe the goldfinch’s movement?Quick, jerky and darting like a lizard.
Q5. What happens to the tree when the bird comes?It comes alive, like a machine switched on – full of movement and sound.
Q6. What happens when the birds leave?The tree returns to silence and stillness.
Q7. Which figure of speech compares the tree to a machine?Metaphor.
Q8. Which season is mentioned?September (early autumn), hence yellowing leaves.
Q9. What does the ‘well of shadow’ suggest?The tree’s thick, deep shade, mysterious like a well.
Q10. Name two works by Ted Hughes.The Hawk in the Rain and Crow.

9. Long Q&A

L1. Describe how the poet shows the contrast between stillness and activity.The poem begins with a still, silent tree in lazy sunlight; only pods rattle. The goldfinch’s arrival breaks this calm: the bird’s fast movement and sharp cries energise the tree so that it appears alive like a machine. After the birds depart, silence returns. This three-step pattern (silence → activity → silence) highlights how fragile and sudden the moments of life in nature are.
L2. Explain the relationship between the tree and the goldfinch.They are interdependent. The bird finds food and shelter in the tree (seeds), while the tree gains life, movement and sound from the bird. Hughes treats them as parts of a single living system, not separate things.
L3. Discuss the imagery used in the poem.Visual: yellow sunlight and leaves. Auditory: rattling pods, squeaky bird calls. Kinetic: the lizard-like darting of the bird, the trembling tree. These images make the scene vivid for the reader.
L4. What is the significance of the title?‘Top’ points to the crown of the tree where the action takes place. The title directs us to the tree as the main character, which changes from lifeless to lively.
L5. What message does the poem give?Nature is alive and interconnected; even small creatures can transform the world around them; we should observe nature closely and with wonder.
