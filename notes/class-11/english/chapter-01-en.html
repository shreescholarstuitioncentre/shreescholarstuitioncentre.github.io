✎ The Portrait of a Lady

Class 11 English (Hornbill) · Ch.1 · Khushwant Singh · UP Board

1. About the Author

Khushwant Singh (1915–2014) was an Indian novelist, journalist and historian. Famous works: Train to Pakistan, Delhi, A History of the Sikhs. This chapter is an autobiographical memoir about his grandmother.

3. Summary (Detailed)

(a) Grandmother's appearance

The author says his grandmother was very old, short, fat, slightly bent, with silvery white hair and a wrinkled face. She looked like a winter landscape in the mountains, with snow over everything. She always wore white, murmured prayers and moved her fingers on a rosary.

(b) Village days

Parents lived in the city; the child stayed with her in the village. She woke him up, bathed and dressed him, and walked him to the temple school (the priest taught alphabets and prayers). She fed stale chapatis to village dogs following them.

(c) City life

They moved to the city. She could no longer take him to school as his school taught English, laws of gravity, Archimedes' principle, western music – things she considered unholy. She stopped talking to him much. She spun at her spinning wheel and fed sparrows in the courtyard.

(d) Author goes abroad

He went to university abroad for five years. She was sad but didn't show it; she sang songs of the home-coming of warriors at the drum.

(e) Return and death

She was at the station, happy, no change. She didn't spin that day; she beat the drum with village women, sang. Next morning she fell ill with fever and died peacefully while praying. Hundreds of sparrows sat silently around her body; she was cremated; sparrows didn't eat the bread crumbs and flew away.

7. Short Questions & Answers

Q1. Who was the author's best friend as a child?His grandmother.
Q2. How does the author describe her face?Old, wrinkled and pale, so wrinkled that no more wrinkles could be added; she resembled a winter landscape.
Q3. Why did the grandmother stop talking to him in the city?He studied English, science and music, which she thought were unholy, and she could no longer help in his studies.
Q4. What did she do daily at city home?Spun at her wheel, read scriptures, fed sparrows in the afternoon.
Q5. What did she do when he left for abroad?She did not show grief; she kissed his forehead, as if it were the last time.
Q6. Why did she sing and beat the drum on his return?She was overjoyed; it was her way of celebrating his safe return.
Q7. How did the sparrows react to her death?Thousands sat silently; they didn't chirp or eat crumbs, and flew away after the funeral.
Q8. How did she spend her last hours?She prayed with the rosary, told everyone not to waste time mourning, and died peacefully.

8. Long Questions & Answers

L1. Describe the grandmother's character.She was loving, religious, simple and selfless. She cared for him in the village, accepted his modern ways without complaint, hid her sadness at parting, and remained cheerful. Her death was peaceful; even sparrows mourned her. Her love never changed in time.
L2. How did the author's relationship with her change?In the village they were close: she bathed, dressed and walked him to school. In the city, his English education made them drift; their bond shifted from talk to quiet companionship, but her love stayed same. After he returned, the bond was clear again.
L3. Why is the title "The Portrait of a Lady" apt?The chapter paints a word-picture of her looks, habits and nature, from white hair to rosary. It captures her whole personality and so the title fits.
