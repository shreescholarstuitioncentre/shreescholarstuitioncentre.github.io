Letter Writing — Class 11 English (Writing Skills) Notes

1. What is a Letter?

A letter is a written message sent to a person or an organisation. The style depends on the receiver: formal for officials and organisations, informal for friends and relatives.

2. Types of Letters

TypeExamplesStyle
Formalapplication, letter to the editor, complaint, business/order letter, enquirypolite, brief, formal words
Informal (personal)to friend, brother, parents, relativefriendly, warm, simple words

3. Parts of a Letter

  1. Sender's address: top right (not needed in some official letters with letterhead).
  2. Date: below the address.
  3. Receiver's address / designation: on the left (formal letters).
  4. Subject line: short summary of the purpose (formal).
  5. Salutation: Sir / Madam (formal); Dear + name (informal).
  6. Body: introduction, details, request or conclusion.
  7. Subscription (closing): Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely / Yours lovingly.
  8. Signature and name.

5. Closing Rules

  • 'Sir/Madam' → 'Yours faithfully'.
  • 'Dear Mr/Ms Name' → 'Yours sincerely'.
  • Friends → 'Yours affectionately'; parents → 'Your loving son/daughter'.

6. Steps to Write a Letter

  1. Read the question; find who is the receiver and what is the purpose.
  2. Decide formal or informal.
  3. Use the full format.
  4. Write the body in short paragraphs: purpose, details, request.
  5. Close politely.
  6. Check spelling, grammar and word limit.

7. Tips

  • Use the name given in the question; if no name is given, use a name like 'Rahul Sharma' carefully, or 'Xyz', as your teacher suggests.
  • Be polite, brief and clear.
  • Keep a margin, neat handwriting and paragraphs.

8. Worked Example (step by step)

Question: Write a letter to the editor of a newspaper about noise pollution near schools.

  1. Step 1 – Receiver: The Editor → formal letter.
  2. Step 2 – Format: sender's address, date, receiver, subject, salutation 'Sir', body, 'Yours faithfully', name.
  3. Step 3 – Body plan: opening line; problem; effect; solution; hope.
  4. Step 4 – Write:
12, Gandhi Nagar,
Ghaziabad – 201001
25 October 2026

The Editor,
The Daily News,
New Delhi.

Subject: Noise pollution near schools

Sir,
Through the columns of your newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to the growing noise pollution near our schools. Loud horns and music disturb classes and harm students' health. The traffic police should ban horns near schools and punish offenders. I hope the authorities will act soon.

Yours faithfully,
Rahul Sharma

11. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. What is a letter?

A written message sent to a person or organisation.

Q. What are the two main types of letters?

Formal and informal.

Q. Give two examples of formal letters.

Application and letter to the editor.

Q. Where is the date written?

Below the sender's address.

Q. What is a subject line?

A short line giving the purpose of the letter.

Q. What is a salutation?

The greeting at the beginning, e.g. Sir, Dear Rohan.

Q. What is a subscription?

The closing words, e.g. Yours faithfully.

Q. When do we write 'Yours faithfully'?

When the letter begins with Sir or Madam.

Q. Explain the parts of a formal letter.

Sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body, subscription and signature.

Q. How is a formal letter different from an informal one?

A formal letter has a receiver's address and a subject line and uses formal words; an informal letter is friendly and has neither.

Q. What should the body of a letter contain?

The introduction of the purpose, the details, and a polite request or conclusion.

Q. What mistakes should be avoided in letters?

Wrong format, slang in formal letters, no subject, long paragraphs, wrong closing and spelling errors.

Q. What is a letter to the editor?

A formal letter in which a reader gives views on a public issue; it is addressed to the editor of a newspaper.

Q. Why is a polite tone important?

It creates a good impression and gets a better response.

Q. Write a short note on the importance of letter writing.

Letters help us communicate formally and personally. They teach us to organise ideas, use correct language and follow social etiquette.
