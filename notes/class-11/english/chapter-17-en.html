✎ Albert Einstein at School — Class 11 Snapshots, Ch 4

Author: Patrick Pringle  |  Genre: Biographical sketch / dramatic episode  |  Setting: Luitpold Gymnasium, Munich, Germany, 1890s

1. Introduction

The story shows young Albert Einstein, later a great scientist, as a free-thinking schoolboy who disliked rote learning and strict military-style discipline. His questions displeased teachers, who called him a poor student. The story proves that a genius is not always recognised in school, and that original thinking needs freedom.

2. Characters

  • Albert Einstein: Curious, independent, honest, brave; dislikes mechanical learning.
  • Greek teacher: Strict, proud, rigid; sees Einstein's questions as disrespect.
  • Headmaster: Representative of the stiff school system.
  • Einstein's parents: His family had moved to Italy (Milan), leaving him in Munich to finish school.

3. Summary

  1. Einstein is in school in Munich. He hates rote memorising and strict discipline and likes to think and question.
  2. His family has moved to Italy; he lives alone and is unhappy. He wants to leave school and join them.
  3. He gets a medical certificate from a doctor saying he needs rest because of nervous exhaustion, giving him a reason to leave.
  4. His Greek teacher tells him that he will never amount to anything, and says that Einstein's presence in the class destroys the respect of the class for him. This hurts but also frees Einstein.
  5. Einstein leaves school and joins his family in Italy. Later he completes studies in Switzerland and becomes one of history's greatest physicists, proving his teacher wrong.

4. Themes

  • Rote learning vs understanding: Education should encourage thinking.
  • Freedom and individuality: Original minds need space.
  • Authority and irony: The teacher who said he would amount to nothing is forgotten; the student becomes world-famous.
  • Courage: Einstein faced authority honestly.

8. Long Q&A

L1. Sketch the character of Einstein as a student. (150 words)Einstein is curious, independent and thoughtful. He dislikes mechanical learning and strict rules, preferring to understand ideas. He is honest and brave in front of authority, and sensitive in loneliness. Teachers see him as rebellious, yet his questions show a scientific mind. His departure from school marks the start of a path to greatness.
L2. How does the story criticise the system of education?It shows teachers valuing obedience and memory over understanding. Such discipline harms creativity and makes a gifted child feel unwanted. The teacher's wrong prediction shows how schools can misjudge talent. The story suggests that education should encourage curiosity and questions.
L3. Why is the title ironic or significant?It shows a future genius as a "problem" student. Readers know his later achievements, which makes the teacher's judgement ironic. The title highlights the gap between school evaluation and real ability.
