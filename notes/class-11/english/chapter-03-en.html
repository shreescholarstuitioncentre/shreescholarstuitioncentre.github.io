1. Introduction

A non-fiction, scientific report (National Geographic style) about the mystery of Pharaoh Tutankhamun (King Tut), the boy-king of Egypt. It tells how his tomb was found and how modern science (X-ray, CT scan) reinvestigated how he died.

2. Animated Timeline: Tut's Story

3. Summary (Detailed)

(a) Who was Tut?

Tutankhamun became pharaoh at about nine and died around 18-19. Egypt's rulers were worshipped as gods; he is remembered because his tomb survived almost intact.

(b) Discovery of the Tomb

In 1922, English archaeologist Howard Carter, backed by Lord Carnarvon, found the tomb in the Valley of the Kings. It held gold treasures, a golden coffin and his mummy. Stories of a "curse of the pharaohs" spread after Carnarvon died in 1923.

(c) First Examination (1925)

Carter's team, with anatomist Douglas Derry, examined the mummy. It was stuck to the coffin by hardened resins, so it was removed in pieces, damaging it. This raised questions later.

(d) 1968 X-ray

A team from the University of Liverpool (led by R. G. Harrison) X-rayed the mummy. They found loose bone fragments inside the skull and a thickening at the back, suggesting a blow to the head, so murder was suspected by rivals such as Ay or Horemheb.

(e) 2005 CT Scan

An Egyptian team led by Dr Zahi Hawass, with a portable CT scanner, took about 1,700 images of the mummy. Findings: no evidence of a blow to the head; bone fragments were probably loosened during the 1925 handling; but a broken left leg existed – possibly an infection after a fracture, which could have killed him.

(f) Face Reconstruction

Three teams (French, American, Egyptian) built a face of Tut from the scans; features looked similar, though final judgment is that his death remains not fully solved.

8. Long Q&A

L1. How did science change the story of Tut's death?Earlier X-rays suggested murder because of skull fragments. The CT scan of 2005 showed these were probably caused by handling in 1925, ruled out a blow, and revealed a leg fracture and possible infection. Science thus replaced guesses with evidence, yet some mystery remains.
L2. Describe the discovery of Tut's tomb.In 1922 Howard Carter found the tomb in the Valley of the Kings after years of search. It had gold treasures and the mummy. Carnarvon's death in 1923 led to the curse legend, though scientists see no proof.
L3. Why is the chapter titled "The Saga Continues"?Because the study of Tut did not end with the 1922 find: examinations in 1925, 1968 and 2005 kept adding new facts, and questions still remain.
