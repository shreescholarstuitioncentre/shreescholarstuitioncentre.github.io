🎈 Childhood — Markus Natten 🎈

Class 11 English (Hornbill / UP Board) • Poem Notes

1. About the Poet

Markus Natten – a modern poet. Very little biographical detail is widely available, so for exams focus on the poem itself. “Childhood” is included in the Class 11 Hornbill book. (Write only what your textbook gives.)

2. Central Idea (≈150 words)

“Childhood” is a reflective poem in which the poet asks: when and where did my childhood go? He cannot name a single day when it ended. He suggests it faded as he grew up and began to see the world differently. He started to notice the hypocrisy of adults, who say one thing and do another, and he realised that ideas like heaven and hell were not simply places to be found in space, but belonged to the mind and to life itself. With such knowledge, wonder and innocence slowly vanished and he began to hide behind pretence, like grown-ups. At the end he imagines that childhood has gone to a forgotten place, hidden in the face of an infant. The poem is about loss of innocence, the pain of growing up, and the beauty of a child’s pure, honest view of life. It leaves us nostalgic and thoughtful.

4. Stanza-wise Summary & Explanation

Part 1 – The question

The poet asks when his childhood left. Was it when he understood that heaven and hell are not found in outer space? Childhood’s simple beliefs were challenged by thinking and reasoning. Device: rhetorical question.

Part 2 – Growing awareness

Did it go when he realised that adults can be hypocrites, and that he too had begun to hide his real self? Knowledge and self-consciousness replaced innocence.

Part 3 – The lost place

The poet asks where it went. He concludes it has gone to some forgotten place, hidden in the face of an infant: innocence survives in new children. Tone: sad, nostalgic, wondering.

6. Literary Devices

Rhetorical questions (When did it go? Where?). Personification: childhood ‘goes’ to a place. Contrast: child vs adult, innocence vs hypocrisy. Imagery: infant’s face. Repetition of questions for emphasis. Free verse / simple language. Tone: reflective, nostalgic.

9. Long Q&A

L1. Why can’t the poet say exactly when his childhood ended?Childhood does not end on a single day; it fades gradually. Each new realisation, such as the unreal nature of simple beliefs and adults’ hypocrisy, removes a little innocence. By the time he notices, it has already gone.
L2. Discuss the poem’s theme of hypocrisy.The poet notices that adults often do not practise what they say. Seeing this destroys a child’s trust and teaches him to pretend, too. This is one of the main reasons innocence disappears.
L3. What does the ending suggest?Childhood is not truly destroyed; it moves to a hidden place in an infant’s face. Every baby carries the innocence we have lost, giving hope and a cyclic view of life.
L4. Comment on the style of the poem.The poem uses simple language, rhetorical questions and free verse. The question–answer pattern gives a thoughtful, conversational tone.
L5. Is the poem sad or hopeful?Both. It mourns lost innocence but also finds hope in the infant’s face.
