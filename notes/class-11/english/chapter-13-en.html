👨‍👦 Father to Son — Elizabeth Jennings 👨‍👦

Class 11 English (Hornbill / UP Board) • Poem Notes

1. About the Poet

Elizabeth Jennings (1926–2001) – English poet, associated with a group of poets called “The Movement”. Her poems are known for clear, simple language, controlled form and deep feeling on faith, love, loneliness and human relationships. She wrote A Way of Looking and Collected Poems.

2. Central Idea (≈150 words)

“Father to Son” is spoken by a father who feels he no longer understands his son. They live in the same house, yet a wall of silence separates them. The father is hurt and confused; he does not know where the gap began, or whether he himself is to blame. He remembers the child he once knew and wishes for a way back to closeness. He also thinks of the biblical story of the prodigal son, wishing his son would come home so that he could welcome him with joy and forgiveness. But the son seems distant and the father cannot find the right words to reach him. The poem is about the generation gap, lack of communication and a parent’s unconditional love. It ends on a note of longing and hope: although understanding is missing, the father’s love remains strong and he hopes for reconciliation, and that the gap can be bridged by patience, openness and affection.

4. Summary & Explanation (Part-wise)

Part 1 – The father’s confusion

The father admits he cannot understand his son though they live together. They are like strangers. He feels he has failed and wonders about his fault.

Part 2 – The silence and the gap

Between them is silence. The father wants to reach out but does not know how. He remembers the closeness of the past and compares it to the distance now.

Part 3 – The prodigal son & hope

He longs for his son to return like the prodigal son of the Bible, so that he can welcome and forgive him. The poem ends with hope that love will heal the gap.

6. Literary Devices

Allusion: the Biblical Prodigal Son. Contrast: past closeness vs present distance. Imagery: silence as a barrier. Metaphor: the wall/gap between hearts. First-person voice (dramatic monologue-like). Simple, plain diction. Tone: sad, regretful, loving, hopeful.

9. Long Q&A

L1. Describe the relationship between the father and son.They live in the same house but are emotionally distant. The father loves his son but cannot understand him; the son appears silent and remote. Their relationship is marked by sadness, misunderstanding and longing for closeness.
L2. What is the generation gap? How is it shown?It is the difference in ideas, values and behaviour between age groups. In the poem it appears as silence and the father’s inability to understand his son’s world.
L3. Explain the allusion to the prodigal son.In the Bible a son leaves home, wastes his wealth and returns repentant, and the father welcomes him joyfully. The poet’s father wishes for such a reunion, showing his readiness to forgive.
L4. Is the poem pessimistic or hopeful?It is mostly sad because there is no understanding, but it contains hope in the father’s lasting love and his wish for reconciliation.
L5. What message does the poem give?Parents and children must communicate openly and patiently; love must be expressed, and generational differences can be bridged with understanding.
