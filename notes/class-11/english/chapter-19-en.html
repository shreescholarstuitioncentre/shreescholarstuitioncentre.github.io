✎ The Ghat of the Only World — Class 11 Snapshots, Ch 6

Author: Amitav Ghosh  |  Genre: Memoir / tribute essay  |  Subject: The poet Agha Shahid Ali (1949–2001)

1. Introduction

Amitav Ghosh remembers his friend Agha Shahid Ali, a Kashmiri-born, American-based poet, who died of brain cancer in December 2001. The essay is a tribute to a life lived with courage, wit, love of poetry and friendship. It also shows how a person's roots (Kashmir, India, Islam, Hindu ghats) stay with him across continents. The title comes from Shahid's poem about the ghat (river steps), meaning the shore of this one world, the only world we know.

2. Characters

  • Agha Shahid Ali: Poet, Kashmiri-born, lived in the USA; witty, warm, generous, brave in illness.
  • Amitav Ghosh (narrator): Close friend, writer; shares memories and mourns.
  • Shahid's family and friends: Gather around him in Amherst, Massachusetts, in his last days.

3. Summary

  1. Ghosh first recalls Shahid as a lively friend and fine poet, devoted to poetry and music (especially ghazals and Begum Akhtar).
  2. In 2001 Shahid is told he has brain cancer. He keeps his humour and continues to write and meet friends.
  3. The friends gather at his home in Amherst. They talk, laugh and recall poems and memories; Shahid refuses to be gloomy.
  4. Shahid dies in December 2001. His funeral blends faith and friendship; poets and loved ones remember him.
  5. Ghosh ends by thinking of Shahid's idea of the ghat, the shore of this only world, where one steps from this life to the next, and keeps Shahid alive through memory.

4. Themes

  • Friendship and love: Deep affection between writers.
  • Death and courage: Facing death with humour and dignity.
  • Roots and identity: Kashmiri, Indian and American belonging.
  • Poetry and memory: Art keeps people alive.
  • Religious harmony: Shahid's open, secular spirit that loves both Hindu and Muslim traditions.

8. Long Q&A

L1. Sketch the character of Agha Shahid Ali. (150 words)Shahid is a gifted poet, witty and affectionate. He loves poetry, music and friends and mixes Kashmiri, Indian and American worlds. Even when facing a fatal illness he stays brave and humorous, comforting those around him. His open, secular mind respects many faiths. He is remembered as a man of warmth and courage.
L2. Explain the significance of the title.A ghat is where life and death meet: people bathe, pray and say farewell at it. "The only world" suggests that this world is all we truly know. The title thus expresses Shahid's passing and the idea that life is a shore from which one steps away, yet memory remains.
L3. How does the essay show the author's friendship with Shahid?Ghosh recalls shared talks, poems and laughter. He stays close during Shahid's illness and records his bravery. His tender, personal tone makes the essay a living tribute that preserves the friend's spirit.
