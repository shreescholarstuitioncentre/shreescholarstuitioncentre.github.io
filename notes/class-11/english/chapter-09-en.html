A Photograph — Shirley Toulson (Class 11 Hornbill, Poem) Notes

1. About the Poet

Shirley Toulson (1924–2010) was an English poet and writer. Her poems often deal with family, memory and everyday life. A Photograph is a short, moving poem about her mother.

2. Form & Setting

  • Form: a short poem of three stanzas, in free, simple language.
  • Speaker: the poet, looking at an old photograph.
  • Setting: the seaside shown in the picture and the poet's present room.
  • Tone: reflective, nostalgic, sad.

3. Central Idea (150 words)

The poem 'A Photograph' by Shirley Toulson is a quiet reflection on time, memory and loss. The poet looks at an old photograph of her mother, taken when she was twelve, paddling at the seaside with two cousins. The girls are laughing, carefree and full of life. The poet imagines the scene and the joy of that long-ago summer day, which the old picture has frozen. Years later, the mother has died, but the sea remains the same, a symbol of nature's permanence compared with human life, which passes. In the last stanza, the poet realises that she can no longer share her mother's laughter or her grief with her. She feels a deep, speechless sorrow. The poem thus shows how a simple photograph can bring back the past, yet also remind us of what is gone. It also teaches us to value loved ones while they are with us.

Word count: 150.

4. Stanza-wise Summary

  1. Stanza 1: The poet describes a faded photograph of her mother as a twelve-year-old girl, at the seaside with two cousins. They are paddling and laughing, and someone has taken their picture.
  2. Stanza 2: The poet reflects on the picture. The sea is the same, but the girl in it has grown old and died. She seems to hear the laughter of that day.
  3. Stanza 3: The poet admits that the loss is deep and cannot be put into words; she is silent before the memory of her mother.

5. Themes

  • Passing of time and change.
  • Memory and the power of a photograph.
  • Death and loss of a loved one.
  • Contrast between nature's permanence and human life.
  • Silence and grief that words cannot express.

10. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'A Photograph'?

Shirley Toulson.

Q. What does the photograph show?

The poet's mother at the age of twelve with two cousins at the seaside.

Q. What are the girls doing?

Paddling in the sea and laughing.

Q. How old was the mother in the photo?

Twelve.

Q. What has happened to the mother now?

She has died.

Q. What does the sea symbolise?

Permanence of nature and time.

Q. What is the tone of the poem?

Reflective, nostalgic and sad.

Q. How does the poet react in the end?

She feels deep, speechless grief.

Q. What is the central idea of the poem?

The poem shows how a photograph can bring back the past, yet makes us feel the pain of loss and the passing of time.

Q. Why does the poet feel sad?

Because the girl in the photograph is no longer alive, and the poet cannot share her laughter or grief.

Q. Explain the contrast in the poem.

The photograph shows a happy, carefree past, while the present is full of loss and silence. The sea stays the same, but human life does not.

Q. What is the significance of the title?

The photograph is a means of memory; it freezes one moment and sets off the poet's reflection.

Q. What poetic devices are used?

Imagery, contrast, symbolism and nostalgia.

Q. Why does the poet say words cannot express her loss?

Deep grief goes beyond language; silence shows its depth.

Q. What message does the poem give?

Life is short, so we should value our loved ones and cherish memories.
