The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse — William Saroyan (Class 11 Snapshots, Ch. 1) Notes

1. About the Author

William Saroyan (1908–1981) was an Armenian-American writer from Fresno, California. His stories show poor immigrant families with humour, warmth and honesty. He won a Pulitzer Prize for his play The Time of Your Life (1940).

2. Setting & Narrator

  • Place: Fresno, California, USA; a poor Armenian community.
  • Time: a summer of the Great Depression era.
  • Narrator: Aram Garoghlanian, a nine-year-old boy, tells the story as a memory.

3. Theme

  • Innocence and the joy of childhood adventure.
  • Honesty and honour (the family was known for never stealing).
  • Poverty vs. richness of spirit.
  • Love for animals; the difference between 'borrowing' and 'stealing'.
  • Forgiveness and community bonds.

4. Summary (step by step)

  1. Early morning: Aram wakes at dawn and sees a beautiful white horse in the yard. His cousin Mourad is riding it.
  2. Mourad: He is thirteen and the family thinks him a little 'crazy'. He loves horses.
  3. Shock: Aram knows the family is poor and cannot buy a horse; Mourad's answer: he 'stole' it, but he says he will return it.
  4. Honour: Their family is famous for honesty, so Aram worries. Mourad says it is borrowing, not stealing, since he will not sell it.
  5. Riding: The boys hide the horse in a barn in an old vineyard and ride it for about two weeks. Aram learns to ride and enjoys it.
  6. Uncle Khosrove: A loud, stubborn uncle who says 'It is no harm'. He tells people to be quiet.
  7. Owner: The horse belongs to John Byro, a farmer who owned it and once lost it.
  8. Discovery: Byro recognizes his horse; the horse neighs at its owner's voice.
  9. Ending: The boys return it; Byro, pleased to see it healthier, forgives them. The summer ends with them missing the horse.

5. Characters

  • Aram – honest, innocent, loyal to cousin.
  • Mourad – bold, imaginative, horse-lover, rule-bender.
  • Uncle Khosrove – loud, proud, stubborn.
  • John Byro – farmer, owner of the horse; kind and forgiving.

6. Important Words

  • Vineyard – land of grapes
  • Mischief – playful trouble
  • Honour – respect for truth and duty
  • Immigrant – person who settles in another country
  • Stubborn – refusing to change
  • Tempt – attract to do something

9. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse'?

William Saroyan.

Q. Who is the narrator?

Aram Garoghlanian, a nine-year-old boy.

Q. Who is Mourad?

Aram's thirteen-year-old cousin, considered 'crazy' by the family.

Q. Who owned the horse?

John Byro, a farmer.

Q. What was the family famous for?

Honesty; no one in the family would steal.

Q. Where did the boys hide the horse?

In a barn in an old vineyard.

Q. Who is Uncle Khosrove?

A loud, stubborn and proud uncle.

Q. How long did they keep the horse?

About two weeks, as recalled in the story.

Q. Was Mourad a thief? Give reasons.

Mourad took the horse, but his aim was to ride and then return it, never to sell it. He calls it borrowing. Though legally wrong, his love for horses and his return of the animal show he is not a hardened thief.

Q. Why did the family consider Mourad crazy?

Because of his wild, unpredictable behaviour and his obsession with horses.

Q. How did John Byro recognize the horse?

He knew his horse; it neighed when it heard his voice.

Q. How does the story end?

The boys return the horse. Byro sees it is healthier and forgives them.

Q. Characterise Aram.

Aram is innocent, obedient and truthful, yet loyal to his cousin. He feels uneasy about taking the horse but enjoys the adventure.

Q. Discuss honesty as a theme.

The story presents family honour as the family's real wealth. The boys' guilt and the final return stress that honesty matters even in poverty.

Q. What is the tone of the story?

Humorous, warm and nostalgic.
