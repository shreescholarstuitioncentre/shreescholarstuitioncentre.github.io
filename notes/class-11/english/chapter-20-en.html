Birth — A.J. Cronin (Class 11 Snapshots, Ch. 7) Notes

1. About the Author

A.J. Cronin (1896–1981) was a Scottish doctor turned novelist. His fiction draws on medical life and social concerns. Birth is an extract from his novel The Citadel, featuring Dr Andrew Manson.

2. Setting & Narrator

  • Place: a small mining town in Wales.
  • Time: night, the early 20th century.
  • Narrator: third-person; the story follows young Dr Andrew Manson.

3. Theme

  • Duty and devotion of a doctor.
  • Persistence: not giving up when hope seems lost.
  • Value of life; the miracle of birth.
  • Skill, courage and compassion in medicine.
  • Relief, gratitude and human emotion.

4. Summary (step by step)

  1. Call at night: Young Dr Andrew Manson is called to attend a woman in labour.
  2. Arrival: He goes to her house and finds the mother, Susan, weak and exhausted.
  3. Delivery: The baby is born, but appears lifeless; the nurse thinks it is dead.
  4. Choice: Manson refuses to give up on the baby and turns to the mother, who is also in danger.
  5. Resuscitation: He tries to revive the infant, using alternate hot and cold water and patient effort.
  6. Success: After long effort, the baby starts to breathe and cries.
  7. Mother: He then makes sure the mother is safe.
  8. Ending: Exhausted but joyful, he leaves with deep satisfaction; the family is grateful.

5. Characters

  • Dr Andrew Manson – young, devoted, brave, persistent.
  • The mother (Susan) – weak and exhausted, yet hopeful.
  • The father – anxious and thankful.
  • The nurse / helper – experienced, but more ready to accept the loss.

6. Important Words

  • Labour – process of childbirth
  • Stillborn – born dead
  • Resuscitate – revive
  • Persistence – not giving up
  • Anxious – worried
  • Compassion – deep care for others

9. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. Who wrote 'Birth'?

A.J. Cronin, a Scottish doctor and novelist.

Q. From which novel is it taken?

The Citadel.

Q. Who is the doctor in the story?

Dr Andrew Manson.

Q. Why was Manson called at night?

To attend a woman in labour.

Q. What was the first problem after birth?

The baby appeared stillborn.

Q. What did Manson use to revive the baby?

Alternate hot and cold water along with persistent effort.

Q. Why is the story titled 'Birth'?

It shows the birth of a baby and also the birth of hope and of the doctor's confidence.

Q. Describe Dr Manson.

He is young and dedicated. He refuses to give up on the baby, remains calm in crisis and shows compassion and skill.

Q. Why did he give priority first to the baby, then to the mother?

He saw a chance to save the baby; once she breathed, the mother's safety needed care.

Q. How does the nurse's attitude differ from the doctor's?

She is ready to accept the baby's death, while he persists and revives it.

Q. What qualities of a good doctor does the story show?

Dedication, skill, calmness, empathy and persistence.

Q. What is the mood at the end?

Joyful, relieved and hopeful.

Q. Discuss the theme of persistence.

Manson continues despite exhaustion and apparent failure. His persistence saves a life, showing that effort can overcome the odds.

Q. What message does the story give?

Never give up hope; medical duty is a service to life.

Q. What does the story say about the value of human life?

Every life is precious; even a tiny chance is worth fighting for.
