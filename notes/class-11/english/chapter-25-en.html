Note-Making — Class 11 English (Writing Skills) Notes

1. What is Note-making?

Note-making is writing the main ideas of a passage in a short, organised form, using headings, sub-headings, abbreviations and symbols. It is used for study, revision, lectures and reports.

2. Why do we make notes?

  • To save time during revision.
  • To remember key points.
  • To organise ideas logically.
  • To prepare for writing a summary.

3. Format (parts of notes)

  1. Title: a short heading that gives the main idea of the passage.
  2. Headings and sub-headings: main points and their parts. Number them (1., 1.1, 1.1.1) or use I, A, a.
  3. Points: short phrases, not full sentences. Use only needed words.
  4. Abbreviations / symbols: use standard or self-made ones (e.g. govt., &, →, etc.).
  5. Key to abbreviations: list the abbreviations you have made, with meaning.
  6. Consistency: follow the same style and indentation throughout.

4. Steps to follow

  1. Read the passage carefully at least twice.
  2. Underline key words and ideas.
  3. Choose a suitable title.
  4. Find the main headings and sub-headings.
  5. Write points in short, clear phrases.
  6. Use abbreviations and symbols.
  7. Give a key to abbreviations.
  8. Check order, numbering and spelling.

5. Dos and Don'ts

  • Do: use telegraphic phrases, indent, number, keep logical order.
  • Do: keep to the passage; do not add your own ideas.
  • Don't: copy full sentences.
  • Don't: use unfamiliar abbreviations without a key.
  • Don't: forget the title.

7. Worked Example (step by step)

Passage:

Reading is a habit that gives many benefits. First, it improves the mind: it builds vocabulary, sharpens thinking and increases knowledge. Second, it helps health: it reduces stress and improves sleep. Third, it is cheap, since libraries lend books free and e-books cost little.
  1. Step 1 – Read: the passage speaks about benefits of reading: for the mind, for health, for cost.
  2. Step 2 – Title: 'Benefits of Reading'.
  3. Step 3 – Main headings: (1) Mental, (2) Health, (3) Economy.
  4. Step 4 – Sub-points: take only key words under each heading.
  5. Step 5 – Abbreviations: vocab. = vocabulary; lib. = library.
Title: Benefits of Reading
1. Mental
  1.1 ↑ vocab.
  1.2 sharper thinking
  1.3 ↑ knowledge
2. Health
  2.1 ↓ stress
  2.2 better sleep
3. Economy
  3.1 free books in lib.
  3.2 cheap e-books
Key: vocab. = vocabulary; lib. = library; ↑ = increase; ↓ = decrease

10. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. What is note-making?

Writing the key ideas of a text in short, organised form.

Q. What is the first thing in the notes?

The title.

Q. Should notes be in full sentences?

No, in short phrases.

Q. What is a key to abbreviations?

A list showing what each abbreviation stands for.

Q. Why should we number points?

To show order and the level of each idea.

Q. Give two common abbreviations.

govt. (government), info. (information).

Q. What are the steps of note-making?

Read, underline, title, headings, points, abbreviations, key, check.

Q. How do you choose a title?

It must be short and cover the main idea of the passage.

Q. Explain headings and sub-headings.

Headings show the main ideas; sub-headings show the parts of each idea. Together they give a clear, logical structure.

Q. How is note-making different from summary?

Notes are short points with headings and abbreviations; a summary is a short passage in sentences.

Q. Why do we use abbreviations?

They save time and space, but a key must be given.

Q. What mistakes should one avoid?

Copying sentences, skipping the title, using unlisted abbreviations and adding personal views.

Q. Write a note on the importance of note-making.

Note-making helps recall, organises ideas and saves time during revision. It also trains students to find the main ideas of a text.

Q. What is the use of indentation?

It shows how a point is related to a higher point.

Q. Can we add our own ideas?

No, notes must stay within the passage.
