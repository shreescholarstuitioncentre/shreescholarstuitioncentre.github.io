✎ The Browning Version — Class 11 Hornbill, Ch 6

Author: Terence Rattigan  |  Genre: One-act play (extract)  |  Setting: Sitting room of the Crocker-Harrises' flat, a public (boarding) school in England

1. Introduction

The play shows a strict, lonely Classics teacher, Andrew Crocker-Harris, who is retiring through ill health. Students fear and mock him; his wife Millie despises him. A small act of kindness from the boy Taplow breaks his hard shell and reveals the feeling man beneath. Theme: hidden humanity, loneliness, kindness and the failure of a teacher's career.

2. Characters (with relationship diagram)

  • Andrew Crocker-Harris ("the Crock"): Master of the Lower Fifth, scholarly, rigid, reserved; feared, not loved. Retiring early because of heart trouble, with no pension and a lesser post offered.
  • John Taplow: A 16-year-old pupil, sensitive and brave. Mimics the Crock for fun but is truly fond of him. Brings him a gift.
  • Frank Hunter: Young Science master, friendly; mimics the Crock, yet shows him sympathy. Millie's lover.
  • Millie: Crock's wife; sharp, unfaithful, cruel in words; she humiliates her husband.

3. Summary of the Extract

  1. Taplow comes to the Crock's flat for extra work in Greek. The Crock is late; Hunter arrives and chats. Taplow imitates the Crock's dry way of speaking; Hunter laughs, and Taplow says the Crock is really "not so bad".
  2. Taplow reveals the Crock has given him a promotion to the next class, but it is an act of favour, not of the usual strictness. He says he is fond of him.
  3. Millie enters. She and Hunter talk; she makes cruel remarks about Andrew's coldness and the boy's gift.
  4. Taplow had earlier given the Crock a gift: Browning's translation of Aeschylus' Agamemnon, with an inscription "God from afar looks graciously upon a gentle master." The Crock is deeply moved, nearly weeping.
  5. Millie pours scorn on the gift and implies Taplow only wants favours. The Crock, shaken, keeps dignity; his shell cracks and we see his pain.

4. Themes

  • Hidden humanity: Behind stern behaviour lies deep feeling.
  • Loneliness and failure: The Crock has no friends, no love, no pension.
  • Kindness: One small gift gives meaning to his life.
  • Cruelty: Millie's words wound; Hunter's and Taplow's mockery too.
  • Education: Teachers who teach by fear are disliked.

5. Literary Points

Irony (a feared master secretly loved) · Characterisation through dialogue · Title: Robert Browning's translation of Agamemnon – the Crock gives it a heart-felt meaning · Stage directions show emotion.

6. Difficult Words

Mimic – imitate · Remove – promotion to next form · Crammer – a school coaching for exams · Severe – harsh · Reserved – keeping feelings hidden.

7. Short Q&A

Q1. Who is the author?Terence Rattigan.
Q2. Why is Crocker-Harris called "the Crock"?Pupils nickname him so because he is old, strict and in poor health; the name suggests a worn-out man.
Q3. What did Taplow give him?Browning's verse translation of Aeschylus' Agamemnon, with a kind inscription.
Q4. How did the Crock react to the gift?He was moved to tears; the unexpected kindness broke his emotional reserve.
Q5. What is Hunter's attitude to the Crock?Friendly and sympathetic; he laughs at the mimicry but is uneasy about Millie's treatment of Andrew.
Q6. Why is the Crock retiring?Because of ill health (heart trouble), and no pension is given.
Q7. How does Millie behave?Cold, sarcastic and cruel; she insults Andrew and doubts the boy's motive.
Q8. What is "dramatic irony" here?The audience knows about Millie's affair and Crock's feelings while characters do not.

8. Long Q&A

L1. Sketch the character of Andrew Crocker-Harris. (150 words)Andrew is a learned but strict Classics master. Pupils fear him and mock him. He hides his feelings behind a cold manner, and his marriage is loveless. His career ends in failure, with no pension. Yet Taplow's gift reveals his tender side; he is moved to tears. He is thus a tragic but human figure whose strictness covers a lonely heart.
L2. What is the significance of Taplow's gift?The gift, Browning's translation of Agamemnon, shows that at least one pupil sees the man behind the master. The inscription calls him gentle. It gives the Crock proof that he mattered, breaks his reserve, and forms the climax of the play. It contrasts with Millie's cruelty.
L3. Discuss the theme of kindness vs cruelty.Millie's words and the pupils' mockery show cruelty; Hunter's concern and Taplow's gift show kindness. The play suggests that one kind act can heal long loneliness, while thoughtless cruelty deepens it.
