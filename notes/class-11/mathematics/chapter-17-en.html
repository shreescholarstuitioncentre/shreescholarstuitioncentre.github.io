Class 11 Maths – Ch 18: Differentiation

Handwritten-style notes • UP Board / NCERT

1. Derivative as Rate of Change

Average rate of change of y=f(x) over [x, x+h] = Δy/Δx = [f(x+h)−f(x)]/h. Taking h→0 gives the instantaneous rate:

dy/dx = limΔx→0 Δy/Δx
Examples: velocity v=ds/dt; acceleration a=dv/dt; rate of change of area of circle dA/dr = 2πr. dy/dx>0 ⇒ y increasing, <0 ⇒ decreasing.

2. Derivative as Slope of Tangent

As Q slides to P along the curve, the secant PQ becomes the tangent at P. Slope of secant = Δy/Δx → slope of tangent.

Slope of tangent at x=a: m = f'(a) = tan θ

Equation of tangent at (x₁,y₁): y − y₁ = f'(x₁)(x − x₁). Normal slope = −1/f'(x₁). Tangent ∥ x-axis ⇔ f'=0.

3. Definition of Derivative

First principle (ab-initio):

f'(x) = limh→0 [f(x+h) − f(x)] / h

Notations: f'(x), dy/dx, Dy, y₁. Left derivative (h→0⁻) and right derivative (h→0⁺); derivative exists iff both are equal.

Differentiable ⇒ continuous, but not conversely: |x| is continuous at 0 but not differentiable there (corner).

4. Algebra of Derivatives (Sum, Difference, Product, Quotient)

  • Constant multiple: (cu)' = c·u'
  • Sum: (u+v)' = u'+v'
  • Difference: (u−v)' = u'−v'
Product: (uv)' = u'v + uv'
Quotient: (u/v)' = (u'v − uv') / v² , v≠0

Proof of product rule: [u(x+h)v(x+h) − u(x)v(x)]/h = u(x+h)·[v(x+h)−v(x)]/h + v(x)·[u(x+h)−u(x)]/h. As h→0 (u continuous): u·v' + v·u'. ∎

5. Derivatives of Polynomial Functions

  • d/dx (c) = 0   d/dx (x) = 1
d/dx (xⁿ) = n xⁿ⁻¹

Proof: f'(x)=limh→0 [(x+h)ⁿ − xⁿ]/h = limz→x (zⁿ−xⁿ)/(z−x) = n xⁿ⁻¹. ∎

So d/dx (a₀xⁿ + a₁xⁿ⁻¹ + … + aₙ) = n a₀ xⁿ⁻¹ + (n−1) a₁ xⁿ⁻² + …

6. Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions

f(x)f'(x)
sin xcos x
cos x−sin x
tan xsec²x
cot x−cosec²x
sec xsec x tan x
cosec x−cosec x cot x

Proofs of sin x and cos x use first principle with sin C − sin D formula and lim sin h/h = 1; the rest follow from the quotient rule.

Q3. Physical meaning?Instantaneous rate of change of f with respect to x.
Q4. State the product rule.(uv)'=u'v+uv'.
Q5. State the quotient rule.(u/v)'=(u'v−uv')/v².
Q6. d/dx(cos x)?−sin x.
Q7. Does continuity imply differentiability?No. |x| at 0 is a counter-example.
