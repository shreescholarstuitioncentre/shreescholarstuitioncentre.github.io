Rectangular Cartesian Coordinates

Class 11 • Chapter 10 • Handwritten-style notes
Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. Cartesian Coordinate System

Given by René Descartes. Two perpendicular number lines meet at a point; together they fix the position of every point of a plane. This plane is the Cartesian (coordinate) plane.

2. Coordinate Axes

Horizontal line XOX′ = x-axis; vertical line YOY′ = y-axis. They meet at the origin O(0,0).

Right of O and above O are positive; left of O and below O are negative.

Any point on the x-axis is (a, 0); on the y-axis is (0, b).

3. Quadrants

The axes divide the plane into 4 quadrants: I (+,+), II (−,+), III (−,−), IV (+,−), counted anticlockwise.

Points lying on the axes belong to no quadrant.

4. Coordinates of a Point

A point is written as an ordered pair (x, y): x = abscissa (signed distance from y-axis), y = ordinate (signed distance from x-axis).

(a, b) ≠ (b, a) unless a = b. Distance from x-axis = |y|, from y-axis = |x|, from origin = √(x²+y²).

Reflection of (x, y): in x-axis → (x, −y); in y-axis → (−x, y); in origin → (−x, −y).

5. Distance Formula

Distance between A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂):

AB = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]

Proof: draw AC ∥ x-axis and BC ⊥ it; in right triangle ACB, AB² = AC² + BC² (Pythagoras) = (x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)². Watch the diagram build.

Uses: collinear if AB + BC = AC; isosceles/equilateral/right triangle by comparing sides; parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle, square tests.

6. Section Formula

P divides AB internally in ratio m : n:

P = ( (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m+n) , (my₂ + ny₁)/(m+n) )

Externally in ratio m : n:

P = ( (mx₂ − nx₁)/(m−n) , (my₂ − ny₁)/(m−n) )

The x-axis divides AB in ratio −y₁ : y₂; the y-axis divides it in −x₁ : x₂ (negative ⇒ external).

7. Midpoint Formula

Put m = n = 1 in the section formula:

M = ( (x₁+x₂)/2 , (y₁+y₂)/2 )

Centroid of a triangle: ((x₁+x₂+x₃)/3, (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3). Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other (same midpoint).

8. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY Find the distance between (2, 3) and (5, 7).
  1. Step 1: x₂−x₁ = 3, y₂−y₁ = 4
  2. Step 2: d = √(3² + 4²) = √(9+16)
  3. Step 3: = √25 = 5
Example 2 EASY Find the midpoint of (−2, 4) and (6, −8).
  1. Step 1: x = (−2+6)/2 = 2
  2. Step 2: y = (4+(−8))/2 = −2
  3. Step 3: Midpoint = (2, −2)
Example 3 EASY In which quadrant does (−3, 5) lie? Find its reflection in the x-axis.
  1. Step 1: x < 0, y > 0 → second quadrant
  2. Step 2: Reflection in x-axis: (x, −y)
  3. Step 3: = (−3, −5)
Example 4 MEDIUM Find the point on the x-axis equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9).
  1. Step 1: Let the point be P(x, 0)
  2. Step 2: PA² = PB²: (x−2)² + 25 = (x+2)² + 81
  3. Step 3: x² − 4x + 29 = x² + 4x + 85
  4. Step 4: −8x = 56 → x = −7; P = (−7, 0)
Example 5 MEDIUM Find the point dividing (1, −3) and (−3, 9) internally in the ratio 1 : 3.
  1. Step 1: m = 1, n = 3, (x₁,y₁) = (1,−3), (x₂,y₂) = (−3,9)
  2. Step 2: x = (1·(−3) + 3·1)/4 = 0
  3. Step 3: y = (1·9 + 3·(−3))/4 = 0
  4. Step 4: Point = (0, 0)
Example 6 MEDIUM In what ratio does the x-axis divide the join of (2, −3) and (5, 6)?
  1. Step 1: Ratio = −y₁ : y₂ = 3 : 6 = 1 : 2 (positive → internal)
  2. Step 2: x = (1·5 + 2·2)/3 = 3
  3. Step 3: Point = (3, 0), ratio 1 : 2
Example 7 HARD Show that A(−4,−1), B(−2,−4), C(4,0), D(2,3) form a rectangle.
  1. Step 1: AB = √(4+9) = √13, CD = √(4+9) = √13
  2. Step 2: BC = √(36+16) = √52, DA = √(36+16) = √52 (opposite sides equal)
  3. Step 3: AC = √(64+1) = √65, BD = √(16+49) = √65 (diagonals equal)
  4. Step 4: Parallelogram with equal diagonals ⟹ rectangle ∎
Example 8 HARD Show that (0,0), (3,√3), (0,2√3) are vertices of an equilateral triangle.
  1. Step 1: OA = √(9+3) = 2√3
  2. Step 2: AB = √(9+3) = 2√3
  3. Step 3: OB = 2√3
  4. Step 4: All sides equal ⟹ equilateral ∎
Example 9 HARD Find x if the distance between (x, 2) and (3, −6) is 10.
  1. Step 1: √[(x−3)² + (2+6)²] = 10
  2. Step 2: (x−3)² + 64 = 100 → (x−3)² = 36
  3. Step 3: x − 3 = ±6
  4. Step 4: x = 9 or x = −3
Example 10 HARD Show that (1,−1), (2,1), (4,5) are collinear.
  1. Step 1: AB = √(1+4) = √5
  2. Step 2: BC = √(4+16) = √20 = 2√5
  3. Step 3: AC = √(9+36) = √45 = 3√5
  4. Step 4: AB + BC = √5 + 2√5 = 3√5 = AC ⟹ collinear ∎

9. Short Q&A

Q1. State the distance formula.

Ans AB = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²].

Q2. What are abscissa and ordinate?

Ans x-coordinate is the abscissa; y-coordinate is the ordinate.

Q3. State the internal section formula.

Ans P = ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n)).

Q4. Which quadrant is (−,+)?

Ans Second quadrant.

Q5. Write the midpoint formula.

Ans ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2).
