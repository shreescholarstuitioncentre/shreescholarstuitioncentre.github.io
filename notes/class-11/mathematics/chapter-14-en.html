Conic Section – Hyperbola

Class 11 • Chapter 15 • Handwritten-style notes
Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. Definition

A hyperbola is the locus of a point P that moves so that the difference of its distances from two fixed points (foci F₁, F₂) is constant: |PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a, with 2a < F₁F₂ = 2c.

It is a conic section with e > 1, got by cutting a double cone with a plane parallel to its axis. It has two separate branches.

2. Terms: Foci, Transverse & Conjugate Axis

Foci: (±c, 0). Centre C: midpoint of F₁F₂. Vertices: A, A′ = (±a, 0).

Transverse axis: line segment A′A through the foci, length 2a.

Conjugate axis: segment B′B through the centre ⟂ transverse axis, length 2b, B, B′ = (0, ±b) (these points are not on the curve).

Auxiliary rectangle: sides x = ±a, y = ±b; its diagonals are the asymptotes. Directrices: x = ±a/e.

3. Standard Equation (Derivation)

Take F₁(−c, 0), F₂(c, 0), |PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a (watch the diagram):

√[(x+c)² + y²] − √[(x−c)² + y²] = ±2a

Square twice and simplify: (c² − a²)x² − a²y² = a²(c² − a²)

Put b² = c² − a² (so c² = a² + b², unlike the ellipse) and divide by a²b²:

x²/a² − y²/b² = 1

4. Asymptotes

Asymptotes are lines the curve approaches but never meets as x → ∞. For x²/a² − y²/b² = 1:

y = ± (b/a) x    (i.e. x²/a² − y²/b² = 0)

For y²/a² − x²/b² = 1: y = ± (a/b) x. They are the diagonals of the auxiliary rectangle.

Rectangular hyperbola: a = b, asymptotes ⟂, equation x² − y² = a², e = √2.

5. Eccentricity & Latus Rectum

e = c/a > 1

b² = c² − a² ⟹ b² = a²(e² − 1). Foci (±ae, 0).

Latus rectum = 2b²/a, ends (±c, ±b²/a).

Focal distances of P(x₁, y₁) on the right branch: PF₁ = ex₁ + a, PF₂ = ex₁ − a; difference 2a.

Tip: the variable with the positive sign is the transverse axis direction.

7. Properties

Symmetric about both axes; no part of the curve lies between x = −a and x = a.

Parametric form: (a sec θ, b tan θ). Auxiliary circle: x² + y² = a².

Conjugate hyperbola of x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 is −x²/a² + y²/b² = 1; both share the same asymptotes.

Point (x₁, y₁): x₁²/a² − y₁²/b² − 1 > 0 inside (a branch), < 0 outside.

Real life: cooling towers, LORAN navigation, sonic boom, telescope mirrors.

8. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY For x²/16 − y²/9 = 1 find foci, vertices, axes, e, latus rectum and asymptotes.
  1. Step 1: a² = 16 → a = 4; b² = 9 → b = 3
  2. Step 2: c = √(a²+b²) = √25 = 5 → foci (±5, 0); vertices (±4, 0)
  3. Step 3: Transverse axis 2a = 8, conjugate axis 2b = 6; e = c/a = 5/4
  4. Step 4: Latus rectum = 2b²/a = 18/4 = 9/2; asymptotes y = ±(3/4)x
Example 2 EASY For 16x² − 9y² = 144 find foci and e.
  1. Step 1: Divide by 144: x²/9 − y²/16 = 1
  2. Step 2: a = 3, b = 4, c = √(9+16) = 5
  3. Step 3: Foci (±5, 0)
  4. Step 4: e = 5/3
Example 3 EASY For y²/16 − x²/9 = 1 find foci, vertices, e and asymptotes.
  1. Step 1: Positive term is y² → transverse axis along y: a² = 16, b² = 9
  2. Step 2: c = √25 = 5 → foci (0, ±5); vertices (0, ±4)
  3. Step 3: e = 5/4
  4. Step 4: Asymptotes y = ±(a/b)x = ±(4/3)x
Example 4 MEDIUM Find the hyperbola with vertices (±2, 0) and foci (±3, 0).
  1. Step 1: a = 2, c = 3
  2. Step 2: b² = c² − a² = 9 − 4 = 5
  3. Step 3: x²/4 − y²/5 = 1
Example 5 MEDIUM Find the hyperbola with foci (0, ±√10) passing through (2, 3).
  1. Step 1: Foci on y-axis: y²/a² − x²/b² = 1, a² + b² = 10
  2. Step 2: (2,3): 9/a² − 4/b² = 1; b² = 10 − a² → a⁴ − 23a² + 90 = 0
  3. Step 3: a² = 5 or 18; a² = 18 gives b² < 0 ✘ → a² = 5, b² = 5
  4. Step 4: y²/5 − x²/5 = 1, i.e. y² − x² = 5 (check: 9 − 4 = 5 ✔)
Example 6 MEDIUM Find e and the foci of a rectangular hyperbola x² − y² = a².
  1. Step 1: Write x²/a² − y²/a² = 1 → b = a
  2. Step 2: c² = a² + b² = 2a² → c = a√2
  3. Step 3: e = c/a = √2
  4. Step 4: Foci (±a√2, 0)
Example 7 HARD Prove that the latus rectum of x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 is 2b²/a and that b² = a²(e² − 1).
  1. Step 1: b² = c² − a² = a²e² − a² = a²(e² − 1) ✔
  2. Step 2: Latus rectum through (c, 0): c²/a² − y²/b² = 1
  3. Step 3: y² = b²(c²/a² − 1) = b²·b²/a² → y = ±b²/a
  4. Step 4: Length = 2b²/a ∎
Example 8 HARD Prove that for P(x₁, y₁) on the right branch PF₁ − PF₂ = 2a.
  1. Step 1: PF₁² = (x₁+c)² + y₁², y₁² = b²(x₁²/a² − 1)
  2. Step 2: = x₁²(1 + b²/a²) + 2cx₁ + c² − b² = e²x₁² + 2aex₁ + a²
  3. Step 3: = (ex₁ + a)²; similarly PF₂² = (ex₁ − a)² (ex₁ > a)
  4. Step 4: PF₁ − PF₂ = (ex₁ + a) − (ex₁ − a) = 2a ∎
Example 9 HARD Show that y = ±(b/a)x are asymptotes of x²/a² − y²/b² = 1.
  1. Step 1: For large x: y = (b/a)√(x² − a²) = (b/a) x √(1 − a²/x²)
  2. Step 2: ≈ (b/a) x (1 − a²/2x²) = (b/a)x − ab/(2x)
  3. Step 3: Difference from line y = (b/a)x is −ab/(2x) → 0 as x → ∞
  4. Step 4: So the curve approaches y = (b/a)x (similarly y = −(b/a)x) ∎
Example 10 HARD Find e of a hyperbola whose asymptotes are y = ±(4/3)x (transverse axis on x).
  1. Step 1: b/a = 4/3
  2. Step 2: e² = 1 + b²/a² = 1 + 16/9 = 25/9
  3. Step 3: e = 5/3
  4. Step 4: (e.g. a = 3, b = 4, c = 5)

9. Short Q&A

Q1. Define a hyperbola.

Ans Locus of a point whose distances from two fixed points (foci) differ by a constant 2a (< distance between foci); e > 1.

Q2. State c, a, b relation.

Ans c² = a² + b² (for the ellipse it is c² = a² − b²).

Q3. What are asymptotes of x²/a² − y²/b² = 1?

Ans y = ±(b/a)x.

Q4. Define a rectangular hyperbola.

Ans One with a = b; asymptotes are perpendicular; e = √2.

Q5. Write the latus rectum.

Ans 2b²/a.
