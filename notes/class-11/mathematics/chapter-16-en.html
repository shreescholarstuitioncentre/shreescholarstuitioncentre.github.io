Class 11 Maths – Ch 17: Limit of a Function

Handwritten-style notes • UP Board / NCERT

1. Intuitive Idea of Limit

If f(x) gets closer and closer to a number L as x gets closer to a (from both sides, but x ≠ a), then L is the limit: limx→a f(x) = L.

  • LHL = limx→a⁻ f(x) (x slightly less than a): put x = a−h, h→0⁺.
  • RHL = limx→a⁺ f(x): put x = a+h, h→0⁺.
Limit exists ⇔ LHL = RHL (finite)
The limit depends on values near a, not at a. f(a) may even be undefined.

Algebra of limits

If lim f = l and lim g = m: lim(f±g)=l±m; lim(fg)=lm; lim(cf)=cl; lim(f/g)=l/m (m≠0).

2. Limits of Polynomial Functions

For a polynomial f, simply substitute:

limx→a f(x) = f(a)

Standard result:

limx→a (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹
Valid for any rational n. Example: lim (x⁵−32)/(x−2)=5·2⁴=80.

3. Limits of Rational Functions

f(x)=p(x)/q(x).

  • q(a)≠0: limit = p(a)/q(a).
  • 0/0 form: factorise and cancel (x−a), or rationalise (when roots are present), then substitute.
  • x→∞: divide numerator and denominator by the highest power of x; 1/xᵏ→0.
limx→∞ (a xⁿ+…)/(b xⁿ+…) = a/b
Indeterminate forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, ∞−∞, 0·∞. These need simplification; they are not answers.

4. Limits of Trigonometric Functions

x is in radians. Basic: limx→0 sin x = 0, lim cos x = 1.

limx→0 sin x / x = 1  |  lim tan x / x = 1  |  lim (1−cos x)/x² = ½  |  lim sin(ax)/(bx) = a/b

Proof of sin x/x → 1 (sandwich): for 0<x<π/2, area △OAB < sector OAB < △OAC gives cos x < sin x/x < 1. As x→0, cos x→1, so sin x/x→1. ∎

Trick: make the angle inside sin/tan equal to the denominator, e.g. sin3x/x = 3·(sin3x/3x)→3.

5. Continuity (Basic)

f is continuous at x=a if:

LHL = RHL = f(a)
  1. f(a) is defined,
  2. lim f(x) exists,
  3. the limit equals f(a).

Discontinuity types: removable (limit exists ≠ f(a) or f(a) undefined), jump (LHL≠RHL), infinite.

Polynomials, sin x, cos x are continuous everywhere. Sum, product, and quotient (denominator≠0) of continuous functions are continuous. Graph of continuous f can be drawn without lifting the pen.

Animated diagram: limit of x² as x→2

The curve y=x² is drawn. Two points slide along it: green from the left (LHL), blue from the right (RHL). Both approach height 4, so lim x→2 x² = 4.

EasyFind limx→2 (x⁵−32)/(x−2).32=2⁵. Use formula n·aⁿ⁻¹ = 5·2⁴ = 80.
MediumFind limx→2 (x²−5x+6)/(x²−4).Numerator=(x−2)(x−3); denominator=(x−2)(x+2). Cancel (x−2): (x−3)/(x+2) → (−1)/4 = −1/4.
MediumFind limx→0 (√(1+x)−1)/x.0/0. Multiply by conjugate (√(1+x)+1):
numerator = (1+x)−1 = x.
Expression = x/[x(√(1+x)+1)] = 1/(√(1+x)+1) → 1/2 = ½.
MediumFind limx→∞ (3x²+2x)/(5x²−1).Divide by x²: (3+2/x)/(5−1/x²). As x→∞, 2/x→0, 1/x²→0 ⇒ 3/5.

Short & Long Questions

Q1. Define limit of a function.limx→a f(x)=L if f(x) can be made as close to L as we wish by taking x close enough to a (x≠a).
Q2. Condition for existence of limit?LHL = RHL, both finite.
Q3. Does limit depend on f(a)?No. It depends only on values near a.
Q4. Evaluate limx→0 (sin x)/x and state why x must be in radians.1. The area argument (sector area = ½r²θ) holds only for radian measure.
Q5. Evaluate limx→∞ (1/x).0.
Q6. Find limx→0 (tan x)/x.(sin x/x)·(1/cos x) → 1·1 = 1.
Q7. State the three conditions of continuity at a point.f(a) defined; limit exists; limit = f(a).
Q8. Is f(x)=1/x continuous at 0?No; f(0) is not defined (infinite discontinuity).
Q9. Prove lim sin x/x=1 (long).See Section 4 – sandwich theorem with areas.
