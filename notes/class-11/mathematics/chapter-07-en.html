Binomial Theorem

Class 11 • Chapter 8 • Handwritten-style notes
Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. History

  • Pingala (India, ~200 BC) described the triangle as Meru Prastara; Halayudha explained it later.
  • Omar Khayyam (Persia) and Yang Hui (China) used it for roots/expansions.
  • Blaise Pascal (1654) studied it in detail, so in Europe it is called Pascal's Triangle.
  • Isaac Newton extended the theorem to fractional/negative powers.

2. Statement

For any natural number n:

(a+b)ⁿ = nC0 aⁿ + nC1 aⁿ⁻¹b + nC2 aⁿ⁻²b² + … + nCn bⁿ

i.e. (a+b)ⁿ = Σ nCr aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ, r = 0 to n.

3. Proof (Induction)

Step 1 (n=1): (a+b)¹ = a+b = 1C0 a + 1C1 b ✔ true.

Step 2: assume true for n = k: (a+b)ᵏ = Σ kCr aᵏ⁻ʳbʳ.

Step 3: multiply by (a+b) and collect equal powers; the coefficient of aᵏ⁺¹⁻ʳ bʳ becomes kCr + kC(r−1).

Step 4: using kCr + kC(r−1) = (k+1)Cr, we get (a+b)ᵏ⁺¹ = Σ (k+1)Cr aᵏ⁺¹⁻ʳ bʳ, true for n = k+1. By induction, true for all n ∎

4. Expansion of a Binomial

Number of terms = n+1. Power of a falls n→0, power of b rises 0→n; sum of powers in each term = n.

Coefficients are equidistant from ends equal: nCr = nC(n−r).

(a−b)ⁿ: signs alternate (+ − + − …).

(1+x)ⁿ = 1 + nx + n(n−1)/2!·x² + … + xⁿ

Sum of all coefficients: put a=b=1 → 2ⁿ. Even-place sum = odd-place sum = 2ⁿ⁻¹.

5. Pascal's Triangle

Row n lists the coefficients of (a+b)ⁿ. Each number = sum of the two numbers just above it (nCr = (n−1)C(r−1) + (n−1)Cr). Watch the triangle build row by row.

Rows: (a+b)² → 1 2 1; (a+b)³ → 1 3 3 1; (a+b)⁴ → 1 4 6 4 1; (a+b)⁵ → 1 5 10 10 5 1.

6. General Term & Middle Term

T(r+1) = nCr aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ

Middle term: n even → one term, T(n/2 + 1). n odd → two terms, T((n+1)/2) and T((n+3)/2).

Term independent of x: write the general term, equate the power of x to 0, find r.

Coefficient of xᵏ: equate the power of x to k, find r, then take the coefficient.

7. Applications

  • Finding specific terms, coefficients, middle terms
  • Evaluating numbers like (1.01)⁵, 99⁵, (√2+1)⁵+(√2−1)⁵
  • Divisibility / remainder problems, e.g. 9ⁿ⁺¹ − 8n − 9 divisible by 64
  • Proving identities such as ΣnCr = 2ⁿ

8. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY Expand (x+y)⁴.
  1. Step 1: n=4, coefficients from Pascal row 4: 1 4 6 4 1
  2. Step 2: Powers of x fall 4→0, of y rise 0→4
  3. Step 3: = x⁴ + 4x³y + 6x²y² + 4xy³ + y⁴
Example 2 EASY Expand (2+x)³.
  1. Step 1: = 3C0·2³ + 3C1·2²·x + 3C2·2·x² + 3C3·x³
  2. Step 2: = 8 + 3·4x + 3·2x² + x³
  3. Step 3: = 8 + 12x + 6x² + x³
Example 3 EASY Find the 5th term of (x+2)⁷.
  1. Step 1: General term T(r+1) = 7Cr x⁷⁻ʳ 2ʳ; 5th term → r = 4
  2. Step 2: T5 = 7C4 x³ 2⁴ = 35 × 16 x³
  3. Step 3: = 560x³
Example 4 MEDIUM Evaluate (√2+1)⁵ + (√2−1)⁵.
  1. Step 1: Odd-place terms double, even-place cancel: = 2[5C0(√2)⁵ + 5C2(√2)³ + 5C4(√2)]
  2. Step 2: (√2)⁵ = 4√2, 5C2(√2)³ = 10·2√2 = 20√2, 5C4√2 = 5√2
  3. Step 3: = 2[4√2+20√2+5√2] = 2 × 29√2
  4. Step 4: = 58√2
Example 5 MEDIUM Find the middle term of (x + 1/x)¹⁰.
  1. Step 1: n = 10 even → middle term is T(6)
  2. Step 2: T6 = 10C5 x⁵ (1/x)⁵ = 10C5 x⁰
  3. Step 3: 10C5 = 252, so middle term = 252
Example 6 MEDIUM Find the coefficient of x⁵ in (x+3)⁸.
  1. Step 1: T(r+1) = 8Cr x⁸⁻ʳ 3ʳ
  2. Step 2: Need 8−r = 5 → r = 3
  3. Step 3: Coefficient = 8C3 × 3³ = 56 × 27 = 1512
Example 7 HARD Find the term independent of x in (2x² − 1/x)⁹.
  1. Step 1: T(r+1) = 9Cr (2x²)⁹⁻ʳ (−1/x)ʳ = 9Cr 2⁹⁻ʳ (−1)ʳ x¹⁸⁻³ʳ
  2. Step 2: Independent of x → 18 − 3r = 0 → r = 6
  3. Step 3: T7 = 9C6 · 2³ · (−1)⁶ = 84 × 8
  4. Step 4: = 672
Example 8 HARD Show that 9ⁿ⁺¹ − 8n − 9 is divisible by 64 (n ∈ N).
  1. Step 1: 9ⁿ⁺¹ = (1+8)ⁿ⁺¹ = 1 + (n+1)·8 + (n+1)C2·8² + …
  2. Step 2: = 1 + 8n + 8 + 64[(n+1)C2 + (n+1)C3·8 + …]
  3. Step 3: So 9ⁿ⁺¹ − 8n − 9 = 64 × [integer]
  4. Step 4: Hence divisible by 64 ∎
Example 9 HARD Prove nC0 + nC1 + … + nCn = 2ⁿ.
  1. Step 1: By the theorem (a+b)ⁿ = Σ nCr aⁿ⁻ʳbʳ
  2. Step 2: Put a = 1, b = 1
  3. Step 3: (1+1)ⁿ = nC0 + nC1 + … + nCn
  4. Step 4: So the sum = 2ⁿ ∎
Example 10 HARD If the coefficients of x² and x³ in (3+ax)⁹ are equal, find a.
  1. Step 1: T3 = 9C2 3⁷ (ax)² → coefficient 9C2·3⁷·a²
  2. Step 2: T4 = 9C3 3⁶ (ax)³ → coefficient 9C3·3⁶·a³
  3. Step 3: Equate: 36·3⁷a² = 84·3⁶a³ → 36·3 = 84a
  4. Step 4: a = 108/84 = 9/7

9. Short Q&A

Q1. State the binomial theorem.

Ans (a+b)ⁿ = Σ nCr aⁿ⁻ʳbʳ for natural n; it has n+1 terms.

Q2. How many terms are in (a+b)ⁿ and (a+b+c)ⁿ-type? Give (a+b)ⁿ.

Ans (a+b)ⁿ has n+1 terms.

Q3. What is Pascal's triangle?

Ans A triangular array where each entry is the sum of the two above it; row n gives coefficients of (a+b)ⁿ.

Q4. Write the general term.

Ans T(r+1) = nCr aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ.

Q5. What is the sum of the coefficients in (1+x)ⁿ?

Ans Put x = 1: 2ⁿ.
