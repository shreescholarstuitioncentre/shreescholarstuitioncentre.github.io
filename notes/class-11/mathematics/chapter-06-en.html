Permutations & Combinations

Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. Fundamental Principle of Counting

Multiplication Principle: if one job can be done in m ways and, after it, a second job in n ways, then both together can be done in m × n ways (AND → multiply).

Addition Principle: if a job can be done in m ways OR in n ways (not together), total = m + n (OR → add).

Diagram: 2 shirts × 3 pants = 6 outfits (watch the tree draw itself).

2. Factorial Notation

  • n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × … × 3 × 2 × 1 (n a natural number). Example: 5! = 120.
  • 0! = 1 by definition. Also n! = n × (n−1)!
  • Useful: n!/(n−r)! = n(n−1)…(n−r+1). Factorial of negative or fraction is not defined.

3. Permutations & nPr

Permutation = arrangement where ORDER matters. Number of arrangements of n different things taken r at a time (no repetition):

nPr = n! / (n−r)  (0 ≤ r ≤ n)

Special: nPn = n!, nP0 = 1, nP1 = n. Diagram: 5P3 = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 (fill 3 slots).

With repetition: r places, n choices each → nʳ.

Identical objects: n things, p alike of one kind, q alike of another → n! / (p! q!).

Circular arrangement: n different things in a circle → (n−1)! (if clockwise = anticlockwise, (n−1)!/2).

Together / not together: treat the group as 1 unit, multiply by the group's internal arrangements; 'not together' = total − together.

4. Combinations & nCr

Combination = selection where ORDER does NOT matter.

nCr = n! / [ r! (n−r)! ]

Properties: nCr = nC(n−r); nC0 = nCn = 1; nCr + nC(r−1) = (n+1)Cr; if nCx = nCy then x = y or x + y = n.

Selection of r from n with conditions: 'at least one' = total − none; 'a particular thing always included' → nC(r−1) from (n−1).

5. Relation between nPr and nCr

nPr = r! × nCr

Reason: first SELECT r things (nCr ways), then ARRANGE them (r! ways). So nCr = nPr / r!.

6. Applications

  • Forming numbers from digits (check 0 in first place)
  • Arranging letters of words (vowels together, alternate etc.)
  • Committees / teams with conditions
  • Handshakes: nC2; diagonals of polygon: nC2 − n; triangles from points: nC3 (minus collinear)
  • Circular tables, necklaces

7. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY Find the value of 8P3.
  1. Step 1: Formula: nPr = n(n−1)…(n−r+1)
  2. Step 2: 8P3 = 8 × 7 × 6
  3. Step 3: = 336
Example 2 EASY Find the value of 5C2.
  1. Step 1: 5C2 = 5!/(2! 3!) = (5×4)/(2×1)
  2. Step 2: = 10
Example 3 EASY How many 4-digit numbers can be formed from digits 1–9 without repetition?
  1. Step 1: Arrangement of 4 from 9 different digits
  2. Step 2: = 9P4 = 9×8×7×6
  3. Step 3: = 3024
Example 4 MEDIUM Find n if nP4 = 360.
  1. Step 1: nP4 = n(n−1)(n−2)(n−3) = 360
  2. Step 2: Write 360 = 6×5×4×3
  3. Step 3: Compare: n = 6
Example 5 MEDIUM How many words (meaningful or not) can be made from DELHI so that vowels are together?
  1. Step 1: Vowels: E, I → treat as one unit
  2. Step 2: Units: D, L, H, [EI] = 4 → 4! = 24
  3. Step 3: Inside the unit E, I can swap → 2! = 2
  4. Step 4: Total = 24 × 2 = 48
Example 6 MEDIUM A committee of 3 is chosen from 5 men and 4 women with at least 1 woman. Find the ways.
  1. Step 1: Total = 9C3 = 84
  2. Step 2: No woman (all men) = 5C3 = 10
  3. Step 3: At least 1 woman = 84 − 10 = 74
Example 7 HARD Find the number of arrangements of the letters of MISSISSIPPI.
  1. Step 1: Letters = 11: I×4, S×4, P×2, M×1
  2. Step 2: Formula n!/(p!q!r!) = 11!/(4! 4! 2!)
  3. Step 3: = 39916800 / (24 × 24 × 2) = 39916800 / 1152
  4. Step 4: = 34650
Example 8 HARD Prove: nCr + nC(r−1) = (n+1)Cr.
  1. Step 1: LHS = n!/[r!(n−r)!] + n!/[(r−1)!(n−r+1)!]
  2. Step 2: Take n!/[r!(n−r+1)!] common: = n!/[r!(n−r+1)!] × [(n−r+1) + r]
  3. Step 3: = n!(n+1)/[r!(n+1−r)!]
  4. Step 4: = (n+1)!/[r!(n+1−r)!] = (n+1)Cr = RHS ∎
Example 9 HARD 5 men and 5 women sit around a round table so that no two women are together. Find the ways.
  1. Step 1: Seat 5 men in a circle: (5−1)! = 24
  2. Step 2: They create 5 gaps; seat 5 women in gaps: 5! = 120
  3. Step 3: Total = 24 × 120 = 2880
Example 10 HARD If 2nC3 : nC2 = 12 : 1, find n.
  1. Step 1: 2nC3 = 2n(2n−1)(2n−2)/6, nC2 = n(n−1)/2
  2. Step 2: Ratio = [2n(2n−1)·2(n−1)/6] × [2/(n(n−1))] = 4(2n−1)/3
  3. Step 3: 4(2n−1)/3 = 12 ⟹ 2n−1 = 9
  4. Step 4: n = 5

8. Short Q&A

Q1. State the fundamental principle of counting.

Ans If an event happens in m ways and another in n ways, both in sequence give m×n ways (multiplication); either one (not both) gives m+n ways (addition).

Q2. Why is 0! = 1?

Ans From n! = n×(n−1)!, put n = 1: 1! = 1×0! so 0! = 1. It also makes nCn = n!/(n!0!) = 1 true.

Q3. Difference between permutation and combination?

Ans Permutation counts arrangements (order matters); combination counts selections (order does not matter). nPr = r!·nCr.

Q4. Prove nCr = nC(n−r).

Ans nC(n−r) = n!/[(n−r)!(n−(n−r))!] = n!/[(n−r)! r!] = nCr.

Q5. How many diagonals does a polygon of n sides have?

Ans Join any 2 of n vertices: nC2; remove n sides → n(n−3)/2.

(a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 6 (d) 4

✔ (b) 12 — 4!/2!

4. 0! equals

(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) undefined (d) ∞

✔ (b) 1

(a) 720 (b) 120 (c) 60 (d) 24

✔ (b) 120 — (6−1)!

(a) false (b) true (c) only n>5 (d) none

✔ (b) true — nPr = r!·nCr, 3! = 6

(a) 90 (b) 45 (c) 100 (d) 20

✔ (b) 45 — 10C2
