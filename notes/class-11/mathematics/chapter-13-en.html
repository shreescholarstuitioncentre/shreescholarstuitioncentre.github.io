Conic Section – Ellipse

Class 11 • Chapter 14 • Handwritten-style notes
Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. Definition

An ellipse is the locus of a point P that moves so that the sum of its distances from two fixed points (foci F₁, F₂) is constant: PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a, and 2a > F₁F₂.

It is a conic section: a plane cutting a cone (not parallel to base or generator) gives a closed oval. If the foci coincide, the ellipse becomes a circle.

Also: locus of a point whose distance from a focus is e times its distance from a directrix, 0 < e < 1.

2. Foci, Major & Minor Axis (Terms)

Foci: F₁(−c, 0), F₂(c, 0). Centre C: midpoint of F₁F₂.

Major axis: longest chord through the foci, length 2a; ends A, A′ = vertices (±a, 0).

Minor axis: chord through the centre ⟂ major axis, length 2b; ends B, B′ = (0, ±b). Here a > b.

Latus rectum: chord through a focus ⟂ major axis. Directrices: x = ± a/e.

3. Standard Equation (Derivation)

Take F₁(−c, 0), F₂(c, 0), PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a (watch the diagram):

√[(x+c)² + y²] + √[(x−c)² + y²] = 2a

Isolate one root, square twice and simplify: (a² − c²)x² + a²y² = a²(a² − c²)

Put b² = a² − c² and divide by a²b²:

x²/a² + y²/b² = 1    (a > b)

4. Eccentricity & Latus Rectum

e = c/a  (0 < e < 1)

c² = a² − b² ⟹ b² = a²(1 − e²). Foci (±ae, 0), directrices x = ±a/e.

e → 0: nearly a circle; e → 1: flatter ellipse (see diagram).

Latus rectum = 2b²/a; ends (±c, ±b²/a).

5. Two Standard Forms

x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 (a>b)x²/b² + y²/a² = 1 (a>b)

6. Properties

Symmetric about both axes and the origin.

Focal distances of P(x₁, y₁): PF₁ = a + ex₁, PF₂ = a − ex₁; sum = 2a.

Parametric form: (a cos θ, b sin θ). Auxiliary circle: x² + y² = a².

Point (x₁, y₁): x₁²/a² + y₁²/b² − 1 < 0 inside, = 0 on, > 0 outside.

Real life: planets move in elliptical orbits with the sun at a focus (Kepler); whispering galleries; arches.

7. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY For x²/25 + y²/9 = 1 find foci, vertices, axes, e and latus rectum.
  1. Step 1: a² = 25 → a = 5; b² = 9 → b = 3 (a > b: major axis on x)
  2. Step 2: c = √(a²−b²) = √16 = 4 → foci (±4, 0); vertices (±5, 0)
  3. Step 3: Major axis = 10, minor axis = 6; e = c/a = 4/5
  4. Step 4: Latus rectum = 2b²/a = 18/5
Example 2 EASY For 16x² + 25y² = 400 find foci and e.
  1. Step 1: Divide by 400: x²/25 + y²/16 = 1
  2. Step 2: a = 5, b = 4, c = √(25−16) = 3
  3. Step 3: Foci (±3, 0)
  4. Step 4: e = 3/5
Example 3 EASY For x²/9 + y²/25 = 1 find foci, vertices and e.
  1. Step 1: 25 > 9 → major axis along y: a² = 25, b² = 9
  2. Step 2: c = √(25−9) = 4 → foci (0, ±4); vertices (0, ±5)
  3. Step 3: e = 4/5
Example 4 MEDIUM Find the ellipse with vertices (±5, 0) and foci (±4, 0).
  1. Step 1: a = 5, c = 4
  2. Step 2: b² = a² − c² = 25 − 16 = 9
  3. Step 3: x²/25 + y²/9 = 1
Example 5 MEDIUM Find the ellipse with major axis on the x-axis passing through (4, 3) and (−1, 4).
  1. Step 1: Let x²/A + y²/B = 1. (4,3): 16/A + 9/B = 1; (−1,4): 1/A + 16/B = 1
  2. Step 2: Put u = 1/A, v = 1/B: 16u + 9v = 1, u + 16v = 1
  3. Step 3: u = 1 − 16v → 16 − 256v + 9v = 1 → v = 15/247, u = 7/247
  4. Step 4: 7x² + 15y² = 247
Example 6 MEDIUM Find the ellipse with foci (±2, 0) and e = 1/2.
  1. Step 1: c = 2, e = c/a = 1/2 → a = 4
  2. Step 2: b² = a² − c² = 16 − 4 = 12
  3. Step 3: x²/16 + y²/12 = 1
Example 7 HARD Find e if the latus rectum equals half the minor axis.
  1. Step 1: Latus rectum = 2b²/a; half minor axis = b
  2. Step 2: 2b²/a = b → b = a/2
  3. Step 3: e² = 1 − b²/a² = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4
  4. Step 4: e = √3/2
Example 8 HARD Prove that for P(x₁, y₁) on the ellipse, PF₁ = a + ex₁ and PF₂ = a − ex₁; hence PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a.
  1. Step 1: y₁² = b²(1 − x₁²/a²); PF₁² = (x₁+c)² + y₁²
  2. Step 2: = x₁²(1 − b²/a²) + 2cx₁ + c² + b² = e²x₁² + 2aex₁ + a²
  3. Step 3: = (a + ex₁)² → PF₁ = a + ex₁ (a + ex₁ > 0)
  4. Step 4: Similarly PF₂ = a − ex₁; sum = 2a ∎
Example 9 HARD Prove that the latus rectum of x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 is 2b²/a.
  1. Step 1: Latus rectum passes through F(c, 0): x = c
  2. Step 2: c²/a² + y²/b² = 1 → y² = b²(1 − c²/a²) = b²·b²/a²
  3. Step 3: y = ±b²/a
  4. Step 4: Length = 2b²/a ∎
Example 10 HARD Find the ellipse with foci (0, ±5) and major axis of length 26.
  1. Step 1: Foci on the y-axis → x²/b² + y²/a² = 1
  2. Step 2: 2a = 26 → a = 13; c = 5
  3. Step 3: b² = a² − c² = 169 − 25 = 144
  4. Step 4: x²/144 + y²/169 = 1

8. Short Q&A

Q1. Define an ellipse.

Ans Locus of a point whose distances from two fixed points (foci) add up to a constant 2a (> distance between foci).

Q2. Write the standard equation and the relation among a, b, c.

Ans x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 (a > b); b² = a² − c².

Q3. Define eccentricity.

Ans e = c/a, with 0 < e < 1; b² = a²(1 − e²).

Q4. What is the latus rectum of an ellipse?

Ans Chord through a focus perpendicular to the major axis; length 2b²/a.

Q5. What happens when e = 0?

Ans The foci coincide with the centre and the ellipse becomes a circle.
