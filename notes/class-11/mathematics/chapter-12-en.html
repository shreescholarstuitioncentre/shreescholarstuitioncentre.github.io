Conic Section – Parabola

Class 11 • Chapter 13 • Handwritten-style notes
Based on NCERT with practice in the style of Avtaar, Rajeev, Vidya & Sumitra Arora (UP Board).

1. Definition

A parabola is the locus of a point that moves so that its distance from a fixed point (focus) equals its distance from a fixed line (directrix), the focus not lying on the directrix.

It is a conic section with eccentricity e = 1, obtained by cutting a cone with a plane parallel to a generator (slant line) of the cone.

2. Focus, Directrix, Vertex, Axis (Terms)

Focus F: the fixed point. Directrix: the fixed line.

Axis: line through the focus perpendicular to the directrix; the parabola is symmetric about it.

Vertex V: point where the axis meets the parabola (midpoint of the focus and the foot of the axis on the directrix).

Latus rectum: chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis; length 4a. Focal distance of P = PF. Double ordinate = chord ⟂ axis.

3. Standard Equation (Derivation)

Take vertex at origin, focus F(a, 0), directrix x = −a (a > 0). Let P(x, y) be on the parabola, PM ⟂ directrix. Then PF = PM (watch the diagram):

√[(x−a)² + y²] = x + a

Square: x² − 2ax + a² + y² = x² + 2ax + a²

y² = 4ax

4. Four Standard Forms

EquationOpensFocusDirectrixAxisLatus rectum
y² = 4axright(a, 0)x = −ay = 04a; ends (a, ±2a)
y² = −4axleft(−a, 0)x = ay = 04a; ends (−a, ±2a)
x² = 4ayup(0, a)y = −ax = 04a; ends (±2a, a)
x² = −4aydown(0, −a)y = ax = 04a; ends (±2a, −a)

5. Properties

Vertex of all four is (0, 0). The curve is symmetric about its axis and extends to infinity.

Focal distance of P(x₁, y₁) on y² = 4ax is x₁ + a.

Parametric form of y² = 4ax: (at², 2at).

Shifted parabola: (y − k)² = 4a(x − h): vertex (h, k), focus (h + a, k), directrix x = h − a, axis y = k.

Position of (x₁, y₁) w.r.t. y² = 4ax: y₁² − 4ax₁ > 0 outside, = 0 on, < 0 inside.

Real life: headlights, satellite dishes (rays parallel to the axis reflect through the focus), bridges, projectile path.

6. Solved Examples (Easy → Hard)

Example 1 EASY For y² = 12x find vertex, focus, directrix and latus rectum.
  1. Step 1: Compare with y² = 4ax: 4a = 12 → a = 3
  2. Step 2: Vertex (0,0); focus (a,0) = (3,0)
  3. Step 3: Directrix x = −a = −3
  4. Step 4: Latus rectum = 4a = 12
Example 2 EASY For x² = 8y find focus, directrix and latus rectum.
  1. Step 1: x² = 4ay: 4a = 8 → a = 2
  2. Step 2: Focus (0, a) = (0, 2)
  3. Step 3: Directrix y = −2
  4. Step 4: Latus rectum = 8
Example 3 EASY For y² = −16x find focus and directrix.
  1. Step 1: y² = −4ax: 4a = 16 → a = 4
  2. Step 2: Opens left; focus (−a, 0) = (−4, 0)
  3. Step 3: Directrix x = a = 4
Example 4 MEDIUM Find the parabola with focus (3, 0) and directrix x = −3.
  1. Step 1: Focus (a,0) = (3,0) → a = 3; directrix x = −3 ✔ (vertex origin)
  2. Step 2: Axis = x-axis, opens right: y² = 4ax
  3. Step 3: y² = 12x
Example 5 MEDIUM Find the parabola with vertex at origin, axis along the x-axis, passing through (2, 3).
  1. Step 1: Point is in the first quadrant → y² = 4ax
  2. Step 2: Put (2,3): 9 = 4a·2 → a = 9/8
  3. Step 3: y² = 4(9/8)x = (9/2)x
  4. Step 4: 2y² = 9x
Example 6 MEDIUM Find the parabola with focus (0, −2) and directrix y = 2.
  1. Step 1: Vertex = midpoint of focus and directrix foot = (0,0)
  2. Step 2: Focus below the vertex → opens down: x² = −4ay, a = 2
  3. Step 3: x² = −8y
Example 7 HARD Find the parabola with focus (1, 1) and directrix x + y + 1 = 0.
  1. Step 1: PF² = (x−1)² + (y−1)²
  2. Step 2: PM² = (x+y+1)²/2
  3. Step 3: PF² = PM²: 2(x²+y²−2x−2y+2) = x²+y²+2xy+2x+2y+1
  4. Step 4: x² + y² − 2xy − 6x − 6y + 3 = 0
Example 8 HARD Prove that the latus rectum of y² = 4ax has length 4a.
  1. Step 1: Latus rectum passes through F(a,0) ⟂ axis → line x = a
  2. Step 2: Put x = a: y² = 4a·a = 4a² → y = ±2a
  3. Step 3: Endpoints (a, 2a), (a, −2a)
  4. Step 4: Length = 2a − (−2a) = 4a ∎
Example 9 HARD Prove that the focal distance of P(x₁, y₁) on y² = 4ax is x₁ + a.
  1. Step 1: Directrix x = −a; PM ⟂ directrix
  2. Step 2: PM = x₁ − (−a) = x₁ + a
  3. Step 3: By definition PF = PM
  4. Step 4: So PF = x₁ + a ∎
Example 10 HARD Find the points on y² = 8x whose focal distance is 6.
  1. Step 1: 4a = 8 → a = 2; focal distance = x + a = 6
  2. Step 2: x = 4
  3. Step 3: y² = 8 × 4 = 32 → y = ±4√2
  4. Step 4: Points (4, 4√2) and (4, −4√2)

7. Short Q&A

Q1. Define a parabola.

Ans Locus of a point equidistant from a fixed point (focus) and a fixed line (directrix); eccentricity e = 1.

Q2. Write the standard equation and its focus and directrix.

Ans y² = 4ax; focus (a, 0); directrix x = −a.

Q3. What is the latus rectum?

Ans The chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis; its length is 4a.

Q4. Give the parametric form of y² = 4ax.

Ans x = at², y = 2at.

Q5. What is the focal distance of (x₁, y₁) on y² = 4ax?

Ans x₁ + a.
