Class 11 Maths – Ch 20: Probability

Handwritten-style notes • UP Board / NCERT

1. Random Experiment

An experiment whose outcome cannot be predicted in advance, though all possible outcomes are known and it can be repeated under the same conditions. Examples: tossing a coin, rolling a die, drawing a card.

  • Outcome: a result of one trial.
  • Sample space S: set of all outcomes. Coin: {H,T}. Die: {1,…,6}. Two coins: {HH,HT,TH,TT}.
  • Sample point: each element of S.

2. Event

Any subset E of S. Event E occurs if the outcome belongs to E. Simple (elementary) event: one sample point; compound event: more than one.

Operations: A∪B (A or B), A∩B (A and B), A' (not A), A−B (A but not B).

3. Impossible and Certain Events

  • Impossible event = ∅ (empty set), never occurs, P=0. Eg. getting 7 on a die.
  • Certain (sure) event = S, always occurs, P=1. Eg. getting a number ≤6 on a die.

4. Exhaustive and Mutually Exclusive Events

  • Mutually exclusive: cannot occur together: A∩B = ∅.
  • Exhaustive: together cover S: E₁∪E₂∪…∪Eₙ = S.
  • Mutually exclusive & exhaustive (partition): E.g. A and A'.
  • Equally likely: no outcome is preferred.
Die: {1,2,3} and {4,5,6} are exclusive and exhaustive. {even} and {≥3} are not exclusive (4,6 common).

5. Axiomatic Probability

Kolmogorov’s axioms, for sample space S:

  1. P(E) ≥ 0 for every event E.
  2. P(S) = 1.
  3. If E and F are mutually exclusive, P(E∪F) = P(E) + P(F).

Consequences: P(∅)=0; 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1; for outcomes ωᵢ: ΣP(ωᵢ)=1.

6. Probability of an Event

For equally likely outcomes:

P(E) = n(E) / n(S) = favourable outcomes / total outcomes
Odds in favour of E = P(E) : P(E'). Odds against = P(E') : P(E).

7. Probability of ‘Not’ Event

A' (complement) = event ‘not A’.

P(A') = 1 − P(A)

Proof: A∪A'=S, A∩A'=∅ ⇒ P(A)+P(A')=P(S)=1. ∎ Use when ‘at least one’ is asked.

8. Probability of ‘And’ Event

‘A and B’ = A∩B, outcomes common to both.

P(A∩B) = n(A∩B)/n(S)

If A, B mutually exclusive: P(A∩B)=0. Also P(A only)=P(A)−P(A∩B); P(neither)=1−P(A∪B).

9. Probability of ‘Or’ Event (Addition Theorem)

‘A or B’ = A∪B.

P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)
Mutually exclusive: P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B)

For three events: P(A∪B∪C)=ΣP(A)−ΣP(A∩B)+P(A∩B∩C).

The rectangle is sample space S. Circle A appears (green), then B (blue), then their overlap A∩B (red, ‘and’). The whole coloured area is A∪B (‘or’); the region outside is (A∪B)'. Formula shown at the end.

Short & Long Questions

Q1. Define random experiment.An experiment with known possible outcomes but unpredictable single result, repeatable under same conditions.
Q2. What is a sample space?Set of all possible outcomes.
Q3. Define impossible and sure event.Impossible = ∅ (P=0); sure = S (P=1).
Q4. Define mutually exclusive events.Events that cannot occur together: A∩B=∅.
Q5. State axioms of probability.P(E)≥0; P(S)=1; P(E∪F)=P(E)+P(F) for exclusive E,F.
Q6. Can probability be 1.2 or −0.3?No. 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
Q7. If P(A)=3/5, find P(not A).1−3/5=2/5.
Q8. Prove the addition theorem (long).See Section 9 and solved example: split A∪B into disjoint parts.
Q9. Difference between exclusive and exhaustive events?Exclusive: no common outcome. Exhaustive: union is S.
