2. Algae

Chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, autotrophic, largely aquatic (fresh/marine), also on moist stones, as symbionts (lichens). Size: microscopic Chlamydomonas to giant kelps. Reproduction: vegetative (fragmentation), asexual (zoospores), sexual – isogamous (Ulothrix, Spirogyra), anisogamous (Eudorina), oogamous (Volvox, Fucus). Economic use: carbon fixation (half of total on earth), food (Porphyra, Laminaria, Sargassum), agar (Gelidium, Gracilaria), algin, carrageen, hydrocolloids; Chlorella – space food.

Chlorophyceae (green algae)

Chl a,b; cell wall: inner cellulose, outer pectose; 1–many pyrenoids in chloroplast store starch (some oil). Marine: Ulva, Chara? (fresh), Volvox, Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas. Flagella 2–8, equal, apical.

Phaeophyceae (brown algae)

Mostly marine; holdfast, stipe, frond; Chl a,c + fucoxanthin (brown); food: laminarin, mannitol; cellulose wall + algin gel; 2 laterally inserted flagella (one long tinsel, one short whiplash). Ectocarpus, Dictyota, Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus.

Rhodophyceae (red algae)

Chl a,d + phycoerythrin (red); deep waters; food: floridean starch; no flagella; oogamous; Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium.

3. Bryophytes

"Amphibians of plant kingdom": live on land but need water for fertilisation. No true root/stem/leaf – rhizoids. Main body = haploid gametophyte. Antheridium → biflagellate antherozoids; archegonium (flask-shaped) → single egg. Zygote → sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule) attached to gametophyte; meiosis in capsule → haploid spores → germinate to gametophyte. Economic: Sphagnum peat (fuel), packing; mosses with lichens colonise rocks, soil erosion check.

Liverworts (Marchantia): dorsiventral thallus; asexual by gemmae in gemma cups. Mosses (Funaria, Polytrichum, Sphagnum): juvenile protonema → leafy gametophore; vegetative by fragmentation/budding; sporophyte more elaborate.

4. Pteridophytes

First terrestrial vascular plants (xylem + phloem); in damp shady places. Main body = sporophyte (true root, stem, leaves – small microphylls in Selaginella, large macrophylls in ferns). Sporangia on sporophylls, clustered into strobili (Selaginella, Equisetum). Spores → small multicellular free-living prothallus (gametophyte, needs cool damp place). Homosporous (most) vs heterosporous (Selaginella, Salvinia: mega & micro spores; female gametophyte retained on parent – precursor of seed habit).

5. Gymnosperms

Seeds naked (ovules not enclosed in ovary). Tall trees, shrubs (Sequoia – tallest tree). Roots: taproot; Pinus mycorrhiza; Cycas coralloid roots with N-fixing cyanobacteria. Leaves: needle-like (Pinus, reduces water loss), pinnate (Cycas). Heterosporous; microsporophylls → male cone (pollen grains = male gametophyte); megasporophylls → female cone. Wind pollination; pollen tube; no external water needed; female gametophyte (endosperm, n) bearing archegonia retained in ovule; after fertilisation ovule → seed. Examples: Cycas, Pinus, Cedrus, Ginkgo, Thuja.

6. Angiosperms

Flowering plants; ovules inside ovary, becoming seeds within fruits. Smallest: Wolffia; tallest: Eucalyptus (>100 m). Classes: Dicotyledons (2 cotyledons, reticulate venation, tetra/pentamerous) & Monocotyledons (1 cotyledon, parallel venation, trimerous). Stamen: anther with microsporangia → pollen; Pistil: ovule has embryo sac (7-celled, 8-nucleate). Double fertilisation: syngamy (egg + male gamete → zygote 2n) + triple fusion (2 polar nuclei + male gamete → PEN 3n endosperm). Sporophyte dominant; gametophytes highly reduced.

7. Life Cycle Patterns

Haplontic (dominant haploid gametophyte; zygote only diploid; zygotic meiosis): Volvox, Spirogyra. Diplontic (dominant diploid sporophyte; gametes only haploid): gymnosperms, angiosperms, Fucus. Haplo-diplontic (both multicellular): bryophytes, pteridophytes, Ectocarpus, Polysiphonia, kelps.

8. Q & A (Short, Long & MCQ)

Q1. Name three types of classification.

Ans: Artificial, natural, phylogenetic.

Q2. Why are bryophytes called amphibians of plant kingdom?

Ans: They live on land but need water for antherozoids to swim to egg.

Q3. What is a gemma?

Ans: Green multicellular asexual bud in gemma cups of Marchantia; detaches and grows into new thallus.

Q4. What is agar? Source?

Ans: Gelling polysaccharide from Gelidium and Gracilaria (red algae); used in culture media, ice-cream, jellies.

Q5. Differentiate homosporous and heterosporous.

Ans: Homosporous: one kind of spore. Heterosporous: mega & microspores, e.g. Selaginella, Salvinia; leads to seed habit.

Q6. What are coralloid roots?

Ans: Special roots of Cycas with N-fixing cyanobacteria (Anabaena).

Q7. Why does Pinus have needle-like leaves?

Ans: Reduced surface area, sunken stomata, thick cuticle – to reduce water loss.

Q8. What is double fertilisation?

Ans: Fusion of one male gamete with egg (syngamy) and another with 2 polar nuclei (triple fusion) – unique to angiosperms.

Q9. Compare green, brown and red algae (long).

Ans: See table: pigments (chl a,b / a,c+fucoxanthin / a,d+phycoerythrin), reserve food (starch / laminarin, mannitol / floridean starch), flagella, examples.

Q10. Describe life cycle of a moss (long).

Ans: Spore → protonema → leafy gametophyte (n) → antheridia & archegonia → fertilisation in water → zygote → sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule) → meiosis → spores.

Q11. Explain haplo-diplontic cycle.

Ans: Both haploid and diploid phases are multicellular, e.g. ferns: sporophyte dominant, prothallus free-living.

Q12. Why gymnosperms do not need water for fertilisation?

Ans: Pollen tube carries male gametes directly to egg.
