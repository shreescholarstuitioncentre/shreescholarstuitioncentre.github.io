Class 11 Biology – Ch 5: Morphology of Flowering Plants

1. Root

Types: Tap root (primary root + laterals; dicots – mustard, gram), Fibrous root (primary root short-lived, many equal roots; monocots – wheat, rice), Adventitious root (arise from parts other than radicle – grass, Monstera, banyan).

Regions of root tip:

① Root cap – thimble-like, protects tender apex. ② Meristematic zone – small thin-walled cells, rapid division. ③ Elongation zone – cells enlarge, root lengthens. ④ Maturation zone – differentiation; root hairs absorb water & minerals.

Modifications: Storage – tap root: carrot, turnip (fusiform/napiform), sweet potato (adventitious). Support – prop roots (banyan), stilt roots (maize, sugarcane). Respiration – pneumatophores (Rhizophora, mangrove).

2. Stem

Develops from plumule; nodes & internodes; bears buds, leaves, flowers, fruits; functions: conduction, support, spread of branches. Modifications: Underground storage/perennation – potato (tuber), ginger, turmeric (rhizome), Colocasia (corm), onion (bulb). Tendrils – climbing: cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon. Thorns – protection: Citrus, Bougainvillea. Flattened (phylloclade) – Opuntia photosynthesise. Sub-aerial – runner (grass), stolon (mint, jasmine), offset (Eichhornia), sucker (banana, pineapple).

3. Leaf

Parts: leaf base (may have stipules; pulvinus in legumes), petiole, lamina (with veins & veinlets).

Venation: reticulate (dicots), parallel (monocots). Types: simple vs compound – pinnately (neem) & palmately (silk cotton). Phyllotaxy: alternate (China rose, mustard, sunflower), opposite (guava, Calotropis), whorled (Alstonia). Modifications: tendrils (pea), spines (Opuntia), fleshy (onion, garlic), phyllode (Australian acacia), insectivorous – pitcher plant, Venus flytrap.

5. The Flower

Types: bisexual / unisexual; actinomorphic (radial: mustard), zygomorphic (bilateral: pea), asymmetric (canna); trimerous/tetramerous/pentamerous; bracteate/ebracteate. Position of ovary: hypogynous (superior ovary – mustard, brinjal), perigynous (rose, plum), epigynous (inferior – guava, cucumber).

Calyx (sepals, protective); Corolla (petals, attract pollinators). Aestivation (arrangement in bud): valvate (Calotropis), twisted (China rose), imbricate (cassia), vexillary (pea/bean: standard, wings, keel).

Androecium: stamens (anther + filament). Cohesion: monadelphous (China rose), diadelphous (pea), polyadelphous (citrus); epipetalous (stamens on petals: brinjal). Gynoecium: carpels (stigma, style, ovary); apocarpous (free: lotus) / syncarpous (fused: tomato).

Placentation: marginal (pea), axile (China rose, tomato), parietal (mustard), free central (Dianthus), basal (sunflower).

6. Fruit & Seed

Fruit = mature ovary; wall pericarp = epicarp + mesocarp + endocarp. Drupe – mango, coconut (one-seeded, from monocarpellary superior ovary). Parthenocarpic – fruit without fertilisation (banana).

Dicot seed (gram/bean): seed coat (outer testa, inner tegmen), hilum (scar), micropyle (pore); embryo = embryonal axis (radicle + plumule) + two cotyledons (food). Monocot seed (maize): endospermic; scutellum (single cotyledon), coleoptile (covers plumule), coleorhiza (covers radicle); aleurone layer separates endosperm.

8. Q & A (Short, Long & MCQ)

Q1. What is a pneumatophore?

Ans: Vertical breathing root of mangrove (Rhizophora) growing upward out of mud to take in oxygen.

Q2. Differentiate tap root and fibrous root.

Ans: Tap: one main root with branches, dicots. Fibrous: many equal roots from stem base, monocots.

Q3. Name the root regions where cell division and absorption occur.

Ans: Meristematic zone – division; maturation zone with root hairs – absorption.

Q4. What are tendrils? Give a stem and a leaf example.

Ans: Slender coiled climbing structures; stem tendril – cucumber, watermelon; leaf tendril – pea.

Q5. What is phyllotaxy? Its types?

Ans: Arrangement of leaves on stem; alternate, opposite, whorled.

Q6. Differentiate racemose and cymose inflorescence.

Ans: Racemose: unlimited growth, acropetal. Cymose: limited growth, basipetal.

Q7. What is aestivation? Types?

Ans: Arrangement of sepals/petals in bud: valvate, twisted, imbricate, vexillary.

Q8. Define placentation and list its types.

Ans: Arrangement of ovules in ovary: marginal, axile, parietal, free central, basal.

Q9. Describe the structure of a dicot seed (long).

Ans: Testa, tegmen, hilum, micropyle; embryo: radicle, plumule, two fleshy cotyledons; non-endospermic (gram).

Q10. Describe the maize grain (long).

Ans: Fused seed coat and fruit wall; aleurone; endosperm; scutellum; embryo with plumule (coleoptile) and radicle (coleorhiza).

Q11. Write floral formula of Solanaceae (to find).

Ans: ⊕ ⚥ K(5) C(5) A5 G(2): actinomorphic, bisexual; calyx 5 united; corolla 5 united; stamens 5 epipetalous; 2 fused carpels, superior ovary.

Q12. Why is Fabaceae corolla called papilionaceous?

Ans: Butterfly-like: one standard, two wings, two fused keel petals.
