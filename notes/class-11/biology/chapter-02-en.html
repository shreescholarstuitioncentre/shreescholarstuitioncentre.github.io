Class 11 Biology – Ch 2: Biological Classification

Why classify? Millions of organisms need an orderly system. Early: Linnaeus (2 kingdoms: Plantae, Animalia). Problem: bacteria, fungi, unicellular algae didn't fit (e.g. Euglena has both plant & animal traits).

1. Five Kingdom Classification (R.H. Whittaker, 1969)

Basis: cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, phylogenetic relationship.

2. Kingdom Monera

Bacteria are the sole members – most abundant, found everywhere (soil, hot springs, deep oceans, inside our body). Shapes:

(a) Archaebacteria

Live in harsh habitats; cell wall lacks peptidoglycan. Halophiles (salty areas), Thermoacidophiles (hot springs), Methanogens (marsh, guts of ruminants – make biogas/methane).

(b) Eubacteria ("true bacteria")

Rigid cell wall + flagellum if motile. Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae: Nostoc, Anabaena) are photosynthetic, form blooms, fix N2 in heterocysts. Chemosynthetic autotrophs oxidise inorganic substances (nitrates, ammonia) – help recycle nutrients. Heterotrophs – decomposers, curd formation, antibiotics, N-fixation (Rhizobium); pathogens cause cholera, typhoid, tetanus, citrus canker.

Mycoplasma: smallest living cells, no cell wall, survive without O2; many pathogenic. Reproduction: binary fission, spores; sexual: DNA transfer (conjugation, transformation, transduction).

3. Kingdom Protista

Link between monera and higher kingdoms; eukaryotes with flagella/cilia.

Chrysophytes

Diatoms & golden algae. Diatom wall = silica, two overlapping shells → "diatomaceous earth" (soil-like, indestructible; used in polishing, filtration of oils/syrups). Planktons; chief producers in oceans.

Dinoflagellates

Yellow, green, brown, blue, red; stiff cellulose plates; two flagella (one longitudinal, one transverse). Gonyaulax red tide – toxins kill fish.

Euglenoids

Fresh water; no cell wall, protein-rich flexible pellicle; two flagella (long + short). Photosynthetic in light, heterotrophic in dark – Euglena links plants & animals.

Slime moulds

Saprophytic; form plasmodium (grows, spreads many feet). Under unfavourable conditions form fruiting bodies with spores.

Protozoans

Heterotrophs (predators/parasites). 4 groups: Amoeboid (pseudopodia; Amoeba, Entamoeba), Flagellated (Trypanosoma – sleeping sickness), Ciliated (Paramecium, cilia + gullet), Sporozoans (Plasmodium – malaria).

4. Kingdom Fungi

Heterotrophs; wall of chitin & polysaccharides; body = hyphae forming mycelium. Coenocytic (aseptate) or septate. Storage: glycogen. Some are symbiotic: lichens (algae+fungus), mycorrhiza (fungus+roots).

5. Kingdoms Plantae & Animalia

Plantae: eukaryotic chlorophyll-containing; cellulose wall; includes algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms; alternation of generations. Animalia: heterotrophic, holozoic, no cell wall, mostly motile, grow to definite form, sexual reproduction.

6. Viruses, Viroids, Prions, Lichens

Virus: D.J. Ivanowsky (1892) – TMV; M.W. Beijerinck – "contagium vivum fluidum"; W.M. Stanley (1935) crystallised it. Nucleoprotein; inert outside, living inside host. Genetic material = DNA or RNA (never both); protein coat = capsid made of capsomeres. Bacteriophage = ds DNA; plant viruses mostly ss RNA; animal viruses ss/ds RNA or DNA. Diseases: mumps, smallpox, herpes, influenza, AIDS; plants: mosaic, leaf rolling.

Viroid (T.O. Diener, 1971): free RNA, no protein coat, low molecular weight – causes potato spindle tuber disease. Prions: abnormally folded proteins – Mad cow (BSE), CJD in humans. Lichens: symbiosis of algal partner (phycobiont) + fungal partner (mycobiont); algae make food, fungus gives shelter & absorbs water; pollution indicators (don't grow in polluted air).

7. Q & A (Short, Long & MCQ)

Q1. Who proposed Five Kingdom classification and when?

R.H. Whittaker, 1969.

Q2. Name the five kingdoms.

Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.

Q3. Why is Euglena called a link between plants and animals?

It has chlorophyll and photosynthesises in light (plant trait) but becomes heterotrophic in dark and has a flexible pellicle, no cell wall and flagella (animal traits).

Q4. What are diatomaceous earth and its uses?

Deposits of silica cell walls of diatoms; indestructible; used for polishing, filtration of oils and syrups.

Q5. What is red tide? Which organism causes it?

Rapid multiplication of Gonyaulax (dinoflagellate) turns sea red; toxins kill fish.

Q6. Define heterocyst.

Specialised thick-walled cell in cyanobacteria (Nostoc, Anabaena) for nitrogen fixation.

Q7. Why are archaebacteria able to survive in harsh conditions?

Their cell wall lacks peptidoglycan and has special membrane lipids, enabling life in salt, heat and acid.

Q8. Why are Mycoplasma special?

Smallest living cells, lack cell wall, survive without oxygen, can be pathogenic.

Q9. Distinguish virus and viroid.

Virus: DNA/RNA with protein capsid. Viroid: only low-molecular-weight free RNA, no capsid.

Q10. What are lichens? State their ecological importance.

Symbiotic association of alga and fungus; pollution indicators, pioneer colonisers of rocks, source of dyes/food.

Q11. Distinguish the four classes of fungi (long).

See table: Phycomycetes – aseptate, zygospore; Ascomycetes – asci/ascospores; Basidiomycetes – basidiospores, mushrooms; Deuteromycetes – asexual only. Give examples of each.

Q12. Describe the structure of a virus (long).

Genetic core (DNA/RNA) enclosed by capsid of capsomeres; bacteriophage has head, collar, tail sheath, base plate & tail fibres. Replicates only inside host using host machinery.
