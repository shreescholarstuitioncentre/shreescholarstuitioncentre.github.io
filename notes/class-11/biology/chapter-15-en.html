❤️ Class 11 Biology – Body Fluids and Circulation

1. Blood

Blood is a fluid connective tissue – plasma (55%) + formed elements (45%).

Plasma: water 90–92%, proteins 6–8% (fibrinogen – clotting; globulins – defence; albumins – osmotic balance), minerals (Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, HCO₃⁻, Cl⁻), glucose, amino acids, lipids. Plasma without clotting factors = serum.

Rh group: Rh antigen (D) on RBC of ~80% humans (Rh⁺). An Rh⁻ mother carrying Rh⁺ foetus forms anti-Rh antibodies at first delivery; the next Rh⁺ foetus may suffer erythroblastosis foetalis (destruction of foetal RBC). Prevented by giving the mother anti-Rh antibodies just after the first delivery.

Coagulation

Injury → platelets release thromboplastin (clotting factors) → with Ca²⁺ converts prothrombin → thrombin → thrombin converts fibrinogen → fibrin threads → network traps RBC, WBC and platelets = clot. Clot inside vessels = thrombus (coronary thrombosis). Heparin is an anticoagulant.

Lymph

Tissue fluid that drains into lymph vessels: colourless, has lymphocytes but few proteins, no RBC. Functions: returns fluid to blood, carries fat from intestinal lacteals, immune responses.

2. Circulatory Patterns

Open (arthropods, molluscs – blood in open spaces) and closed (annelids, chordates). Fishes: 2-chambered heart, single circulation. Amphibians and reptiles (except crocodiles): 3-chambered, incomplete double circulation. Birds and mammals: 4-chambered, complete double circulation.

3. Human Heart

Mesodermal, fist-sized, in thoracic cavity slightly tilted left; enclosed in double-walled pericardium with pericardial fluid. Four chambers: 2 atria (thin-walled), 2 ventricles (thick; left thicker). Atrio-ventricular septum has openings with valves: tricuspid (right) and bicuspid/mitral (left); semilunar valves at the pulmonary artery and aorta ensure one-way flow.

Nodal tissue (autoexcitable): SA node (right upper atrial wall; pacemaker, 70–75 impulses/min) → AV node (lower left of right atrium) → AV bundle (bundle of His) → right and left bundle branches → Purkinje fibres in ventricles. Heart is myogenic.

Cardiac cycle

One cycle = 0.8 s (about 72 beats/min). Atrial systole 0.1 s; ventricular systole 0.3 s; joint diastole 0.4 s. Stroke volume ≈ 70 mL; cardiac output = SV × heart rate ≈ 5 L/min. Heart sounds: lub (S1) – closure of tricuspid and bicuspid valves at start of ventricular systole; dub (S2) – closure of semilunar valves at end of ventricular systole.

ECG (electrocardiogram)

Recorded by electrocardiograph with 3 electrodes (two wrists, left ankle). P wave = atrial depolarisation (→ atrial contraction); QRS complex = ventricular depolarisation (→ ventricular contraction); T wave = ventricular repolarisation (→ relaxation). Number of QRS complexes per minute = heart rate; abnormalities indicate disease.

4. Double Circulation

Systemic: left ventricle → aorta → arteries → tissues → veins → vena cava → right atrium. Pulmonary: right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium. Blood passes twice through the heart per cycle. Hepatic portal system: intestine → hepatic portal vein → liver → hepatic vein → inferior vena cava. Coronary circulation: supplies the heart muscle.

5. Regulation of Cardiac Activity

Intrinsic (nodal) control plus neural control by the cardiac centre in medulla: sympathetic nerves increase rate and strength of contraction (↑ cardiac output); parasympathetic (vagus) decreases rate. Hormonal: adrenal medulla (adrenaline, noradrenaline) increases cardiac output.

6. Disorders

Hypertension: BP above 140/90 (normal 120/80 mm Hg; systolic/diastolic); harms heart, brain, kidney. Coronary artery disease (atherosclerosis): deposits of calcium, fat, cholesterol, fibrous tissue narrow the arteries. Angina pectoris: acute chest pain when insufficient O₂ reaches the heart muscle (more in elderly). Heart failure: heart cannot pump enough blood (congestive HF – lungs congested); not the same as cardiac arrest (heart stops) or heart attack (heart muscle damaged).

8. Short & Long Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. What is serum?

Plasma without clotting factors such as fibrinogen.

Q2. Why are RBCs biconcave and without nucleus?

Biconcave shape gives larger surface; no nucleus leaves more space for haemoglobin.

Q3. Where are RBC formed and destroyed?

Formed in red bone marrow; destroyed in spleen.

Q4. Name types of WBCs.

Neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, lymphocytes, monocytes.

Q5. Role of platelets.

Release clotting factors and form clot to stop bleeding.

Q6. Why is blood group O universal donor?

Its RBC lack A and B antigens.

Q7. Explain Rh incompatibility.

Rh⁻ mother with Rh⁺ foetus makes anti-Rh antibodies that destroy RBC of later Rh⁺ foetus (erythroblastosis foetalis).

Q8. Describe blood clotting.

Thromboplastin → prothrombin to thrombin (Ca²⁺) → fibrinogen to fibrin → clot.

Q9. What is lymph? Functions.

Fluid with lymphocytes; returns fluid to blood, absorbs fat, immunity.

Q10. Compare open and closed circulation.

Open: blood in sinuses (arthropods). Closed: blood in vessels (chordates, annelids).

Q11. Why is heart called myogenic?

Heartbeat is initiated by the muscle’s own nodal tissue (SAN), not nerves.

Q12. Why is SA node called pacemaker?

It generates the highest rate of impulses, setting the heart rate.

Q13. Write the sequence of impulse conduction.

SAN → AVN → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.

Q14. Explain heart sounds.

Lub: AV valves close; dub: semilunar valves close.

Q15. What does the ECG show?

P: atrial depolarisation; QRS: ventricular depolarisation; T: ventricular repolarisation.

Q16. Define double circulation.

Blood passes twice through the heart: pulmonary and systemic circuits.

Q17. What is hepatic portal system?

Vein carrying blood from the intestine to the liver before the heart.

Q18. Differentiate heart failure, heart attack and cardiac arrest.

Failure: weak pumping; attack: muscle damage; arrest: heart stops beating.

Q19. What is angina pectoris?

Chest pain due to insufficient oxygen supply to heart muscle.
