Class 11 Biology • Unit 5: Human Physiology

1. Endocrine Glands & Hormones

Endocrine glands are ductless; they pour secretions (hormones) directly into blood. Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals that act as intercellular messengers, produced in trace amounts. They act on target cells having specific receptors, then are removed or destroyed. Chemically: peptide/protein (insulin, glucagon, pituitary hormones), steroid (cortisol, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone), iodothyronines (thyroxine), amino-acid derivatives (adrenaline).

3. Hormones of Heart, Kidney & GI Tract

Heart (atrial wall): ANF – dilates vessels → lowers blood pressure. Kidney (juxtaglomerular cells): erythropoietin – stimulates RBC formation. GI tract: gastrin (gastric juice), secretin (pancreatic bicarbonate, bile), CCK (pancreatic enzymes, gall-bladder contraction), GIP (inhibits gastric secretion, stimulates insulin).

4. Mechanism of Hormone Action (Animated)

Protein hormones (cAMP pathway): hormone binds membrane receptor → G-protein activates adenylate cyclase → ATP → cAMP → activates protein kinase → enzymes → response. Other second messengers: IP₃, DAG, Ca²⁺.
Steroid/thyroid hormones: cross the membrane, bind intracellular receptors; the complex enters the nucleus, binds DNA and regulates gene expression (mRNA → proteins).

5. Solved Questions

Q1. Why is the pituitary called the "master gland"?

Its tropic hormones (TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH) control other endocrine glands – though it is itself regulated by the hypothalamus.

Q2. Name two antagonistic hormones for blood Ca²⁺.

PTH (raises) and thyrocalcitonin (lowers).

Q3. Why is iodised salt recommended?

Iodine is needed to make thyroxine; deficiency causes simple goitre.

Q4. Differentiate insulin and glucagon.

Insulin: β-cells, lowers glucose (glucose → glycogen). Glucagon: α-cells, raises glucose (glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis).

Q5. Long: Describe the mechanism of hormone action.

Write both pathways from Section 4 and draw the cAMP diagram with labels.

Q6. A patient passes huge urine volume with no glucose. Which hormone is deficient?

ADH → diabetes insipidus (no glucose in urine, unlike diabetes mellitus).

MCQs

1. Hormone of emergency: (a) insulin (b) adrenaline (c) PTH (d) melatonin

Ans: (b).

2. Erythropoietin is secreted by: (a) liver (b) kidney (c) heart (d) spleen

Ans: (b).

3. Which stimulates gall-bladder contraction? (a) gastrin (b) secretin (c) CCK (d) GIP

Ans: (c).

4. Steroid hormones act through: (a) cAMP (b) intracellular receptors (c) IP₃ (d) Ca²⁺ channels

Ans: (b).

5. Addison's disease is due to deficiency of: (a) cortisol (b) insulin (c) GH (d) thyroxine

Ans: (a).

Quick revision: Hypothalamus → pituitary → target glands • Insulin ↓ / glucagon ↑ glucose • PTH ↑ / TCT ↓ Ca²⁺ • Peptides: cAMP; steroids: gene expression.
