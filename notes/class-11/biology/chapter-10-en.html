✏️ Class 11 Biology – Cell Cycle & Cell Division

1. Cell Cycle

The sequence of events by which a cell duplicates its genome, grows and divides is the cell cycle. In a human cell it lasts about 24 hours; yeast takes ~90 min.

Interphase (~95% of time) = G1 (growth, protein/enzyme synthesis; cell may exit to G0 quiescent stage) → S (DNA replication; DNA amount 2C→4C, chromosome number stays 2n; centriole duplicates in animal cell) → G2 (protein synthesis for spindle, further growth). M phase (~1 hr) = actual division.

2. Mitosis (Equational division)

Occurs in somatic cells; 2n → 2 daughter cells with 2n. Aids growth, repair, asexual reproduction.

(animated: stages repeat automatically)

Prophase

Chromatin condenses into chromosomes, each with 2 chromatids joined at centromere; centrosomes move to poles and spindle starts forming; nucleolus and nuclear envelope disappear; Golgi, ER vanish.

Metaphase

Nuclear envelope fully gone. Chromosomes are at their most condensed (best stage to count/study). Kinetochores attach to spindle fibres; chromosomes align on the metaphase plate (equator).

Anaphase

Centromeres split; sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles (each now a chromosome). Centromere leads, arms trail.

Telophase

Chromosomes decondense into chromatin; nuclear envelope and nucleolus reappear around each set; Golgi and ER reform. Reverse of prophase.

Cytokinesis

Animal cell: furrow forms and deepens (cleavage). Plant cell: cell plate forms from the centre outward (rigid wall) – the cell plate becomes middle lamella. Karyokinesis = nuclear division; cytokinesis = cytoplasm division.

3. Meiosis (Reductional division)

In germ cells; 2n → 4 haploid (n) cells. One DNA replication, two divisions.

Meiosis I

Prophase I (longest) has 5 sub-stages: Leptotene (chromosomes condense), Zygotene (synapsis, synaptonemal complex → bivalents), Pachytene (crossing over by recombinase; tetrads), Diplotene (synaptonemal complex dissolves; chiasmata visible), Diakinesis (terminalisation of chiasmata, nuclear envelope breaks). Metaphase I: bivalents on equator. Anaphase I: homologous chromosomes separate (chromatids stay joined). Telophase I: 2 haploid cells (dyad).

Meiosis II

Like mitosis, without DNA replication (interkinesis is short): Prophase II, Metaphase II, Anaphase II (chromatids separate), Telophase II → 4 haploid cells (tetrad).

Significance: keeps chromosome number constant across generations; crossing over and random assortment give genetic variation, the raw material of evolution.

5. Solved numerical examples

Ex.1 (Easy) A cell has 2n=8. Chromosomes after mitosis in each daughter cell?
Step 1: mitosis keeps number same. Step 2: each daughter = 2n = 8.
Ex.2 (Medium) 2n=46 in a human cell. Find chromosomes and chromatids at G2, anaphase I and gamete.
G2: 46 chromosomes, 92 chromatids (after S). Anaphase I: 46 chromosomes still (homologues separate, 23 to each pole). Gamete: n = 23.
Ex.3 (Hard) How many meiotic divisions to make 100 sperms?
1 meiocyte → 4 sperms, so 100/4 = 25 meiocytes (25 meiosis). For 100 ova: 100 meiocytes (1 ovum each).
Ex.4 (Proof idea) Mitosis is equational: DNA doubles once (2C→4C) and halves once (4C→2C) so each daughter has 2C = parent. Meiosis: doubles once, halves twice (4C→2C→C) so gametes are haploid.

6. Short & Long Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Define cell cycle.

Series of events in which a cell grows, replicates DNA and divides into two daughter cells.

Q2. Why is S phase called synthetic phase?

DNA replicates here; DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C but chromosome number remains same.

Q3. What is G0 phase?

Quiescent stage: cells exit G1 and stay metabolically active but do not divide (e.g. heart cells).

Q4. Why is metaphase best to study chromosomes?

Chromosomes are most condensed and lie on one plane, so shape and number are clear.

Q5. Difference between karyokinesis and cytokinesis.

Karyokinesis is division of nucleus; cytokinesis is division of cytoplasm.

Q6. How does cytokinesis differ in plants?

Plants have a cell wall, so a cell plate forms centrifugally; animals show furrowing.

Q7. What is crossing over? Where and when?

Exchange of segments between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes at pachytene of prophase I; enzyme recombinase.

Q8. What are chiasmata?

X-shaped points of crossing over seen in diplotene.

Q9. What is synapsis?

Pairing of homologous chromosomes in zygotene, forming a synaptonemal complex.

Q10. Significance of mitosis.

Growth, tissue repair, replacement of cells, asexual reproduction, constant chromosome number.

Q11. Significance of meiosis.

Haploid gametes, constant chromosome number, genetic variation and evolution.

Q12. Why is meiosis I called reductional?

Chromosome number halves from 2n to n as homologues separate.

7. MCQs (tap for answer)

MCQ1. Longest phase of cell cycle? a) M b) Interphase c) Prophase d) Telophase

b) Interphase.

MCQ2. DNA replication occurs in: a) G1 b) S c) G2 d) M

b) S phase.

MCQ3. Chromosomes align on equator in: a) Prophase b) Metaphase c) Anaphase d) Telophase

b) Metaphase.

MCQ4. Crossing over occurs in: a) Leptotene b) Zygotene c) Pachytene d) Diplotene

c) Pachytene.

MCQ5. Chiasmata are visible in: a) Diplotene b) Zygotene c) Leptotene d) Anaphase II

a) Diplotene.

MCQ6. Cell plate formation is seen in: a) Animal b) Plant c) Bacteria d) Virus cell

b) Plant cell.

MCQ7. Meiosis II resembles: a) Mitosis b) Binary fission c) Budding d) None

a) Mitosis.

MCQ8. Meiosis produces: a) 2 diploid b) 4 haploid c) 2 haploid d) 4 diploid
