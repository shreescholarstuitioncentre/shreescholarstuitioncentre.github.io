Class 11 Biology – Ch 6 & 7: Anatomy of Plants and Structural Organisation in Animals

Chapter 6 – Anatomy of Flowering Plants

1. Meristematic & Permanent Tissues

Meristems: apical (root/shoot tips → primary growth), intercalary (grasses, base of internodes), lateral (vascular cambium, cork cambium → secondary growth, girth). Permanent tissues: Simple – parenchyma (storage, photosynthesis), collenchyma (corners thickened with pectin; flexibility to young stem), sclerenchyma (lignified dead cells: fibres, sclereids; mechanical strength). Complex – xylem & phloem.

2. Tissue Systems

(a) Epidermal system: single layer outermost; cuticle (cutin, reduces water loss); stomata – two guard cells (bean-shaped in dicots, dumb-bell in grasses) + subsidiary cells; root hairs (unicellular, absorb); trichomes (multicellular, reduce transpiration).

(b) Ground tissue system: everything except epidermis & vascular bundles – cortex, pericycle, pith, medullary rays; in leaves mesophyll (palisade + spongy).

(c) Vascular system: Xylem = tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres (dead, conduct water), xylem parenchyma (only living cells). Phloem = sieve tube elements, companion cells (control sieve tubes), phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres; conducts food. Vascular bundles: radial (roots; xylem & phloem on different radii), conjoint (stem, leaf). Open = cambium present (dicot stem); closed = no cambium (monocot stem). Endarch protoxylem towards pith (stem); exarch towards periphery (root).

5. Frog (Rana tigrina)

Morphology: lives in water & land; skin moist, slippery, mucous; olive-green with dark spots above, pale yellow below; no tail; head + trunk; colour change (camouflage), hibernation (winter), aestivation (summer). Forelimbs 4 digits, hind limbs 5 webbed digits. Male: vocal sacs, nuptial pads on first digit of forelimb (sexual dimorphism).

Digestive system: short alimentary canal (carnivore). Mouth → buccal cavity (bilobed sticky tongue) → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → intestine (duodenum + ileum) → rectum → cloaca. Liver (bile via gall bladder), pancreas (pancreatic juice). Food → HCl & gastric juices → chyme → duodenum → bile & pancreatic juice → absorption in ileum (villi, microvilli).

Respiration: skin (cutaneous, in water & hibernation), buccopharyngeal cavity, lungs (pulmonary; on land) – pair of elongated sac-like lungs.

Circulation: closed; 3-chambered heart (2 atria, 1 ventricle) in pericardium; sinus venosus, truncus arteriosus (conus). Blood: plasma + nucleated RBCs (with haemoglobin), WBC, platelets. Portal systems: hepatic portal, renal portal. Mixed blood in ventricle. Lymph system present.

Nervous system: CNS (brain in cranium + spinal cord), PNS (10 pairs cranial nerves), ANS. Brain: forebrain (olfactory lobes, paired cerebral hemispheres, diencephalon), midbrain (optic lobes), hindbrain (cerebellum, medulla oblongata). Sense organs: touch cells, taste buds, nasal epithelium, eyes (simple), internal ear (membranous labyrinth; tympanum visible).

Excretion/Reproduction: ureotelic; kidneys, ureters, cloaca. Male: pair of testes → vasa efferentia → Bidder's canal → ureter → cloaca. Female: pair of ovaries → oviducts → cloaca. External fertilisation in water; tadpole larva → metamorphosis (indirect development).

6. Q & A (Short, Long & MCQ)

Q1. What is a closed vascular bundle?

Ans: One without cambium between xylem and phloem – monocot stem; no secondary growth.

Q2. Distinguish xylem and phloem.

Ans: Xylem: water/minerals, dead cells except parenchyma, tracheids/vessels. Phloem: food, sieve tubes + companion cells (living).

Q3. Role of guard cells and subsidiary cells.

Ans: Regulate opening/closing of stomata; subsidiary cells assist them.

Q4. What are bulliform cells?

Ans: Large colourless cells in upper epidermis of grasses; roll leaf in water stress.

Q5. Draw differences between dicot and monocot stem (long).

Ans: Use table: hypodermis, ground tissue, bundle arrangement, open/closed, phloem parenchyma, water cavity.

Q6. What are annual rings? How do they help?

Ans: Alternate spring (light) and autumn (dark) wood layers per year; age determination of tree.

Q7. Why do monocots lack secondary growth?

Ans: Closed bundles without cambium.

Q8. Name the types of epithelial tissue and one function each.

Ans: Squamous – diffusion; cuboidal – secretion; columnar – absorption; ciliated – movement of particles.

Q9. Distinguish tendon and ligament.

Ans: Tendon: muscle to bone; ligament: bone to bone; both dense regular connective tissue.

Q10. Differentiate three kinds of muscle (long).

Ans: Skeletal – striated voluntary; smooth – non-striated involuntary; cardiac – striated involuntary branched with intercalated discs.

Q11. Why is frog's heart called 3-chambered? Is blood mixed?

Ans: 2 atria + 1 ventricle; mixing occurs in ventricle.

Q12. Where does fertilisation occur in frog and what is Bidder's canal?

Ans: External, in water. Bidder's canal: connects kidney tubules with vasa efferentia in male; carries sperm to ureter.

Q13. State differences between male and female frog.

Ans: Male: vocal sacs, nuptial pads. Female: absent.

Q14. What is hibernation and aestivation?

Ans: Winter and summer inactivity respectively to avoid extreme temperature.
