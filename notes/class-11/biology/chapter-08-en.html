Class 11 Biology – Ch 8: Cell – The Unit of Life

1. Cell Theory

Robert Hooke (1665) saw cells in cork; Leeuwenhoek saw first living cell; Robert Brown (1831) discovered nucleus; Schleiden (1838, plants) & Schwann (1839, animals) proposed cell theory; Virchow (1855): omnis cellula-e cellula (new cells from pre-existing cells). Theory: (i) all living organisms are made of cells and their products; (ii) cell is the structural & functional unit; (iii) new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Size: smallest – Mycoplasma (0.3 µm); bacteria 3–5 µm; human RBC ~7 µm; largest isolated cell – ostrich egg; longest – nerve cell.

2. Prokaryotic Cell

Bacteria, blue-green algae, Mycoplasma. No membrane-bound organelles, no true nucleus.

Cell envelope: glycocalyx (loose slime layer or tough capsule), cell wall (peptidoglycan; shape & protection – Gram +ve thick, Gram –ve with outer membrane), plasma membrane. Mesosome: membrane infolding – cell wall formation, DNA replication, respiration. Flagellum: filament, hook, basal body. Pili (conjugation), fimbriae (attachment). Ribosomes: 70S (50S + 30S), polysomes. Inclusion bodies: phosphate granules, cyanophycean granules, glycogen, gas vacuoles (blue-greens). Nucleoid: single circular naked DNA; plasmids – extra small circular DNA (antibiotic resistance; used in biotechnology).

Plant vs animal: plants have cell wall, plastids, large central vacuole, no centrioles (higher plants); animals have centrioles, small vacuoles, no cell wall.

Cell membrane – Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972)

Phospholipid bilayer (hydrophilic heads out, hydrophobic tails in), integral and peripheral proteins, cholesterol; lipids quasi-fluid → lateral movement. Transport: passive (simple/facilitated diffusion, osmosis, down gradient) & active (against gradient, ATP; Na⁺–K⁺ pump).

Cell wall

Algae: cellulose, galactans, mannans; plants: cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, proteins. Middle lamella (Ca-pectate) glues cells; primary wall; secondary wall; plasmodesmata connect cytoplasm.

Endomembrane system

ER: network of tubules; RER (ribosomes; protein synthesis & secretion), SER (lipids, steroids, detoxification). Golgi (Camillo Golgi, 1898): cisternae with cis (forming) & trans (maturing) faces; packages, modifies & dispatches; glycosylation; forms lysosomes. Lysosomes: vesicles with hydrolytic enzymes (acidic pH) – "suicide bags". Vacuoles: tonoplast; plants – sap, storage, turgidity; Amoeba – contractile (osmoregulation), food vacuoles.

Mitochondria – powerhouse of the cell

Double membrane; outer smooth, inner folded into cristae (↑ surface area, ATP synthase F1 particles); matrix – enzymes of Krebs cycle, circular DNA, 70S ribosomes; semi-autonomous, divides by fission; 0.2–1.0 µm.

Plastids

Chloroplast (green; chlorophyll): double membrane, stroma (enzymes, DNA, 70S ribosomes) & grana (stacks of thylakoids; stroma lamellae). Chromoplasts – carotene, xanthophyll (flower, fruit colour). Leucoplasts – colourless: amyloplast (starch), elaioplast (oil), aleuroplast (protein).

Ribosomes, microbodies, cytoskeleton

Ribosomes (George Palade, 1953): rRNA + protein, no membrane; 80S = 60S + 40S. Microbodies: peroxisomes, glyoxysomes. Cytoskeleton: microtubules, microfilaments, intermediate filaments – shape, support, movement. Cilia & flagella: hair-like outgrowths of plasma membrane; axoneme 9+2 microtubules; basal body 9+0 (triplets); cilia short (oar-like), flagella long (undulating).

Centrosome & Nucleus

Centrosome has two centrioles at right angles; each 9 triplets (cartwheel), form basal bodies & spindle. Nucleus: nuclear envelope (2 membranes, perinuclear space, nuclear pores), nucleoplasm, nucleolus (rRNA synthesis, ribosome assembly, no membrane), chromatin (DNA + histones; euchromatin light/active, heterochromatin dark/inactive). Chromosome: centromere + kinetochore; metacentric (middle), sub-metacentric, acrocentric, telocentric (terminal); some have satellite.

4. Q & A (Short, Long, Numerical & MCQ)

Q1. State the cell theory and who modified it.

Ans: Schleiden–Schwann: cells are units of life. Virchow added: new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Q2. What are plasmids? Why important?

Ans: Small circular extra-chromosomal DNA of bacteria; carry resistance genes; used as vectors in genetic engineering.

Q3. Differentiate 70S and 80S ribosomes.

Ans: 70S (50S+30S) – prokaryotes, mitochondria, plastids; 80S (60S+40S) – eukaryotic cytoplasm.

Q4. What is a mesosome?

Ans: Infolding of bacterial plasma membrane – helps in cell wall formation, DNA replication, respiration.

Q5. Why are lysosomes called suicide bags?

Ans: On cell damage they burst and their hydrolytic enzymes digest the cell itself.

Q6. Why is mitochondrion called semi-autonomous?

Ans: It has its own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes and makes some proteins, but depends on nucleus for others.

Q7. Why does inner mitochondrial membrane form cristae?

Ans: To increase surface area for ATP synthase and electron transport.

Q8. Distinguish RER and SER.

Ans: RER: ribosomes, protein synthesis, flattened. SER: no ribosomes, lipid & steroid synthesis, tubular.

Q9. Describe fluid mosaic model (long).

Ans: Phospholipid bilayer with tails inward; integral and peripheral proteins float; cholesterol; membrane quasi-fluid; allows lateral movement and selective permeability.

Q10. Describe the structure of nucleus (long).

Ans: Envelope with pores, nucleoplasm, nucleolus, chromatin; chromosomes with centromere, kinetochore; types by centromere position.

Q11. Differentiate prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells (long).

Ans: Use the comparison table: nucleus, DNA, ribosome, organelles, division.

Q12. Numerical (to find): a cell appears 2 cm long in a micrograph taken at 400×. Find actual size.

Ans: Given: image size = 2 cm = 20,000 µm; magnification = 400. Formula: actual = image ÷ magnification. Step 1: 20,000 ÷ 400. Step 2: = 50 µm. Answer: 50 µm (0.05 mm).

Q13. Numerical (to find): actual size of a bacterium is 2 µm; at 1000× find image size.

Ans: Image = actual × magnification = 2 × 1000 = 2000 µm = 2 mm.

Q14. What is the difference between centriole and basal body?

Ans: Centriole: 9 triplets cartwheel, forms spindle. Basal body: similar structure at base of cilium/flagellum.
