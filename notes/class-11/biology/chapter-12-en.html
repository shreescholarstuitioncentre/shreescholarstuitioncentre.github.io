🔥 Class 11 Biology – Respiration in Plants

1. Introduction

Respiration is the oxidation of food (glucose) in cells to release energy stored as ATP. Glucose is broken down step by step (not burnt at once) so energy is captured in small usable packets. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (686 kcal). Plants have no special organs: gases diffuse through stomata and lenticels.

2. Glycolysis (EMP pathway)

Occurs in the cytoplasm, in all organisms, with or without O₂. Glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvic acid (3C). Scientists: Embden, Meyerhof, Parnas.

Steps: (1) Glucose + ATP → glucose-6-P (hexokinase). (2) → fructose-6-P (isomerase). (3) + ATP → fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (PFK, key regulatory enzyme). (4) splits (aldolase) → DHAP + PGAL (interconvertible). (5) PGAL oxidised, P added → 1,3-BPGA, NAD⁺→NADH (×2). (6) → 3-PGA, ATP formed (substrate-level). (7) → 2-PGA → PEP. (8) PEP → pyruvate, ATP formed. Investment: 2 ATP; yield: 4 ATP; net 2 ATP + 2 NADH.

3. Fermentation (Anaerobic)

When O₂ is absent, pyruvate is converted in the cytoplasm so that NAD⁺ is regenerated and glycolysis continues.

Alcoholic (yeast, some plant tissue): Pyruvate → acetaldehyde + CO₂ (pyruvate decarboxylase) → ethanol (alcohol dehydrogenase; NADH→NAD⁺). Lactic acid (muscle, bacteria): Pyruvate → lactic acid (lactate dehydrogenase), no CO₂. Net gain only 2 ATP; much energy stays in ethanol/lactate. Yeast tolerates ~13% alcohol.

4. Aerobic Respiration

Pyruvate enters mitochondria (matrix). Oxidative decarboxylation: pyruvate + CoA + NAD⁺ → acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH (pyruvate dehydrogenase; Mg²⁺ needed).

Krebs (TCA / citric acid) cycle — in the matrix

Acetyl CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) → citrate (6C, citrate synthase) → isocitrate → (NADH, CO₂) α-ketoglutarate (5C) → (NADH, CO₂) succinyl CoA → succinate (GTP→ATP, substrate-level) → fumarate (FADH₂; succinate dehydrogenase, in inner membrane) → malate → OAA (NADH). OAA is regenerated, so the cycle continues. Per acetyl CoA: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP, 2 CO₂.

ETS & Oxidative phosphorylation — inner mitochondrial membrane

Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) → ubiquinone → Complex III (cyt bc₁) → cyt c → Complex IV (cyt a-a₃, copper) → O₂ → H₂O. FADH₂ enters at Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase). Complexes I, III, IV pump H⁺ into the intermembrane space; proton gradient flows back through ATP synthase (F₀ channel, F₁ makes ATP). Oxygen is the final electron acceptor; it drives the whole chain.

6. Amphibolic Pathway

Respiration is amphibolic: it is catabolic (breaks glucose) and also anabolic. Fatty acids → acetyl CoA; glycerol → PGAL; proteins → amino acids → pyruvate/acetyl CoA/Krebs intermediates; and Krebs intermediates (e.g. α-KG, OAA) are drawn off to make amino acids. So respiratory intermediates are a common pool for all foods.

7. Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

RQ = Volume of CO₂ evolved ÷ Volume of O₂ consumed.

8. Solved Examples

Ex.1 (Easy) Net ATP in glycolysis? Used 2, formed 4 → net 2.
Ex.2 (Easy) RQ of glucose: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. RQ = 6/6 = 1.
Ex.3 (Medium) RQ of palmitic acid: C₁₆H₃₂O₂ + 23O₂ → 16CO₂ + 16H₂O. RQ = 16/23 = 0.7.
Ex.4 (Medium, to find) RQ of malic acid: C₄H₆O₅ + 3O₂ → 4CO₂ + 3H₂O → 4/3 = 1.33.
Ex.5 (Hard) ATP from 3 glucose (aerobic, classical): 3 × 38 = 114 ATP.
Ex.6 (Hard) Efficiency: glucose = 2870 kJ/mol; ATP = 30.6 kJ/mol. 38 × 30.6 = 1163 kJ; 1163/2870 × 100 ≈ 40%. Rest is lost as heat.
Ex.7 (Proof) Why Krebs cycle yields 2 CO₂ per turn: acetyl CoA gives 2 C into the cycle; isocitrate → α-KG and α-KG → succinyl CoA each release one CO₂, so 2 carbons leave and OAA (4C) is regenerated.
Ex.8 (Hard) ATP per glucose in fermentation: 2 ATP; aerobic gives 19× more (38/2).

9. Short & Long Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Where does glycolysis occur?

In the cytoplasm of all cells.

Q2. Why is glycolysis called the common pathway?

It is the first step for both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

Q3. Name the key enzyme regulating glycolysis.

Phosphofructokinase (PFK).

Q4. What is substrate-level phosphorylation?

ATP formed directly by transfer of phosphate from a substrate (1,3-BPGA, PEP, succinyl CoA).

Q5. Why does fermentation occur?

To regenerate NAD⁺ from NADH so glycolysis can continue without O₂.

Q6. Difference between alcoholic and lactic fermentation.

Alcoholic gives ethanol + CO₂ (yeast); lactic gives lactic acid, no CO₂ (muscle).

Q7. Where does Krebs cycle occur?

Mitochondrial matrix (succinate dehydrogenase is in inner membrane).

Q8. Why is the Krebs cycle called a cycle?

OAA, the starting acceptor, is regenerated at the end.

Q9. What is the final electron acceptor in ETS?

Molecular oxygen, forming water.

Q10. What is oxidative phosphorylation?

ATP synthesis using energy from e⁻ transport and proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Q11. Why is respiration amphibolic?

It involves both breakdown (catabolism) and synthesis (anabolism) from intermediates.

Q12. Define RQ. RQ of fats?

CO₂ evolved/O₂ consumed; fats ≈ 0.7 as they are poor in oxygen and need more O₂.
