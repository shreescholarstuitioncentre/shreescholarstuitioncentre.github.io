💧 Class 11 Biology – Excretory Products and their Elimination

1. Nitrogenous Wastes

Ammonia, urea and uric acid are produced by protein/nucleic acid breakdown; their form depends on habitat and water availability.

TypeWasteExamplesFeatures
AmmonotelicAmmoniaBony fishes, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insectsMost toxic, needs much water; diffuses via gills/body surface; kidney has little role
UreotelicUrea (made in liver)Mammals, terrestrial amphibians, marine fishesLess toxic, some water saved; some urea retained in kidney matrix to keep osmolarity
UricotelicUric acidReptiles, birds, land snails, insectsLeast toxic; excreted as pellet/paste with minimal water loss

Excretory structures: Protonephridia (flame cells) – Platyhelminthes, rotifers, Amphioxus; nephridia – earthworm; Malpighian tubules – insects (cockroach); antennal/green glands – prawn; kidneys – vertebrates.

2. Human Excretory System

A pair of kidneys (reddish-brown, bean-shaped, 10–12 cm long, 120–170 g) in the abdominal cavity between the last thoracic and 3rd lumbar vertebra; ureters → urinary bladder → urethra. Kidney: hilum (depression where ureter, blood vessels, nerves enter), renal pelvis with calyces; outer cortex, inner medulla in renal pyramids, cortex extends as columns of Bertini. Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons (functional units).

Nephron

Glomerulus (tuft of capillaries; afferent arteriole in, efferent arteriole out) + Bowman’s capsule = renal (Malpighian) corpuscle (in cortex). Renal tubule: PCT (cuboidal epithelium with brush border), loop of Henle (descending + ascending limb), DCT, opening into collecting duct. Cortical nephrons: short loop, mostly in cortex. Juxtamedullary nephrons: long loop deep in medulla, with well-developed vasa recta (absent/reduced in cortical). Efferent arteriole forms peritubular capillaries.

3. Urine Formation

Three steps: glomerular filtration, reabsorption, secretion.

Glomerular filtration (ultrafiltration)

About 1100–1200 mL blood/min flows through kidneys (~1/5 of cardiac output). The filtration barrier has 3 layers: endothelium of glomerular capillaries, basement membrane, and podocytes (filtration slits) of Bowman’s capsule. All plasma components except large proteins filter. GFR ≈ 125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day. JG apparatus (juxtaglomerular cells + macula densa) regulates GFR: ↓GFR → JG cells release renin → angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → II (vasoconstrictor ↑BP/GFR; also stimulates aldosterone from adrenal cortex → Na⁺ and water reabsorption) – the RAAS.

Reabsorption (~99% of filtrate)

PCT: nearly all glucose, amino acids, 70–80% electrolytes and water; active (glucose, amino acids, Na⁺) and passive (water, nitrogenous wastes). Descending limb: permeable to water, almost impermeable to electrolytes → filtrate becomes concentrated. Ascending limb: impermeable to water, transports electrolytes out → filtrate dilutes. DCT: conditional reabsorption of Na⁺ and water; reabsorbs HCO₃⁻; selective secretion of H⁺, K⁺, NH₃. Collecting duct: large amount of water reabsorbed → concentrated urine; urea enters medullary interstitium; H⁺ and K⁺ secreted.

Tubular secretion

H⁺, K⁺, NH₃ secreted into filtrate (PCT, DCT, collecting duct) – maintains ionic and acid-base balance.

4. Concentration of Filtrate – Counter-current Mechanism

The two limbs of loop of Henle and the vasa recta run in opposite directions (counter-current). The interstitial osmolarity rises from 300 mOsm/L in the cortex to ~1200 mOsm/L in the inner medulla, due to NaCl and urea. NaCl moves out of the ascending limb → exchanged to the descending limb of vasa recta, returned by the ascending vasa recta; urea enters the loop (thin segment) and recycles. This gradient lets the collecting duct draw water out so human urine can be 4× concentrated (1200 mOsm/L).

5. Regulation of Kidney Function

ADH (vasopressin, from posterior pituitary): osmoreceptors detect ↑ osmolarity or ↓ volume → ADH released → DCT/collecting duct water reabsorption ↑ → ↓ urine. Lack of ADH → diabetes insipidus (large dilute urine). RAAS (above). ANF (atrial natriuretic factor, from heart when BP ↑): vasodilation, opposes RAAS. Micturition: bladder stretches → signal to CNS → motor message → bladder contraction and relaxation of urethral sphincter (a reflex; voluntary control).

Urine: 1–1.5 L/day, light yellow (urochrome), pH ~6.0, 25–30 g urea/day. Glycosuria (glucose) and ketonuria (ketone bodies) indicate diabetes mellitus. Other organs: lungs (CO₂, water), liver (bilirubin, cholesterol, hormones), skin (sweat: NaCl, urea, lactic acid; sebum: sterols, waxes).

6. Disorders

Uremia: accumulation of urea in blood → kidney failure. Haemodialysis: blood from an artery of the arm, with heparin, passes through coiled cellophane (cuprophan) tube in dialysing fluid (like plasma without nitrogenous wastes); wastes diffuse out; cleaned blood returned to a vein after anti-heparin. Kidney transplant is the best long-term option. Renal calculi: stones of crystallised oxalates etc. in kidney. Glomerulonephritis: inflammation of glomeruli.

7. Solved Examples

Ex.1 (Easy) GFR 125 mL/min. Per day: 125 × 60 × 24 = 180,000 mL = 180 L.
Ex.2 (Easy) Urine = 1.5 L. Reabsorbed = 180 − 1.5 = 178.5 L.
Ex.3 (Medium) % reabsorbed = 178.5/180 × 100 = 99.2% (≈99%).
Ex.4 (Medium, to find) Concentration factor of urine: 1200/300 = 4 times blood osmolarity.
Ex.5 (Medium) Net filtration pressure = glomerular hydrostatic − (capsular hydrostatic + blood colloid osmotic) = 60 − (20 + 30) = 10 mm Hg (typical values).
Ex.6 (Hard) Kidney blood flow is 1200 mL/min; cardiac output 5000 mL/min. Fraction = 1200/5000 = 0.24 ≈ about 1/5.
Ex.7 (Proof) Why are birds uricotelic? Embryo develops in a closed shelled egg with limited water; ammonia/urea would be toxic or need water. Uric acid is insoluble, crystallises and is stored harmlessly → water conserved. Hence uric acid is the best waste for egg-laying land animals.
Ex.8 (Application) A person drinks 2 L of water. Effect: osmolarity ↓ → ADH ↓ → less water reabsorbed → more dilute urine.
Ex.9 (Application) Diabetic patient shows glycosuria: filtered glucose exceeds the PCT’s reabsorption capacity (transport maximum), so it appears in urine.

8. Short & Long Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Define excretion.

Removal of metabolic nitrogenous wastes from the body.

Q2. Differentiate ammonotelic, ureotelic and uricotelic.

Ammonia (aquatic; much water), urea (mammals; moderate water), uric acid (birds/reptiles; minimal water).

Q3. Name excretory organs of earthworm, prawn, insects, flatworms.

Nephridia, antennal glands, Malpighian tubules, flame cells (protonephridia).

Q4. Where are kidneys located?

Retroperitoneal, between last thoracic and 3rd lumbar vertebra.

Q5. What is a nephron? Name its parts.

Functional unit of kidney: Bowman’s capsule, glomerulus, PCT, loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct.

Q6. Differentiate cortical and juxtamedullary nephrons.

Cortical: short loop, no/poor vasa recta. Juxtamedullary: long loop, well-developed vasa recta.

Q7. What is ultrafiltration?

Filtration of blood plasma (without large proteins) under pressure across three layers into Bowman’s capsule.

Q8. What is GFR?

Volume filtered by all nephrons per minute; ~125 mL/min (180 L/day).

Q9. Role of JG apparatus.

Senses GFR/BP; releases renin → RAAS to raise GFR.

Q10. Describe reabsorption in PCT.

Glucose, amino acids, Na⁺ actively reabsorbed; water and 70–80% electrolytes follow.

Q11. Why is descending limb permeable to water only?

It lets water leave by osmosis, concentrating the filtrate.

Q12. Explain counter-current mechanism.

Opposite flows in loop limbs and vasa recta maintain a medullary gradient up to 1200 mOsm/L, concentrating urine.

Q13. Role of ADH.

Increases water reabsorption in DCT and collecting duct; prevents diuresis.

Q14. Define micturition.

Release of urine from bladder, a reflex with voluntary control.

Q15. Role of ANF.

Causes vasodilation, lowers BP and opposes RAAS.

Q16. What is haemodialysis?

Artificial kidney: blood passes through cellophane tube in dialysing fluid to remove wastes.

Q17. What are renal calculi?

Stones of crystallised salts (oxalates) in the kidney.

Q18. What is glycosuria?

Presence of glucose in urine, indicating diabetes mellitus.

Q19. Why does urine become yellow?

Presence of urochrome.
