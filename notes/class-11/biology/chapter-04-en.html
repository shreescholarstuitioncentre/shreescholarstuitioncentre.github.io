Class 11 Biology – Ch 4: Animal Kingdom

1. Basis of Classification

(a) Levels of organisation: cellular (Porifera), cell-tissue (Cnidaria, Ctenophora), organ (Platyhelminthes), organ-system (Aschelminthes onwards).

(b) Symmetry:

(c) Germ layers: Diploblastic – ectoderm + endoderm with undifferentiated mesoglea (Cnidaria, Ctenophora). Triploblastic – ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm (Platyhelminthes onwards).

(d) Coelom (body cavity lined by mesoderm): acoelomate (flatworms), pseudocoelomate (cavity not fully lined – roundworms), coelomate (annelids to chordates).

(e) Segmentation (metamerism): body divided into repeating units, e.g. earthworm. (f) Notochord: mesodermal rod on dorsal side during embryonic development – present in Chordata, absent in non-chordates.

3. Phylum Chordata

Four features: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail; also ventral heart and closed circulation.

Urochordata (Tunicata): notochord only in larval tail – Ascidia, Salpa, Doliolum. Cephalochordata: notochord from head to tail, persists through life – Branchiostoma (Amphioxus). Vertebrata: notochord replaced by vertebral column; 2–4 chambered ventral heart; kidneys; paired fins/limbs.

4. Q & A (Short, Long & MCQ)

Q1. Define coelom.

Ans: A body cavity fully lined by mesoderm; its presence defines coelomates.

Q2. What are choanocytes?

Ans: Flagellated collar cells lining the spongocoel of sponges; create water current and trap food.

Q3. What are cnidoblasts?

Ans: Stinging cells on tentacles of Cnidaria containing nematocysts for defence and prey capture.

Q4. What are comb plates?

Ans: Eight external rows of fused cilia in Ctenophora used for locomotion.

Q5. Name excretory structures of flatworms, annelids and arthropods.

Ans: Flame cells, nephridia, Malpighian tubules.

Q6. Why are Hemichordates no longer chordates?

Ans: They have no true notochord; the buccal diverticulum earlier called notochord is now separate.

Q7. What is metamerism?

Ans: Serial segmentation of the body into similar units, e.g. earthworm.

Q8. What is the function of the air bladder in fishes?

Ans: Regulates buoyancy; Chondrichthyes lack it and must keep swimming to avoid sinking.

Q9. Differentiate Chondrichthyes and Osteichthyes (long).

Ans: Cartilage vs bone; placoid vs cycloid/ctenoid scales; ventral mouth vs terminal; no operculum vs operculum; no air bladder vs air bladder; internal vs external fertilisation.

Q10. State four salient features of Chordata (long).

Ans: Notochord, dorsal hollow single nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail; also closed circulation and ventral heart.

Q11. Why are birds adapted to flight?

Ans: Feathers, wings, pneumatic bones, air sacs, 4-chambered heart, streamlined body, no urinary bladder.

Q12. Name egg-laying and pouched mammals.

Ans: Egg-laying: Ornithorhynchus; pouched: Macropus.
