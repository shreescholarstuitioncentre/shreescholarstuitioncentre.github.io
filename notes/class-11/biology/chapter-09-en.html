Class 11 Biology – Ch 9: Biomolecules

1. Chemical Analysis of Living Tissue

Grind tissue in trichloroacetic acid → slurry; filter: filtrate = acid-soluble pool (small molecules, MW < 1000 Da – amino acids, sugars, nucleotides); retentate = acid-insoluble fraction (macromolecules: proteins, polysaccharides, nucleic acids; lipids are retained as membrane fragments). Burning tissue leaves ash = inorganic elements (Ca, Mg, K, Na…). Elements: C, H, O, N most abundant; compounds: water (70–90%), proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids.

2. Primary & Secondary Metabolites

PrimarySecondary
Amino acids, sugars, nucleotides, fatty acids – identifiable functions in growth & normal physiology; found in all organismsNot directly involved in growth – ecological importance (plants, fungi, microbes): alkaloids (morphine, codeine), flavonoids, anthocyanins, carotenoids, terpenoids, essential oils, toxins (abrin, ricin), lectins (concanavalin A), drugs (vinblastine, curcumin), polymeric substances (rubber, gums, cellulose)

3. Proteins

Amino acid: α-carbon bearing –NH2, –COOH, H and R group; 20 types – acidic (glutamic acid), basic (lysine), neutral (valine), aromatic (tyrosine, phenylalanine, tryptophan); exist as zwitterions (+/– charges). Peptide bond (–CO–NH–) joins them: protein = heteropolymer. Functions: collagen (most abundant protein in animal world), RuBisCO (most abundant in the biosphere), insulin, antibodies, receptors, enzymes, transport (GLUT-4).

Structures: Primary – sequence of amino acids (N-terminal → C-terminal). Secondary – α-helix (right-handed, H-bonds) or β-pleated sheet. Tertiary – 3-D folding (hydrophobic interactions, H-bonds, ionic bonds, disulphide bridges) – gives biological activity. Quaternary – two or more polypeptides, e.g. adult haemoglobin (2α + 2β).

4. Polysaccharides (Carbohydrates)

Monosaccharides (glucose) → disaccharide (sucrose = glucose + fructose; maltose) by glycosidic bond. Starch (plant reserve; helical, holds I2 → blue), glycogen (animal reserve; branched), cellulose (β-1,4 glucose; straight, no I2 colour; plant cell wall), inulin (fructose polymer), chitin (N-acetyl glucosamine; arthropod exoskeleton, fungal wall). Reducing end at one side, non-reducing end at other.

5. Lipids

Water-insoluble; not macromolecules but in membranes. Fats/oils = glycerol + fatty acids (esters; fat = solid, oil = liquid at room temperature). Saturated (no double bond; palmitic acid C16) vs unsaturated. Phospholipids (lecithin): glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate + choline (polar head, non-polar tails) – membrane. Cholesterol – steroid.

6. Nucleic Acids

Nucleoside = base + sugar; nucleotide = + phosphate; joined by phosphodiester bonds. Purines: A, G; pyrimidines: C, U (RNA), T (DNA). Sugar: ribose (RNA), deoxyribose (DNA). DNA: Watson & Crick (1953) double helix; antiparallel strands; A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds); 10 bp/turn, pitch 34 Å, rise per bp 3.4 Å; Chargaff's rules (A=T, G=C; A+G = T+C). RNA: single strand – mRNA, tRNA, rRNA.

7. Enzymes

Biological catalysts – almost all proteins (exception: ribozymes – RNA). They lower the activation energy of reaction (not altering equilibrium). Catalytic site = active site (crevice in tertiary structure). Mechanism: E + S → ES complex → EP → E + P (lock-and-key / induced fit – Koshland). Fastest: carbonic anhydrase.

Factors affecting activity

Temperature: optimum (~37 °C human); low → inactive (not destroyed), high → denaturation. pH: optimum (pepsin 1.5–2, salivary amylase 6.8, trypsin ~8). Substrate concentration: velocity rises to Vmax; Km = [S] at ½Vmax (Michaelis–Menten). Inhibitors: competitive (resembles substrate, e.g. malonate for succinic dehydrogenase; sulpha drugs) – overcome by ↑ substrate; non-competitive – binds elsewhere.

Cofactors: prosthetic group (tightly bound – haem in catalase), co-enzyme (loosely, e.g. NAD, NADP with niacin; vitamin-derived), metal ion (Zn²⁺ in carboxypeptidase). Apoenzyme + cofactor = holoenzyme.

8. Q & A (Short, Long, Numerical & MCQ)

Q1. Differentiate primary and secondary metabolites.

Ans: Primary: common, essential for growth (sugars, amino acids). Secondary: not needed for growth but ecological roles (alkaloids, pigments, toxins).

Q2. What is the acid-soluble pool?

Ans: Small molecules (MW <1000 Da) in filtrate after grinding tissue with TCA.

Q3. Why is RuBisCO important?

Ans: Most abundant protein on earth; fixes CO2 in photosynthesis.

Q4. What is a peptide bond?

Ans: Covalent –CO–NH– linkage formed by dehydration between COOH of one and NH2 of another amino acid.

Q5. Why does cellulose not give blue colour with iodine?

Ans: Straight chain without helical coiling, so iodine is not held, unlike starch.

Q6. Why are enzymes denatured at high temperature?

Ans: Heat breaks weak bonds, destroying tertiary structure and active site.

Q7. Differentiate competitive and non-competitive inhibition.

Ans: Competitive: inhibitor resembles substrate, binds active site, reversed by more substrate. Non-competitive: binds elsewhere, changes enzyme shape.

Q8. What are ribozymes?

Ans: Catalytic RNA molecules, an exception to the rule that enzymes are proteins.

Q9. Explain four levels of protein structure (long).

Ans: Primary – sequence; secondary – helix/sheet; tertiary – 3-D fold with disulphide bridges; quaternary – subunits, as in Hb (2α+2β).

Q10. Explain mechanism of enzyme action (long).

Ans: Active site binds substrate (induced fit) → ES complex → transition state with lower activation energy → products released, enzyme unchanged.

Q11. Classify enzymes (long).

Ans: Six classes: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases, with reactions and examples.

Q12. To find: DNA has 20% adenine. Find % of T, G and C.

Ans: Chargaff: A = T → T = 20%. A + T = 40%. Remaining = 60%, G = C → each 30%. Answer: T 20%, G 30%, C 30%.

Q13. To find: length and turns of DNA with 1000 bp.

Ans: Rise = 3.4 Å per bp → length = 1000 × 3.4 = 3400 Å = 0.34 µm. 10 bp per turn → turns = 1000 ÷ 10 = 100.

Q14. To find: Vmax = 80 µmol/min. At what rate is [S] = Km?

Ans: Km is [S] at ½Vmax → V = 80 ÷ 2 = 40 µmol/min.
