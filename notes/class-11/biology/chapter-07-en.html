🐸 Class 11 Biology – Structural Organisation in Animals

1. Animal Tissues

A tissue is a group of similar cells (with intercellular substance) performing a specific function. Four types: epithelial, connective, muscular, neural.

A. Epithelial tissue

Free surface, rests on a basement membrane; little intercellular matrix; avascular.

Simple epithelium (one layer): Squamous – flat, thin; walls of blood vessels, air sacs of lungs; diffusion. Cuboidal – cube-shaped; ducts of glands, tubular parts of nephron; secretion and absorption (brush border in PCT). Columnar – tall; stomach and intestine lining; secretion/absorption. Ciliated – cilia on columnar/cuboidal cells; bronchioles, fallopian tubes; move particles/ova. Glandular: unicellular (goblet cells) or multicellular (salivary gland); exocrine (ducts) or endocrine (ductless). Compound epithelium (many layers): skin, buccal cavity, pharynx, ducts; protection against abrasion.

Cell junctions: Tight (stop leakage), Adhering (cementing), Gap (cytoplasmic communication).

B. Connective tissue

Links and supports; cells + fibres + ground substance. Loose: areolar (below skin; fibroblasts, macrophages, mast cells), adipose (fat storage). Dense regular: tendons (muscle→bone), ligaments (bone→bone); dense irregular: skin. Specialised: cartilage (chondrocytes in lacunae; solid, pliable matrix; ear pinna, nose tip), bone (hard, calcium salts, osteocytes; support, protect), blood (fluid matrix plasma + RBC, WBC, platelets).

D. Neural tissue

Neurons (excitable; cell body, dendrites, axon) and neuroglia (support, make up >50% of volume). Brain, spinal cord, nerves.

2. Frog (Rana tigrina)

Morphology: lives on land and water; poikilotherm (cold-blooded); shows camouflage/mimicry; hibernation (winter) and aestivation (summer) to escape extremes. Skin smooth, slippery, moist, olive green with dark spots. Body: head + trunk, no neck/tail. Eyes bulged with nictitating membrane; tympanum receives sound; nostrils. Forelimbs 4 digits, hindlimbs 5 webbed digits. Sexual dimorphism: male has vocal sacs and copulatory pad on first digit of forelimb; female lacks them.

Digestive system

Short alimentary canal (carnivorous): mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → intestine → rectum → cloaca. Bilobed tongue catches insects; maxillary and vomerine teeth. Liver (bile in gall bladder), pancreas. Food in stomach (HCl, gastric juice) → chyme; in duodenum bile and pancreatic juice act → chyle; absorbed through villi and microvilli of intestine; undigested waste → cloaca.

Respiration

Three ways: cutaneous (in water, through moist skin; dissolved O₂), buccopharyngeal (on land), pulmonary (pair of elongated pink lungs in thorax; air via nostrils → buccal cavity → lungs). During aestivation/hibernation, skin respiration.

Circulation

Closed system; 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle) in pericardium, plus sinus venosus and truncus arteriosus. Sinus venosus receives blood from vena cavae → right atrium; left atrium receives oxygenated blood from lungs; both pour into the ventricle where mixing occurs → truncus → systemic and pulmonary arcs. Portal systems: hepatic portal (gut→liver), renal portal (legs→kidneys). RBCs are nucleated, biconvex. Lymph (lymphatic system) is also present.

Excretion & Osmoregulation

A pair of compact dark red kidneys; ureters; cloaca; urinary bladder. Ureotelic (excretes urea). Male: urinogenital duct; female: separate ureter and oviduct.

Control & Coordination

Nervous: CNS (brain – forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain – in cranium; spinal cord), PNS (10 pairs of cranial nerves), autonomic NS. Endocrine glands: pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, pineal, pancreatic islets, adrenals, gonads. Sense organs: touch, taste, smell, vision, hearing (eye, internal ear, no external ear).

Reproduction

Male: a pair of yellow ovoid testes → vasa efferentia → Bidder’s canal → kidney → urinogenital duct → cloaca. Female: a pair of ovaries → oviducts → cloaca; lays ~2500–3000 ova at a time. External fertilisation in water (monsoon). Development is indirect: zygote → tadpole → metamorphosis → adult.

3. Solved Examples

Ex.1 (Easy) Tissue lining the alveoli? Thin, flat for diffusion → simple squamous epithelium.
Ex.2 (Easy) Tendon vs ligament: tendon links muscle to bone; ligament links bone to bone (both dense regular connective tissue).
Ex.3 (Medium, to find) Chambers of frog heart? 2 atria + 1 ventricle = 3 (plus sinus venosus, truncus).
Ex.4 (Medium) Trace oxygenated blood: lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → ventricle (mixes) → truncus arteriosus → body.
Ex.5 (Hard) A female lays 2500 ova; 60% fertilised, 40% of those reach tadpole stage. Tadpoles = 2500 × 0.6 = 1500; × 0.4 = 600.
Ex.6 (Proof) Frog is poikilothermic: its body temperature varies with surroundings (no thermoregulation, hence hibernation/aestivation). Mixed blood in the 3-chambered heart gives lower metabolic rate than birds/mammals with complete separation.
Ex.7 (Hard) Which tissue lets the heart beat without fatigue? Cardiac muscle – branched, striated, intercalated discs pass impulses rapidly.

4. Short & Long Q&A (tap to open)

Q1. Define tissue.

Group of similar cells with intercellular substance, performing a particular function.

Q2. Name four basic animal tissues.

Epithelial, connective, muscular, neural.

Q3. What is basement membrane?

Thin non-cellular layer below epithelium that anchors it to the connective tissue.

Q4. Differentiate simple and compound epithelium.

Simple: one layer, for absorption/secretion; compound: several layers, for protection.

Q5. Functions of ciliated epithelium.

Moves particles/mucus in bronchioles; moves ova in fallopian tubes.

Q6. Name junctions in epithelium and roles.

Tight (seal), adhering (cement), gap (communication).

Q7. What is adipose tissue?

Loose connective tissue storing fat below skin; insulation.

Q8. Cartilage vs bone.

Cartilage: pliable matrix, chondrocytes. Bone: hard calcified matrix, osteocytes.

Q9. Why is cardiac muscle involuntary?

Not under conscious control; intercalated discs let it contract rhythmically.

Q10. What are neuroglia?

Supporting cells of neural tissue that protect and nourish neurons.

Q11. Why is frog poikilothermic?

Body temperature changes with environment.

Q12. What is aestivation and hibernation?

Dormancy in summer and in winter respectively.

Q13. Explain how frog respires.

Cutaneous (water), buccopharyngeal and pulmonary (land) respiration.

Q14. Describe frog heart.

Three-chambered heart with sinus venosus and truncus arteriosus; mixed blood in ventricle.

Q15. What is hepatic portal system?

Veins carry blood from the intestine to the liver before it enters the heart.

Q16. Why fertilisation external in frog?

Eggs are laid and fertilised in water; no copulation organ.
