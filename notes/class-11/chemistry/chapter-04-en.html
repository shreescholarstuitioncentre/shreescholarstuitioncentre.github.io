Class 11 Chemistry — Chapter 4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

Handwritten-style notes (UP Board / NCERT pattern). Easy, Medium, Hard.

1. Valence Electrons and Kossel–Lewis Approach

Valence electrons are those in the outermost shell; they take part in bonding. Atoms bond to reach a noble-gas (octet, or duplet for H) configuration. Lewis symbol: dots around the symbol (Na·, :Cl̈·).

2. Ionic Bond

Formed by complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻; Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻; Na⁺Cl⁻. Favoured by: low ionization enthalpy of metal, high negative electron gain enthalpy of non-metal, high lattice enthalpy (energy to separate 1 mole of ionic solid into gaseous ions; Born–Haber cycle). Properties: high m.p., hard and brittle, conduct when molten/dissolved, soluble in water.

3. Covalent Bond and Lewis Structures

Formed by mutual sharing of electron pairs: H–H (single), O=O (double), N≡N (triple). Steps for Lewis structure: count total valence e⁻ (adjust for charge) → skeleton (least electronegative central) → single bonds → octets on outer atoms → leftover on centre → multiple bonds if needed. Formal charge = V − L − B/2 (V valence e⁻, L lone-pair e⁻, B bonding e⁻); best structure has lowest formal charges. Octet exceptions: incomplete (BeCl₂, BF₃), expanded (PCl₅, SF₆), odd-electron (NO, NO₂).

4. Bond Parameters

Bond length: distance between nuclei (H–H 74 pm, C–C 154, C=C 134, C≡C 120 pm); decreases with bond order. Bond angle: angle between bonds (CH₄ 109.5°, NH₃ 107°, H₂O 104.5°). Bond enthalpy: energy to break 1 mole of bonds in gas (H–H 435.8; N≡N 946 kJ/mol); increases with bond order. Bond order = number of bonds (N₂ 3, O₂ 2); higher order → shorter, stronger bond.

5. Polar Character and Dipole Moment

Unequal sharing between atoms of different electronegativity gives partial charges δ⁺, δ⁻. μ = q × d (1 D = 3.336×10⁻³⁰ C m). Vector quantity. HF 1.91 D, H₂O 1.85 D, NH₃ 1.47 D; zero for symmetric CO₂, BF₃, CH₄, CCl₄ (cancelling). Percent ionic character = (μ observed / μ ionic) × 100.

6. Covalent Character of Ionic Bond (Fajans' Rules)

Covalent character increases with (i) small cation, (ii) large anion, (iii) high charges on ions, (iv) cation with pseudo-noble-gas (18 e⁻) configuration. Eg. LiCl more covalent than NaCl; AlCl₃ covalent.

7. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

Bond forms by overlap of half-filled atomic orbitals with opposite spins; greater overlap → stronger bond. σ bond: head-on (axial) overlap, strong, free rotation. π bond: sideways overlap of p orbitals, weaker, restricts rotation. Single = 1σ; double = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π.

8. Resonance

When one Lewis structure cannot describe a molecule, the true structure is a resonance hybrid of canonical forms; it is more stable than any of them. O₃: two forms, both bonds 1.5 order (128 pm each). CO₃²⁻: three forms, bond order 4/3. Benzene: two Kekulé forms, C–C order 1.5.

9. VSEPR Theory and Geometry of Molecules

Electron pairs around central atom repel and arrange to minimise repulsion. Order of repulsion: lp–lp > lp–bp > bp–bp, so lone pairs reduce bond angles.

10. Hybridization

Mixing of orbitals of similar energy to form equal new hybrid orbitals. Steric number (BP + LP) decides it.

sp: 50% s, linear, 180° (BeCl₂, C₂H₂). sp²: trigonal planar 120° (BF₃, C₂H₄). sp³: tetrahedral 109.5° (CH₄; NH₃, H₂O also sp³ with lone pairs). dsp²: square planar (Ni(CN)₄²⁻). sp³d: trigonal bipyramidal, 90° axial/120° equatorial (PCl₅). sp³d²: octahedral 90° (SF₆, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ is d²sp³).

11. Molecular Orbital Theory (Qualitative)

Atomic orbitals combine to form molecular orbitals (LCAO): bonding (lower energy, stabilises) and antibonding (higher, marked *). Rules: Aufbau, Pauli, Hund. Order for O₂, F₂: σ1s σ*1s σ2s σ*2s σ2pz π2px=π2py π*2px=π*2py σ*2pz; for up to N₂, π2p comes below σ2pz. Bond order = ½(Nb − Na). Positive → stable. Unpaired electrons → paramagnetic (O₂), all paired → diamagnetic (N₂). Orders: H₂ 1, He₂ 0, N₂ 3, O₂ 2, F₂ 1.

12. Hydrogen Bonding

Attraction between H bonded to highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and a lone pair of another such atom (X–H···Y), 10–40 kJ/mol. Intermolecular (H₂O, HF, NH₃, alcohols): high b.p. and solubility; ice has open cage structure, so is less dense than water. Intramolecular (o-nitrophenol): chelation, lower b.p., steam-volatile versus p-isomer.

13. Solved Numericals

Easy. Find hybridization and shape of NH₃.
N has 5 valence e⁻: 3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair. Steric number = 4 → sp³. Shape: pyramidal (107°, lone pair compresses angle).
Medium. Bond order and magnetism of O₂, O₂⁺, O₂⁻.
O₂ (16 e⁻): Nb = 10, Na = 6, BO = ½(10−6) = 2, two unpaired e⁻ in π* → paramagnetic. O₂⁺ (15 e⁻): Na = 5, BO = 2.5, paramagnetic. O₂⁻ (17 e⁻): Na = 7, BO = 1.5, paramagnetic. Stability: O₂⁺ > O₂ > O₂⁻.
Hard. HCl: μ observed = 1.03 D, bond length 127 pm. Find percent ionic character.
μ ionic = e × d = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ × 127×10⁻¹² = 2.035×10⁻²⁹ C m = 2.035×10⁻²⁹ / 3.336×10⁻³⁰ = 6.10 D. % ionic = 1.03 / 6.10 × 100 = 16.9%.

14. Questions & Answers

Q1. State the octet rule.
Atoms combine to have eight electrons in the valence shell (noble gas configuration).
Q2. Define bond enthalpy.
Energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond in gaseous molecules.
Q3. Why is CO₂ non-polar though C=O bonds are polar?
Linear shape; the two bond dipoles are equal and opposite, net μ = 0.
Q4. Why does H₂O have bond angle 104.5° while CH₄ has 109.5°?
Two lone pairs in H₂O repel bond pairs more (lp–lp > lp–bp > bp–bp), compressing the angle.
Q5. Why is ice lighter than water?
Hydrogen bonding creates open hexagonal cage structure with empty spaces.
Q6. Long: Explain sp³, sp², sp hybridization with examples.
sp³: one s + three p → four orbitals at 109.5° (CH₄). sp²: one s + two p → three at 120° planar (C₂H₄, one unhybridized p forms π). sp: one s + one p → two at 180° (C₂H₂, two p form two π bonds).
Q7. Long: Discuss MO diagram of O₂ and its paramagnetism.
16 electrons fill σ1s² σ*1s² σ2s² σ*2s² σ2pz² π2p² π2p² π*2p¹ π*2p¹. Two unpaired electrons in degenerate π* orbitals make it paramagnetic; BO = 2.
Q8. MCQ: Hybridization of C in C₂H₄? (a) sp (b) sp² (c) sp³ (d) dsp²
(b) sp², planar.
Q9. MCQ: Shape of XeF₄? (a) tetrahedral (b) square planar (c) see-saw (d) pyramidal
(b) square planar (4 BP + 2 LP).
Q10. MCQ: Bond order of N₂? (a) 2 (b) 2.5 (c) 3 (d) 1
(c) 3.
