Chapter 14 : Environmental Chemistry

Class 11 Chemistry (UP Board) • handwritten-style notes

Environmental chemistry = study of sources, reactions, transport, effects and fate of chemical species in the environment.

1. Environmental pollution

Pollutant: a substance that lowers environmental quality. Classification: primary (emitted directly: SO₂, CO, dust) and secondary (formed by reaction: PAN, O₃). Biodegradable (sewage, dung – broken down by bacteria) vs non-biodegradable (DDT, plastic, Hg, Pb). Also by medium – air, water, soil. Effects: disease, ecosystem damage, climate change.

Tropospheric – particulate pollutants

Dust (solid particles), smoke (solid/liquid particles from combustion), mist (liquid droplets), fumes (from condensation of vapours), smog. Fine particles (< 2.5 µm, PM2.5) go deep into the lungs; lead, asthma, cancer, damage to buildings. Control: cyclone separators, scrubbers, electrostatic precipitators.

Stratospheric pollution

Mainly depletion of the ozone layer (see section 5).

3. Global warming and greenhouse effect

Process: short-wave solar radiation warms the Earth, which emits long-wave infrared; greenhouse gases absorb it and re-radiate part back ⇒ Earth stays warm (natural greenhouse effect, essential for life). Human activity raises these gases, causing global warming.

Main GHGs: CO₂ (largest contribution), CH₄ (paddy fields, cattle, waste), N₂O (fertilisers), CFCs, O₃, water vapour. Effects: melting polar ice, sea-level rise, coastal floods, drought/desertification, spread of disease. Control: biofuels/renewables, afforestation, energy efficiency.

Acid rain

Normal rain has pH ≈ 5.6 (due to H₂CO₃); pH < 5.6 ⇒ acid rain. From SO₂/NO₂: 2SO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2H₂SO₄; 4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4HNO₃. Effects: damages crops/forests, acidifies soil, kills aquatic life, corrodes marble (Taj Mahal: CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂). Control: low-sulphur fuel, scrubbers, liming.

4. Smog

Classical (London) smogPhotochemical (Los Angeles) smog
Cool, humid climate; smoke + fog + SO₂Warm, dry, sunny climate
Reducing (due to SO₂), worse at night/morningOxidising (O₃, PAN), worse at noon
Cause: coal and fuel burningCause: vehicle emissions (NOx, hydrocarbons)

Photochemical smog reactions: NO₂ →(hν) NO + O; O + O₂ → O₃; NO + O₃ → NO₂ + O₂; hydrocarbons + O₃/NO → HCHO, acrolein, PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate). Effects: eye irritation, respiratory illness, cracking of rubber, plant damage. Control: catalytic converters, plants that metabolise NOx such as Pinus, Juniperus, Quercus.

5. Depletion of the ozone layer

In the stratosphere (15–35 km): O₂ + hν(UV) → 2O; O + O₂ ⇌ O₃ – these cycles absorb harmful UV-B (280–315 nm). CFCs (e.g. CF₂Cl₂, freon) are inert, so reach the stratosphere where UV frees Cl•: CF₂Cl₂ →(UV) Cl• + •CF₂Cl; chain reaction: Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂; ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂. One Cl• destroys thousands of O₃. Antarctic ozone hole: on polar stratospheric clouds ClONO₂ + HCl → Cl₂ + HNO₃; in spring sunlight Cl₂ → 2Cl•. Effects: skin cancer, cataract, crop and plankton damage. Remedy: Montreal Protocol (1987), HFC/hydrocarbon substitutes.

BOD: amount of O₂ needed by bacteria to oxidise organic matter in a unit volume of water; higher BOD ⇒ more pollution. Drinking water limits (BIS/WHO): fluoride 1 ppm (> 2 ppm mottled teeth), lead 50 ppb, nitrate 50 ppm, sulphate 500 ppm, pH 6.5–8.5.

7. Soil pollution

Insecticides (DDT, aldrin, BHC), herbicides (sodium chlorate NaClO₃, sodium arsenite Na₃AsO₃). DDT is non-biodegradable ⇒ stored in fat, biomagnified along the food chain. Effects: kills soil microbes, contaminates groundwater, thins bird eggshells. Alternatives: neem, pheromone traps, biological control, organic farming.

8. Industrial waste and its management

Biodegradable: sewage, food waste, dung ⇒ biogas/compost. Non-biodegradable: plastics, metals, glass, mercury. Disposal: segregation, sanitary landfill, incineration, recycling, reuse. Principle: 3R – Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.

9. Green chemistry

Using chemistry to prevent pollution with little/no waste and less toxic substances. Principles: prevent pollution at source, high atom economy, less hazardous reagents, renewable feedstock, use catalysts, safer solvents. Examples: liquid CO₂ + detergent replaces tetrachloroethene in dry cleaning; H₂O₂ instead of chlorine for bleaching; ethene + O₂ (Pd catalyst, water) → acetaldehyde (> 90% yield).

10. Solved examples (step by step)

Example 1: [H⁺] of normal rain (pH = 5.6)?

pH = –log[H⁺] ⇒ [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵·⁶ = 10⁰·⁴×10⁻⁶ = 2.51×10⁻⁶ mol/L.

Example 2: H₂SO₄ formed from 6.4 kg SO₂ (acid rain)?

SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄ (1:1 mole ratio). SO₂ = 64, H₂SO₄ = 98. 64 : 98 = 6.4 : x ⇒ x = 9.8 kg.

Example 3: Lead limit in drinking water is 50 ppb; maximum in 10 L?

1 ppb = 1 µg/L ⇒ 50 µg/L × 10 L = 500 µg = 0.5 mg.

Prove: Cl• is a catalyst in ozone depletion

(i) Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂; (ii) ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂. Adding, Cl• and ClO• cancel: O₃ + O → 2O₂. Cl• is regenerated, so it is a catalyst.

11. Questions & Answers

Very short / short

Q1. What is a pollutant?

A substance that harms environmental quality.

Q2. Primary vs secondary pollutants?

Primary are emitted directly (SO₂); secondary form by reaction (PAN, O₃).

Q3. Why is CO poisonous?

It binds Hb forming carboxyhaemoglobin, reducing O₂ transport.

Q4. pH of acid rain?

Below 5.6.

Q5. Effect of acid rain on the Taj Mahal?

CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂ corrodes marble.

Q6. Full form of PAN?

Peroxyacetyl nitrate.

Q7. One difference between London and Los Angeles smog?

London: reducing, cool humid; Los Angeles: oxidising, warm dry.

Q8. Why is the ozone layer important?

It absorbs harmful UV-B radiation.

Q9. Why are CFCs considered inert?

Unreactive in the troposphere, so they reach the stratosphere.

Q10. What does BOD indicate?

Amount of organic pollution in water.

Q11. What is eutrophication?

Nutrient-rich water causes algal growth and loss of dissolved O₂.

Q12. One example of green chemistry?

Liquid CO₂ in dry cleaning.

Long answer

Q1. Explain the greenhouse effect and global warming.

Sunlight warms Earth, which emits IR; greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFC) absorb it and send part back. Human emissions increase these gases, raising average temperature – global warming; effects and control are in the notes.

Q2. Describe formation and control of photochemical smog.

In sunlight NO₂ → NO + O; O + O₂ → O₃; with O₃/NO hydrocarbons form HCHO, acrolein, PAN. Control: catalytic converters, reduced vehicle emissions, plants that metabolise NOx.

Q3. Explain ozone depletion by CFCs.

UV splits CFC → Cl•. Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂; ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂. The chain destroys many O₃ per Cl•. Polar stratospheric clouds explain the Antarctic ozone hole.
