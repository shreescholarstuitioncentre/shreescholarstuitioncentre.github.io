Class 11 Chemistry — Chapter 2: Structure of Atom

Handwritten-style notes (UP Board / NCERT pattern). Easy, Medium, Hard.

1. Discovery of Fundamental Particles

Electron (J.J. Thomson, 1897)

Cathode-ray tube at low pressure and high voltage: rays travel from cathode to anode, deflected by electric/magnetic fields toward the positive plate, so negatively charged. Independent of gas/electrode material, so electrons are universal. e/m = 1.758820 × 10¹¹ C/kg. Millikan's oil-drop: charge e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; mass = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

Proton (Goldstein, 1886 — canal rays)

Anode rays are positive; with hydrogen gas the particle is the proton: charge +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mass 1.6726 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (≈1837 × electron).

Neutron (Chadwick, 1932)

⁹₄Be + ⁴₂He → ¹²₆C + ¹₀n. Neutral, mass 1.6749 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

2. Atomic Number, Mass Number, Isotopes, Isobars

Atomic number Z = number of protons (= electrons in neutral atom). Mass number A = protons + neutrons. Notation: ᴬ_Z X. Neutrons = A − Z.
Isotopes: same Z, different A (¹H, ²H deuterium, ³H tritium; ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl) — same chemical properties, different physical. Isobars: same A, different Z (⁴⁰Ar, ⁴⁰Ca). Isotones: same neutrons (¹³C, ¹⁴N).

3. Atomic Models

Thomson (1898) — Plum pudding

Atom is a positively charged sphere with electrons embedded like seeds in a watermelon; overall neutral. Failed: could not explain Rutherford's scattering experiment.

Rutherford (1911) — α-scattering

α-particles fired at thin gold foil (~100 nm). Observations: most passed straight (atom mostly empty); few deflected by small angles (positive centre); very few (~1 in 20,000) bounced back (dense nucleus). Model: nucleus (radius ~10⁻¹⁵ m, atom ~10⁻¹⁰ m) holds positive charge and mass; electrons revolve around it. Limitations: accelerating electrons should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus (unstable atom); gave no electron arrangement or line spectra.

Bohr (1913)

  1. Electrons move in fixed circular orbits (stationary states) without emitting energy. 2. Angular momentum quantised: mvr = nh/2π. 3. Energy emitted/absorbed only on jumping orbits: ΔE = E₂ − E₁ = hν.
rn = 52.9 n²/Z pm   En = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸ Z²/n² J = −13.6 Z²/n² eV
1/λ = 1.097×10⁷ (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) m⁻¹ (Rydberg)

Spectral series of H: Lyman (n₁=1, UV), Balmer (2, visible), Paschen (3, IR), Brackett (4), Pfund (5). Limitations of Bohr: works only for H-like (one-electron) species; cannot explain fine spectra, Zeeman and Stark effects; ignores dual nature and uncertainty principle; cannot explain molecule formation (bonding).

4. Dual Nature, de Broglie and Heisenberg

Light shows wave and particle behaviour; de Broglie (1924) said matter too: λ = h/mv = h/p (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s). Significant only for microscopic particles (confirmed by Davisson–Germer electron diffraction).
Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle: position and momentum of a microscopic particle cannot be known simultaneously with certainty: Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π. This destroyed the idea of definite orbits → led to orbitals (3-D regions of high electron probability), from Schrödinger's equation (ψ² = probability density).

5. Shells, Subshells, Orbitals and Quantum Numbers

Shell n has n subshells and n² orbitals; max electrons 2n². Subshell capacity: s 2, p 6, d 10, f 14. Orbital angular momentum = √[l(l+1)] h/2π. Radial nodes = n−l−1; angular nodes = l; total = n−1.

6. Shapes of Orbitals

s: spherical, non-directional (1 orbital). p: dumb-bell, px, py, pz along axes (3, one nodal plane). d: five orbitals — dxy, dyz, dzx, dx²−y² (cloverleaf, 4 lobes) and dz² (two lobes + donut ring).

7. Filling of Orbitals and Electronic Configuration

Aufbau: fill lowest energy first, by increasing (n+l); if equal, lower n first. Order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s 5f 6d 7p.
Pauli: no two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers same — an orbital holds max 2 electrons of opposite spin.
Hund: in degenerate orbitals, electrons occupy singly with parallel spin first, then pair.

Examples: N (7) 1s²2s²2p³; Fe (26) [Ar]3d⁶4s²; Cr (24) [Ar]3d⁵4s¹; Cu (29) [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹.

8. Stability of Half-filled and Fully-filled Orbitals

Half-filled (p³, d⁵, f⁷) and fully-filled (p⁶, d¹⁰) subshells are extra stable because of (i) symmetrical distribution and (ii) greater exchange energy (more exchanges possible between parallel-spin electrons). Hence Cr and Cu take one electron from 4s.

9. Solved Numericals

Easy. Find p, n, e in ²³₁₁Na and Na⁺.
p = Z = 11. n = A − Z = 23 − 11 = 12. Na: e = 11. Na⁺ lost one e: e = 10.
Medium. de Broglie wavelength of electron moving at 2.19×10⁶ m/s.
λ = h/mv = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (9.109×10⁻³¹ × 2.19×10⁶) = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / 1.995×10⁻²⁴ = 3.32×10⁻¹⁰ m = 332 pm.
Hard. (a) Energy and radius for n = 2 of He⁺. (b) Wavelength of H Balmer first line (3→2).
(a) Z = 2: E = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸ × 4/4 = −2.18×10⁻¹⁸ J; r = 52.9 × 4/2 = 105.8 pm.
(b) 1/λ = 1.097×10⁷(1/4 − 1/9) = 1.097×10⁷ × 5/36 = 1.524×10⁶ m⁻¹; λ = 6.56×10⁻⁷ m = 656 nm (red).

10. Questions & Answers

Q1. Who discovered the electron and how?
J.J. Thomson (1897), cathode ray discharge tube experiments.
Q2. What are isotopes? Give example.
Same atomic number, different mass number: ¹H, ²H, ³H.
Q3. State Pauli exclusion principle.
No two electrons in an atom can have identical set of four quantum numbers.
Q4. State Hund's rule.
Pairing in degenerate orbitals starts only after each is singly filled with parallel spins.
Q5. Why is Cr 3d⁵4s¹ and not 3d⁴4s²?
Half-filled d⁵ is more stable (symmetry, exchange energy).
Q6. Long: Describe Rutherford's α-scattering experiment and conclusions.
See section 3: gold foil; most pass (empty space), few deflect (positive nucleus), very few rebound (dense, small nucleus). Nucleus ~10⁻¹⁵ m; atom ~10⁻¹⁰ m.
Q7. Long: Explain the four quantum numbers.
See table in section 5, with permitted values and the physical meaning of each.
Q8. MCQ: Maximum electrons in n=3? (a) 8 (b) 18 (c) 32 (d) 9
(b) 18 = 2n².
Q9. MCQ: Number of orbitals in d subshell? (a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 1
(b) 5 = 2l+1 with l=2.
Q10. MCQ: Which is allowed? (a) n=2,l=2 (b) n=3,l=2,m=−2 (c) n=1,l=1 (d) n=2,l=0,m=1
(b); l must be less than n and |m| ≤ l.
