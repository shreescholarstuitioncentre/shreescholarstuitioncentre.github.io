Chapter 11 : s-Block Elements

Class 11 Chemistry (UP Board) • handwritten-style notes

Remember: s-block = elements whose last electron enters the s-orbital. Group 1 → ns¹, Group 2 → ns².

1. Group 1 : Alkali Metals

Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr. Called "alkali" because they react with water to form strong alkalis (hydroxides). Configuration: [noble gas] ns¹.

PropertyTrend Li→CsReason
Atomic / ionic radiusIncreasesNew shell added
Ionization enthalpyDecreasesOuter e⁻ is farther, loosely held
Hydration enthalpyDecreases (Li⁺ highest)Small ion = high charge density
Reducing power (aq.)Li is highestVery large hydration energy
Melting pointDecreasesWeaker metallic bonding

Flame test

Li – crimson red, Na – golden yellow, K – violet, Rb – red-violet, Cs – blue. Reason: excited electrons fall back and emit visible light.

Chemical properties

4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O (oxide); Na → Na₂O₂ (peroxide); K, Rb, Cs → superoxide (KO₂). 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂↑ (more violent down the group). They dissolve in liquid ammonia giving a blue solution (ammoniated electrons).

2. Anomalous behaviour of Lithium

Reason: very small size, high charge density, high polarising power, no d-orbitals.

• Li forms only the normal oxide • LiOH, Li₂CO₃, LiNO₃ decompose on heating (4LiNO₃ → 2Li₂O + 4NO₂ + O₂) • LiCl is deliquescent (LiCl·2H₂O) • LiHCO₃ exists only in solution.

Li–Mg diagonal relationship: similar ionic size and polarising power. Both are hard, form nitrides with N₂ (Li₃N, Mg₃N₂), have sparingly soluble carbonates and fluorides, weak hydroxides, and deliquescent chlorides.

3. Important compounds of Sodium

(a) Sodium carbonate (washing soda) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O – Solvay process

NaCl + NH₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → NaHCO₃↓ + NH₄Cl. Then 2NaHCO₃ →(Δ) Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. Ammonia is recovered: 2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → 2NH₃ + CaCl₂ + 2H₂O. Uses: softening hard water, glass, soap, paper, laundry.

(b) Sodium chloride (NaCl)

From sea water by evaporation (solar salt). Purification: pass HCl gas into saturated solution → pure NaCl crystallises (common-ion effect). Uses: food, making Na₂CO₃, NaOH, Cl₂.

(c) Sodium hydroxide (caustic soda)

Castner–Kellner cell: electrolysis of brine (Hg cathode, graphite anode): Cl₂ at anode, sodium amalgam at cathode → reacts with water to give NaOH + H₂. Uses: soap, paper, rayon, petroleum refining.

(d) Sodium hydrogencarbonate (baking soda) NaHCO₃

Pass CO₂ through Na₂CO₃ solution: Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → 2NaHCO₃. Uses: baking powder, fire extinguishers, antacid.

4. Biological importance of Na⁺ and K⁺

Na⁺ – in extracellular fluid; nerve-impulse transmission, water balance, transport across membranes. K⁺ – in intracellular fluid; activates enzymes, glucose oxidation, Na⁺–K⁺ pump (3Na⁺ out, 2K⁺ in).

5. Group 2 : Alkaline Earth Metals

Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra; configuration ns². Harder than group 1, with higher density, melting point and ionization enthalpy (form M²⁺). Flame: Ca – brick red, Sr – crimson, Ba – apple green (Be, Mg none, as electrons are hard to excite). Reactivity increases Be→Ra. Oxides MO are basic (BeO amphoteric). Hydroxide solubility and basicity increase down the group; sulphate/carbonate solubility decreases.

6. Anomalous behaviour of Beryllium

Very small size, high electronegativity → covalent compounds; maximum covalency 4 (no d-orbitals).

Be–Al diagonal relationship: both have amphoteric oxides/hydroxides, covalent chlorides (Lewis acids), are rendered passive by HNO₃, and carbides give CH₄ on hydrolysis (Be₂C, Al₄C₃).

7. Compounds of Calcium

Quicklime CaO

CaCO₃ →(1070–1270 K) CaO + CO₂ (remove CO₂ continuously so the reaction goes forward). CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (slaked lime, exothermic). Uses: cement, NaOH, sugar purification, flux in furnaces. Lime water turns milky with CO₂: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O.

Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O

2CaSO₄·2H₂O (gypsum) →(393 K) 2CaSO₄·½H₂O + 3H₂O. Sets to gypsum with water. Uses: plaster for fractures, statues, dentistry. Above 473 K gives dead burnt (anhydrous) plaster, which does not set.

Cement

Raw material: limestone + clay + a little gypsum. Heated in a rotary kiln at ~1670 K to give clinker; grinding with 2–3% gypsum gives cement (gypsum slows setting). Constituents: Ca₃SiO₅ (≈50%), Ca₂SiO₄ (≈26%), Ca₃Al₂O₆ (≈11%). Uses: buildings, bridges, concrete.

8. Biological importance of Mg and Ca

Mg²⁺ – central atom of chlorophyll, activates ATP-linked enzymes. Ca²⁺ – bones/teeth (Ca₃(PO₄)₂), blood clotting, muscle contraction, nerve transmission.

9. Solved examples (step by step)

Example 1: How much CaO from 100 g CaCO₃?

Step 1: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. Step 2: molar mass CaCO₃ = 40+12+48 = 100; CaO = 56. Step 3: 100 g CaCO₃ gives 56 g CaO. Answer: 56 g.

Example 2: NaHCO₃ needed to make 5.3 g Na₂CO₃?

2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. Na₂CO₃ = 106 g/mol; 5.3 g = 0.05 mol. Mole ratio 2:1 ⇒ NaHCO₃ = 0.10 mol = 0.10×84 = 8.4 g.

Prove: Li–Mg diagonal relationship (with reasons)

Li⁺ (76 pm) and Mg²⁺ (72 pm) have similar radii; the charge/size ratio (polarising power) is alike. Hence both give nitrides, similar thermal decomposition of carbonates, etc.

10. Questions & Answers

Very short / short

Q1. Why are they called alkali metals?

They form strongly basic hydroxides (MOH) with water.

Q2. Why are alkali metals stored in kerosene?

They react quickly with air's O₂ and moisture.

Q3. Why is Li⁺ hydration enthalpy highest?

Smallest size ⇒ highest charge density ⇒ attracts more water molecules.

Q4. Why is the Na flame yellow?

Excited electrons return and emit 589 nm yellow light.

Q5. Why is IE of group 2 higher than group 1?

Smaller size and larger nuclear charge.

Q6. Write the equation for slaked lime.

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat.

Q7. Formula of plaster of Paris?

CaSO₄·½H₂O.

Q8. Why is gypsum added to cement?

To slow down the setting time.

Q9. Is aqueous Na₂CO₃ acidic or basic?

Basic (pH > 7) due to hydrolysis giving OH⁻.

Q10. Why are Be compounds covalent?

Small size, high polarising power (Fajans' rules).

Long answer

Q1. Describe the Solvay process.

Passing CO₂ into ammoniacal brine precipitates the sparingly soluble NaHCO₃; it is filtered and heated to get Na₂CO₃. CO₂ comes from heating limestone, and NH₃ is recycled from NH₄Cl using Ca(OH)₂. CaCl₂ is the by-product.

Q2. Explain anomalous behaviour of Li and its similarity with Mg.

Small size and high polarising power make Li differ from the rest (only oxide, forms nitride, nitrate gives Li₂O). Like Mg it forms nitride, deliquescent chloride, and sparingly soluble carbonate/fluoride.

Q3. Explain the manufacture of cement.

Limestone and clay are ground and heated in a rotary kiln at ~1670 K to form clinker; the cooled clinker is ground with 2–3% gypsum. With water it hydrolyses and hydrates into a hard mass.

MCQs

Q1. Strongest reducing agent (aqueous)? (a) Li (b) Na (c) K (d) Cs

Correct answer: (a) Li – Li, due to highest hydration energy.

Q2. Flame colour of K? (a) Yellow (b) Violet (c) Red (d) Green

Correct answer: (b) Violet – K gives violet.

Q3. Gypsum heated at 393 K gives? (a) CaO (b) CaSO₄ (c) Plaster of Paris (d) CaCO₃

Correct answer: (c) Plaster of Paris – CaSO₄·½H₂O forms.

Q4. Amphoteric oxide? (a) MgO (b) BeO (c) CaO (d) BaO

Correct answer: (b) BeO – BeO is amphoteric.

Q5. Formula of baking soda? (a) Na₂CO₃ (b) NaOH (c) NaHCO₃ (d) NaCl

Correct answer: (c) NaHCO₃ – Sodium hydrogencarbonate.
