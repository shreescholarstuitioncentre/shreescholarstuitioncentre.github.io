Class 11 Chemistry — Chapter 5: Thermodynamics

Handwritten-style notes (UP Board / NCERT pattern). Easy, Medium, Hard.

1. System, Surroundings and Types of System

System = part of universe under study; surroundings = rest of the universe; the boundary separates them. Open: exchanges matter and energy (open beaker). Closed: exchanges only energy (stoppered flask). Isolated: exchanges neither (thermos flask, ideal).

2. Properties and State Functions

Extensive properties depend on amount (mass, volume, U, H, S, G). Intensive do not (T, p, density, concentration). Ratio of two extensive properties is intensive. State function: value depends only on initial and final state, not path (U, H, S, G, p, V, T). Heat q and work w are path functions.

3. Work, Heat and Internal Energy

Heat (q): energy transfer due to temperature difference. Work (w): pressure–volume work w = −pext ΔV. Sign convention (IUPAC): heat absorbed by system +q, work done on system +w. Free expansion (pext = 0): w = 0. Isothermal reversible expansion of ideal gas: w = −2.303 nRT log(V₂/V₁). Internal energy U = total energy of system (state function).

First law: energy can be neither created nor destroyed. ΔU = q + w. Isothermal ideal gas: ΔU = 0 → q = −w. Adiabatic: ΔU = w. Constant volume: ΔU = qv.

4. Enthalpy, Heat Capacity and Measurement

Enthalpy H = U + pV; at constant pressure ΔH = qp. ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT (Δng = moles gaseous products − reactants; R = 8.314 J/mol K). Exothermic ΔH < 0; endothermic ΔH > 0.
Heat capacity C = q/ΔT (extensive). Specific heat c = q/(m ΔT) (water 4.18 J/g K). Molar: Cp − Cv = R.
Measuring ΔU: bomb calorimeter (constant volume, qv = ΔU). Measuring ΔH: open (constant pressure) calorimeter, qp = ΔH.

5. Hess's Law and Types of Enthalpy

Hess's law: total enthalpy change of a reaction is the same whether in one step or several (H is a state function).

Standard enthalpy of formation ΔfH°: forming 1 mole from elements in reference state (zero for elements). ΔrH° = ΣΔfH°(products) − ΣΔfH°(reactants). Combustion ΔcH°: burning 1 mole completely in O₂ (always negative). Atomization ΔaH°: 1 mole gaseous atoms from the substance (CH₄(g) → C(g) + 4H(g)). Bond dissociation enthalpy: breaking 1 mole of a bond in gas (always positive); ΔrH ≈ Σbonds broken − Σbonds formed. Sublimation: solid → gas (ΔsubH = ΔfusH + ΔvapH). Phase transformation: fusion of ice 6.01 kJ/mol, vaporization of water 40.79 kJ/mol. Ionization: ΔiH (removal of electron), also acid/base ionization enthalpy. Dilution: enthalpy change when more solvent is added to a solution; neutralization of strong acid–strong base ≈ −57.1 kJ/mol.

6. Entropy and Second Law

Entropy S = measure of randomness/disorder (state function, extensive). ΔS = qrev/T (J/K). Increases: solid < liquid < gas, dissolution, heating, Δng > 0. Second law: for a spontaneous process, entropy of universe increases: ΔStotal = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0; heat cannot flow spontaneously from cold to hot.

7. Spontaneity and Gibbs Free Energy

Spontaneous: occurs on its own (ice melting above 0°C, gas expanding). Non-spontaneous: needs external energy (water uphill). G = H − TS; ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. ΔG < 0 spontaneous, = 0 equilibrium, > 0 non-spontaneous. Also ΔG° = −2.303 RT log K = −nFE°.

8. Third Law

Entropy of a perfectly crystalline substance is zero at absolute zero (0 K). It allows calculation of absolute entropies.

9. Solved Numericals

Easy. System absorbs 500 J heat and does 200 J work. Find ΔU.
q = +500 J; work done BY system → w = −200 J. ΔU = q + w = 500 − 200 = +300 J.
Medium (ΔH → ΔU). N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, ΔH = −92.4 kJ at 298 K. Find ΔU.
Δng = 2 − 4 = −2. ΔU = ΔH − ΔngRT = −92.4 − (−2)(8.314×10⁻³)(298) = −92.4 + 4.96 = −87.4 kJ.
Medium (Hess). Given C + O₂ → CO₂ (−393.5) and CO + ½O₂ → CO₂ (−283.0), find ΔH for C + ½O₂ → CO.
Keep first; reverse second: CO₂ → CO + ½O₂ (+283.0). Add: C + O₂ → CO + ½O₂, i.e. C + ½O₂ → CO. ΔH = −393.5 + 283.0 = −110.5 kJ.
Hard. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂: ΔH = +178.3 kJ, ΔS = +160.5 J/K. Find ΔG at 298 K and minimum T for spontaneity.
ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = 178.3 − 298 × 0.1605 = 178.3 − 47.8 = +130.5 kJ (non-spontaneous). For ΔG < 0: T > ΔH/ΔS = 178300/160.5 = ≈ 1111 K.

10. Questions & Answers

Q1. Define state function with example.
Property depending only on initial and final state: U, H, S, G.
Q2. Differentiate extensive and intensive properties.
Extensive depend on quantity (mass, V); intensive do not (T, density).
Q3. State the first law of thermodynamics.
Energy of an isolated system is constant; ΔU = q + w.
Q4. Why ΔH = ΔU for reactions with Δng = 0?
ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT; the second term vanishes.
Q5. What is Hess's law and its use?
Enthalpy change is path independent; used to find ΔH of reactions that cannot be measured directly (C → CO).
Q6. Long: Derive ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT.
H = U + pV, so ΔH = ΔU + pΔV; for ideal gas pΔV = ΔngRT; hence ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT.
Q7. Long: Discuss spontaneity using ΔH, ΔS and ΔG.
ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Use the four-case table: (−,+) always spontaneous; (+,−) never; (−,−) at low T; (+,+) at high T.
Q8. MCQ: Which is NOT a state function? (a) H (b) U (c) q (d) S
(c) q.
Q9. MCQ: Entropy increases in (a) freezing (b) condensation (c) evaporation (d) crystallization
(c) evaporation.
Q10. MCQ: ΔG < 0 means (a) non-spontaneous (b) equilibrium (c) spontaneous (d) impossible
(c) spontaneous.
