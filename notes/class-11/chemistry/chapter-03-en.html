Class 11 Chemistry — Chapter 3: Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Properties

Handwritten-style notes (UP Board / NCERT pattern). Easy, Medium, Hard.

1. Need for Classification

Over 118 elements are known. Classifying them in a table makes study systematic, helps predict properties of unknown elements and relate properties to electronic structure.

2. History of the Periodic Table

Döbereiner (1829) — Triads: middle element's atomic mass is average of the other two (Li, Na, K).
Newlands (1865) — Law of Octaves: every 8th element similar; works only up to calcium.
Lothar Meyer (1869): atomic volume curve.
Mendeleev (1869): properties are periodic function of atomic masses; left gaps and predicted eka-aluminium (Ga), eka-silicon (Ge), eka-boron (Sc); but anomalies (Ar–K, Te–I, Co–Ni).
Moseley (1913): used X-ray spectra and showed atomic number is the true basis.

3. Modern Periodic Law

Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers. Properties repeat because electronic configurations repeat.

4. Modern (Long-Form) Periodic Table

18 groups (vertical), 7 periods (horizontal). Period number = outermost shell n. Lengths: 1st 2, 2nd 8, 3rd 8, 4th 18, 5th 18, 6th 32, 7th (incomplete). Group number: s-block = number of valence e⁻ (1,2); p-block = 10 + valence e⁻ (13–18); d-block = (n−1)d + ns electrons (3–12). f-block: lanthanoids (4f) and actinoids (5f) placed below.

Metals (left, ~78%), non-metals (right top), metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te along the zig-zag line).

5. Periodic Trends

Atomic radius

Covalent radius (half the bond length of identical atoms, Cl: 99 pm), metallic radius (half the distance between nuclei in metal, Na: 186 pm), van der Waals radius (non-bonded). Order: vdW > metallic > covalent. Decreases across period (Zeff up), increases down group (more shells).

Inert gas radius

Noble gas radii are van der Waals radii (no bonds), so they look unusually large at the end of each period.

Ionic radius

Cation < parent atom (fewer electrons, more pull); anion > parent atom (more repulsion). Isoelectronic species: larger Z → smaller radius: O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺.

Ionization enthalpy (ΔiH)

Energy to remove the outermost electron from isolated gaseous atom: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻. IE₁ < IE₂ < IE₃. Increases across period, decreases down group. Factors: atomic size, nuclear charge, shielding, stable (half/full) subshells. Exceptions: Be > B, Mg > Al (s electrons held more tightly than p); N > O, P > S (half-filled p³). Order in period 2: Li < B < Be < C < O < N < F < Ne.

Electron gain enthalpy (ΔegH)

Enthalpy change when gaseous atom gains an electron. More negative = more energy released. Cl (−349 kJ/mol) is most negative; Cl > F > Br > I (F's small size causes inter-electronic repulsion). Noble gases positive; N, Be, Mg low/positive due to stable configurations. Second gain (O⁻ → O²⁻) is positive.

Electronegativity

Tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared pair. Pauling scale: F 4.0, O 3.5, N 3.0, Cl 3.0, C 2.5, H 2.1. Mulliken: χ = (IE + EA)/2. Increases across period, decreases down group.

Valency

Combining capacity: for s and p blocks = number of valence e⁻ (or 8 − valence e⁻). Along a period valency rises 1→4 then falls to 0; in a group it stays the same. Oxidation state is also used for transition elements.

6. IUPAC Nomenclature of Elements (Z > 100)

Digits: 0 = nil, 1 = un, 2 = bi, 3 = tri, 4 = quad, 5 = pent, 6 = hex, 7 = sept, 8 = oct, 9 = enn. Join digits and add -ium; symbol = first letters of the roots. Eg. 101 un-nil-un-ium = Unnilunium (Unu); 104 Unnilquadium (Unq); 107 Unnilseptium (Uns). Later official names: 113 Nihonium (Nh), 114 Flerovium (Fl), 115 Moscovium (Mc), 116 Livermorium (Lv), 117 Tennessine (Ts), 118 Oganesson (Og).

7. Solved Numericals

Easy. Find period, group and block of Z = 17.
Config 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵. Outer n = 3 → period 3. p-block: group = 10 + 7 = 17. Block = p (chlorine, halogen).
Medium. Arrange O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺ in increasing radius.
All have 10 e⁻ (isoelectronic). Nuclear charge: O 8, F 9, Na 11, Mg 12. More protons pull harder, smaller radius. Order: Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < F⁻ < O²⁻.
Hard. Give IUPAC name and symbol of Z = 114, with period, group, block.
Digits 1,1,4 = un, un, quad + ium → Ununquadium (Uuq) (now Flerovium). Config [Rn]5f¹⁴6d¹⁰7s²7p²: n = 7 → period 7; p-block, group = 10 + 4 = 14; p-block.

8. Questions & Answers

Q1. State modern periodic law.
Properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.
Q2. Why did Moseley's work improve Mendeleev's table?
Atomic number, not mass, decides position; it fixed anomalies like Ar–K.
Q3. Define ionization enthalpy.
Energy needed to remove the most loosely held electron from an isolated gaseous atom.
Q4. Why is IE of N higher than O?
N has stable half-filled 2p³; O has paired 2p⁴ with repulsion.
Q5. Why is electron gain enthalpy of Cl more negative than F?
F is very small, so incoming electron faces strong repulsion from dense 2p electrons.
Q6. Long: Explain atomic radius trends in a period and group.
Period: nuclear charge rises with same shell, Zeff increases, radius decreases. Group: new shells added, shielding increases, radius increases. Noble gases show vdW radius jump.
Q7. Long: Discuss factors affecting ionization enthalpy.
Atomic size (larger, lower IE), nuclear charge (higher, higher IE), shielding (more, lower IE), subshell stability (half/full, higher IE), penetration s > p > d > f.
Q8. MCQ: Most electronegative element? (a) Cl (b) O (c) F (d) N
(c) F = 4.0.
Q9. MCQ: Isoelectronic species with smallest radius? (a) O²⁻ (b) F⁻ (c) Na⁺ (d) Mg²⁺
(d) Mg²⁺, highest nuclear charge.
Q10. MCQ: Name for Z = 105? (a) Unnilpentium (b) Unnilquadium (c) Unnilhexium (d) Ununpentium
(a) un-nil-pent-ium.
