Class 11 Chemistry · Appendix: p-Block Elements (Group 13 & 14, Competitive Exam)

Syllabus: Group 13 (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl) · Group 14 (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb) · Trends · Important compounds · Exam tips

1. General facts

p-block = groups 13–18, last electron enters np. Group 13: ns²np¹; Group 14: ns²np². Going down: metallic character rises (B non-metal, Al–Tl metals; C non-metal, Si/Ge metalloids, Sn/Pb metals).
Inert pair effect: heavier elements keep ns² electrons unused. So Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺ stable; Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺ stable. Hence Pb(IV) (PbO₂) is an oxidant, Sn(II) (SnCl₂) is a reductant.

2. Group 13

Anomalous boron: small size, high ionisation enthalpy → forms only covalent compounds, no B³⁺. Ionisation enthalpy (kJ/mol): B 801, Al 577, Ga 579, In 558, Tl 589 (irregular because of poor d/f shielding). Boron compounds are electron-deficient (BF₃ is a Lewis acid; sextet).
Al: amphoteric: 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂; Al₂O₃ amphoteric; AlCl₃ exists as dimer Al₂Cl₆ (completes octet).

3. Group 14

Catenation: C ≫ Si > Ge ≈ Sn (C–C bond 348 kJ/mol is strong). Allotropes of carbon: Diamond sp³, 3D network, hardest, insulator. Graphite sp², layers 0.335 nm apart, conductor, lubricant. Fullerene C₆₀ football shape, 20 hexagons + 12 pentagons, sp².

4. Solved Examples

Easy: Hybridisation of B in BF₃ and [BF₄]⁻?

BF₃: 3 bond pairs, no lone pair → sp², trigonal planar. [BF₄]⁻: 4 bond pairs → sp³, tetrahedral.

Medium: 6.2 g H₃BO₃ reacts with NaOH. Mass of NaOH needed? (H₃BO₃ = 62, NaOH = 40)

H₃BO₃ is monobasic: 1 mol : 1 mol. Moles = 6.2/62 = 0.1. NaOH = 0.1 × 40 = 4 g.

Hard: % of boron in borax (B = 10.8, Na = 23, O = 16, H = 1).

Molar mass: 46 + 43.2 + 112 + 180 = 381.2 g/mol. B in 1 mol = 4 × 10.8 = 43.2. %B = 43.2/381.2 × 100 = 11.33%.

5. Q & A

Q1. Why does boron form only covalent compounds?

High sum of IE₁+IE₂+IE₃ and small size (Fajans' rule) make B³⁺ formation impossible.

Q2. Why is BF₃ a weaker Lewis acid than BCl₃?

Strong pπ–pπ back-donation from F lone pairs into empty B orbital reduces B's electron deficiency.

Q3. Why is graphite a conductor but diamond is not?

Graphite has one delocalised π electron per C; in diamond all four are in σ bonds.

Q4. MCQ: Which is most oxidising? (a) SnCl₂ (b) PbO₂ (c) CO (d) SiO₂

Answer: (b) PbO₂ — Pb⁴⁺ is unstable (inert pair effect), so it accepts electrons.

Q5. Why doesn't Si form Si=Si like C=C?

Large Si atoms overlap poorly sideways, so pπ–pπ bonds are weak; Si prefers sigma bonds / SiO₂ network.

Q6. Why is AlCl₃ a dimer?

Al is electron deficient (6 e⁻); two Cl bridge via lone pairs to give each Al an octet.

Q7. What is the inert pair effect? Give two consequences.

Reluctance of ns² electrons to take part in bonding in heavy elements. (i) Tl⁺ stable, (ii) Pb²⁺ more stable than Pb⁴⁺.

Q8. Why is CO poisonous?

It forms carboxyhaemoglobin, so haemoglobin cannot carry O₂.
