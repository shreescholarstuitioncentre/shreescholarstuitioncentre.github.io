Chapter 1 · Chemical Reactions & Equations

Burning of Magnesium Ribbon (Combination)

Clean the ribbon with sandpaper, hold with tongs and burn: it burns with a dazzling white flame forming white MgO powder collected on a watch glass. Never look directly at the flame. It is a combination and exothermic reaction (also oxidation of Mg).

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Electrolysis of Water (Decomposition – Electrolytic)

Passing current through acidified water splits it. Hydrogen collects at the cathode in double volume; oxygen at the anode. Gas test: H₂ burns with a pop, O₂ rekindles a glowing splinter.

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

Thermal Decomposition of Lead Nitrate

Heating white lead nitrate gives yellow lead oxide, brown fumes of nitrogen dioxide and oxygen. One reactant breaks into several products; heat is absorbed (endothermic).

2Pb(NO₃)₂ →(heat) 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂

Displacement: Iron Nail in Copper Sulphate

Iron is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper: blue solution turns pale green and the nail gets a brown copper coating.

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

Quick notes

• Balancing (hit & trial) obeys the Law of Conservation of Mass; write physical states (s) (l) (g) (aq).

• Types: combination, decomposition (thermal, electrolytic, photolytic – 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂), displacement, double displacement (precipitate: Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl), redox.

• Oxidation = gain of O / loss of H; reduction = loss of O / gain of H. Exothermic releases heat (CH₄ burning); endothermic absorbs.

• Corrosion: rusting of iron, black silver, green copper. Rancidity: oxidation of fats – prevent with nitrogen flushing, air-tight containers, antioxidants.

Chapter 2 · Acids, Bases & Salts

Zinc + Dilute H₂SO₄: Testing Hydrogen

Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen. Gas passed through soap solution forms bubbles; a burning candle brought near burns the gas with a pop sound.

Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂

Acids Conduct Electricity (H⁺ ions)

Acids give H⁺ (H₃O⁺) and bases give OH⁻ in water, so the bulb glows. Glucose and alcohol do not ionise – bulb stays off.

HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻

Preparation of HCl Gas & Litmus Test

HCl gas is dry and does not change dry litmus, but in moisture it forms H⁺ ions and turns wet blue litmus red – acidic character appears only in water.

NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl

pH Scale

pH 0–14 measures H⁺ concentration: <7 acidic, 7 neutral, >7 basic. Universal indicator shows colours. Tooth decay begins below pH 5.5; antacids neutralise stomach acid.

pH < 7 acid · = 7 neutral · > 7 base

Water of Crystallisation (Copper Sulphate)

Blue crystals have 5 molecules of fixed water. Heating removes it giving white powder; adding water restores blue. Gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O, washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O also have it.

CuSO₄·5H₂O ⇌ CuSO₄ + 5H₂O

Quick notes

• Acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation). Acid + metal carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + CO₂ (turns lime water milky).

• Dilution is exothermic: always add acid to water slowly with stirring, never water to acid.

• Chlor-alkali: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂. Bleaching powder CaOCl₂ (Cl₂ + Ca(OH)₂): bleaching, disinfecting water.

• Baking soda NaHCO₃ (antacid, baking; on heating gives Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂). Washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (cleaning, hard water).

• Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O: gypsum heated at 373 K; sets into gypsum with water; used for casts and toys.

Chapter 3 · Metals & Non-metals

Action of Steam on Metals

Metals like Al, Zn, Fe react with steam giving metal oxide and hydrogen. Na, K react violently with cold water; Mg with hot water; Cu, Ag, Au do not react.

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Formation of Ionic Compounds (Electron Dot)

Na gives 1 electron to Cl; Mg gives 2 electrons to two Cl atoms. Both reach a stable octet. Ionic compounds: high melting/boiling points, hard crystals, soluble in water, conduct when molten/aqueous.

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ · Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Electrolytic Refining of Copper

Impure copper is anode, pure copper strip is cathode, acidified CuSO₄ electrolyte. Cu dissolves from anode and deposits as pure Cu on cathode; impurities settle as anode mud.

Anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ · Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

Quick notes

• Physical properties: lustre, malleable, ductile, conductors (Ag, Cu best; Pb, Hg poor), Ga & Cs melt on palm; non-metals dull, brittle (except graphite conducts, diamond hard).

• Metal oxides are basic; Al₂O₃, ZnO are amphoteric (react with both acids and bases). Aqua regia (3 HCl : 1 HNO₃) dissolves gold and platinum.

• Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.

• Metallurgy: enrichment of ore (remove gangue) → low reactivity: roasting (excess air, sulphide ores) / calcination (limited air, carbonates) → reduction with C → high reactivity (Na, Al): electrolysis of molten compound.

• Corrosion prevention: painting, galvanisation (zinc), tinning, anodising, alloying – steel, brass (Cu+Zn), bronze (Cu+Sn), amalgam (Hg).

Chapter 4 · Carbon & its Compounds

Electron Dot Structures: CH₄, H₂O, CO₂

Carbon has 4 valence electrons and cannot form C⁴⁺ or C⁴⁻, so it shares electrons (covalent bonds) – tetravalency. Catenation: C–C chains, branches and rings give millions of compounds. Covalent compounds: low melting points, poor conductors.

CH₄ · H₂O (2 lone pairs) · O=C=O

Structural Isomers (Butane)

Same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms. Butane has 2 isomers, pentane has 3. Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (single bonds), alkenes CₙH₂ₙ (double bond), alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (triple bond).

C₄H₁₀

Esterification (Ethanol + Ethanoic Acid)

Heated in a water bath with conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, ethanol and ethanoic acid give a sweet-smelling ester. Alkaline hydrolysis of ester is saponification (soap making).

CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O

Micelle Formation – Cleansing Action of Soap

Soap molecule: ionic hydrophilic head + long hydrocarbon hydrophobic tail. Tails dissolve in grease, heads face water, forming a micelle that traps dirt which rinses away. In hard water (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) soap forms scum; detergents do not.

Soap: RCOO⁻Na⁺

Quick notes

• Allotropes: diamond (hard, tetrahedral), graphite (layers, conducts), fullerene C₆₀ (football shape).

• Functional groups: –OH alcohol, –CHO aldehyde, >C=O ketone, –COOH carboxylic acid, –Cl/–Br halo. Homologous series: members differ by –CH₂– (14 u).

• IUPAC: prefix (position/branch) + word root (C count: meth, eth, prop, but, pent) + suffix (ane/ene/yne; ol, al, one, oic acid).

• Combustion: saturated burn with clean blue flame; unsaturated give yellow sooty flame. Oxidation by alkaline KMnO₄ / acidified K₂Cr₂O₇: ethanol → ethanoic acid.

• Addition: unsaturated + H₂ (Ni catalyst) → saturated (vegetable ghee). Substitution: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (sunlight).

• Ethanol: 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂; conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K dehydrates to ethene. Ethanoic acid + NaHCO₃ → CO₂ ↑ (brisk effervescence).
