Chapter 9 · Light: Reflection & Refraction

Concave Mirror – Image Formation

Converging mirror. Laws of reflection: ∠i = ∠r and incident ray, normal, reflected ray lie in one plane. Ray parallel to axis passes through F; ray through F becomes parallel. Six cases: object at ∞, beyond C, at C, between C & F, at F, between F & P (virtual, magnified, erect).

1/v + 1/u = 1/f · m = −v/u = h′/h · R = 2f

Convex Mirror – Image Formation

Diverging mirror. Always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image behind the mirror (object at ∞ or anywhere in front) – used as rear-view mirror for a wide field of view. Dotted lines show backward extension of reflected rays.

1/v + 1/u = 1/f · f is +ve

Refraction through a Glass Slab

Light bends towards the normal on entering a denser medium and away on leaving. Emergent ray is parallel to incident ray (e = i) but shifted sideways: lateral displacement. Snell’s law: sin i / sin r = constant = n.

n = sin i / sin r = c / v

Convex Lens – Image Formation

Converging lens. Parallel ray goes through F₂; ray through optical centre goes straight. Six cases like a concave mirror: at ∞, beyond 2F₁, at 2F₁, between F₁ & 2F₁, at F₁, between F₁ & O (virtual, magnified, erect – magnifying glass). Shown: object beyond 2F₁ → real, inverted, diminished image.

1/v − 1/u = 1/f · m = v/u · P = 1/f(m) dioptre

Concave Lens – Image Formation

Diverging lens. Always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image on the same side as the object (object at ∞: image at F₁; object anywhere else: between F₁ and O). Its focal length and power are negative.

1/v − 1/u = 1/f · f, P are negative

Quick notes

• New Cartesian sign convention: distances measured from pole/optical centre; along incident light = +, opposite = −; up = +, down = −.

• Refractive index n = c/v (absolute); n₂₁ = v₁/v₂ (relative). 1 dioptre = power of lens with f = 1 m.

Chapter 10 · The Human Eye & the Colourful World

Structure of the Human Eye

Cornea: does most refraction. Iris: controls pupil size. Pupil: light entry window. Lens: fine-tunes focus. Ciliary muscles: change lens curvature (power of accommodation). Retina: forms real inverted image. Optic nerve: carries signals to the brain. Near point 25 cm, far point infinity.

Normal vision: 25 cm to ∞

Defects of Vision & Correction

Myopia: far objects blurred; eyeball too long / lens too curved → corrected by concave lens. Hypermetropia: near objects blurred; eyeball short / lens too flat → corrected by convex lens. Presbyopia: weak ciliary muscles with age → bifocal lenses (concave top, convex bottom).

Myopia: concave · Hypermetropia: convex

Prism, Dispersion & Recombination

A prism bends light by angle of deviation D. White light splits into VIBGYOR (violet bends most, red least) because each colour has a different speed in glass. Newton’s second inverted prism recombines the spectrum into white light. Rainbow: dispersion + internal reflection + refraction in raindrops (sun behind observer).

V I B G Y O R

Quick notes

• Atmospheric refraction: twinkling of stars, advanced sunrise & delayed sunset (~2 min), apparent raised position of stars.

• Tyndall effect: scattering by small particles. Sky is blue (short wavelengths scatter most); sun looks red at sunrise/sunset (longer path, blue scattered away).

Chapter 11 · Electricity

Ohm’s Law Circuit

At constant temperature, current through a conductor is proportional to potential difference: V = IR. Ammeter is connected in series, voltmeter in parallel across the resistor; rheostat changes current. Current I = Q/t (ampere), potential difference V = W/Q (volt).

V = IR · I = Q/t · V = W/Q

V–I Graph

For an ohmic conductor the graph is a straight line through the origin; its slope gives resistance R.

R = V / I

Series & Parallel Combination

Series: same current, voltages add, total R is larger. Parallel: same voltage, currents add, total R is smaller than the smallest resistor – household appliances are in parallel.

Rs = R₁+R₂+… · 1/Rp = 1/R₁+1/R₂+…

Quick notes

• Resistance R = ρl/A: ↑ with length, ↓ with area; ρ (ohm-metre) depends on material and temperature.

• Joule heating H = I²Rt. Power P = VI = I²R = V²/R (watt). 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 1 unit.

Chapter 12 · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

Solenoid & Electromagnet

Field lines are closed curves, never intersect, go N → S outside and S → N inside. A current-carrying wire (right-hand thumb rule: thumb = current, curled fingers = field) and a circular loop also create fields. Soft iron core inside a solenoid makes a temporary electromagnet.

B ↑ with current & number of turns

Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force, maximum when it is perpendicular to the field. Stretch thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular. Basis of the electric motor.

F = BIl sin θ

Domestic Electric Circuit & Safety

Live (red), neutral (black), earth (green); 220 V in India. Earth wire carries leakage current to the ground and prevents shocks. Fuse / MCB breaks the circuit during overloading and short-circuiting.

Short circuit = very large current

Quick notes

• Fuse wire: low melting point alloy, rated in amperes. Overloading: too many appliances on one socket.
