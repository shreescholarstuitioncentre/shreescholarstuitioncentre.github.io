Chapter 5 · Life Processes

Cross-section of a Leaf – Photosynthesis

Chloroplasts in palisade cells absorb light, split water and reduce CO₂ to carbohydrates. CO₂ enters through stomata; vein supplies water & minerals. Raw materials: CO₂, water, sunlight, chlorophyll.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O →(light, chlorophyll) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Opening & Closing of Stomata

Guard cells swell with water (turgid) and the pore opens for gas exchange and transpiration; when water leaves they shrink and the pore closes.

Turgid → open · Flaccid → closed

Nutrition in Amoeba

Holozoic: Amoeba engulfs food with finger-like pseudopodia (phagocytosis) forming a food vacuole; enzymes digest it, nutrients diffuse, undigested waste is expelled.

Phagocytosis → Food vacuole → Egestion

Human Digestive System

Mouth: amylase starts starch digestion. Stomach: HCl, pepsin (proteins). Liver bile emulsifies fats; pancreas gives trypsin and lipase; small intestine completes digestion and villi absorb nutrients; large intestine absorbs water.

Starch →(amylase) sugar · Proteins →(pepsin, trypsin) amino acids · Fats →(lipase) fatty acids

Breakdown of Glucose (Respiration)

Glycolysis in cytoplasm gives pyruvate. With oxygen it is fully broken down in mitochondria (more ATP). Without oxygen: ethanol + CO₂ in yeast; lactic acid in muscles (cramps).

ATP = energy currency of the cell

Human Heart & Double Circulation

Four chambers prevent mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood. Blood passes twice through the heart per cycle: body → right side → lungs → left side → body. Arteries: thick walls, carry blood away; veins: valves, bring blood back; capillaries: one cell thick for exchange.

Right heart → lungs → Left heart → body

Structure of a Nephron (Urine Formation)

Glomerular filtration in Bowman’s capsule; tubule reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, salts and water selectively; remaining urine passes through collecting duct → ureter → bladder → urethra.

Filtration → Reabsorption → Urine

Quick notes

• Nutrition: autotrophic (photosynthesis); heterotrophic – saprophytic (fungi), parasitic (Cuscuta, tapeworm), holozoic (humans).

• Breathing: ribs lift and diaphragm flattens → air enters; gas exchange in alveoli; haemoglobin carries O₂. Aerobic gives 38 ATP vs anaerobic 2 ATP.

• Plants: xylem carries water & minerals (transpirational pull, one-way up); phloem carries sucrose (translocation, both ways, needs ATP). Lymph returns fluid and carries fats.

• Excretion in plants: O₂/CO₂ by stomata, waste stored in old xylem, resins/gums, shed leaves/bark. Dialysis (artificial kidney) filters blood in kidney failure.

Chapter 6 · Control & Coordination

Structure of a Neuron

Dendrite receives information → cell body → axon carries electrical impulse → nerve ending releases chemicals across the synapse to the next neuron or muscle (neuromuscular junction).

Dendrite → Cell body → Axon → Synapse

Reflex Arc

A reflex is a sudden, involuntary response (e.g. touching a hot object). The impulse takes a short path through the spinal cord without waiting for the brain, so the response is very fast.

Receptor → Sensory → Spinal cord → Motor → Effector

Human Brain

Forebrain: thinking, memory, voluntary actions, hunger centre. Midbrain & hindbrain: cerebellum (balance), pons and medulla (involuntary actions). Protected by cranium and cerebrospinal fluid.

Cranium + CSF = protection

Endocrine Glands in Humans

Hormones are chemical messengers. Adrenaline speeds heart rate (fight or flight); insulin lowers blood sugar (deficiency → diabetes); thyroxine regulates metabolism (iodine deficiency → goitre). Feedback: high blood sugar → insulin ↑ → sugar falls → insulin ↓.

Feedback mechanism

Quick notes

• Plants: nastic (Mimosa touch – not growth), tropic (growth): phototropism (shoot to light), geotropism (root down), hydrotropism (root to water), chemotropism (pollen tube to ovule).

• Phytohormones: auxin (cell elongation, bends shoot), gibberellin (stem growth), cytokinin (cell division, fruits & seeds), abscisic acid (inhibits growth, wilting, leaf fall).

Chapter 7 · How do Organisms Reproduce?

Binary Fission in Amoeba

Nucleus divides first, then the cell constricts and splits into two identical daughter cells. Leishmania also divides binarily; Plasmodium by multiple fission.

1 → 2 daughter cells

Flower: Longitudinal Section & Pollen Tube

Stamen (anther + filament) is male; carpel (stigma, style, ovary) is female. Pollen lands on stigma (pollination), a pollen tube grows through the style to the ovule; male gamete fuses with egg (fertilisation); ovule → seed, ovary → fruit.

Pollination → Fertilisation → Seed

Human Male Reproductive System

Testes (in scrotum, cooler) make sperm and testosterone; sperm pass through vas deferens, mix with secretions of seminal vesicle and prostate and leave through the urethra.

Testis → Vas deferens → Urethra

Human Female Reproductive System

Ovaries release one egg monthly into the fallopian tube where fertilisation happens; the zygote implants in the uterus lining, the placenta nourishes the foetus. If unfertilised, the lining breaks – menstruation (every ~28 days).

Ovary → Tube → Uterus

Quick notes

• Variation: DNA copying is not perfect – variations help species survive environmental change.

• Asexual: fragmentation (Spirogyra), regeneration (Planaria), budding (Hydra, yeast), spore formation (Rhizopus), vegetative propagation (Bryophyllum, layering, grafting, cutting, tissue culture).

• Puberty: facial hair & voice change in boys, breast development & menstruation in girls. Self vs cross pollination; unisexual (papaya) vs bisexual (hibiscus) flowers; triple fusion forms endosperm.

• Health: STDs – gonorrhoea, syphilis (bacterial); warts, HIV-AIDS (viral). Contraception: condoms (barrier), pills (chemical), Copper-T (IUCD), vasectomy/tubectomy (surgical).

Chapter 8 · Heredity

Mendel’s Monohybrid Cross (Punnett Square)

Mendel used pea plants (short life cycle, clear contrasting traits). Crossing tall (TT) and dwarf (tt) gave all tall F₁ (Tt); self-pollinating F₁ gave tall and dwarf in 3 : 1 – the dwarf trait reappeared, so traits are inherited independently.

3 : 1 phenotype · 1 : 2 : 1 genotype

Dihybrid Cross (Two Traits)

Round-yellow (RRYY) × wrinkled-green (rryy) gives all round-yellow F₁; F₂ shows four types in 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, including new combinations – proof that traits are inherited independently.

9 : 3 : 3 : 1

Sex Determination in Humans

Humans have 23 chromosome pairs; the last pair is XX in females and XY in males. Every egg carries X; sperm carries X or Y, so the father’s sperm decides the baby’s sex.

XX = girl · XY = boy

Quick notes

• Genes on DNA code for proteins/enzymes which express traits (e.g. plant height hormone). Dominant trait shows in F₁; recessive reappears in F₂. Variations accumulate over generations and give survival advantages.

• Some animals (turtle) use environmental temperature for sex determination, others use genes (humans).

Chapter 13 · Our Environment

Trophic Levels & 10% Energy Law

Producers capture solar energy; only ~10% passes to each next level (rest lost as heat, respiration). So food chains are short (3–4 levels) and energy flow is unidirectional. Decomposers recycle nutrients.

Grass → Deer → Lion

Biological Magnification

Non-biodegradable chemicals such as DDT enter food chains and accumulate more at each higher trophic level; humans at the top carry the maximum amount (found in wheat, vegetables, milk).

Concentration ↑ up the chain

Quick notes

• Ecosystem: biotic (producers, consumers: herbivore/carnivore/omnivore, decomposers) + abiotic (light, soil, water). Natural: forest, pond; artificial: crop field, aquarium.

• Ozone (O₃) shields from UV; CFCs from refrigerators/aerosols deplete it – UNEP 1987 agreement cut CFC use.

• Garbage: biodegradable (compost) vs non-biodegradable (plastic) – use landfills, recycling, composting, sewage treatment; reduce, reuse, recycle.
