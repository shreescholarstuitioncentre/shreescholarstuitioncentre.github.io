Part 1 · Grammar: Tenses, Articles, Reordering of Sentences

1. Tenses (Kaal)

Tense shows the time of an action — Present, Past or Future. Each has 4 forms: Simple, Continuous, Perfect and Perfect Continuous = 12 tenses.

All 12 tenses

TenseStructureExample
Simple PresentS + V1 (s/es)He plays cricket daily.
Present ContinuousS + is/am/are + V1+ingHe is playing now.
Present PerfectS + has/have + V3He has played.
Present Perfect Cont.S + has/have been + V1+ingHe has been playing since 5.
Simple PastS + V2He played yesterday.
Past ContinuousS + was/were + V1+ingHe was playing.
Past PerfectS + had + V3He had played before I came.
Past Perfect Cont.S + had been + V1+ingHe had been playing for an hour.
Simple FutureS + will/shall + V1He will play tomorrow.
Future ContinuousS + will be + V1+ingHe will be playing.
Future PerfectS + will have + V3He will have played by then.
Future Perfect Cont.S + will have been + V1+ingHe will have been playing for 2 hours.

Signal words

TenseSignal words
Simple Presentalways, usually, daily, every day, never
Present Continuousnow, at present, at this moment, look!, listen!
Present Perfectjust, already, yet, ever, never, recently, so far
Perfect Continuoussince (point of time), for (period of time)
Simple Pastyesterday, last week, ago, in 2010
Simple Futuretomorrow, next week, soon, tonight

Remember: after he/she/it (singular) add s/es to the verb in Simple Present: He goes, She writes. With I/we/you/they use the plain verb. since = from a point of time; for = for a period.

Solved Examples

Change into Present Continuous:
Ex 1. She writes a letter.
✔ She is writing a letter.
Why: is/am/are + V1+ing.
Fill in the blank:
Ex 2. I ___ (live) in Ghaziabad since 2015.
✔ have been living
Why: ‘since’ (point of time) → Present Perfect Continuous: has/have been + V1+ing.
Identify the tense:
Ex 3. They had finished the work before the bell rang.
✔ Past Perfect
Why: had + V3 shows the action that happened first in the past.
Correct the error:
Ex 4. He go to school daily.
✔ He goes to school daily.
Why: Singular subject (he/she/it) takes V1 + s/es in Simple Present.
Make negative and interrogative:
Ex 5. She sings well.
✔ Negative: She does not sing well. Interrogative: Does she sing well?
Why: Simple Present uses does/do + V1 for negatives and questions.
Fill in the blank:
Ex 6. By next June, I ___ (complete) my course.
✔ will have completed
Why: ‘by + future time’ → Future Perfect: will have + V3.
Choose the correct form:
Ex 7. Look! The baby ___ (cry).
✔ is crying
Why: ‘Look!’ shows an action happening right now → Present Continuous.

2. Articles (a, an, the)

Articles are the words a, an, the. A / An are indefinite (any one, singular, countable noun). The is definite (a particular one).

Rules at a glance

ArticleUse / उपयोग
abefore a consonant SOUND: a book, a university, a European
anbefore a vowel SOUND: an apple, an hour, an honest man, an MLA
theparticular noun: The book on the table is mine.
theunique things: the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky
therivers, seas, mountains (ranges), holy books: the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Gita
thesuperlatives & ordinals: the best, the first
themusical instruments: She plays the guitar.
no articleproper nouns, languages, meals, games: Ram, Hindi, lunch, cricket
no articleplural/uncountable in general: Milk is white. Dogs bark.

Trick: sound matters, not spelling! an hour (silent h), a university (starts with 'yoo' sound).

Solved Examples

Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 1. ___ umbrella is useful in rain.
✔ An
Why: ‘umbrella’ begins with a vowel sound.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 2. He waited for ___ hour.
✔ an
Why: ‘hour’ has a silent h, so it begins with a vowel sound.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 3. She is ___ European.
✔ a
Why: ‘European’ begins with a ‘yu’ (consonant) sound.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 4. ___ sun rises in the east.
✔ The
Why: Unique things (sun, moon, sky) take ‘the’.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 5. ___ rich should help ___ poor.
✔ The … the
Why: the + adjective = the whole group of people.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 6. He is ___ M.A. in English.
✔ an
Why: M is pronounced ‘em’ — a vowel sound.
Fill in a / an / the:
Ex 7. ___ Ganga is a holy river.
✔ The
Why: Names of rivers take ‘the’.

3. Reordering of Sentences

In reordering you get jumbled words or parts and must arrange them into a meaningful sentence. English order: Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time.

Steps: (1) Find the subject (who?). (2) Find the verb. (3) Add object/complement. (4) Add place, then time. (5) Read it aloud — does it make sense? Questions: Wh-word + helping verb + subject + main verb.

Example: school / goes / to / daily / Ravi → Ravi goes to school daily.   Question: you / where / live / do → Where do you live?

Solved Examples

Arrange into a meaningful sentence:
Ex 1. daily / early / gets up / Rita
✔ Rita gets up early daily.
Why: Subject + Verb + manner + time.
Arrange into a meaningful sentence:
Ex 2. my / doctor / is / a / father
✔ My father is a doctor.
Why: Subject (My father) + verb (is) + complement (a doctor).
Arrange into a meaningful sentence:
Ex 3. done / have / the / you / homework
✔ Have you done the homework?
Why: In a yes/no question the helping verb comes first.
Arrange into a meaningful sentence:
Ex 4. where / you / do / live
✔ Where do you live?
Why: Wh-word + helping verb + subject + main verb.
Arrange the parts (P, Q, R, S):
Ex 5. P: to the market | Q: went | R: my mother | S: yesterday
✔ R Q P S → My mother went to the market yesterday.
Why: Subject (R) → verb (Q) → place (P) → time (S).
Arrange the parts (P, Q, R, S):
Ex 6. P: there was | Q: Once upon a time | R: a poor farmer | S: in a village
✔ Q P R S → Once upon a time there was a poor farmer in a village.
Why: Time opener first, then ‘there was’, then the noun, then the place.

Part 2 · Grammar: Voice, Narration, Punctuation

1. Voice (Active & Passive)

Voice shows whether the subject does the action (Active) or receives it (Passive).
Active: Ram writes a letter.   Passive: A letter is written by Ram.

Steps: (1) Find Subject, Verb, Object. (2) Object becomes the new subject. (3) Use the correct form of 'be' + V3. (4) Add 'by' + old subject (I→me, he→him, we→us, they→them).

Tense-wise passive formula

TensePassive form
Simple Presentis/am/are + V3
Present Continuousis/am/are being + V3
Present Perfecthas/have been + V3
Simple Pastwas/were + V3
Past Continuouswas/were being + V3
Past Perfecthad been + V3
Simple Futurewill/shall be + V3
Future Perfectwill/shall have been + V3
Modals (can, may, must)modal + be + V3

Imperative: Open the door → Let the door be opened.   Question: Who wrote this? → By whom was this written?
Note: Intransitive verbs (no object) cannot become passive: He sleeps. The 'by…' part can be dropped if unknown: English is spoken here.

Solved Examples

Change into Passive voice:
Ex 1. Ravi writes a letter.
✔ A letter is written by Ravi.
Why: Simple Present → is/am/are + V3.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 2. They are building a house.
✔ A house is being built by them.
Why: Present Continuous → is/am/are + being + V3.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 3. She has completed the work.
✔ The work has been completed by her.
Why: Present Perfect → has/have + been + V3.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 4. The teacher will teach us grammar.
✔ We will be taught grammar by the teacher.
Why: Simple Future → will be + V3.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 5. He was reading a novel.
✔ A novel was being read by him.
Why: Past Continuous → was/were + being + V3.
Change into Active voice:
Ex 6. The window was broken by Mohan.
✔ Mohan broke the window.
Why: Passive Simple Past (was + V3) → Active V2.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 7. Open the door.
✔ Let the door be opened.
Why: Imperative → Let + object + be + V3.
Change into Passive voice:
Ex 8. Who wrote this book?
✔ By whom was this book written?
Why: Who → By whom; then was/were + subject + V3.

2. Narration (Direct & Indirect Speech)

Direct speech gives the exact words in inverted commas. Indirect speech reports the meaning.
Direct: He said, "I am happy."   Indirect: He said that he was happy.

3 rules: (1) Remove comma and quotes; add that. (2) Change tense if the reporting verb is past. (3) Change pronouns and time/place words.

Tense change

am/is/arewas/were
Simple Present (plays)Simple Past (played)
Simple Past (played)Past Perfect (had played)
will/shallwould
can/maycould/might
has/have + V3had + V3
is/am/are + ingwas/were + ing

Time & place words

DirectIndirect
nowthen
todaythat day
tomorrowthe next day
yesterdaythe previous day
herethere
thisthat
agobefore

Pronouns: 1st person → subject of reporting verb; 2nd → object; 3rd → no change.
Questions: said to → asked; Wh-word stays; yes/no → if/whether; no '?'.
Commands: said to → ordered/requested/advised + to-infinitive.
Exclamation: exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise that…
No tense change if the reporting verb is present/future or the speech is a universal truth.

Solved Examples

Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 1. He said, “I am happy.”
✔ He said that he was happy.
Why: Reporting verb is past → am becomes was, I becomes he.
Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 2. She said to me, “Where do you live?”
✔ She asked me where I lived.
Why: Wh-question → asked + same wh-word; statement order; no question mark.
Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 3. He said to me, “Will you help me?”
✔ He asked me whether I would help him.
Why: Yes/no question → asked + if/whether; will → would.
Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 4. The teacher said to the boys, “Do your homework.”
✔ The teacher ordered the boys to do their homework.
Why: Order/request → ordered/requested + to + V1.
Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 5. Mother said, “The sun rises in the east.”
✔ Mother said that the sun rises in the east.
Why: A universal truth keeps its tense — no change.
Change into Indirect speech:
Ex 6. He said, “Alas! I have lost my pen.”
✔ He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his pen.
Why: Exclamation → exclaimed with joy/sorrow; have lost → had lost.
Change into Direct speech:
Ex 7. He told me that he would come the next day.
✔ He said to me, “I will come tomorrow.”
Why: would → will, the next day → tomorrow; add inverted commas.

3. Punctuation

Punctuation marks work like traffic signals for the reader. Capital letters are used for: the first word of a sentence, the pronoun I, proper nouns, days, months and the first word of a quotation.

MarkName / UseExample
.Full stop — end of statementRam is a boy.
,Comma — list, short pause, addressRam, come here.
?Question mark — after questionWho are you?
!Exclamation — strong feelingWow! Great!
;Semicolon — joins related clausesHe is poor; he is honest.
:Colon — before list/explanationBuy: pen, book.
'Apostrophe — possession, contractionRahul's, don't
" "Inverted commas — exact wordsHe said, "Go."
-Hyphen — joins wordswell-known

Solved Examples

Punctuate:
Ex 1. what is your name
✔ What is your name?
Why: Capital letter at the start and a question mark at the end.
Punctuate:
Ex 2. rahul said i am going to delhi
✔ Rahul said, “I am going to Delhi.”
Why: Capitals for names; comma + inverted commas for exact words.
Punctuate:
Ex 3. wow what a beautiful scene
✔ Wow! What a beautiful scene!
Why: Exclamation mark shows strong feeling.
Punctuate:
Ex 4. she bought apples oranges bananas and grapes
✔ She bought apples, oranges, bananas and grapes.
Why: Commas separate items in a list; ‘and’ joins the last two.
Punctuate:
Ex 5. this is rahuls book
✔ This is Rahul’s book.
Why: Apostrophe + s shows possession.
Punctuate:
Ex 6. he is poor he is honest
✔ He is poor; he is honest.
Why: Semicolon joins two closely related clauses.
Use capital letters:
Ex 7. my friend lives in mumbai. he visits us on sunday.
✔ My friend lives in Mumbai. He visits us on Sunday.
Why: Capitals for sentence start, names of places and days.

Part 3 · Composition: Letter, Application, Paragraph, Report & Article

1. Application (to Principal)

Format: To, The Principal, School name, Place → Date → Subject → Sir/Madam → Body (With due respect… + reason + request) → Yours obediently, Name, Class, Roll No.

Model — Leave for fever:
To
The Principal
Govt. Inter College, Ghaziabad
Date: 5 November 2026
Subject: Application for two days' leave.
Sir,
With due respect, I beg to say that I am suffering from fever and cannot attend school for two days. Kindly grant me leave from 5 to 6 November.
Yours obediently,
Aman Kumar
Class IX, Roll No. 12

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Application for sick leave
To, The Principal, XYZ Inter College, Ghaziabad. Date: 5 November 2026 Subject: Application for sick leave. Respected Sir, With due respect, I beg to say that I am suffering from high fever since yesterday. The doctor has advised me complete rest for three days. So, I cannot attend school from 5 to 7 November 2026. I shall be highly obliged if you kindly grant me leave for these three days. Yours obediently, Aman Kumar Class IX, Roll No. 14
✎ Tip: Respected Sir → body → Yours obediently. Always mention the number of days.
Ex 2: Application for fee concession
To, The Principal, XYZ Inter College, Ghaziabad. Date: 5 November 2026 Subject: Application for fee concession. Respected Sir, I beg to state that I am a student of Class IX-A in your school. My father is a small shopkeeper and his income is very low, so he cannot pay my fees easily. I have secured 85% marks in the last examination and I am regular and disciplined. I, therefore, request you to kindly grant me a fee concession. I shall be thankful to you. Yours obediently, Rohit Sharma Class IX-A, Roll No. 21
✎ Tip: Give your reason + your good record, then make the request politely.

2. Formal Letter (to Editor / Officer)

Format: Sender's address → Date → Receiver → Subject → Sir/Madam → Problem → effect → request → Yours faithfully, Name. Use 'Through the columns of your esteemed daily…' for an Editor.

Model — Complaint about garbage:
15, Nehru Nagar, Ghaziabad
5 November 2026
The Editor, The Daily News, Lucknow
Subject: Garbage problem in our colony.
Sir,
Through the columns of your esteemed daily, I want to draw the attention of the authorities to the heaps of garbage in our colony. They spread diseases and bad smell. I request the municipal officer to clean the area at once.
Yours faithfully,
Rohit Verma

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Letter to the Editor — air pollution
12, Nehru Nagar, Ghaziabad. 5 November 2026 The Editor, The Daily Times, New Delhi. Subject: Increasing air pollution in our city. Sir, Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities towards the growing air pollution in our city. Smoke from vehicles, factories and burning of garbage has made breathing difficult, especially for children and the old. Many people are suffering from cough and eye problems. I request the authorities to ban the burning of waste, plant more trees and check polluting vehicles. I hope you will publish my letter soon. Yours faithfully, Sanjay Verma
✎ Tip: Sender’s address and date at top-left; ‘Sir’ as salutation; end with Yours faithfully.
Ex 2: Complaint to the Health Officer — garbage
12, Gandhi Nagar, Ghaziabad. 5 November 2026 The Health Officer, Nagar Nigam, Ghaziabad. Subject: Complaint about heaps of garbage in our locality. Sir, I wish to bring to your notice that garbage has not been lifted from our colony for the last ten days. Heaps of waste are giving out a bad smell, and flies and mosquitoes are spreading diseases. I request you to kindly send sweepers at once and arrange regular cleaning. Thanking you, Yours faithfully, Mohit Gupta
✎ Tip: State the problem, how long it has lasted, its effect, and your request — in that order.

3. Informal (Personal) Letter

Format: Sender's address → Date → Dear + name → Opening (I hope you are well) → Main news → Closing → Yours affectionately/lovingly, Name. No receiver's address; tone is friendly.

Model — Invite a friend for Diwali:
12, Rose Lane, Meerut
1 November 2026
Dear Sanjay,
I hope you are well. Diwali is coming and we will celebrate it with joy. Please come to my house for the festival. We shall light diyas, eat sweets and enjoy fireworks. My parents also want to meet you.
Yours lovingly,
Vikas

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Letter to a friend — congratulating on success
15, Shastri Nagar, Ghaziabad. 5 November 2026 Dear Sanjay, I hope this finds you in the best of health. I was delighted to know that you have passed the half-yearly examination with first division. I congratulate you from the bottom of my heart. Your hard work has paid off. Keep it up and make us proud. Please convey my regards to your parents. Yours lovingly, Rohit
✎ Tip: Dear + first name; friendly tone; close with Yours lovingly / Your friend.
Ex 2: Letter to a younger brother — advice on studies
15, Shastri Nagar, Ghaziabad. 5 November 2026 Dear Mohan, I was sad to learn from Mother’s letter that you are not studying regularly. Studies need daily effort. Make a timetable and study for at least three hours every day. Avoid too much TV and mobile, and take care of your health. If you ever find any subject difficult, write to me. I will help you gladly. Write to me soon. Your loving brother, Anil
✎ Tip: Give simple, kind advice — no harsh words in a personal letter.

4. Paragraph Writing

Tips: 80–100 words • Title • Introduce → describe (2–3 points) → conclude • Short, simple sentences • Present tense for general topics.

Model — My School:
My school is Government Inter College. It is a large building with a big playground and a library. There are about 1,000 students and 40 teachers. Our teachers are kind and hardworking. We celebrate all festivals and national days here. I am proud of my school.

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Paragraph: Importance of Discipline
Discipline means following rules and living in an orderly way. It is very important in every walk of life. A disciplined student reaches school on time, obeys teachers and completes his work regularly. Soldiers win wars because of discipline. Great people like Mahatma Gandhi followed strict discipline in their lives. Without discipline there would be chaos in homes, schools and society. Discipline teaches us punctuality, self-control and respect for others. So, we should make discipline a habit from childhood.
✎ Tip: Start with a definition, give 2–3 points, end with a message. About 80–100 words.
Ex 2: Paragraph: Air Pollution
Air pollution means mixing of harmful gases and dust in the air. Smoke from vehicles and factories, burning of waste and cutting of trees are its main causes. It causes cough, asthma and eye problems, and it also harms plants and animals. Delhi-NCR faces heavy smog every winter. To reduce pollution we should use public transport, plant more trees and avoid burning garbage. If everyone does a little, our air will become clean and healthy again.
✎ Tip: Meaning → causes → effects → solution is a good order.
Ex 3: Paragraph: Diwali
Diwali is the festival of lights. It is celebrated every year in October or November to mark the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya. People clean and decorate their houses, light diyas and worship Goddess Lakshmi. Children enjoy sweets and fireworks, and families exchange gifts. However, we should celebrate a green Diwali by avoiding loud crackers, because they cause air and noise pollution. Diwali teaches us that light always defeats darkness.
✎ Tip: Add one social message (green Diwali) to make the paragraph stronger.

5. Report Writing

Format: Heading → By (reporter) + place + date → What, when, where → Events in order → Outcome. Use past tense, third person, often passive voice.

Model — Annual Sports Day:
Annual Sports Day Held
By Neha Singh, Ghaziabad, 10 Nov 2026
The annual sports day of Govt. Inter College was celebrated on 9 November. The chief guest was the District Magistrate. Races, long jump and relay were held. Students took part with great enthusiasm. Prizes were given by the chief guest, and the day ended with the national anthem.

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Report: Annual Sports Day
Annual Sports Day Celebrated at XYZ Inter College By: Staff Reporter | Ghaziabad, 5 November 2026 The Annual Sports Day of XYZ Inter College, Ghaziabad was celebrated with great enthusiasm on 4 November 2026 at the school ground. The Chief Guest, District Sports Officer Mr. R. K. Sharma, inaugurated the function by hoisting the flag at 9 a.m. Students took part in races, long jump, kabaddi and relay. House Red won the overall trophy. Prizes were given away by the Chief Guest, who praised the students and encouraged them to take up sports regularly. The function ended with a vote of thanks by the Principal at 1 p.m.
✎ Tip: Heading + by-line + place/date; write in the past tense and passive voice; answer who, what, when, where.
Ex 2: Report: Road accident
Road Accident near Ghaziabad on NH-9 By: Staff Reporter | Ghaziabad, 5 November 2026 A bus collided with a truck on NH-9 near Ghaziabad at about 7 a.m. on 4 November 2026 due to dense fog. Ten persons were injured and were rushed to the District Hospital by passers-by. The police reached the spot soon, cleared the traffic and started an inquiry. The doctors said that all the injured were out of danger. The police have advised drivers to drive slowly in fog.
✎ Tip: Mention cause, injured persons, action taken and the final advice.

6. Article Writing

Format: Title → By (name) → Introduction → Body (2 paragraphs: causes/effects/solutions) → Conclusion with advice. Present tense, 100–120 words.

Model — Save Water:
Water is life. But we waste it every day. Rivers are getting polluted and ground water is going down. We should close taps after use, repair leaking pipes and harvest rain water. If we do not save water today, our children will suffer tomorrow. So, let us save every drop.

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Article: The Importance of Reading Books
The Importance of Reading Books By: Neha Singh Books are our best friends. They give us knowledge, joy and good values without asking anything in return. A person who reads regularly learns new words, thinks clearly and speaks confidently. Books take us to different places and times through stories and history, and they teach us how great people overcame difficulties. Today many students spend hours on mobile phones and games. This wastes time and weakens the habit of reading. Parents and teachers should encourage children to visit libraries and read daily, even for half an hour. In short, a good book is a lifelong companion. Let us all make reading a part of our daily life.
✎ Tip: Title + by-line; introduction → body (problem and solution) → conclusion starting with ‘In short’ / ‘Therefore’.
Ex 2: Article: Save Water, Save Life
Save Water, Save Life By: Amit Kumar Water is the most precious gift of nature. Every living being needs it, yet we waste it carelessly. Leaking taps, long showers and washing cars with pipes waste thousands of litres every day. At the same time, groundwater is going down in many cities. We can save water by closing taps after use, repairing leaks, harvesting rainwater and reusing water for plants. Schools and media should run campaigns to spread awareness. Therefore, every drop counts. If we do not save water today, our children will have to pay for it tomorrow.
✎ Tip: Give facts, then practical suggestions, then an emotional closing line.

Part 4 · Unseen Passage & Translation (Hindi to English)

1. Unseen Passage: 4-Step Method

Step 1: Read the passage twice. Step 2: Read the questions and underline the answers in the passage. Step 3: Write answers in your own simple words, in full sentences. Step 4: Check spelling and grammar.

Passage 1: Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram in 1931 in a poor family. As a boy, he sold newspapers to help his father. He studied hard, became a scientist and led India's missile programme. In 2002 he became the President but remained simple. Students loved him because he inspired them to dream big.
Q1. Where was Kalam born? → Rameswaram.
Q2. How did he help his father? → He helped his father by selling newspapers.
Q3. Why did students love him? → Because he inspired them to dream big.
Q4. Find a word from the passage meaning 'to fill with the urge to do something'. → inspire.

Passage 2: Punctuality means doing every work at the right time. A punctual person is respected by all. He never wastes time and is successful in life. Great men like Gandhiji were very punctual. Students should make a time-table and follow it. Time once lost never comes back.
Q1. What does punctuality mean? → Doing every work at the right time.
Q2. Who is respected by all? → A punctual person.
Q3. What should students do? → They should make a time-table and follow it.
Q4. Give a suitable title. → Value of Punctuality.

Solved Examples

Ex 1: Passage 1 (story) — with solved questions
Ravi was a poor boy who sold newspapers near a railway station. One day he found a purse full of money on a bench. He could have kept it, but he thought of the owner’s worry. He went to the station master and handed it over. The owner, a rich businessman, was very happy and wanted to reward Ravi with money. Ravi refused politely and said that he had only done his duty. The businessman was so impressed that he paid for Ravi’s education. Q1. What did Ravi sell? Ans. Ravi sold newspapers. Q2. Where did he find the purse? Ans. He found it on a bench near the railway station. Q3. Why did Ravi return the purse? Ans. He was honest and thought of the owner’s worry. Q4. Find a word from the passage that means ‘a gift for a good deed’. Ans. Reward. Q5. Write the opposite of ‘poor’. Ans. Rich. Q6. The businessman paid for Ravi’s — (a) house (b) education (c) food. Ans. (b) education. Q7. Give a suitable title. Ans. The Honest Newspaper Boy.
✎ Tip: Read the questions first, find the answer lines in the passage, and answer in full sentences using your own words.
Ex 2: Passage 2 (factual) — with solved questions
Trees are our best friends. They give us fresh air, fruits, wood and shade. They absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen, which we need to breathe. Trees also prevent soil erosion and bring rain. Cutting trees in large numbers has caused pollution and global warming. So we should plant more trees and protect the existing ones. Q1. What do trees release? Ans. Trees release oxygen. Q2. Name two things that trees give us. Ans. Trees give us fruits and wood. Q3. What has the cutting of trees caused? Ans. It has caused pollution and global warming. Q4. Find a word that means ‘to take in’. Ans. Absorb. Q5. True or False: Trees prevent soil erosion. Ans. True. Q6. Give a suitable title. Ans. Trees — Our Best Friends. Q7. What is the message of the passage? Ans. We should plant more trees and protect the existing ones.
✎ Tip: For ‘True/False’ and ‘word meaning’ questions, copy the exact word from the passage.

2. Translation Rules (Hindi → English)

Word order: Hindi = Subject + Object + Verb. English = Subject + Verb + Object.
Example: राम आम खाता है → Ram eats a mango.

Hindi ending → Tense

Hindi ending / हिंदीTense / Structure
…ता/ती/ते हैSimple Present — V1(s/es)
…रहा/रही/रहे हैPresent Continuous — is/am/are + V-ing
…चुका/या/ए हैPresent Perfect — has/have + V3
…ता/ती/ते थाPast Habitual — used to / Simple Past
…रहा थाPast Continuous — was/were + V-ing
…आ/ई/ए (गया, खाया)Simple Past — V2
…चुका थाPast Perfect — had + V3
…गा/गी/गेSimple Future — will/shall + V1

Rules: (1) Find subject, verb, tense first. (2) 'ने' is not translated: उसने लिखा = He wrote. (3) को/से/में → to / from, with / in. (4) Do not translate word by word. (5) Use a/an/the carefully. (6) Mention 'since' for point of time and 'for' for period.

Solved Examples

Translate into English:
Ex 1. राम रोज़ स्कूल जाता है।
✔ Ram goes to school daily.
Why: ‘…ता है’ + ‘रोज़’ → Simple Present.
Translate into English:
Ex 2. वह अभी पढ़ रही है।
✔ She is reading now.
Why: ‘…रही है’ → Present Continuous (is + V-ing).
Translate into English:
Ex 3. मैंने अपना काम कर लिया है।
✔ I have done my work.
Why: ‘…लिया है’ → Present Perfect (have + V3); ‘ने’ is not translated.
Translate into English:
Ex 4. वे पिछले साल दिल्ली गए थे।
✔ They went to Delhi last year.
Why: ‘पिछले साल’ is a past time word → Simple Past (V2).
Translate into English:
Ex 5. हम कल मेले में जाएँगे।
✔ We shall go to the fair tomorrow.
Why: ‘…एँगे’ → Simple Future (shall/will + V1).
Translate into English:
Ex 6. मैं दो घंटे से पढ़ रहा हूँ।
✔ I have been reading for two hours.
Why: ‘…से … रहा हूँ’ with a period of time → Present Perfect Continuous with ‘for’.
Translate into English:
Ex 7. बारिश हो रही थी।
✔ It was raining.
Why: ‘…रही थी’ → Past Continuous (was + V-ing).
Translate into English:
Ex 8. शिक्षक ने छात्रों को पढ़ाया।
✔ The teacher taught the students.
Why: ‘ने’ is dropped; verb in V2 (Simple Past).
Translate into English:
Ex 9. जब मैं पहुँचा, ट्रेन जा चुकी थी।
✔ When I reached, the train had left.
Why: ‘…चुकी थी’ → Past Perfect (had + V3).
Translate into English:
Ex 10. मुझे आम पसंद हैं।
✔ I like mangoes.
Why: ‘मुझे … पसंद है’ = I like …
