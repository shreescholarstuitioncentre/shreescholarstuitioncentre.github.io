Creative Writing

1. What is Creative Writing?

Creative writing is writing that expresses imagination, feelings and original ideas instead of only facts. It includes stories, poems, plays, speeches, articles and diary entries.

2. Forms of Creative Writing

FormFeatures
Short storycharacters, plot, theme; beginning, middle, end
Poemrhythm, imagery, feelings, figures of speech
Articletitle, byline, introduction, body, conclusion
Speechgreeting, topic, arguments, close, thanks
Diary entrydate, personal thoughts, first person
Dialoguespeaker names/quotes, natural conversation

3. Elements of a Story

  • Plot: the sequence of events.
  • Characters: people (or animals) in the story.
  • Setting: time and place.
  • Conflict: the problem or struggle.
  • Theme: the main message.
  • Point of view: first or third person.

4. Plot Stages

  1. Exposition (Beginning): introduces characters and setting.
  2. Rising action: problem grows.
  3. Climax: the most exciting moment.
  4. Falling action: events calm down.
  5. Resolution (End): the problem is solved; the moral appears.

5. Steps to Write a Story

  1. Read the outline or opening line, if given.
  2. Choose a title.
  3. Plan the five plot stages.
  4. Begin with an interesting hook.
  5. Use description, dialogue and feelings.
  6. Keep a single point of view and tense.
  7. End with a clear conclusion or moral.
  8. Check spelling, grammar and word limit.

6. Tips for Good Creative Writing

  • Show, don't just tell: use senses and details.
  • Use simple, vivid words and figures of speech.
  • Keep it original; avoid copying.
  • Vary sentence length.
  • Revise and cut extra words.

7. Worked Example (step by step)

Task: Write a short story on the theme 'Kindness'.

  1. Step 1 – Theme: kindness is rewarded.
  2. Step 2 – Characters and setting: a schoolgirl, Meera, on a rainy day.
  3. Step 3 – Conflict: she has no umbrella but sees a person in need.
  4. Step 4 – Plot plan: the five stages in the diagram.
  5. Step 5 – Write: turn each stage into one or two paragraphs.
  6. Step 6 – Revise: check tense, title and the moral.
The Lost Umbrella
Beginning: It was raining heavily when Meera left school. She had forgotten her umbrella.
Rising action: At the gate, she saw an old woman shivering with no shelter. Meera hesitated, then remembered her mother's words, 'Kindness costs nothing.'
Climax: She ran back and borrowed a school umbrella for the woman, getting drenched herself.
Falling action: The woman blessed her, and a classmate offered Meera his raincoat.
End: Meera went home wet but happy, and learned that small kindness returns in unexpected ways.

11. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. What is creative writing?

Writing that uses imagination and original ideas.

Q. Name some forms of creative writing.

Story, poem, article, speech, diary entry and dialogue.

Q. What is a plot?

The sequence of events in a story.

Q. What is a climax?

The most exciting or important point in a story.

Q. What is setting?

The time and place of a story.

Q. What is conflict?

The problem or struggle in a story.

Q. What is a theme?

The central message of a story.

Q. What is the first stage of a plot?

Exposition (beginning).

Q. Explain the five stages of a plot.

Exposition introduces characters and setting; rising action builds the problem; the climax is the turning point; falling action eases the tension; resolution ends the story.

Q. How do you begin a story attractively?

With a hook such as a surprising event, a dialogue, a question or a vivid description.

Q. Why is a title important?

It attracts the reader and hints at the theme.

Q. What mistakes should be avoided in story writing?

Changing tense, a weak beginning, no conflict, no ending, and long descriptions.

Q. How is creative writing different from factual writing?

Creative writing uses imagination and feelings; factual writing gives information and facts.

Q. What is point of view?

The angle from which the story is told, first person (I) or third person (he/she).

Q. Write a short note on the importance of creative writing.

It develops imagination, vocabulary and expression. It also helps students share ideas and feelings in an original way.

Essay Writing

1. What is an Essay?

An essay is a short, organised piece of prose on one topic. It presents the writer's ideas, facts or feelings in paragraphs with a clear beginning, middle and end.

2. Types of Essays

TypePurposeExample topic
Descriptivedescribes a person, place or thingA Visit to a Fair
Narrativetells a story or experienceA Memorable Journey
Argumentative / Discursivegives arguments for and againstIs Homework Necessary?
Reflectiveshares thoughts and feelingsMy Idea of Happiness
Expositoryexplains and informsImportance of Trees

3. Structure of an Essay

  1. Title: short and clear.
  2. Introduction (1 paragraph): introduces the topic and draws interest.
  3. Body (2–4 paragraphs): main points, one idea per paragraph, with reasons, facts or examples.
  4. Conclusion (1 paragraph): sums up and ends with a thought, advice or hope.

4. Steps to Write an Essay

  1. Read the topic and understand the type.
  2. Brainstorm ideas and note them down.
  3. Prepare an outline: introduction, body points, conclusion.
  4. Write the first paragraph to introduce the topic.
  5. Develop each point in its own paragraph.
  6. Use linkers to connect paragraphs.
  7. Write a strong conclusion.
  8. Revise for spelling, grammar and word limit.

5. Tips for a Good Essay

  • Keep language simple, correct and clear.
  • Use a formal, balanced style; avoid slang.
  • Stay on the topic.
  • Use facts, examples, a quotation or a proverb where it suits.
  • Keep the length within the limit given.
  • Do not repeat points; do not include new ideas in the conclusion.

6. Useful Linkers

  • Beginning: to begin with, nowadays, in today's world
  • Adding: moreover, in addition, also
  • Contrast: however, on the other hand
  • Ending: in conclusion, to sum up, thus

7. Worked Example (step by step)

Topic: The Importance of Trees (expository essay).

  1. Step 1 – Understand: explain why trees matter.
  2. Step 2 – Brainstorm: oxygen, shade, fruit, soil, rain, birds, deforestation.
  3. Step 3 – Outline: Intro; benefits to people; benefits to nature; threats; Conclusion.
  4. Step 4 – Write: turn each outline point into a paragraph.
  5. Step 5 – Revise: check grammar, linkers and length.
The Importance of Trees
Introduction: Trees are called the lungs of the earth. They give us life in many ways, and a world without them would be hard to imagine.
Body 1: Trees give oxygen and absorb carbon dioxide, which keeps the air clean. They also give shade, fruit, wood and medicines.
Body 2: They hold the soil, prevent floods, bring rain and give homes to birds and animals.
Body 3: Yet forests are cut for roads, factories and houses. This causes pollution, floods and global warming.
Conclusion: We must plant more trees and protect existing ones. If we save trees today, they will save us tomorrow.

Introduction, body and conclusion.

Descriptive, narrative, argumentative, reflective and expository.

Sum up the points and end with a final thought.

One.

Letter Writing

1. What is a Letter?

A letter is a written message sent to a person or an organisation. The style depends on the receiver: formal for officials and organisations, informal for friends and relatives.

2. Types of Letters

TypeExamplesStyle
Formalapplication, letter to the editor, complaint, business/order letter, enquirypolite, brief, formal words
Informal (personal)to friend, brother, parents, relativefriendly, warm, simple words

3. Parts of a Letter

  1. Sender's address: top right (not needed in some official letters with letterhead).
  2. Date: below the address.
  3. Receiver's address / designation: on the left (formal letters).
  4. Subject line: short summary of the purpose (formal).
  5. Salutation: Sir / Madam (formal); Dear + name (informal).
  6. Body: introduction, details, request or conclusion.
  7. Subscription (closing): Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely / Yours lovingly.
  8. Signature and name.

4. Formal vs. Informal Letter

PointFormalInformal
Receiver's addressyesno
Subject lineyesno
SalutationSir / MadamDear Rohan
ClosingYours faithfullyYours lovingly / affectionately
Languageformal, no slangfriendly, simple

5. Closing Rules

  • 'Sir/Madam' → 'Yours faithfully'.
  • 'Dear Mr/Ms Name' → 'Yours sincerely'.
  • Friends → 'Yours affectionately'; parents → 'Your loving son/daughter'.

6. Steps to Write a Letter

  1. Read the question; find who is the receiver and what is the purpose.
  2. Decide formal or informal.
  3. Use the full format.
  4. Write the body in short paragraphs: purpose, details, request.
  5. Close politely.
  6. Check spelling, grammar and word limit.

7. Tips

  • Use the name given in the question; if no name is given, use a name like 'Rahul Sharma' carefully, or 'Xyz', as your teacher suggests.
  • Be polite, brief and clear.
  • Keep a margin, neat handwriting and paragraphs.

Note-Making

1. What is Note-making?

Note-making is writing the main ideas of a passage in a short, organised form, using headings, sub-headings, abbreviations and symbols. It is used for study, revision, lectures and reports.

2. Why do we make notes?

  • To save time during revision.
  • To remember key points.
  • To organise ideas logically.
  • To prepare for writing a summary.

3. Format (parts of notes)

  1. Title: a short heading that gives the main idea of the passage.
  2. Headings and sub-headings: main points and their parts. Number them (1., 1.1, 1.1.1) or use I, A, a.
  3. Points: short phrases, not full sentences. Use only needed words.
  4. Abbreviations / symbols: use standard or self-made ones (e.g. govt., &, →, etc.).
  5. Key to abbreviations: list the abbreviations you have made, with meaning.
  6. Consistency: follow the same style and indentation throughout.

4. Steps to follow

  1. Read the passage carefully at least twice.
  2. Underline key words and ideas.
  3. Choose a suitable title.
  4. Find the main headings and sub-headings.
  5. Write points in short, clear phrases.
  6. Use abbreviations and symbols.
  7. Give a key to abbreviations.
  8. Check order, numbering and spelling.

5. Dos and Don'ts

  • Do: use telegraphic phrases, indent, number, keep logical order.
  • Do: keep to the passage; do not add your own ideas.
  • Don't: copy full sentences.
  • Don't: use unfamiliar abbreviations without a key.
  • Don't: forget the title.

6. Common Abbreviations

Sub-titling

1. What is Sub-titling?

A sub-title (or sub-heading) is a short heading given to a part (paragraph or section) of a passage or article. It tells the reader what that part is about. Sub-titling means writing such headings for each part.

2. Title vs. Sub-title

PointTitleSub-title
Coverswhole passageone section / paragraph
Numberonemany
Positiontopbefore each section
Usemain ideaguides the reader

3. Features of a Good Sub-title

  • Short: usually 2–6 words.
  • Relevant: matches the content of the part.
  • Catchy: interesting, clear and natural.
  • Not a sentence: often a noun phrase; no full stop.
  • Consistent: same style for all sub-titles.
  • Distinct: each sub-title different from the others.

4. Steps to Follow

  1. Read the whole passage.
  2. Divide it into parts (usually one per paragraph).
  3. Find the main idea of each part.
  4. Write a short phrase for the idea.
  5. Check that it is brief, clear and relevant.
  6. Keep all sub-titles in a similar style.

5. Dos and Don'ts

  • Do: use key words from the paragraph.
  • Do: keep headings parallel (e.g. all noun phrases).
  • Don't: write long sentences.
  • Don't: copy the first line.
  • Don't: use vague words like 'Paragraph one'.

6. Worked Example (step by step)

Passage:

Para 1: Many students feel stress before exams. Their hearts beat fast and they cannot sleep. Some forget what they have studied.
Para 2: Experts advise making a time-table, revising daily and taking short breaks. Regular sleep and light exercise also keep the mind fresh.
Para 3: Parents can help by staying calm and avoiding comparison. Words of support give children confidence to face exams.
  1. Step 1 – Read: the passage is about exam stress.
  2. Step 2 – Divide: three paragraphs, so three sub-titles.
  3. Step 3 – Main idea of Para 1: students show signs of stress → 'Signs of Exam Stress'.
  4. Step 4 – Para 2: advice by experts → 'Expert Tips to Cope'.
  5. Step 5 – Para 3: role of parents → 'Support from Parents'.
  6. Step 6 – Check: short, parallel noun phrases, all relevant.
Answer:
Para 1: Signs of Exam Stress
Para 2: Expert Tips to Cope
Para 3: Support from Parents

Summarising

1. What is a Summary?

A summary is a short version of a passage in your own words. It keeps only the main ideas and leaves out examples, repetition, quotations and extra details. It is written as a connected paragraph.

2. Features of a Good Summary

  • Short: about one-third of the passage (check your question).
  • Complete: all main ideas are included.
  • Own words: do not copy sentences.
  • Connected: sentences flow logically, using linkers.
  • Objective: no personal opinion.
  • Correct: grammar, spelling and one consistent tense (usually present or past), in third person.

3. Steps to Follow

  1. Read the passage twice.
  2. Find the main idea of each paragraph.
  3. Leave out examples, repeated points, quotations and descriptions.
  4. Prepare short notes (a note-making draft helps).
  5. Write the summary in your own words.
  6. Join points with linkers (and, but, because, therefore).
  7. Count words and revise to the limit.
  8. Give a title, if asked.

4. Summary vs. Notes vs. Precis

PointNotesSummary
Formheadings and pointsone paragraph
Languagephrases, abbreviationsfull sentences
Userevisionquick overview
Lengthflexibleabout 1/3

5. Dos and Don'ts

  • Do: use own words, keep order of ideas, stay objective.
  • Don't: copy, add own ideas, include examples or quotations.
  • Don't: exceed the word limit.

6. Useful Linkers

  • Addition: and, also, moreover
  • Contrast: but, however, although
  • Cause: because, since
  • Result: so, therefore, thus

7. Worked Example (step by step)

Passage:

Water is essential for life, yet millions of people lack safe drinking water. Rivers are polluted by factories and sewage, and groundwater is being pumped faster than rain can refill it. For example, in some cities wells have dried up completely. Experts say the problem will grow as populations rise. They suggest saving rainwater, reusing waste water and making laws against polluting rivers. Citizens, too, must stop wasting water, because each drop saved helps the next generation.
  1. Step 1 – Main idea: water crisis and need to save water.
  2. Step 2 – Key points: (a) lack of safe water, (b) causes: polluted rivers, groundwater overuse, (c) experts' warning and solutions, (d) citizens' role.
  3. Step 3 – Delete: the example about dried wells.
  4. Step 4 – Rewrite: convert points into connected sentences in own words.
  5. Step 5 – Check: shorter than the passage, about one-third, no copying.

Summary:

Title: Water Crisis
Many people lack safe water because rivers are polluted and groundwater is overused. Experts warn that the problem will increase, and recommend rainwater harvesting, reuse of waste water and stricter laws. Citizens must also avoid wasting water to protect the future.

11. Questions & Answers (Short + Long)

Q. What is a summary?

A short form of a passage in our own words that gives its main ideas.

Q. What is the usual length of a summary?

About one-third of the passage.

Q. Should we copy sentences from the passage?

No, use own words.

Q. Should examples be included?

No, they are omitted.

Q. In which person is a summary written?

Third person.

Q. What are linkers?

Words that join ideas, e.g. and, but, because, therefore.

Read the passage carefully.

A summary must be objective and show only the author's ideas.

Notes give the main ideas; the summary is written from them.

Q. List the features of a good summary.

Short, complete, objective, in own words, connected, correct.

Examples, repetition, quotations, description and minor details.

Q. Explain the steps of summarising.

Read, find main ideas, delete examples, make notes, write in own words, join with linkers, check length, add title.

Q. Why is tense consistency important?

It keeps the paragraph clear and correct.

Q. Write the importance of summarising.

It trains students to find the main ideas and express them briefly. It saves time and improves writing.

Where these skills are tested

  • Class 11 and 12 exams: article, letter, notice, note making and summary appear regularly.
  • SSC (CGL, CHSL) descriptive papers: letter, application and essay are commonly asked.
  • CUET, CLAT, NDA, state exams: comprehension, précis and verbal ability.

Scoring tips and common mistakes

  • Use the right salutation and closing. Wrong pairs lose marks.
  • Never exceed the word limit. Use paragraphs and a clear title.
  • Do not copy lines from the passage in a summary.
  • Read one editorial daily and write a 3-line summary.
  • Learn 5 new words daily and use each in a sentence.
  • Video subtitles: one short line per scene, time-code as hh:mm:ss, sound cues in [brackets].
  • Practise previous-year papers with a timer.
