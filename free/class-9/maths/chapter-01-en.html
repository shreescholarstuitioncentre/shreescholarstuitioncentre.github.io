Class 9 Maths Revision Chart

17 chapters · key definitions and formulas

1. Number Systems

Formulas

p/q form, q ≠ 0√2 = 1.414… (non-terminating, non-recurring)N ⊂ W ⊂ Z ⊂ Q ⊂ R

Rational: terminating or non-terminating recurring decimals. Irrational: non-terminating, non-recurring (√2, π). Every real number lies on the number line. Sum/product of a rational and an irrational is irrational.

2. Exponents

Formulas

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿaᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿa⁰ = 1, a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿa^(1/n) = ⁿ√a

Laws of exponents hold for real a > 0 and rational powers. Same base: add powers when multiplying, subtract when dividing. (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ.

3. Rationalisation

Formulas

1/(√a + √b) = (√a − √b)/(a − b)(√a + √b)(√a − √b) = a − b1/√a = √a/a

Remove the root from the denominator by multiplying numerator and denominator by its conjugate. e.g. 1/(2+√3) = 2 − √3.

4. Algebraic Identities

Formulas

(a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b²(a−b)² = a² − 2ab + b²a² − b² = (a+b)(a−b)(a+b+c)² = a²+b²+c²+2ab+2bc+2ca(a+b)³ = a³+b³+3ab(a+b)a³+b³ = (a+b)(a²−ab+b²)a³−b³ = (a−b)(a²+ab+b²)If a+b+c=0 then a³+b³+c³ = 3abc

Use identities to expand, factorise and find values quickly.

5. Polynomials

Formulas

p(x) = aₙxⁿ + … + a₁x + a₀Zero: p(k) = 0

Degree: highest power of x. Linear (1), quadratic (2), cubic (3). A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes. Exponents must be whole numbers; constant polynomial has degree 0.

6. Remainder & Factor Theorem

Formulas

Remainder theorem: p(x) ÷ (x − a) → remainder p(a)Factor theorem: (x − a) is a factor ⇔ p(a) = 0

To factorise a cubic, find a root by trial (±1, ±2…), then divide. For (ax + b), the root is −b/a.

7. Coordinate Geometry

Formulas

Point (x, y): x = abscissa, y = ordinateOrigin (0, 0)

Quadrants: I (+,+), II (−,+), III (−,−), IV (+,−). Points on the x-axis are (x, 0); on the y-axis (0, y).

8. Linear Equations in Two Variables

Formulas

ax + by + c = 0 (a, b not both 0)x-axis: y = 0 · y-axis: x = 0

Has infinitely many solutions; its graph is a straight line. Every point on the line is a solution. y = mx is a line through the origin.

9. Introduction to Euclid's Geometry

Formulas

Things equal to the same thing are equal to one anotherThe whole is greater than the part

Axioms are common notions; postulates are geometry-specific. Fifth postulate: if a line meets two lines making interior angles on one side < 180°, the lines meet on that side. Two distinct points have exactly one line through them.

10. Lines and Angles

Formulas

Linear pair = 180°Vertically opposite angles are equalAngle sum of triangle = 180°Exterior angle = sum of two interior opposite angles

Transversal on parallel lines: corresponding, alternate interior angles are equal; co-interior angles are supplementary. Converse also holds.

11. Triangles

Formulas

SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS, RHS congruenceAngles opposite equal sides are equalSum of any two sides > third side

Greater side has greater opposite angle and vice versa. CPCT: corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal. AAA and SSA are not congruence rules.

12. Quadrilaterals

Formulas

Angle sum = 360°Midpoint theorem: DE = ½ BC, DE ∥ BC

Parallelogram: opposite sides/angles equal, diagonals bisect each other. Rectangle: diagonals equal. Rhombus: diagonals bisect at 90°. Square has all properties. One pair of opposite sides equal and parallel ⇒ parallelogram.

13. Circles

Formulas

Equal chords subtend equal angles at centreAngle at centre = 2 × angle at circumferenceAngles in same segment are equalCyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles sum to 180°

Perpendicular from centre bisects a chord; the line from centre to midpoint of chord is perpendicular. Angle in a semicircle = 90°. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

14. Surface Areas and Volumes

Formulas

Cuboid: V = lbh, TSA = 2(lb+bh+hl)Cube: V = a³, TSA = 6a²Cylinder: V = πr²h, CSA = 2πrh, TSA = 2πr(r+h)Cone: V = ⅓πr²h, CSA = πrl, l² = r²+h²Sphere: V = 4/3 πr³, SA = 4πr²Hemisphere: V = ⅔πr³, CSA = 2πr², TSA = 3πr²

CSA = curved surface area, TSA = total surface area, l = slant height.

15. Statistics

Formulas

Mean = Σfx / ΣfMedian: middle valueMode: most frequent valueClass mark = (upper + lower)/2

Mean, median and mode are measures of central tendency. For even n, median = average of the two middle values. Range = highest − lowest. Use bar graphs, histograms and frequency polygons.

16. Probability

Formulas

P(E) = favourable trials / total trials0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1P(E) + P(not E) = 1

Empirical probability is based on experiments. Sure event = 1, impossible event = 0. Die: P(even) = 3/6 = ½. Coin: P(H) = ½.

17. Heron's Formula

Formulas

s = (a+b+c)/2Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]Equilateral: (√3/4)a²Isosceles: (b/4)√(4a²−b²)

Use when all three sides are known. Quadrilateral area: split into two triangles with a diagonal.
