Beehive – Prose

1. The Fun They Had

Isaac Asimov

In the year 2157, Margie and Tommy find a real printed book. They learn that children once went to a school building and studied with a human teacher and classmates. Margie, who has a mechanical teacher at home, imagines how much fun that was.

2. The Sound of Music

Deborah Cowley

Part I: Evelyn Glennie, who gradually became deaf, learnt to feel music through vibrations and became a famous percussionist. Part II: Bismillah Khan, shehnai maestro of Banaras, made the shehnai a classical instrument and played at the Red Fort on 15 August 1947. Both show that passion and hard work overcome obstacles.

3. The Little Girl

Katherine Mansfield

Five-year-old Kezia fears her stern father. Trying to make a pincushion for his birthday, she cuts up papers that turn out to be his speech, and he punishes her harshly. Later, when she has a nightmare, her father comforts her and their relationship changes.

4. A Truly Beautiful Mind

Biography of Albert Einstein

Einstein was a slow talker as a child and disliked rote schooling. He developed the theory of relativity, won the 1921 Nobel Prize for the photoelectric effect, and later left Germany under the Nazis. A pacifist, he regretted the use of atomic weapons and valued simplicity and curiosity.

5. The Snake and the Mirror

Vithal Nagesh Shirodkar

A doctor narrates how a snake entered his room one night. He stayed calm and still, and the snake was attracted by its own reflection in the mirror, which gave him a chance to escape. The story shows presence of mind in danger, told with humour.

6. My Childhood

A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Kalam recalls growing up in Rameswaram with a loving, modest family. Friends from different religions and teachers like Sivasubramania Iyer taught him tolerance. He saw social divisions but also kindness, and learnt that education and unity can change society.

7. Packing

Jerome K. Jerome

The narrator insists on packing for a boat trip while George and Harris lazily watch. He makes a muddle: he forgets things, repacks several times and drives everyone mad. The humorous piece shows how overconfidence leads to chaos.

8. Reach for the Top

Santosh Yadav; Maria Sharapova

Part I: Santosh Yadav from Rewari, Haryana, overcame family opposition and became the first woman to climb Mount Everest twice (1992, 1993). Part II: Maria Sharapova moved to the US as a child, trained hard and won Wimbledon at 17. Both stress courage, discipline and sacrifice.

9. The Bond of Love

Kenneth Anderson

A family keeps a bear cub, Bruno, as a pet. He becomes affectionate and playful but grows too big for the home and is moved to a zoo. The story shows the strong bond between humans and animals and the pain of parting.

10. Kathmandu

Vikram Seth

A travel account of Kathmandu: the Hindu temple of Pashupatinath and the Buddhist stupa of Boudhanath. The writer describes the lively crowds, the mix of religions and the cultural harmony of the city.

11. If I Were You

Douglas James

Gerrard, an actor, is alone at home when Duffy, a criminal who looks exactly like him, enters planning to kill him and take his place. Gerrard stays calm and cleverly tricks him into a trap, so that Duffy is caught by the police.

Beehive – Poems (Central Idea)

1. The Road Not Taken

Robert Frost

Life is full of choices. The poet chooses the less-travelled road and says it made all the difference; we must make our own decisions and accept their consequences.

2. Wind

Subramania Bharati

The wind breaks weak things and helps the strong. The poet urges us to be strong, build firm homes and hearts, and not fear hardships.

3. Rain on the Roof

Coates Kinney

The sound of rain on the roof brings back sweet memories of childhood and the mother's lullaby. Nature can soothe the mind and revive the past.

4. The Lake Isle of Innisfree

W.B. Yeats

The poet longs to leave city life and live in peace at Innisfree, in a small cabin with bean rows and bees. Nature gives peace of mind.

5. A Legend of the Northland

Phoebe Cary

An old woman baked tiny cakes for Saint Peter but kept refusing to give them. Greed and selfishness turned her into a woodpecker. It teaches generosity.

6. No Men Are Foreign

James Kirkup

All human beings are alike and share the same earth; we should not hate or fight others. War and hatred harm everyone, so we should live in universal brotherhood.

7. The Duck and the Kangaroo

Edward Lear

A humorous nonsense poem: the Duck asks the Kangaroo to take him for a ride on her back; they travel together and form a friendship. It speaks of friendship and adventure.

8. On Killing a Tree

Gieve Patel

Killing a tree is not easy: it takes a long time to grow and it resists being destroyed. The poet shows how deeply the tree is rooted and warns against destroying nature.

9. The Snake Trying

William Stafford

The poet tries to scare a snake but sees it struggling; he feels pity and respect for its life. The poem asks us to respect all living beings.

10. A Slumber Did My Spirit Seal

William Wordsworth

The poet once ignored time and death, but Lucy's death shows he is helpless. She now becomes part of nature, rolled round with rocks, stones and trees.

Moments – Supplementary Reader

1. The Lost Child

Mulk Raj Anand

A small boy at a spring fair is tempted by toys, sweets and flowers, loses his parents and weeps in panic. When he finds them he wants nothing else. Theme: a child's security lies in parents' love.

2. The Adventures of Toto

Ruskin Bond

Grandfather buys Toto, a mischievous monkey, for a few rupees. Toto's pranks trouble the family but also delight the narrator. Theme: humour and the bond with animals.

3. Iswaran the Storyteller

R.K. Narayan

Mahendra's cook Iswaran tells imaginative, exaggerated tales that amuse his employer. Theme: the charm of storytelling and a humorous look at human nature.

4. In the Kingdom of Fools

Karnataka folk tale

In a kingdom where everything costs the same, a guru warns his greedy disciple to leave, but he stays. The disciple is condemned to die, and the guru saves him by a trick that makes the foolish king hang himself. Theme: greed and foolishness cause ruin; heed wise advice.

5. The Happy Prince

Oscar Wilde

A statue covered in gold and jewels sees the poverty in the city. With the help of a swallow, it gives away its gold leaves, ruby and sapphire eyes to the poor; the swallow dies of cold and the statue is melted. God calls the lead heart and the dead bird most precious. Theme: selfless compassion.

6. Weathering the Storm in Ersama

Prashant Panda

After the 1999 super cyclone in Odisha, Prashant, a young volunteer, helped survivors with food, clothing and cremation of the dead, and helped rebuild the community. Theme: courage and selfless service in disaster.

7. The Last Leaf

O. Henry

Johnsy, sick with pneumonia, believes she will die when the last ivy leaf falls. Old painter Behrman secretly paints a leaf on the wall that never falls, giving her hope; she recovers while he dies of pneumonia. Theme: sacrifice and hope.

8. A House Is Not a Home

Zan Gaudioso

The narrator recalls a house fire and understands that a house is only bricks; love and family make a home. Theme: relationships matter more than possessions.

9. The Accidental Tourist

Bill Bryson

The writer humorously recounts mishaps during his travels, showing how accidents and unexpected experiences make travel memorable. Theme: humour in everyday mishaps.

10. The Beggar

Anton Chekhov

Lushkoff, a beggar, is made to chop wood by the lawyer Skvortsov; the cook Olga helps him change. He reforms, becomes a clerk and thanks her. Theme: kindness and honest work can transform lives.
