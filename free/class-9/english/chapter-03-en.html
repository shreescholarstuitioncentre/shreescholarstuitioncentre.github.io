1. Voice (Active & Passive)

Voice shows whether the subject does the action (Active) or receives it (Passive).
Active: Ram writes a letter.   Passive: A letter is written by Ram.

Steps: (1) Find Subject, Verb, Object. (2) Object becomes the new subject. (3) Use the correct form of 'be' + V3. (4) Add 'by' + old subject (I→me, he→him, we→us, they→them).

Tense-wise passive formula

Simple Pastwas/were + V3
Past Continuouswas/were being + V3
Past Perfecthad been + V3
Simple Futurewill/shall be + V3
Future Perfectwill/shall have been + V3
Modals (can, may, must)modal + be + V3

Imperative: Open the door → Let the door be opened.   Question: Who wrote this? → By whom was this written?
Note: Intransitive verbs (no object) cannot become passive: He sleeps. The 'by…' part can be dropped if unknown: English is spoken here.

2. Narration (Direct & Indirect Speech)

Direct speech gives the exact words in inverted commas. Indirect speech reports the meaning.
Direct: He said, "I am happy."   Indirect: He said that he was happy.

3 rules: (1) Remove comma and quotes; add that. (2) Change tense if the reporting verb is past. (3) Change pronouns and time/place words.

Pronouns: 1st person → subject of reporting verb; 2nd → object; 3rd → no change.
Questions: said to → asked; Wh-word stays; yes/no → if/whether; no '?'.
Commands: said to → ordered/requested/advised + to-infinitive.
Exclamation: exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise that…
No tense change if the reporting verb is present/future or the speech is a universal truth.

3. Punctuation

Punctuation marks work like traffic signals for the reader. Capital letters are used for: the first word of a sentence, the pronoun I, proper nouns, days, months and the first word of a quotation.
