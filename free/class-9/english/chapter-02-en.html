1. Tenses (Kaal)

Tense shows the time of an action — Present, Past or Future. Each has 4 forms: Simple, Continuous, Perfect and Perfect Continuous = 12 tenses.

All 12 tenses

TenseStructureExample
Simple PresentS + V1 (s/es)He plays cricket daily.
Present ContinuousS + is/am/are + V1+ingHe is playing now.
Present PerfectS + has/have + V3He has played.
Present Perfect Cont.S + has/have been + V1+ingHe has been playing since 5.
Simple PastS + V2He played yesterday.
Past ContinuousS + was/were + V1+ingHe was playing.
Past PerfectS + had + V3He had played before I came.
Past Perfect Cont.S + had been + V1+ingHe had been playing for an hour.
Simple FutureS + will/shall + V1He will play tomorrow.
Future ContinuousS + will be + V1+ingHe will be playing.
Future PerfectS + will have + V3He will have played by then.
Future Perfect Cont.S + will have been + V1+ingHe will have been playing for 2 hours.

Signal words

TenseSignal words
Simple Presentalways, usually, daily, every day, never
Present Continuousnow, at present, at this moment, look!, listen!
Present Perfectjust, already, yet, ever, never, recently, so far
Perfect Continuoussince (point of time), for (period of time)
Simple Pastyesterday, last week, ago, in 2010
Simple Futuretomorrow, next week, soon, tonight

Remember: after he/she/it (singular) add s/es to the verb in Simple Present: He goes, She writes. With I/we/you/they use the plain verb. since = from a point of time; for = for a period.

2. Articles (a, an, the)

Articles are the words a, an, the. A / An are indefinite (any one, singular, countable noun). The is definite (a particular one).

Rules at a glance

ArticleUse / उपयोग
abefore a consonant SOUND: a book, a university, a European
anbefore a vowel SOUND: an apple, an hour, an honest man, an MLA
theparticular noun: The book on the table is mine.
theunique things: the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky
therivers, seas, mountains (ranges), holy books: the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Gita
thesuperlatives & ordinals: the best, the first
themusical instruments: She plays the guitar.
no articleproper nouns, languages, meals, games: Ram, Hindi, lunch, cricket
no articleplural/uncountable in general: Milk is white. Dogs bark.

Trick: sound matters, not spelling! an hour (silent h), a university (starts with 'yoo' sound).

3. Reordering of Sentences

In reordering you get jumbled words or parts and must arrange them into a meaningful sentence. English order: Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time.

Steps: (1) Find the subject (who?). (2) Find the verb. (3) Add object/complement. (4) Add place, then time. (5) Read it aloud — does it make sense? Questions: Wh-word + helping verb + subject + main verb.

Example: school / goes / to / daily / Ravi → Ravi goes to school daily.   Question: you / where / live / do → Where do you live?
