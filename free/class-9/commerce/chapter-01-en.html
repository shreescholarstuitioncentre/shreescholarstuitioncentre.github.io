Class 9 Commerce – Revision

Summary, key points and a practice question for all 28 lessons

1Double Entry System

Summary

Every transaction has two aspects – debit and credit. Total debits always equal total credits.

Key points

  • Personal: debit the receiver, credit the giver
  • Real: debit what comes in, credit what goes out
  • Nominal: debit expenses/losses, credit incomes/gains

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

State the basic rule of double entry.

Every transaction has one debit and one equal credit.

2Office

Summary

An office is where the clerical work of a business is done. It is the nerve centre of the business.

Key points

  • Functions: receive, record, store and supply information
  • Gives management information for decisions
  • Good offices need cleanliness, light and order

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Give two functions of an office.

Collecting information and keeping records.

3Journal

Summary

The journal is the book of original entry where transactions are recorded date-wise before posting to the ledger.

Key points

  • Columns: date, particulars, L.F., debit, credit
  • Recording a transaction is called journalising
  • A short narration follows each entry

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Why is the journal called the book of original entry?

Because transactions are first recorded in it.

4Book Keeping

Summary

Book keeping is the systematic recording of financial transactions in books of account.

Key points

  • Objectives: complete records, know profit or loss
  • Uses: taxation, loans, legal proof
  • Records only money-measurable transactions

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is book keeping?

Recording financial transactions systematically.

5Ledger

Summary

The ledger is the principal book where journal entries are sorted into separate accounts.

Key points

  • Entering journal items in accounts is posting
  • Each account is balanced
  • Debit is the left side, credit the right

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is posting?

Transferring journal entries to ledger accounts.

6Cash Book

Summary

The cash book records all cash receipts and payments. It works as both a journal and a ledger.

Key points

  • Receipts on the debit side, payments on the credit side
  • Types: single, double (cash+bank), triple (+discount)
  • Petty cash book handles small expenses

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Which side shows cash receipts?

The debit side.

7Trial Balance

Summary

A trial balance lists debit and credit balances of all ledger accounts on a date. It checks arithmetical accuracy.

Key points

  • If debit total = credit total, it agrees
  • It cannot catch errors of omission or principle
  • Basis for preparing final accounts

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is the main aim of a trial balance?

To check arithmetical accuracy of the books.

8Indian System of Book Keeping

Summary

This is the traditional Indian accounting method, kept in local languages and Mahajani script.

Key points

  • Main books: cash book, ledger, copy book
  • Simple, cheap and in local language
  • Hundi is linked with the tradition

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

In which language is the Indian system kept?

The local language and Mahajani script.

9Business

Summary

Business is a regular economic activity of producing, buying and selling goods and services to earn profit.

Key points

  • Features: profit motive, risk, regular dealing
  • Divisions: industry, commerce, services
  • Commerce = trade + aids to trade

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name two features of business.

Profit motive and risk.

10Correspondence

Summary

Correspondence is the exchange of messages in writing. It is a main means of communication.

Key points

  • Types: personal, official, business
  • Serves as written proof
  • Needs clear language and courtesy

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name the types of correspondence.

Personal, official and business.

11Copying

Summary

Keeping copies of letters sent is called copying. It provides proof for the future.

Key points

  • Methods: carbon paper, photocopy, scan, e-mail copy
  • Letter copy book is an older method
  • Evidence in disputes

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Why are copies kept?

For records and proof.

12Commercial Correspondence

Summary

Commercial correspondence is the exchange of business letters in a formal layout.

Key points

  • Parts: heading, date, address, salutation, subject, body, close, signature
  • Qualities: clarity, brevity, courtesy, correctness
  • Mention enclosures

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Give two qualities of a good business letter.

Clarity and courtesy.

13Constituents of Money

Summary

In a modern economy money is more than coins and notes; it includes bank deposits and credit instruments.

Key points

  • Coins and notes (currency)
  • Demand deposits in banks
  • Cheques, drafts and other credit instruments

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name two constituents of money.

Currency (coins and notes) and bank deposits.

14Barter System

Summary

Barter is the direct exchange of goods for goods. Its difficulties led to the invention of money.

Key points

  • No double coincidence of wants
  • No common measure of value
  • Goods are hard to divide or store

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is the main difficulty of barter?

Lack of double coincidence of wants.

15Origin and Growth of Money

Summary

Money evolved to remove the difficulties of barter and changed over time.

Key points

  • Commodity money (cattle, grain, shells)
  • Metal money → coins → paper money
  • Credit money (cheques); plastic and digital money (cards, UPI)

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Write the order of growth of money.

Commodity → metal → paper → credit/digital.

16Meaning, Functions, Importance and Evils of Money

Summary

Money is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange. It is vital to an economy but has evils too.

Key points

  • Primary: medium of exchange, measure of value
  • Secondary: store of value, standard of deferred payment
  • Evils: inflation, black money, inequality, speculation

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Give two primary functions of money.

Medium of exchange and measure of value.

17Classification of Money: Metallic and Paper

Summary

Money is mainly classified as metallic and paper money.

Key points

  • Metallic: standard coins and token coins
  • Paper: convertible, inconvertible, representative
  • Modern paper money rests on government credit

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name the two main classes of money.

Metallic and paper.

18Qualities of a Good Money Material

Summary

Material for money should have certain qualities to make exchange easy.

Key points

  • General acceptability, durability, portability
  • Divisibility, homogeneity, stable value
  • Easy to recognise, limited supply

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

List any three qualities.

Durability, portability and divisibility.

19Principles and Methods of Note Issue

Summary

Note issue means putting notes into circulation, backed by reserve rules.

Key points

  • Methods: 100% metallic reserve, fixed fiduciary, proportional reserve, maximum fiduciary, minimum reserve
  • India follows the minimum reserve system (gold and foreign securities)
  • RBI has the right to issue notes

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Which note-issue system does India follow?

The minimum reserve system.

20Present Currency System in India

Summary

India has a paper currency standard. The unit of currency is the rupee (₹).

Key points

  • RBI issues notes of ₹2 and above
  • Government issues ₹1 notes and coins
  • Decimal system: ₹1 = 100 paise

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Who issues notes in India?

The Reserve Bank of India.

21Introduction and Definition of Economics

Summary

Economics studies choice between unlimited wants and limited resources.

Key points

  • Adam Smith: science of wealth
  • Marshall: science of welfare
  • Robbins: science of scarcity and choice

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Who called economics the science of welfare?

Alfred Marshall.

22Scope of Economics

Summary

The scope covers the subject matter and the branches of economics.

Key points

  • Consumption, production, exchange, distribution
  • Micro and macro economics
  • Positive and normative economics

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name the four divisions of economics.

Consumption, production, exchange and distribution.

23Enquiry and Order

Summary

A buyer sends an enquiry to get details of goods and then places an order if satisfied.

Key points

  • Enquiry: price, quality, discount, terms
  • Order: quantity, price, delivery, payment terms
  • Seller replies with a quotation

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What does an enquiry letter ask?

Price, quality and terms of supply.

24Importance of Economics

Summary

Studying economics helps individuals, businesses and governments.

Key points

  • Helps government in policy and planning
  • Explains poverty, unemployment and inflation
  • Helps consumers and producers decide

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Who benefits from economics?

Consumers, businesses and governments.

25Terminology Relating to Economics

Summary

Basic economic terms are needed to understand the subject.

Key points

  • Utility: power to satisfy a want
  • Value and price: worth of goods in money
  • Wealth, income, goods, services, demand, supply

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is utility?

The want-satisfying power of a good.

26Consumption: Meaning, Types and Importance

Summary

Consumption is using goods and services to satisfy wants.

Key points

  • Types: direct and indirect
  • Consumption motivates production
  • Shows the standard of living

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Why is consumption important?

It is the basis of production and shows living standards.

27Classification and Characteristics of Human Needs

Summary

Human wants are unlimited and are grouped by importance.

Key points

  • Necessaries, comforts, luxuries
  • Unlimited and recurring
  • Change with habit and circumstances

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

Name three classes of wants.

Necessaries, comforts and luxuries.

28Project Work

Summary

In project work, students collect information on a topic and prepare a report.

Key points

  • Steps: choose topic, collect data, analyse, write
  • Examples: journal entries of a small shop, family needs survey
  • Neat presentation and a reference list

Practice question (tap to see the answer)

What is the first step of a project?

Choosing a topic.
