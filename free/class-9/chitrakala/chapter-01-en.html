Class 9 Drawing & Painting – Revision

Summary, key points and practice questions for all 10 lessons

1Introduction to Painting

Summary

Painting is the art of expressing feelings, ideas and experiences on a surface using line and colour. Humans first painted on cave walls. It is a powerful way to communicate without words.

Key points

  • Surfaces: paper, canvas, wall, cloth
  • Media: pencil, charcoal, water colour, poster colour, oil, pastel
  • Types: portrait, landscape, still life, mural, abstract
  • Prehistoric cave paintings – Bhimbetka (M.P.)

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What is painting?

Expressing feelings and ideas on a surface using line and colour.

Name two painting media.

Water colour and pastel.

2Elements of Art

Summary

Every artwork is built from basic elements. These are arranged using principles such as balance, rhythm, harmony, proportion, emphasis and unity.

Key points

  • Elements: line, shape, colour, tone, texture, space
  • Principles: balance, rhythm, harmony, proportion, emphasis, unity
  • Tone: how light or dark a colour is
  • Texture: how a surface feels – rough or smooth

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

Name four elements of art.

Line, shape, colour and texture.

What is tone?

The lightness or darkness of a colour or shade.

3Line and Drawing

Summary

Line is the most basic element and forms the outline of an object. Different lines create different moods. In drawing, shading adds depth to an outline.

Key points

  • Types: straight, curved, wavy, zigzag
  • Horizontal – calm; vertical – strength; diagonal – movement
  • Shading: hatching, cross-hatching, stippling
  • Pencil grades: H hard, HB medium, B soft

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What does a horizontal line suggest?

Calmness and stability.

Why do we shade a drawing?

To show depth and solidity.

4Colour and Colour Schemes

Summary

Colour creates mood and atmosphere. It has three properties – hue, value and intensity. The colour wheel helps in choosing colour schemes.

Key points

  • Warm colours: red, orange, yellow
  • Cool colours: blue, green, violet
  • Analogous: neighbouring colours on the wheel
  • Complementary: opposite colours on the wheel
  • Monochromatic: tints and shades of one colour

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

Which colours are warm?

Red, yellow and orange.

State the three properties of colour.

Hue, value and intensity.

5Primary and Secondary Colours

Summary

Primary colours cannot be made by mixing other colours. Mixing two primaries in equal amounts gives a secondary colour. A primary mixed with a neighbouring secondary gives a tertiary colour.

Key points

  • Primary: red, yellow, blue
  • Secondary: orange (R+Y), green (Y+B), violet (R+B)
  • Complementary pairs: red–green, yellow–violet, blue–orange
  • White gives a tint; black gives a shade

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

How is green made?

By mixing yellow and blue.

What is the complement of blue?

Orange.

6History of Indian Painting

Summary

Indian painting is thousands of years old, from prehistoric cave art to Ajanta, miniature schools, Mughal, Rajput, Pahari and the modern Bengal School.

Key points

  • Ajanta: Buddhist themes, Jataka tales, wall paintings
  • Mughal: Persian influence, court scenes, birds and animals
  • Rajput: Mewar, Bundi, Kishangarh (Bani Thani)
  • Pahari: Kangra, Basohli – soft colours, Krishna and love themes
  • Bengal School: Abanindranath Tagore, Nandalal Bose
  • Folk art: Madhubani, Warli, Pattachitra

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What are Ajanta paintings about?

The life of Buddha and Jataka stories.

Name leaders of the Bengal School.

Abanindranath Tagore and Nandalal Bose.

7Landscape Painting

Summary

Landscapes show trees, hills, rivers, sky and fields. To show depth, the scene is divided into foreground, middle ground and background.

Key points

  • Start with the horizon line
  • Near objects: bigger, darker, clearer; far objects: smaller, lighter, hazy
  • Aerial perspective: distant colours look bluish and faded
  • Paint sky first, then land, then details
  • Light and shade add realism

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What is the foreground?

The part of the picture nearest the viewer.

Why do distant objects look faint?

Because of aerial perspective.

8Still Life Drawing

Summary

Still life means drawing a group of non-living objects – fruits, pots, glasses, vases – from observation. It trains the eye for shape, proportion, light and colour.

Key points

  • Arrange objects well with one light source
  • Start with basic forms: sphere, cylinder, cone, cube
  • Steps: arrange → sketch → outline → shade → colour
  • Shadow falls opposite to the light source

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What is a still life?

A group of non-living objects arranged and drawn.

Where does the shadow fall?

Away from the light source.

9Memory Drawing

Summary

Memory drawing means drawing a scene you have seen or experienced, like a fair, market, festival or village. It needs observation, imagination and composition.

Key points

  • Read the topic and picture the scene
  • Use the full page; keep the main figure large and central
  • Show action and movement in figures
  • Choose colours that suit the theme
  • Add small details

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What does memory drawing need?

Observation, imagination and good composition.

Give two topics.

A Diwali market, a village fair.

10Alekhan and Alankaran (Design)

Summary

Alekhan is decorative design. Motifs such as flowers, leaves, birds or geometric shapes are repeated to make patterns used on textiles, pottery, rangoli and buildings.

Key points

  • Motif: natural or geometric unit
  • Types: border, corner, all-over design
  • Symmetry and repetition create beauty
  • Examples: rangoli, alpana, embroidery, block printing

Practice questions (tap to see the answer)

What is a motif?

A basic design unit that is repeated to form a pattern.

Give two uses of design.

Textile printing and rangoli.
