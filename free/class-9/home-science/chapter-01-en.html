Home Management and Economics

1. Elements and Scope of Home Science

Home Science combines knowledge from many sciences for the welfare of family and society. Its aim is to make the home happy, healthy and well organised.

Key points
  • Branches: home management, food and nutrition, textiles, home nursing, child development
  • Scope: family, society and careers (dietician, designer, etc.)
  • Importance: all-round development of a person

2. Home Management in the Context of the Family

Home management is using resources wisely to achieve family goals. It includes planning, organising, controlling and evaluating.

Key points
  • Resources: human (time, energy, knowledge, skills) and material (money, goods)
  • Process: planning → organising → controlling → evaluating
  • The homemaker plays a key role

3. Work Management

Work management means doing tasks by plan so that time and energy are saved and fatigue is reduced.

Key points
  • Work schedules: daily, weekly, monthly, yearly
  • Work simplification and sharing of tasks among family members
  • Causes of fatigue: wrong posture, disorder

4. Family Economy and Basic Needs

Family economy means spending income through a budget and saving. Food, clothing and shelter are basic needs.

Key points
  • Income: monetary and real; budget estimates income and expenditure
  • Budgets: balanced, surplus, deficit
  • Savings give security for the future

Health

5. Meaning of Health and Personal Health Care

According to WHO, health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely absence of disease.

Key points
  • Personal hygiene: skin, hair, teeth, eyes, nails, clothes
  • Balanced diet, exercise, enough sleep and rest
  • Avoid bad habits

6. Local Health Institutions and Services

Many local institutions in villages and towns give health services, such as hospitals and health centres.

Key points
  • Primary/community health centres, district hospital
  • Mother-child welfare centres, family welfare, immunisation
  • Role of municipality/gram panchayat and Red Cross

Environment and Air

7. Effect of Environmental Pollution on Life

Contamination of air, water, noise and soil is environmental pollution, which affects health and life.

Key points
  • Causes: factories, vehicles, garbage, chemical fertilisers
  • Effects: breathing problems, water-borne diseases, hearing loss
  • Remedies: planting trees, waste disposal

8. Air: Importance of Pure Air and Ventilation

Pure air is essential for life. Arrangement for air to enter and leave a house is ventilation.

Key points
  • Air has about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, a little CO₂
  • Natural ventilation: windows, ventilators, doors
  • Artificial: fans, exhaust fans

9. Diseases Caused by Impure Air

Germs spread through impure air and the breath, cough and sneeze of patients, causing infectious diseases.

Key points
  • Diseases: tuberculosis, cold, pneumonia, diphtheria, smallpox, influenza, asthma
  • Spread: droplet infection
  • Prevention: vaccines, cleanliness, ventilated home, isolating patients

Clothing

10. Textile Fibres: Types and Daily Use

Cloth is made of fibres, which are natural or man-made.

Key points
  • Natural: cotton, wool, silk, linen
  • Man-made/synthetic: rayon, nylon, polyester
  • Cotton for summer, wool for winter

11. Personal Grooming: Suitable Dress

Being neat and wearing clothes suited to season, occasion and age is personal grooming.

Key points
  • Factors in choosing clothes: season, age, occasion, profession, budget
  • Colour and design to suit personality
  • Cleanliness and neatness are part of grooming

Food and Nutrition

12. Digestion of Food and Related Physiology

Digestion is the conversion of food into simple substances. It begins in the mouth and continues in the intestines.

Key points
  • Mouth: saliva digests starch
  • Stomach: pepsin and HCl act on protein; small intestine: most digestion and absorption
  • Bile from liver helps fat digestion; large intestine absorbs water

13. Composition, Classification and Functions of Foods

Food contains carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals and water.

Key points
  • Energy giving: carbohydrates, fats
  • Body building: proteins; protective: vitamins, minerals
  • Water is needed for digestion and body temperature

14. Balanced Diet

A diet containing all nutrients in proper amounts is a balanced diet.

Key points
  • Differs with age, sex, work and health
  • Include cereals, pulses, vegetables, fruits, milk
  • Deficiency causes malnutrition

First Aid

15. Principles of First Aid

First aid is immediate help given to a patient or injured person before a doctor arrives.

Key points
  • Stay calm, save life, stop bleeding
  • Give rest and reduce panic
  • Call a doctor quickly; keep a first-aid box

16. Common Household Accidents and Prevention

Accidents like burns, cuts, falls, drowning, electric shock and poisoning occur at home.

Key points
  • Prevention: keep floors dry, keep medicines/chemicals away
  • Care with electrical appliances
  • Supervise children

17. Common Home Remedies and Antidotes

Kitchen items help in minor ailments. In poisoning, immediate treatment and a doctor are essential.

Key points
  • Tulsi, ginger – cough/cold; turmeric – wounds; ajwain – stomach ache; mint – indigestion
  • In poisoning follow textbook measures, then go to hospital
  • Home remedies are not a final cure

18. Triangular and Roller Bandages and Their Use

Bandages cover wounds, stop bleeding and support limbs.

Key points
  • Triangular bandage: head, arm sling, foot
  • Roller bandage: wrist, knee, finger
  • Neither too loose nor too tight; use reef knot

Home Nursing

19. Home Nursing and the Home Nurse

Caring for a patient at home is home nursing and the caregiver is the home nurse.

Key points
  • Qualities: cleanliness, patience, sympathy, discipline
  • Check temperature, pulse, respiration
  • Follow the doctor's instructions

20. The Patient's Room and Its Arrangement

The patient's room should be airy, well lit, quiet and clean.

Key points
  • Little furniture and easy cleaning
  • Suitable temperature and light
  • Separate room for infectious disease

21. The Patient's Bed

The patient's bed should be comfortable, clean and convenient.

Key points
  • Clean wrinkle-free sheets
  • Method of changing the bed with the patient in it
  • Pillows/support for comfort; prevent bedsores
